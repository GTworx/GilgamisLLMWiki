## Biography & Context

Dr. Erdal Polat is a contemporary Turkish historian, academic, and researcher focusing on the cultural, intellectual, and institutional history of the early Turkish Republic. His scholarship investigates the scientific and institutional foundations established during Mustafa Kemal Atatürk’s nation-building process, particularly the role of archaeology, anthropology, and language studies in shaping modern civic identity.

In his comprehensive study published in the *Atatürk Araştırma Merkezi Dergisi* (2024), Polat analyzes the systematic archaeological policies implemented between 1923 and the 1950s, detailing how Atatürk transformed archaeology from an imperial treasure-hunting activity into a rigorous state discipline aimed at proving Anatolia's universal role as a cradle of civilization. Polat examines the life and legacy of Prof. Dr. Ekrem Akurgal, who was sent abroad to Germany by state scholarship in the 1930s and returned to establish the modern school of classical and Anatolian archaeology.
