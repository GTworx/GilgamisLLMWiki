## Biography & Context
**Eric W. Robinson** is Professor of History at Indiana University Bloomington and a leading classical historian specializing in ancient Greek democracy and political institutions. In his landmark monograph [[the-first-democracies-early-popular-government-outside-athens]] (*The First Democracies: Early Popular Government Outside Athens*, *Historia Einzelschriften* 101, Stuttgart: Franz Steiner Verlag, 1997; followed by *Democracy Beyond Athens: Popular Government in the Greek Classical Age*, 2011), Robinson dismantled the Athenocentric orthodoxy that *demokratia* was invented solely in Athens under Solon or Cleisthenes (source: [[the-first-democracies-early-popular-government-outside-athens]], pp. 1–3 [pp. 153–155]).

By combining Aristotle's *Politics*, Herodotus, Aeschylus, and archaic epigraphy across seventeen Greek city-states and colonies—including **Achaea, Acragas, Ambracia, Argos, Chios, Cyrene, Heraclea Pontica, Mantineia, Megara, Naxos, Samos, and Syracuse**—Robinson proved that popular governments (*demokratiai*) emerged independently across the archaic Greek world, Ionian Anatolia, and Magna Graecia by the mid-sixth century BC, well before 480 BC and independent of Athenian constitutional influence.
