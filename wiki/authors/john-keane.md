## Biography & Context
**John Keane** is Professor of Politics at the University of Sydney and at the Wissenschaftszentrum Berlin (WZB), founder of the Centre for the Study of Democracy in London, and one of the world's foremost historians and theorists of democracy. In his monumental work [[the-life-and-death-of-democracy-and-monitory-democracy]] (*The Life and Death of Democracy*, 2009—also featured in Chapter 14 of [[the-secret-history-of-democracy-primitive-democracy]]), Keane wrote the first full-scale global history of democracy in over a century (source: [[the-life-and-death-of-democracy-and-monitory-democracy]], pp. 1–2).

Keane traces democracy across three historical epochs: **Assembly Democracy** (originating c. 2500 BC in ancient Syria-Mesopotamia before moving westward to Phoenicia and Greece), **Representative Democracy** (parliamentary and constitutional territorial states from the medieval era through the Atlantic revolutions), and **Monitory Democracy**—a post-1945 paradigm coined by Keane in which periodic ballot-box elections are embedded within a dense web of extra-parliamentary **"watchdog" institutions** (constitutional courts, human rights commissions, integrity auditors, citizen assemblies, and digital investigative media) that subject political and corporate power to continuous public scrutiny.
