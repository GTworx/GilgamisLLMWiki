## Biography & Context

Graham Hancock (born 2 August 1950 in Edinburgh, Scotland) is a British author, journalist, and independent researcher recognized globally for his investigations into human prehistory, antediluvian civilizations, and ancient mysteries. Formerly the East Africa correspondent for *The Economist* and a writer for *The Sunday Times* and *The Guardian*, Hancock turned his investigative journalism background toward archaeology and mythology in the early 1990s, beginning with *The Sign and the Seal* (1992).

Hancock's work contends that a sophisticated, technologically or spiritually advanced civilization existed during the late Pleistocene epoch and was largely eradicated by catastrophic global upheavals between 12,800 and 11,600 years ago (the Younger Dryas period). He posits that survivors of this lost civilization transmitted fragments of high science, astronomical knowledge, and architectural engineering to hunter-gatherer populations worldwide—giving rise to megalithic structures such as the Giza Pyramids, Göbekli Tepe, Tiwanaku, and ancient Amazonian earthworks. While frequently contested by mainstream academic consensus, Hancock's work has fostered international public interest in archaeoastronomy, paleoclimatology, and the Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis.
