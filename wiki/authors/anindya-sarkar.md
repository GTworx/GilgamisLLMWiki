## Biography & Context
**Anindya Sarkar** is Professor of Geology and Geophysics at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kharagpur and Head of the National Facility for Stable Isotope Geochemistry, specializing in paleoclimatology, stable isotope geochemistry ($\delta^{18}\text{O}$ and $\delta^{13}\text{C}$), and South Asian geoarchaeology. As lead and corresponding author of [[oxygen-isotope-in-archaeological-bioapatites-from-india-harappan-civilization]] (*Nature Scientific Reports* 6:26555, 2016, co-authored with [[vasant-shinde]], Arati Deshpande Mukherjee, and L. S. Rao), Sarkar generated the first continuous, stratigraphically controlled oxygen isotope record of animal tooth and bone bioapatite directly from an excavated Harappan settlement trench at **Bhirrana** along the dried Ghaggar–Hakra (Vedic Saraswati) river channel (source: [[oxygen-isotope-in-archaeological-bioapatites-from-india-harappan-civilization]], pp. 1–7).

By combining bioapatite $\delta^{18}\text{O}_w$ meteoric water reconstructions with Optically Stimulated Luminescence (OSL) pottery dates and $^{14}\text{C}$ ages, Sarkar demonstrated both that the Pre-Harappan Hakra phase at Bhirrana dates back to **>9,000–8,000 years BP** and that Mature Harappan urbanism persisted across millennia of monsoon weakening ($7\text{–}2.8\text{ ka BP}$) by adaptively shifting from water-intensive wheat and barley to drought-resistant millets and rice.
