## Biography & Context
Kurt Lubinski (1899–1969) and Margot Lubinski (née Lewin) were German-Jewish photojournalists and ethnographic photographers who traveled extensively across Europe, Anatolia, Central Asia, and Ethiopia in the late 1920s and 1930s before emigrating from Nazi Germany to the Netherlands, London, and New York. Contributing to *National Geographic* and international illustrated weeklies, their candid street photography documented societies undergoing rapid modernization.

In [[gormedigimiz-turkiye-123-yillik-national-geographic-arsivi]], their September 1933 photographs of Ankara on the eve of the Turkish Republic's tenth anniversary capture the optimism, civic conviviality, and everyday urban life of early Republican citizens.
