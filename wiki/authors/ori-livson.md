## Biography & Context
**Ori Livson** is a mathematical researcher at the **Centre for Complex Systems** and the **School of Computer Science** at the **University of Sydney**, Australia (source: [[the-non-orientable-topology-of-condorcets-paradox]], Title Page & References, pp. 1, 18–20). Livson's research investigates the deep structural and topological dualities linking mathematical logic, self-referential paradoxes, Kurt Gödel's incompleteness theorems, and collective decision-making in complex systems.

In collaboration with [[siddharth-pritam]] and [[mikhail-prokopenko]] in [[the-non-orientable-topology-of-condorcets-paradox]] (2026), Livson extends Yuliy Baryshnikov's simplicial nerve complex framework of strict preferences to resolve Graciela Chichilnisky's 1980 conjecture on the topological nature of Condorcet's Paradox. By distinguishing valid (intransitive) preference cycles from contradictory (transitive) preference cycles, Livson and his co-authors prove that while valid cycles form orientable surfaces (the cylinder $S^1 \times [0,1]$ and 2-sphere $S^2$), contradictory cycles induce **non-orientable manifolds**—specifically the **Klein bottle** $K$ (when unrealised) and the **real projective plane** $\mathbb{RP}^2$ (when realised)—thereby reformulating Arrow's Impossibility Theorem as a topological incompatibility between orientable and non-orientable social choices.
