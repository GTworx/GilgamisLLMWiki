## Biography & Context
**Richard J. Evans** is a classical historian and professor of ancient history (associated with the University of South Africa and Cardiff University) specializing in Greek and Roman political biography, Hellenistic Syracuse, and classical historiography. In his 1998 review article in *Scholia* (ns Vol. 7, pp. 153–155, preserved in `ARTICLES-REPORTS/Democracy/World’s First Democracies book review_.pdf` and transcribed via `qwen3.8:latest` multimodal Vision OCR), Evans critically evaluates [[eric-w-robinson]]'s [[the-first-democracies-early-popular-government-outside-athens]].

Evans highlights how the simultaneous eighth-to-sixth-century BC Greek colonization movement across the Mediterranean and Black Sea naturally catalyzed participatory *polis* constitutions in cities such as **Argos, Chios, Cyrene, Mantineia, Naxos, and Syracuse**, exposing the inadequacy of ancient cyclical theories of constitutional change (*anakyklosis*) and confirming that pre-classical democracy was widespread outside Attica despite the surviving source bias toward Athenian writers (source: [[the-first-democracies-early-popular-government-outside-athens]], pp. 1–3 [pp. 153–155]).
