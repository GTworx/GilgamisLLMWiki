## Biography & Context

Dr. Robert M. Schoch is an American geologist, geophysicist, and associate professor of natural sciences at Boston University. He holds a Ph.D. in geology and geophysics from Yale University.

Schoch gained international prominence in 1991 when, alongside independent Egyptologist John Anthony West, he conducted geological investigations of the Great Sphinx of Giza. Schoch proved that the pronounced undulating erosion patterns on the Sphinx enclosure walls were caused by prolonged, heavy rainfall rather than wind-blown sand weathering. Because the Giza plateau was hyper-arid throughout dynastic Egyptian history (post-3000 BC) and experienced substantial pluvial precipitation only during the transition out of the last Ice Age (c. 10,000–5000 BC), Schoch concluded that the original core carving of the Sphinx dates back at least to the 7th or 6th millennium BC, if not earlier.
