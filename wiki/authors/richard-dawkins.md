## Biography & Context
**Clinton Richard Dawkins** (born 1941) is an eminent British evolutionary biologist, ethologist, Emeritus Fellow of New College, Oxford, and former University of Oxford Professor for Public Understanding of Science (1995–2008). Studying at Oxford under Nobel Prize-winning ethologist Niko Tinbergen, Dawkins achieved worldwide scientific and literary renown with [[the-selfish-gene]] (*The Selfish Gene*, 1976; 2nd ed. 1989; 30th Anniversary ed. 2006) and *The Extended Phenotype* (1982) (source: [[the-selfish-gene]], Intro to 30th Anniversary Ed., pp. vii–xiv).

Synthesizing the mathematical work of [[william-d-hamilton]], George C. Williams, John Maynard Smith, and Robert Trivers, Dawkins established the **gene-centric view of evolution**—distinguishing between potentially immortal digital **replicators** (genes) and transient organismal **vehicles** ("survival machines"). In Chapter 11 of [[the-selfish-gene]], he coined the term **meme** to describe units of cultural transmission evolving via imitation, and in Chapter 12 (*"Nice Guys Finish First"*), he popularized [[robert-axelrod]] and [[william-d-hamilton]]'s [[the-evolution-of-cooperation]], demonstrating why reciprocal cooperation (**TIT FOR TAT**) flourishes in a world of selfish replicators.
