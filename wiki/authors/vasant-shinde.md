## Biography & Context
**Vasant Shivram Shinde** is a preeminent Indian archaeologist, former Vice-Chancellor of Deccan College Post-Graduate and Research Institute in Pune, and Chief Director of the landmark excavations at **Rakhigarhi** (the largest known Harappan metropolis in the Ghaggar–Hakra basin), Girawad, and Farmana. As co-author of [[oxygen-isotope-in-archaeological-bioapatites-from-india-harappan-civilization]] (*Nature Scientific Reports* 6:26555, 2016, with [[anindya-sarkar]]), Shinde helped establish the early Holocene chronology ($>9\text{–}8\text{ ka BP}$ Hakra Ware horizon) and adaptive agro-ecological trajectory of the Indus–Saraswati (Ghaggar–Hakra) civilization (source: [[oxygen-isotope-in-archaeological-bioapatites-from-india-harappan-civilization]], pp. 1–4).

Shinde's fieldwork across the Ghaggar–Hakra tract—demonstrating indigenous continuity from early post-glacial Hakra settlements to Mature Harappan urbanism and decentralized corporate governance—directly complements [[graham-hancock]]'s post-glacial Indian antiquity synthesis in [[underworld-mysterious-origins-of-civilization]] and [[ekrem-akurgal]]'s indigenous Anatolian archaeological synthesis in [[cumhuriyet-donemi-arkeoloji-politikalari-ve-ekrem-akurgal]].
