## Biography & Context
**Morris Chang** (*Chang Chung-mou*, born 1931) is a Taiwanese-American electrical engineer, industrialist, and founder of **Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC)**—the world's most geopolitically indispensable semiconductor manufacturer. Educated at MIT (B.S. and M.S. in Mechanical Engineering) and Stanford University (Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering), Chang spent twenty-five years at **Texas Instruments**, where he pioneered statistical yield optimization and rose to head TI's global semiconductor business before being recruited in 1985 by Taiwanese technocrat **K. T. Li** to build Taiwan's microchip ecosystem (source: [[chip-war-the-fight-for-the-worlds-most-critical-technology]], Cast of Characters & ch. 29, 37, pp. 7, 166–171, 212–217).

As chronicled by [[chris-miller]] in [[chip-war-the-fight-for-the-worlds-most-critical-technology]], Chang founded **TSMC** in 1987 at age fifty-five around a radical strategic innovation: the **pure-play foundry** that pledged *never* to design its own chips or compete with its customers. By forging TSMC's **"Grand Alliance"** of fabless designers (Apple, Nvidia, Qualcomm, AMD), EDA software firms, and equipment makers (ASML), Chang transformed global computing and positioned Taiwan at the center of 21st-century technological deterrence.
