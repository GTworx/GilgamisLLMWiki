## Biography & Context
**Stephen Stockwell** is Professor Emeritus of Journalism and Communication at Griffith University, Australia, and a scholar of political communication, rhetoric, and the global history of democratic institutions. Co-editor (with [[benjamin-isakhan]]) of [[the-secret-history-of-democracy-primitive-democracy]] (*The Secret History of Democracy*, 2011), Stockwell authored the volume's Introduction (*"Democracy and History"*), Chapter 2 (*"Before Athens: Early Popular Government in Phoenicia and Greek City-States"*, pp. 35–48), Chapter 7 (*"Democratic Culture in the Early Venetian Republic"*, pp. 105–120), and the Conclusion (*"Democratizing the History of Democracy"*, pp. 219–224).

Stockwell's research demonstrates how maritime trading networks across Levantine Phoenician city-states (Byblos, Sidon, Tyre, and their western colony Carthage) and early archaic Greek poleis developed powerful citizen assemblies (*'am*) and suffete magistracies that directly mediated the transmission of participatory governance from the Bronze Age Near East into the Mediterranean basin—complementing [[eric-w-robinson]]'s findings in [[the-first-democracies-early-popular-government-outside-athens]].
