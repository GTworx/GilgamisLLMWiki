## Biography & Context
**Benjamin Isakhan** is Professor of International Politics and Founding Director of the *Polis* research network at Deakin University, Australia, and an international authority on Middle Eastern politics, cultural heritage destruction, and the global comparative history of democracy. In [[the-secret-history-of-democracy-primitive-democracy]] (*The Secret History of Democracy*, co-edited with [[stephen-stockwell]], Palgrave Macmillan, 2011), Isakhan challenges the Western academic orthodoxy that democracy originated solely in fifth-century BC Classical Athens (source: [[the-secret-history-of-democracy-primitive-democracy]], pp. 1–34).

Synthesizing Assyriologist [[thorkild-jacobsen]]'s cuneiform studies of ancient Sumer, Akkad, and Babylon with Classical Greek historiography, Isakhan proves in Chapter 1 (*"What is so 'Primitive' about 'Primitive Democracy'?"*) that ancient Middle Eastern city-state assemblies (*ukkin* / *puhrum*), legal codes (from Urukagina's ~2350 BC *amargi* liberty edicts to Ur-Nammu and Hammurabi), and collective judicial courts preceded Cleisthenic Athens by two millennia and were in no measurable sense "primitive." In Chapter 13 (*"The Streets of Iraq: Protests and Democracy after Saddam"*), he connects these deep indigenous traditions of public deliberation to contemporary Middle Eastern civic mobilization.
