## Biography & Context
Tim Ingold, FBA, FRSE (born 1948) is Professor Emeritus of Social Anthropology at the University of Aberdeen and one of the most influential contemporary anthropological theorists, renowned for bridging social anthropology, human ecology, evolutionary biology, art, and architecture.

In [[anthropology-why-it-matters]] (2018), Ingold defines anthropology as a generous, comparative inquiry into 'communal experiments in living'—studying *with* people rather than *about* them to address how humanity can forge a habitable future (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Anthropology_Why_It_Matters_-_Tim_Ingold.pdf, pp. 9–13, 151).
