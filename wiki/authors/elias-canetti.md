## Biography & Context
Elias Canetti (1905–1994) was a Bulgarian-born Sephardic Jewish novelist, playwright, and social theorist who wrote in German in interwar Vienna before emigrating to London in 1938 after the *Anschluss*. Awarded the 1981 Nobel Prize in Literature, Canetti is celebrated for his modernist novel *Die Blendung* (*Auto-da-Fé*, 1935), his monumental study of mass psychology and totalitarianism *Masse und Macht* (*Crowds and Power*, 1960), and his three-volume autobiography.

As analyzed by [[william-collins-donahue]] in [[the-end-of-modernism-elias-canettis-auto-da-fe]], Canetti's portrayal of sinologist Peter Kien, Therese Krumbholz, and Siegfried Fischerle exposes how ivory-tower humanism and Neo-Kantian inwardness capitulate before fascist crowd fanaticism.
