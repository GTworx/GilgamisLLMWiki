## Biography & Context

William Donald Hamilton (1936–2000) was a British evolutionary biologist and Royal Society Research Professor at the University of Oxford. Widely recognized as one of the greatest theoretical biologists of the 20th century, Hamilton formulated the mathematical basis for inclusive fitness theory and kin selection (Hamilton's Rule: $rB > C$) in his seminal 1964 papers.

In collaboration with political scientist Robert Axelrod, Hamilton co-authored the influential 1981 *Science* paper "The Evolution of Cooperation" (winner of the Newcomb Cleveland Prize), which was expanded into Chapter 5 of Axelrod's book *The Evolution of Cooperation*. Hamilton and Axelrod demonstrated how reciprocal altruism and TIT FOR TAT strategies can evolve in biological systems without relatedness, high cognition, or conscious foresight.
