## Biography & Context

Prof. Dr. Ekrem Akurgal (1911–2002) was Turkey's preeminent classical archaeologist, art historian, and humanist intellectual. Selected during the early Republic as part of Atatürk's educational mobilization to send promising youth to Europe ("You are being sent as sparks; you must return as volcanic fires"), Akurgal studied classical archaeology in Berlin under Gerhart Rodenwaldt between 1932 and 1940.

Returning to Turkey, Akurgal served as a professor at Ankara University's Faculty of Language, History, and Geography (DTCF) for over forty years. He conducted landmark excavations at Bayraklı (Old Smyrna), Phocaea (Foça), Pitane (Çandarlı), Erythrai (Ildırı), and Sinope. Akurgal revolutionized world archaeology by proving that archaic Greek art and philosophy were fundamentally ignited and nurtured by Eastern and Anatolian civilizations (Hittite, Hurrian, Urartian, Phrygian, and Lydian), articulating the concept of the "Anatolian Civilizations Synthesis" (*Anadolu Uygarlıkları Sentezi*).
