## Overview & Scope

Turkish Folk Literature (*Türk Halk Edebiyatı*) represents the living memory, philosophical worldview, and aesthetic consciousness of Anatolian civilization. Traditionally divided into Anonymous Folk Literature, Minstrel Literature (*Âşık Edebiyatı*), and Mystical Religious Folk Literature (*Tekke / Tasavvuf Edebiyatı*), it has preserved cultural identity across millennia through oral dissemination, itinerant bardic performance, and community gatherings.

Characterized by transparent vernacular Turkish rather than ornate court idioms, folk poetry operates on syllabic meter (predominantly 7, 8, and 11 syllables) and quatrain structures (*dörtlük*). The tradition carries profound civic responsibilities: the folk poet (*ozan*) serves as the conscience of society, bearing witness to historical trauma, celebrating romantic and natural beauty, and delivering fearless satirical critiques (*taşlama*) against social injustice and tyranny.
