# Genre: Turkology and Comparative Eurasian Philology

**Description**: Linguistic, epigraphic, runological, and onomastic investigation of the Turkic language family, ancient Central Asian runic inscriptions, and their structural relationships with wider Eurasian language groups.
**Key Themes**: [[eurasian-steppe-nomads-silk-road-and-ethnogenesis]], [[archaeology-and-national-identity]], [[panturkism-modernization-and-national-awakening]]
**Last updated**: 2026-11-24

---

## Overview & Scope
Turkology and Comparative Eurasian Philology investigates the linguistic origins, dialectology, and epigraphic records of Turkic-speaking peoples from the third millennium BCE to the modern era. Encompassing Orkhon-Yenisei runiform stelae, Old Uighur Buddhist manuscripts, Qarakhanid lexicography (Mahmûd al-Kâshgharî), and comparative Altaic linguistics, this genre analyzes the phonology, morphology, vowel harmony, and vocabulary that unified nomadic confederations across Inner Eurasia and their historical interactions with Chinese, Mongolic, Uralic, Iranian, and Semitic language spheres.
