## Overview & Scope

Game Theory analyzes situations where the outcome for each participant depends critically on the choices of all participants. Originating in the mathematical economics of John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern (1944) and the non-cooperative equilibrium concepts of John Nash (1950), game theory expanded into political science, evolutionary biology (Maynard Smith), and strategic studies (Thomas Schelling).

In this wiki, game theory serves as a fundamental analytical bridge connecting micro-level strategic choices (e.g., Axelrod's iterated Prisoner's Dilemma) with macro-level geopolitical dynamics (e.g., nuclear deterrence, arms race equilibria, and crisis brinkmanship).
