## Overview & Scope

Game Theory analyzes situations where the outcome for each participant depends critically on the choices of all participants. Originating in the mathematical economics of John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern (1944) and the non-cooperative equilibrium concepts of John Nash (1950), game theory expanded into political science, evolutionary biology (Maynard Smith), strategic studies (Thomas Schelling), and topological social choice theory.

In this wiki, game theory serves as a fundamental analytical bridge connecting micro-level strategic choices (e.g., Axelrod's iterated Prisoner's Dilemma), macro-level geopolitical dynamics (e.g., nuclear deterrence, arms race equilibria, and crisis brinkmanship), and the algebraic-topological structure of cyclic preferences in tournaments and majority voting (e.g., valid intransitive Rock-Paper-Scissors cycles on $S^1 \times [0,1]$ and $S^2$ versus contradictory transitive Condorcet cycles on the Klein bottle $K$ and real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$).

## Key Authors & Theorists

- [[robert-axelrod]]: Pioneer of evolutionary tournament game theory and reciprocity dynamics.
- [[william-d-hamilton]]: Evolutionary biology pioneer applying game theory to biological populations.
- [[frans-osinga]]: Strategic theorist connecting decision models to military theory.
- [[ori-livson]]: Complex systems mathematician analyzing cyclic games and topological social choice.
- [[siddharth-pritam]]: Computational and algebraic topologist modeling preference nerve complexes.
- [[mikhail-prokopenko]]: Complex systems theorist linking multi-agent game dynamics, information theory, and social choice.
