# Genre: Archaeoastronomy

**Summary**: The interdisciplinary study of how ancient civilizations understood, tracked, and incorporated celestial phenomena and astronomical alignments into their sacred architecture, monuments, calendars, and religious mythologies.  
**Last updated**: 2026-09-12

---

## Synthesis

Archaeoastronomy bridges observational astrophysics, geodesy, archaeology, and comparative mythology. It demonstrates that ancient monument builders—from the builders of Giza and Stonehenge to Göbekli Tepe, Tiwanaku, and North American mounds—possessed extraordinary knowledge of equinoxes, solstices, lunar standstills, and the slow 25,920-year cycle of the **precession of the equinoxes**. Key theories within this genre, such as the [[orion-correlation-and-sacred-astronomy]], propose that monuments functioned as stone "computers" locking terrestrial architecture to specific celestial epochs.
