## Overview & Scope
Clean Energy Systems, Hydrogen & Infrastructure Economics investigates the engineering, thermodynamics, and capital allocation required to transition global power and industrial grids to net-zero emissions. Encompassing green hydrogen electrolysis, round-trip storage efficiencies, hourly life-cycle marginal emissions modeling, utility distribution tariff reform, space-based solar power, and commercial battery gigafactory economics.

### Strategic Significance
Decarbonizing energy is the primary physical prerequisite for stabilizing planetary climate. Transforming fossil energy systems into electrified and molecular clean carriers demands unprecedented capital expenditure, transmission expansion, and market design that balances variable renewable intermittency with clean firm reliability.
