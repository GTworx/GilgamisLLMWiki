# Genre: Byzantine and Ottoman Historiography

**Description**: Primary chronicles, administrative documents, imperial transitions, and diplomatic histories detailing the collision, coexistence, and institutional succession between the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire and the Ottoman Empire.
**Key Themes**: [[byzantine-seljuk-ottoman-frontier-and-imperial-transition]], [[ataturk-republican-founding-and-ottoman-turkish-memory]], [[late-ottoman-conflict-palestine-zionism-and-contested-memory]]
**Last updated**: 2026-11-24

---

## Overview & Scope
Spanning from the eleventh-century arrival of the Seljuk Turks in Anatolia to the 1922 collapse of the Sublime Porte, Byzantine and Ottoman Historiography examines how imperial legitimacy, urban governance, frontier warfare, and Mediterranean trade were negotiated across the Marmara and Aegean basins. The genre brings together Byzantine court chronicles (Anna Comnena, Georges Pachymeres), Ottoman travelogues and administrative registers (Evliyâ Çelebi, Kanunnameler), and modern multi-archival syntheses (Lord Kinross, Kenneth W. Harl, İlber Ortaylı), demonstrating the structural and geographic continuities connecting Constantinople to Kostantiniyye.
