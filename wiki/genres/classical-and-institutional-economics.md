# Genre: Classical, Institutional, and Ecological Economics

**Description**: Foundational treatises and critical syntheses exploring the wealth of nations, resource limits, the sociology of money, inclusive vs. extractive institutions, urban capitalism, and macroeconomic stabilization.
**Key Themes**: [[inclusive-institutions-economic-thought-and-ecological-limits]], [[energy-transition-and-storage-economics]], [[political-intuitionism-administrative-state-and-capitalist-democracy]]
**Last updated**: 2026-11-24

---

## Overview & Scope
**Classical, Institutional, and Ecological Economics** traces how societies organize production, value, natural resources, and political institutions from 1776 to the 21st century.

In this wiki, the genre stretches from [[adam-smith]]'s [[the-wealth-of-nations]], [[william-stanley-jevons]]'s [[the-coal-question]], and [[georg-simmel]]'s [[the-philosophy-of-money]], through [[robert-l-heilbroner]]'s [[the-worldly-philosophers]] and [[e-f-schumacher]]'s human-scale [[small-is-beautiful]], to modern institutional and spatial political economy in [[daron-acemoglu]] & [[james-a-robinson]]'s [[why-nations-fail]], [[hakki-yirtici]]'s [[cagdas-kapitalizmin-mekansal-orgutlenmesi]], and [[david-owen]] & [[david-o-robinson]]'s IMF study [[russia-rebounds]].
