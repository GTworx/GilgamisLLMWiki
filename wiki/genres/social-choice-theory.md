## Overview & Scope
**Social Choice Theory** investigates how individual preferences, judgments, and welfare can be aggregated into coherent collective decisions. Originating in the 18th-century voting analyses of the Marquis de Condorcet and Jean-Charles de Borda and formalized in the 20th century by Kenneth Arrow's Impossibility Theorem (1950/1951), Amartya Sen, and Graciela Chichilnisky (1980), the field lies at the intersection of mathematical economics, political philosophy, logic, and topology.

Within the Gilgamis LLM Wiki, Social Choice Theory bridges two major analytical traditions:
1. **Topological Social Choice and Preference Cycles**: In [[the-non-orientable-topology-of-condorcets-paradox]] (2026), [[ori-livson]], [[siddharth-pritam]], and [[mikhail-prokopenko]] resolve Chichilnisky's 1980 conjecture by proving that while valid (intransitive) preference cycles form orientable surfaces (the cylinder $S^1 \times [0,1]$ and 2-sphere $S^2$), **contradictory (transitive) Condorcet cycles** generate **non-orientable manifolds**—specifically the **Klein bottle** $K$ (when unrealised) and the **real projective plane** $\mathbb{RP}^2$ (when realised). They further prove that Arrow's Unrestricted Domain axiom requires **Orientable Social Choices** (Theorem 6), whereas Non-Dictatorship requires **Non-Orientable Social Choices** (Theorem 7), reducing Arrow's Impossibility Theorem to the topological impossibility of a manifold being simultaneously orientable and non-orientable.
2. **Evolutionary and Strategic Escape from Social Dilemmas**: In [[the-evolution-of-cooperation]] (1984), [[robert-axelrod]] examines how decentralized agents overcome static collective-action traps through repeated interaction ("the shadow of the future") and reciprocal strategies such as TIT FOR TAT.
