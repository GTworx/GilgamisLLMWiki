## Overview & Scope

Anatolian Humanism (*Anadolu Hümanizmi*) is a profound philosophical synthesis that emerged in 13th-century Anatolia amidst the geopolitical turbulence of the Mongol invasions and Crusades. Crystallized in the teachings and verses of Yunus Emre, Hacı Bektaş Veli, Mevlana Jalaluddin Rumi, and Abdal Musa, this tradition posits that the divine resides not in external rituals or physical monuments, but within the human heart (*gönül*).

Core philosophical tenets include:
1. **Universal Egalitarianism**: "Viewing seventy-two nations with one single eye" (*Yetmiş iki millete bir göz ile bakmak*), explicitly repudiating religious, racial, and sectarian hierarchies.
2. **The Primacy of Love**: Recognizing compassion and love not merely as sentimental affect, but as an ontological foundation and social science (*sevgi ilimi*).
3. **Peaceful Coexistence & Reconciliation**: Rejecting violent coercion in favor of empathy, spiritual enlightenment, and dialogue.

In the 20th and 21st centuries, poets such as Âşık Veysel, Mahzuni Şerif, and Âşık Borani revitalized this tradition, framing human rights, secular civic equality, and democratic brotherhood through classical humanist idioms.
