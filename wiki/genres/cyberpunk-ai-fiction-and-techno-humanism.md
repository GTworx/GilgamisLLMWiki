## Overview & Scope
**Cyberpunk, AI Fiction, and Techno-Humanism** uses high-craft literary fiction as a moral and sociological laboratory for emerging technologies—asking what remains irreplaceably human when machines think, compose poetry, tutor children, and love.

This genre is anchored by a remarkable contemporary triad: [[neal-stephenson]]'s post-cyberpunk nanotechnology and adaptive-education epic [[the-diamond-age]], [[ian-mcewan]]'s counterfactual Turing-era ethical drama [[machines-like-me]], and [[kazuo-ishiguro]]'s tender, unsettling artificial-friend meditation [[klara-and-the-sun]]—standing directly on the speculative foundation laid by [[mary-shelley]]'s [[the-last-man-volume-one]].
