# Genre: Comparative Religion and Islamic History

**Description**: Historical, archaeological, and scientific investigations tracing ancient Near Eastern religious origins, Iranian pluralism, the early Islamic caliphate, and Islamic Golden Age science.
**Key Themes**: [[abrahamic-origins-sectarian-schism-and-iranian-mesopotamian-continuity]], [[islamic-golden-age-science-and-civilizational-transmission]], [[sufi-ecstasy-spiritual-love-and-the-rumi-shams-axis]]
**Last updated**: 2026-11-23

## Overview & Scope
**Comparative Religion and Islamic History** grounds sacred traditions inside empirical history, cuneiform archaeology, manuscript science, and geopolitical turning points.

Across the corpus, this genre connects [[muazzez-ilmiye-cig]]'s Sumerian-Abrahamic parallels ([[kuran-incil-ve-tevratin-sumerdeki-kokeni]]), critical scholarship on [[richard-c-foltz]]'s survey of Iranian religious pluralism ([[spirituality-in-the-land-of-the-noble-reviews]]), [[lesley-hazleton]]'s narrative history of the Sunni–Shia fracture ([[after-the-prophet-shia-sunni-split]]), [[fuat-sezgin]] & [[eckhard-neubauer]]'s monumental reconstruction of medieval Islamic science ([[islamda-bilim-ve-teknik-science-and-technology-in-islam]]), and historical Sufi studies ([[me-and-rumi-shams-i-tabrizi]], [[the-life-and-work-of-jalal-ud-din-rumi]]).
