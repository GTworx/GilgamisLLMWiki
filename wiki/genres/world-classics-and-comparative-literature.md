# Genre: World Classics and Comparative Literature

**Description**: Canonical narratives, verse cycles, and cross-cultural translations spanning medieval Italy, Seljuk Persia, 19th-century Britain and Russia, modern Turkey, and contemporary multilingual storytelling.
**Key Themes**: [[persian-sufism-skepticism-and-comparative-translation]], [[ottoman-turkish-modernization-poetics-and-temporal-satire]], [[social-critique-and-moral-conscience]]
**Last updated**: 2026-11-22

## Overview & Scope
**World Classics and Comparative Literature** brings timeless literary works from distinct civilizations into dialogue—revealing recurring human struggles with mortality, tyranny, satire, faith, and translation.

Across the corpus, this genre connects [[giovanni-boccaccio]]'s Renaissance realism ([[the-decameron-volume-three]]), [[mary-shelley]]'s Romantic apocalyptic vision ([[the-last-man-volume-one]]), [[leo-tolstoy]]'s Caucasian masterwork ([[hadji-murad]]) and cross-religious ethical inquiry ([[tolstoy-treatise-on-muhammad]]), three landmark scholarly editions of [[omar-khayyam]] ([[the-rubaiyat-of-omar-khayyam-bodleian-facsimile]], [[the-sufistic-quatrains-of-omar-khayyam]], [[the-rubaiyat-of-omar-khayyam-batson-ross]]), [[ahmet-hamdi-tanpinar]]'s Turkish modernist satire ([[saatleri-ayarlama-enstitusu]]), and [[frederic-bibard]]'s graded French storytelling ([[30-french-short-stories-for-complete-beginners]]).
