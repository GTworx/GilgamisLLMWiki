# Genre: Philosophy and Political Economy of Music

**Description**: Theoretical and historical analysis of sound, noise, musical forms, and recording technologies as prophetic mirrors and instruments of social and economic order.

## Overview & Scope
The **Philosophy and Political Economy of Music** investigates how societies draw the boundary between 'noise' (perceived as disorder, violence, and subversion) and 'music' (channelized harmony, ritual order, and aesthetic value). Rather than treating musical history as an isolated sequence of stylistic innovations, this genre examines how musical production networks prefigure transformations in political economy, property rights, and state power.

In the Gilgamis LLM Wiki, this genre is anchored by [[jacques-attali]]'s [[noise-the-political-economy-of-music]] (with contributions by [[fredric-jameson]] and [[susan-mcclary]]) and dialogues with [[thorsten-hennig-thurau]] and [[mark-b-houston]]'s [[entertainment-science-data-analytics-and-practical-theory]] as well as the oral Anatolian minstrel tradition in [[asik-borani-soz-insanin-aynasi]].
