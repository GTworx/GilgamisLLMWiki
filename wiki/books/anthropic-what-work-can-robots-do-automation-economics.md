## Executive Summary
Published in 2026 by Anthropic's research economics team, this 42-page technical inquiry evaluates the physical, sensory, and cognitive boundaries of robotic automation in the global economy. Decomposing over 800 occupational tasks cataloged in the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) O*NET database, Anthropic investigates the friction between digital cognitive capability and physical embodiment. The report identifies 'Moravec's Paradox at scale'—why high-level mathematical and creative reasoning automated rapidly, while fine-motor tactile dexterity (threading wires, handling deformable textiles, non-rigid manipulation) remains extraordinarily difficult and compute-intensive.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Task-Level Dissection**: Demonstrates that whole jobs are rarely automatable; rather, specific physical sub-tasks (pallet sorting, welding) automate while contextual setup remains human-bound.
- **Tactile & Contact Physics Gaps**: The primary limiter of robotics is not vision or language, but tactile feedback and real-time contact dynamics during high-frequency manipulation.
- **Economic Thresholds of Embodiment**: Analyzes capital payback periods across logistics, agriculture, and healthcare, proving that robots require 99.99% operational reliability to displace human labor.
- **Cobot Hybrid Workflows**: Predicts that human-robot collaborative architectures (cobots) will dominate the next decade rather than fully autonomous unmanned facilities.
