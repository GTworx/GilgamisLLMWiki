## Executive Summary

Authored by Tulane University Professor of Classical and Byzantine History **Kenneth W. Harl** (The Great Courses / The Teaching Company, 2014, Copyright p. 2, Bio p. 3; 463 pp.), *The Barbarian Empires of the Steppes* delivers a sweeping thirty-six-lecture historical course surveying 6,000 years of Eurasian steppe ecology, equestrian military revolutions, and nomadic empire-building from the Pontic-Caspian grasslands to the gates of Vienna, Baghdad, Delhi, and Beijing.

Moving systematically from **Steppes and Peoples** and **The Rise of the Steppe Nomads** (Lectures 1–2) through Scythians, Sarmatians, Xiongnu, Kushans, Huns, Avars, **Göktürks**, **Uyğurs**, **Khazars**, **Seljuks**, **Genghis Khan and the Mongol World Empire**, **Tamerlane (Timur)**, **Mughals**, **Safavids**, **Ottomans**, and the final triumph of gunpowder empires (Muscovy and Qing China), Harl shows how steppe horse-archers repeatedly acted as history's ultimate geopolitical catalysts and trade unifiers.

---

## Core Historical Themes

1. **Steppe Ecology & Tactical Asymmetry**: Explains how pastoral mobility, composite reflex bows, stirrups, and decimal command structures allowed small nomadic confederations to defeat vastly larger sedentary armies until artillery and flintlock armies closed the steppe frontier.
2. **State Formation Through Nomad-Sedentary Fusion**: Traces how Turkic and Mongolic dynasties (Seljuks, Ilkhanids, Yuan, Timurids, Mughals, Ottomans) fused steppe military aristocracy with Persian, Islamic, Byzantine, and Chinese bureaucratic traditions to govern enduring multi-ethnic empires.
3. **Pedagogical Clarity & Synthesis**: Includes structured lecture outlines, timelines, glossaries, maps, and curated reading lists, making it an ideal panoramic curriculum across Eurasian history.
