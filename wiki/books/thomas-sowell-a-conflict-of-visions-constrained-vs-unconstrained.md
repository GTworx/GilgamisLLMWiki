## Summary
Masterwork of political sociology and intellectual history diagnosing the fundamental divide in human political conflict: the 'Constrained' (tragic) vision versus the 'Unconstrained' (utopian) vision of human nature.

## Analytical Overview
Sowell demonstrates that centuries of political clashes over justice, equality, and power stem not from differing moral intentions, but from two incompatible visions of human limitations. The Constrained vision (Smith, Burke, the Federalists) accepts human nature as inherently flawed, prioritizing systemic trade-offs, incentives, and rule of law; the Unconstrained vision (Rousseau, Condorcet, Godwin) views human nature as malleable and perfectible, demanding direct solutions to injustice administered by wise articulators.
