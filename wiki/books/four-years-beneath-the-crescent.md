## Executive Summary

Written by Venezuelan soldier of fortune, polyglot, and decorated Ottoman cavalry officer **Rafael de Nogales Méndez** (1879–1936)—who served throughout World War I as Inspector-General of Turkish Forces in Armenia and Military Governor of Egyptian Sinai—and translated from Spanish by poet **Muna Lee** with an Introduction by British military observer **Lt.-Col. Edward Davis** (Charles Scribner's Sons, 1926, Title Page p. 9, Copyright p. 10; 474 pp.), *Four Years Beneath the Crescent* is one of the most extraordinary eyewitness military memoirs of the Ottoman First World War fronts.

As a Catholic Venezuelan volunteer who held high rank inside the Ottoman Army yet remained an outsider to both Entente propaganda and CUP party ideology, Nogales offers stark, unvarnished frontline observations of the **Siege of Van (1915)**, the Eastern Anatolian/Caucasus campaigns, the **Wartime Armenian deportations and massacres** (which he fiercely denounced while distinguishing regular Ottoman soldiers from CUP political operatives and irregulars), the **Sinai and Palestine campaigns** (Gaza, Beersheba, Jerusalem) alongside Liman von Sanders and Mustafa Kemal, and the final 1918 collapse.

---

## Core Primary-Source Value

1. **Rare Neutral-Outsider Military Testimony**: Frequently cited by historians across *all* schools of late-Ottoman historiography because Nogales praised ordinary Turkish soldiers' bravery and endurance while recording brutal wartime atrocities he personally witnessed in Van, Bitlis, and Diyarbakır.
2. **Multi-Front Operational Detail**: Chronicles logistics, desert cavalry tactics, tribal dynamics, and German-Ottoman command friction across the Caucasus, Mesopotamia, and Palestine fronts.
3. **Humanitarian & Political Complexity**: Captures the tragic entanglement of imperial collapse, Great Power proxy warfare, communal violence, and individual moral courage amid total war.
