## Summary
A foundational bioethics and neurotechnology compendium examining the ethical, legal, and human rights frontiers of invasive and non-invasive brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). Featuring Stephen Rainey's *Brain Recording, Mind-Reading, and Neurotechnology*, Rafael Yuste and the Morningside Group's landmark Nature consensus on *Four Ethical Priorities for Neurotechnologies and AI*, and clinical translational insights on implanted neural chips.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
Between 2017 and 2024, commercial ventures (Neuralink, Synchron, Blackrock Neurotech) and academic laboratories achieved astonishing breakthroughs in neural decoding: restoring speech in paralyzed patients, robotic arm manipulation via motor cortex implants, and consumer EEG headband tracking. Recognizing that neural data represents the final uncolonized frontier of human intimacy, leading neuroscientists mobilized to establish international 'Neurorights'.

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
The work defines the five foundational 'Neurorights' proposed by Rafael Yuste and the Morningside Consensus: 1) Cognitive Liberty (the right to control one's own mental processes); 2) Mental Privacy (the right to prevent neural recordings from unauthorized reading); 3) Psychological Continuity and Identity (protecting selfhood from neural stimulation alteration); 4) Fair Access to Augmentation (mitigating genetic/neural cognitive caste divides); and 5) Protection from Algorithmic Bias in Neural Decoding.

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
Stephen Rainey evaluates technical benchmarks: modern deep-learning decoders can reconstruct inner speech phonemes from electrocorticography (ECoG) grids with over 80% accuracy, and non-invasive fMRI models can decode conceptual meaning from visual imagery. The authors highlight severe regulatory vacuums: consumer neuro-wearables (sleep monitors, gaming headbands) operate outside HIPAA health data protections, creating massive commercial risks of covert cognitive surveillance and biometric manipulation.

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
This compendium is the definitive ethical charter for the neurotechnology century. By establishing that brain data is fundamentally distinct from other digital telemetry, it provides the essential legal framework for constitutional amendments protecting cognitive liberty.
