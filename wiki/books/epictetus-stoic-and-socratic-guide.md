## Executive Summary

Written by UC Berkeley classical philosopher **A. A. Long** (2002, Oxford Clarendon Press, pp. 4–8), *Epictetus: A Stoic and Socratic Guide to Life* is the premier modern scholarly study and translated anthology of **Epictetus** (c. 50–135 CE)—the former Phrygian slave who became one of antiquity's greatest practical ethicists and the primary philosophical mentor behind Marcus Aurelius's *Meditations*.

Examining Epictetus's *Discourses* (recorded by his student Arrian) alongside the *Enchiridion*, Long reveals how Epictetus fused rigorous **Stoic ethics** with the lively dialectical **Socratic paradigm** (Chapter 3, p. 8), centering human freedom entirely on **prohairesis** (moral volition/choice) and the razor-sharp **Dichotomy of Control**: separating what is truly *up to us* (judgments, desires, intentions) from what is *not up to us* (body, reputation, wealth, status).

---

## Key Philosophical Contributions

1. **Autonomy From Inside Slavery**: Having survived physical enslavement and lameness, Epictetus demonstrates that chains can bind a body, yet no tyrant can compel a disciplined mind's assent.
2. **Socratic Pedagogical Craft**: Long shows how Epictetus uses sharp dialogical questioning, irony, and concrete everyday examples—not passive preaching—to shock students into self-examination.
3. **Care of the Self & Social Roles**: Clarifies that Stoic detachment (*apatheia*) never means cold apathy; it frees individuals to fulfill civic and familial roles with steady warmth and integrity.
