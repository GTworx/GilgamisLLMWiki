## Executive Summary

First published in 1875 by nineteenth-century American Quaker freethinker and polemicist **Kersey Graves** (1813–1883; Global Grey 2018 edition, pp. 2–5; 352 pp.; consolidated with the 352-pp. duplicate in `Turkiye and Region/`), *The World's Sixteen Crucified Saviors* is a classic—and famously uncritical—artifact of nineteenth-century **Christ-mythicism** and radical comparative religion.

Across thirty-two+ chapters (Contents, pp. 4–5), Graves compiles sweeping parallels between New Testament narratives (virgin birth, December 25th nativity, star of Bethlehem, miracles, crucifixion, resurrection, baptism, Eucharist) and purported "Oriental and heathen saviors"—including Krishna, Buddha, Mithra, Osiris, Horus, Prometheus, Quetzalcoatl, and Attis—arguing that Christian theology was assembled wholesale from earlier solar and mystery myths.

---

## Core Historical & Historiographical Framing

1. **Nineteenth-Century Freethought Polemic**: Captures the Victorian wave of popular skepticism following Darwin and early Orientalist translations (especially Godfrey Higgins's *Anacalypsis*), challenging orthodox literalism through comparative mythography.
2. **Critical Source Evaluation**: Modern historians of religion and classicists overwhelmingly critique Graves for relying on secondary/erroneous nineteenth-century compilations, conflating distinct myths, and asserting "crucifixions" (e.g., for Krishna or Buddha) absent from primary Sanskrit, Pali, or Egyptian texts.
3. **Cultural Afterlife**: Despite lacking academic credibility as primary scholarship, Graves's work heavily influenced twentieth- and twenty-first-century popular mythicist documentaries and online comparative-religion debates, making it an important primary document in the *history of skepticism*.
