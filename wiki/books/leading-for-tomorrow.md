## Executive Summary

*Leading for Tomorrow: Unlocking Human Potential in the Era of Continuous Change and Endless Possibility* (2026), edited by humanistic psychologist Scott Barry Kaufman and technology strategist Chris Shipley with a foreword by strategic inflection point authority Rita McGrath, is a landmark 22-chapter anthology produced through a collaboration between **Thinkers50** and the **Silicon Guild**. The volume addresses a historic civilizational turning point: as generative artificial intelligence (processing 2.5 billion queries daily on ChatGPT alone by fall 2025) collapses technological adoption curves onto one another, organizations no longer pass through discrete disruptions—they inhabit **continuous disruption** (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, Foreword & Intro, pp. ix–xx).

Across 22 essays by world-renowned management thinkers—including Tom Kelley, Scott D. Anthony, Jacqueline Novogratz, Lisa Kay Solomon, Chris Yeh, Jean Oelwang, Sanyin Siang, Nancy Duarte, Chip Conley, Brian Solis, Whitney Johnson, Jonathan Haidt, Caroline Webb, Amy C. Edmondson, Zeynep Ton, April Rinne, Hal Gregersen, Sophie Hamilton, Jennifer Aaker, John Hagel, Morra Aarons-Mele, Safi Bahcall, and Frans Johansson—the book argues that industrial-era productivity metrics (Douglas McGregor's *Theory X* and even *Theory Y*) must give way to Abraham Maslow's **Theory Z** and the **Sailboat Metaphor of Human Needs**. In an era where machines commoditize routine execution and analysis, sustainable competitive advantage shifts to uniquely human capacities: dignity, empathy, psychological safety, moral wisdom, fearless inquiry, and self-transcendence (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, Intro, pp. xix–xxv).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Foreword: Between Revolutions — The Human Bridge to Our Next Golden Age (Rita McGrath)** (pp. ix–xvi)
  * Applies economic historian Carlota Perez's framework (*Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital*, 2002): every technological revolution moves from an *installation phase* (creative destruction, speculative bubbles, inequality, populism) through a *turning point* to a *deployment phase* (Golden Age). Just as post-WWII leaders built the Marshall Plan, Bretton Woods, and the GI Bill, today's leaders must build "permissionless," network-based organizations centered on human flourishing rather than extraction (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, Foreword, pp. ix–xv).

* **Introduction: Toward a Human-Centered Future (Scott Barry Kaufman & Chris Shipley)** (pp. xvii–xxv)
  * *Human Adaptability Beats Operational Efficiency*: Contrasts McGregor's *Theory X* (coercive control) and *Theory Y* (intrinsic motivation) with Maslow's *Theory Z* (self-transcendence and synergy).
  * *The Sailboat Metaphor of Human Needs*: Corrects the myth of "Maslow's pyramid" (which Maslow never drew), replacing it with a dynamic **sailboat**: the hull represents *security* (safety, connection, self-esteem), while opening the sail represents *growth* (exploration, love, purpose). Defines five new leadership competencies, including **resilience as recovery speed** and leadership as *curation* rather than control (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, Intro, pp. xx–xxv).

* **Chapters 1–6: Human Touch, Dignity, and Relational Ecosystems** (pp. 1–74)
  * **Ch. 1 — The Human Touch (Tom Kelley)** (pp. 1–14): Drawing on 30+ years at IDEO and interviews with former Ford CEO Jim Hackett, identifies six future-proof leadership skills AI cannot replace: inspiring shared vision, building trust, making tough moral decisions, managing crises, developing people, and forging proactive "social contracts" with talent.
  * **Ch. 2 — Extraordinary Possibility in Extraordinary Times (Scott D. Anthony)** (pp. 15–28): Shows how leaders spot weak signals and "near misses" before disruption hits (e.g., King & Wood Mallesons in Australia).
  * **Ch. 3 — Rethinking Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs: Dignity Must Come First (Jacqueline Novogratz)** (pp. 29–42): Drawing on Acumen's impact across 700+ million lives, argues that **dignity is agency**—and must sit at the very base of human needs rather than waiting until material needs are met.
  * **Ch. 4 — Futures-Centered Leadership (Lisa Kay Solomon)** (pp. 43–56): Teaches strategic foresight and experimental prototyping (e.g., Georgia Tech coach Eric Reveno's "All Vote, No Play" civic movement).
  * **Ch. 5 — In Theory There Is No Difference (Chris Yeh)** (pp. 57–66): Contrasts the clean elegance of strategic theory with the messy emotional realities of human team members.
  * **Ch. 6 — Deep Connections (Jean Oelwang)** (pp. 67–74): Virgin Unite's founding CEO details how deep relational ecosystems drive systemic planetary and corporate transformation (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, ch. 1–6, pp. 1–74).

* **Chapters 7–13: Gift Economies, Wisdom, AIQ, and Bridging Moral Divides** (pp. 75–162)
  * **Ch. 7 — The Fire We Gift (Sanyin Siang)** (pp. 75–82): Formulates the **modern knowledge gift economy**: when AI makes raw information abundant and free, value shifts from possessing knowledge to the trusted relational connection through which it is gifted.
  * **Ch. 8 — Leading with Empathy in a Whirlwind of Change (Nancy Duarte)** (pp. 83–94): Explains how organizational ceremonies and rituals (such as Salesforce's annual V2MOM alignment and symbolic process-retirements) anchor teams amid turbulence.
  * **Ch. 9 — In the Era of AI, Successful Leaders Value Wisdom (Chip Conley)** (pp. 95–104): Modern Elder Academy founder explains why intergenerational collaboration, pattern recognition, and contextual prompt framing turn seasoned wisdom into an AI superpower.
  * **Ch. 10 — Leading with an Augmented Intelligence Mindset (Brian Solis)** (pp. 105–118): Introduces **AIQ (Augmented Intelligence Quotient)**—upgrading a leader's "mental operating system" beyond mere automation (Stage 1) to human-machine co-innovation.
  * **Ch. 11 — When the Ground Gives Way (Whitney Johnson)** (pp. 119–128): Applies the **S Curve of Learning** to personal and organizational reinvention.
  * **Ch. 12 — Leading Across Divides (Jonathan Haidt & Caroline Webb)** (pp. 129–142): Applies Moral Foundations Theory and behavioral science to bridge ideological, generational, and cultural polarization inside organizations.
  * **Ch. 13 — Inside the Motivated Mind (Caroline Webb)** (pp. 143–162): Translates neuroscience and behavioral economics into daily practices that deactivate defensive brain threat responses (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, ch. 7–13, pp. 75–162).

* **Chapters 14–22: Psychological Safety, Good Jobs, Inquiry, and Escape Velocity** (pp. 163–296)
  * **Ch. 14 — Think Like a Scientist (Amy C. Edmondson)** (pp. 163–180): Connects **psychological safety** with "intelligent failure"—treating strategic assumptions as hypotheses to be tested rigorously.
  * **Ch. 15 — The Good Jobs Strategy (Zeynep Ton)** (pp. 181–196): Demonstrates how investing in frontline worker dignity, living wages, and operational slack lowers costs and boosts profitability.
  * **Ch. 16 — Invisible Wisdom (April Rinne)** (pp. 197–208): Cultivating a "Flux Mindset" and comfort with ambiguity.
  * **Ch. 17 — Reclaiming the Human Art of Inquiry in an Age of Instant Answers (Hal Gregersen)** (pp. 209–220): Why "question bursts" and fearless inquiry matter more than ever when generative AI supplies instant, plausible answers.
  * **Ch. 18 — What Matters Now (Sophie Hamilton & Jennifer Aaker)** (pp. 221–238): Introduces the **AI Mirror Exercise**—using daily and weekly AI reflection prompts to uncover hidden behavioral patterns and align time with deep human values.
  * **Ch. 19 — Beyond Fear (John Hagel)** (pp. 239–248): Shifting organizational narratives from threat-based fear to "passion of the explorer."
  * **Ch. 20 — AI Anxiety Is Real. How You Lead Through It Is a Choice (Morra Aarons-Mele)** (pp. 249–274): Addresses AI-induced loss of meaning and control through transparent communication and emotional processing.
  * **Ch. 21 — 300 Years of Market Shocks, One Clear Lesson (Safi Bahcall)** (pp. 275–280): Historical analysis of "Apex Innovators" who invest counter-cyclically in loonshots during crises.
  * **Ch. 22 — Escape Velocity (Frans Johansson)** (pp. 281–296): How organizations break free of the gravitational well of core-business predictability through iterative, high-volume experimentation (e.g., NVIDIA's GPU neural network pivot, Amazon Web Services) (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, ch. 14–22, pp. 163–296).

## Key Quotes & Citations

* "We no longer live through disruptions; we live inside continuous disruption. And that demands a wholly different kind of leadership—one measured not by productivity but by the capacity to harness the human potential to think, connect, innovate, and act meaningfully and purposefully even as the ground keeps moving beneath us." (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, Intro, p. xviii)
* "When many people think of Maslow, they typically think of the pyramid of needs. But Maslow never actually drew a pyramid... A better metaphor for self-actualization is a sailboat charting its course in the vast unknown of the sea." (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, Intro, p. xxii)
* "Dignity is agency. It derives from being seen, valued, and trusted. It entails having the freedom, power, choice, and opportunity to influence one's own life. Dignity requires seeing no one above or below you but treating others—and being treated—with humanity." (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, ch. 3, p. 29)
* "Connection is the new currency that augments the value of the gift of that knowledge. That's the essence of the modern knowledge gift economy: Value is created not by possession but by connection." (source: BOOKS/Leadership_/2026 LEADING FOR TOMORROW.pdf, ch. 7, p. 77)
