## Executive Summary
This 12-page systems engineering and human-swarm interaction (HSI) research paper investigates the empirical and theoretical limits of whether a single human operator can effectively supervise a decentralized swarm of 100 heterogeneous autonomous robots (aerial drones, ground rovers, and quadruped scouts). By evaluating operator workload metrics, situational awareness decay, and attention switching delays during dynamic obstacle navigation and perimeter search tasks, the authors demonstrate that direct teleoperation collapses when controlling >5 agents. Instead, the paper proves that scalable oversight requires hierarchical intent-based playbooks and adaptive autonomy thresholds.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Cognitive Bandwidth Saturation**: A human supervisor cannot monitor more than 4–6 continuous video feeds or teleoperation streams simultaneously without severe cognitive tunnel vision.
- **Playbook-Based Delegation**: Swarm scaling to 100+ units requires macro-intent commands ('secure perimeter', 'converge on coordinate') where local flocking and collision avoidance are resolved by decentralized algorithms.
- **Heterogeneous Coordination**: Combining aerial recon with ground manipulation requires role-differentiated agent communication protocols that compress status reports into single-glance spatial dashboards.
- **Defense and Disaster Utility**: Validates human-swarm teaming for urban search-and-rescue, wildfire containment, and military reconnaissance.
