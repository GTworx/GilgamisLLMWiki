## Executive Summary

This two-part institutional research dossier by Jones Lang LaSalle (JLL) analyzes the macroeconomic, leasing, and capital markets environment governing data center real estate globally and across North America. JLL explores the widening gulf between soaring enterprise and hyperscale digital absorption and the physical constraints imposed by electrical substation capacity and equipment backlogs. The report details the influx of institutional private capital (infrastructure funds, sovereign wealth funds, private equity) into data center joint ventures and evaluates the rise of edge computing, sustainability certifications, and power purchase strategies across key digital corridors.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Capital Inflows and Infrastructure Fund Consolidation**: Global institutional investors and sovereign wealth funds are committing tens of billions of dollars to data center platforms, viewing digital infrastructure as a premier real asset class with defensive yield and high inflation-hedging qualities.
- **North American Supply Absorption**: North American net absorption surged to record highs in 2023, led by hyperscaler commitments in Northern Virginia, Dallas, Chicago, and Phoenix, driving vacancy rates to near zero in prime submarkets.
- **Power Procurement as Core Competency**: Institutional data center developers have transformed their business models, shifting from traditional real estate developers to specialized energy procurement and utility negotiation firms.
- **Sustainability & Net-Zero Mandates**: Hyperscalers' aggressive carbon neutrality commitments are reshaping procurement criteria, prioritizing facilities with access to round-the-clock green power and zero-water cooling designs.
- **Edge and Regional Expansion**: Even as hyperscale campuses grow in multi-hundred-megawatt increments, localized edge data centers are proliferating to serve IoT, autonomous systems, and enterprise hybrid cloud topologies.
