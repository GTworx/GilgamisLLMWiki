## Executive Summary

*Yörükler* (published posthumously by Türk Dünyası Araştırmaları Vakfı, İstanbul, 1991, 325 pp., ISBN `975-498-044-6`) by Istanbul University Faculty of Economics sociologist and ethnographer Prof. Dr. [[mehmet-eroz]] (1930–1986) is a landmark Turkish sociological and ethnographic monograph—originating from his 1965 doctoral dissertation *"Yürüklerin İktisadî ve İçtimaî Teşkilatı"*—documenting the social, religious, linguistic, and economic institutions of Anatolia's nomadic and semi-nomadic **Yörük** (Oghuz/Turkmen) pastoral tribes before rapid motorization and sedentarization transformed highland *yayla* life (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Yorukler_Mehmet_Eroz.pdf, pp. 1–8).

Grounded in years of direct mountain and *yayla* fieldwork across Western and Southern Anatolia, Eröz rejects rigid economic determinism in favor of a reciprocal, relativist sociology (*"sert bir determinizm yerine, rölâtivist bir izah tarzı"*) demonstrating how modes of pastoral production and social-political institutions mutually shape one another (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Yorukler_Mehmet_Eroz.pdf, Giriş, pp. 9–10). Organized into two comprehensive parts—**Part I: Social Institutions of Turkish Nomads** (ethnic, religious, and linguistic continuities linking Central Asia and Anatolian *aşiret* lineages, social stratification, and family structure) and **Part II: Turkish Nomadic Economy** (sedentarization vs. nomadism, dwellings/*kara çadır*, economic mentality, livestock and artisanal production, labor, exchange, consumption, property regimes, and integration with the national economy)—the work preserves an irreplaceable primary ethnographic record complete with archival photographs (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Yorukler_Mehmet_Eroz.pdf, İçindekiler, pp. 5–8).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Preface & Introduction: Field Ethnography & Reciprocal Economic Sociology (pp. 7–14)**:
  * *Preserving a Vanishing Pastoral World*: Notes how rapid 20th-century modernization replaced camels, horses, and seasonal *yayla* migrations with trucks and refrigerators, giving Eröz's fieldwork enduring comparative value; articulates his non-deterministic methodological thesis linking production systems (*avcılık, çiftçilik, çobanlık*) reciprocally with family, legal, religious, and political institutions (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Yorukler_Mehmet_Eroz.pdf, pp. 7–10).

* **Part I — Social Institutions of Turkish Nomads (*Türk Göçebelerinde İçtimaî Müesseseler*, pp. 15–66)**:
  * *Central Asian–Anatolian Continuities*: Documents deep ethnic ties (*etnik rabıta*, Ch. I), syncretic religious traditions bridging Central Asian steppe cosmology and Anatolian Islam/Alevism (Ch. II), nomadic Turkish linguistic preservation (Ch. III), egalitarian tribal organization and social strata (Ch. IV), and kinship/family structures (Ch. V) (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Yorukler_Mehmet_Eroz.pdf, İçindekiler, p. 5).

* **Part II — Turkish Nomadic Economy (*Türk Göçebe Ekonomisi*, pp. 67–325)**:
  * *Pastoral Production, Exchange & Property*: Classifies nomadic economic activity (Ch. I), analyzes the historical dialectic between settled agriculture and transhumant pastoralism (Ch. II), details black-tent (*kara çadır*) material culture and dwellings (Ch. III), examines pastoral economic mentality (Ch. IV), livestock/weaving production (*istihsal*, Ch. V), employment, barter/market exchange (*mübadele*), consumption (*istihlâk*), communal vs. private property (*mülkiyet*), and the Yörüks' contribution to Türkiye's national economy (Ch. VI–X) (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Yorukler_Mehmet_Eroz.pdf, İçindekiler, pp. 5–6).
