## Executive Summary

Published under the *International Balkan Annual Conference (IBAC)* Book Series, this diplomatic and military study by Romanian historians **Cosmin Ionita** and **Florentina Nitu** investigates the geopolitical maneuverings, tribute obligations, and anti-Ottoman rebellions of Wallachian Prince **Michael the Brave (Mihai Viteazul)** (1558–1601) during the Long Turkish War (1593–1606), analyzing how the Danubian Principalities (Wallachia, Moldavia, Transylvania) navigated vassalage under the Sublime Porte, Habsburg imperial ambitions, and local boyar politics.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Fragility of the Danubian Buffer**: Demonstrates how Ottoman frontier defense relied on cooperative Christian tribute rulers, and how exorbitant fiscal extractions triggered violent systemic revolts.
2. **Proto-Nationalist Myth vs. Machiavellian Pragmatism**: Deconstructs 19th-century Romanian nationalist romanticism, showing Michael's actions were driven by immediate fiscal survival and shifting dynastic realpolitik.
3. **The Strategic Chokepoint of the Lower Danube**: Analyzes how Wallachian resistance disrupted Ottoman grain logistics to Istanbul and stalled Ottoman armies fighting the Habsburgs in Hungary.
