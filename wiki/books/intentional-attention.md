## Executive Summary

Written by sport and performance psychologist **Justin Anderson** (Wiley, 2026, Title Page p. 5; 339 pp.), *Intentional Attention* dismantles the "myth of the naturally clutch performer" (Introduction, p. 13) by grounding focus under pressure in cognitive neuroscience and repeatable training architecture. Drawing on work with Olympic athletes, NFL quarterbacks, Division I golfers, and high-stakes business executives, Anderson argues that physical or technical mastery routinely outpaces mental readiness—creating a **Cognitive Developmental Gap** (Ch. 5, p. 59–67) when the threat-biased "old brain" (amygdala and sympathetic threat-response cycle) hijacks the prefrontal cortex (Ch. 3–4, pp. 39–56).

Rather than prescribing isolated mindfulness tips, Part II introduces the **Intentional Attention Framework (IA Framework)**—a structured "cognitive operating system" (Ch. 6–7, pp. 74–84) beginning with a **Personal Attentional Attribute Inventory** across identity, values, and personal biases (Ch. 8, pp. 87–102) and scaling into sustained attentional transformation under fire (Part III).

## Core Frameworks & Insights

1. **Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Attentional Control (Ch. 1–4, pp. 25–56)**: Explains how internal rumination loops ("practicing the problem", p. 48) and social-judgment threats capture bottom-up attention, and how parasympathetic recovery re-engages prefrontal performance-relevant cues.
2. **The Cognitive Developmental Gap (Ch. 5, pp. 59–67)**: Shows why elite performers can suffer sudden performance drops when conscious awareness outpaces mental-management skill.
3. **Personal Attentional Attribute Inventory (Ch. 8, pp. 87–102)**: Uses structured exercises—Expanding Your Identity Map, the "Get To" Reappraisal (p. 94), and Values Clarification—to decouple self-worth from outcome anxiety.
