## Executive Summary

Published in the centenary issue of *Irish Foreign Affairs* (Vol. 12, No. 2, June 2019, Dublin; 9 pp., visually verified), this paired historical dossier combines an introductory essay by **Pat Walsh** (*"How and why did the British set the Greeks against the Turks?"*, p. 2 [19]) with **Dr. Y. Turan Çetiner**'s documented study (*"One Hundred years ago: The Greek invasion of 'Smyrna' and the last crime of the Great War, May 15, 1919"*, pp. 3–9 [20–26]).

Examining the Paris Peace Conference decision by David Lloyd George, Georges Clemenceau, and Woodrow Wilson to authorize Prime Minister **Eleftherios Venizelos**'s Greek army landing in **İzmir (Smyrna)** on 15 May 1919—despite explicit warnings from King Constantine I, Greek Chief of Staff Ioannis Metaxas, and Ottoman demographic statistics (p. 4 [21])—Çetiner documents how the occupation sparked the **Turkish War of Independence** (Mustafa Kemal landed in Samsun four days later, on 19 May 1919). Centering on the **October 7, 1919 Report of the Inter-Allied Commission of Enquiry** (pp. 5–6 [22–23]), the article quotes verbatim Allied findings holding the Greek High Command responsible for atrocities, Hague Convention violations, and unauthorized inland expansion—later formalized in **Article 59 of the 1923 Treaty of Lausanne**.

## Core Diplomatic & Legal Evidence

1. **British Imperial Proxy Strategy (Walsh, p. 19)**: Explains why financially exhausted Britain—eager to demobilize after Armistice and undercut Italian claims in Anatolia—enlisted Venizelos's *Megali Idea* ambitions as a proxy enforcer of the Treaty of Sèvres.
2. **The 1919 Inter-Allied Commission of Enquiry (pp. 22–23)**: Documents 46 commission hearings and 175 multi-ethnic witnesses across İzmir, Aydın, Menemen, and Ödemiş, concluding that Christian populations had not been in danger prior to landing and that the occupation degenerated into "conquest and crusade."
3. **Path to Lausanne & Greco-Turkish Reconciliation (pp. 24–26)**: Traces the 1921–1922 Anatolian campaign (Sakarya, Great Offensive, Trial of the Six in Athens), Venizelos's 1934 Nobel Peace Prize nomination of Atatürk, and Bülent Ecevit's poem invoking enduring Aegean kinship.
