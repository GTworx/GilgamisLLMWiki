## Summary
Legal analysis of the federal Anti-Boycott Act of 2018 and state-level anti-BDS statutes, evaluating constitutional conflicts with Supreme Court First Amendment precedents protecting politically motivated consumer boycotts (NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware).

## Analytical Overview
### Boycotts as Protected Speech in American Jurisprudence

*The Anti-Boycott Act and the Constitution: Federal Legislation, State Laws, and First Amendment Boycott Protections* delivers an exhaustive legal analysis of the constitutional controversy surrounding federal and state statutes penalizing boycotts of foreign nations.

### Statutory Analysis and Constitutional Conflict

The legal brief evaluates:
- **The Anti-Boycott Act of 2018:** Part of the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), criminalizing compliance with unsanctioned foreign boycotts against countries friendly to the U.S. (specifically Israel).
- **State Anti-BDS Statutes:** Over 35 U.S. states requiring state contractors and public employees to sign certifications pledging they do not boycott Israel.
- **First Amendment Precedent (*NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware*, 1982):** Landmark Supreme Court ruling establishing that politically motivated economic boycotts constitute constitutionally protected speech under the First Amendment, creating an irreconcilable conflict with contemporary anti-boycott legislation.
