## Executive Summary
Published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (NEJM 2025) by Prof. Daniel Palanker (Stanford University), Prof. Frank Holz (University of Bonn), and the international PRIMA Clinical Study Group, this landmark 11-page clinical trial manuscript presents the transformative results of a subretinal photovoltaic micro-implant (the PRIMA system) designed to restore artificial central vision in patients blinded by geographic atrophy secondary to dry age-related macular degeneration (AMD).

The wireless, photovoltaic subretinal microchip (measuring 2 mm $\times$ 2 mm and containing 378 pixel electrodes) was implanted beneath the atrophic fovea, converting near-infrared (NIR) light projected from augmented reality glasses into localized electrical pulses that directly stimulate surviving inner retinal bipolar cells. In the pivotal multi-center clinical trial, patients who had lost central vision demonstrated significant, sustained recovery of visual acuity—enabling them to read letters and words, recognize objects, and navigate indoor spaces without losing their remaining peripheral vision.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Mechanism of Photovoltaic Bionic Vision**: Details how wireless subretinal silicon micro-diodes convert projected 880 nm pulsed infrared patterns into electrical micro-currents that stimulate secondary retinal neurons.
- **Preservation of Peripheral Vision**: Demonstrates that the micro-implant restores central prosthetic vision while leaving undamaged peripheral photoreceptors completely intact, avoiding the tunnel-vision handicap of earlier epiretinal devices.
- **Clinically Meaningful Visual Acuity Gains**: Reports that implanted patients achieved prosthetic visual acuity up to 20/438, representing a statistically and functionally profound improvement over dense central scotoma blindness.
- **Surgical Safety and Long-Term Stability**: Proves the feasibility of trans-scleral or trans-vitreal subretinal delivery, documenting zero implant dislodgments or chronic retinal inflammation over extended multi-year follow-up.
