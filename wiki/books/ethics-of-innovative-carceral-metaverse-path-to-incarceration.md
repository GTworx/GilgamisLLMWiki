## Overview
This radical 2-version criminological and bioethical treatise investigates proposals to utilize virtual reality and immersive metaverses within penal systems and prison rehabilitation. The authors dissect dystopian concepts of 'carceral metaverses'—where inmates serve psychological sentences in synthetic time-accelerated simulations—contrasting them with genuine restorative justice applications like vocational tele-training and family reunification.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Interrogates the terrifying bioethical horizon of 'synthetic incarceration': manipulating neural time perception for punitive detention.
- **Key Finding**: Evaluates humane VR applications: immersive anger management, desensitization therapy, and college education for incarcerated persons.
- **Key Finding**: Establishes human rights benchmarks prohibiting psychological torture, disorientation, and forced reality-distortion in correctional facilities.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Transforming the metaverse into an instrument of carceral control represents the ultimate realization of Bentham's Panopticon: a prison where walls are made of algorithmic perceptions that inmates cannot touch or escape."
