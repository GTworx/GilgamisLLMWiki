## Executive Summary

Edited by IMF economists **David Owen** and **David O. Robinson** (2003, pp. 2–4), *Russia Rebounds* provides an empirical, post-mortem macroeconomic and structural assessment of the Russian Federation's dramatic economic recovery following the **August 1998 sovereign default and ruble collapse**. While many observers expected prolonged post-default depression, Russia achieved rapid GDP rebound, fiscal surpluses, falling inflation, and renewed capital inflows between 1999 and 2002.

Across seven technical chapters by IMF country specialists (David O. Robinson, Timo Välilä, Dak Chua, and colleagues, p. 4), the study disentangles how much of the rebound stemmed from **fortuitous external tailwinds** (steep real ruble depreciation and surging global oil prices) versus **genuine domestic policy reform** (tightened fiscal discipline, the landmark 13% flat personal income tax overhaul, federal treasury expenditure controls, and post-crisis sovereign debt restructuring)—while warning that long-term growth remained vulnerable to lagging banking reform, energy dependence, and weak rule-of-law institutions.

## Seven-Chapter Empirical Map (Contents, p. 4)

1. **Overview & Macroeconomic Policymaking (Chapters 1–2, pp. 8–63)**: Traces monetary stabilization, sterilization of oil-export FX inflows, and exchange-rate management.
2. **Structural Reforms & Growth Outlook (Chapter 3, pp. 64–83)**: Evaluates enterprise governance, utility monopolies, and diversification bottlenecks.
3. **Tax & Public Expenditure Reform (Chapters 4–5, pp. 84–127)**: Analyzes Russia's sweeping tax-code simplification (flat income tax, payroll rationalization) and federal-regional budget discipline.
4. **Banking Crisis Recovery & Sovereign Debt Sustainability (Chapters 6–7, pp. 128–190)**: Examines post-1998 bank restructuring (Sberbank dominance, weak private intermediation) and London/Paris Club debt workouts.
