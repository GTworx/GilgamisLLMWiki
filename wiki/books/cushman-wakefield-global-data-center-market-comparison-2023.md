## Executive Summary

This authoritative two-part global benchmarking study by Cushman & Wakefield's Data Center Advisory Group ranks 63 global data center markets across 13 key parameters, including power availability, land cost, fiber connectivity, tax incentives, vacancy rates, political stability, and natural disaster risk. Northern Virginia retains its ranking as the world's preeminent data center market, followed closely by Dallas/Fort Worth, Silicon Valley, Singapore, London, and Frankfurt. The report details how the explosive demand for AI computing clusters is reshuffling market competitiveness, prioritizing access to reliable, scalable electrical power over historical proximity to metropolitan fiber hubs.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Multivariate Global Market Rankings**: Ranks global ecosystems across 13 core metrics, highlighting how power availability and development pipeline now carry greater weighting than real estate acquisition costs.
- **Northern Virginia's Enduring Hegemony**: Despite severe utility transmission constraints and localized zoning moratoriums, Northern Virginia remains the largest data center market in the world with over 2,500 MW of operational capacity.
- **APAC & European Power Bottlenecks**: Singapore's calibrated lifting of its data center moratorium, alongside strict sustainability criteria in Amsterdam and Dublin, has directed European and Asian capital toward emerging regional corridors (e.g., Johor, Batam, Madrid, Oslo).
- **The Rise of High-Power Hyperscale Mega-Campuses**: The average size of planned data center developments has surged from 20–50 MW five years ago to 200–500 MW+ today, favoring vast rural land parcels over urban infill sites.
- **Incentives and Tax Policy Arbitrage**: State and national sales-and-use tax exemptions on server hardware and electrical infrastructure play a decisive role in site selection economics for multi-billion-dollar hyperscale deployments.
