## Executive Summary

This 135-page flagship collaborative study by Morgan Stanley Research integrates insights across Global Infrastructure, Technology, Utilities, Capital Goods, Construction, and Real Estate equity research teams to model a 6x expansion in global data center capacity through 2035. The report provides a granular macroeconomic and engineering forecast of global power requirements, estimating that data center electricity demand will surge from ~500 TWh in 2023 to over 3,000 TWh by 2035. Morgan Stanley provides detailed financial models on capital requirements ($2+ trillion in global capex), power generation mix (nuclear, natural gas, geothermal, renewables), supply-chain equipment constraints, and equity market beneficiaries across the entire infrastructure stack.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **The 6x Capacity Expansion Supercycle**: Global operational data center capacity is projected to expand from ~50 GW in 2023 to ~300 GW by 2035, representing a generational infrastructure investment cycle exceeding $2 trillion in aggregate capital expenditure.
- **Global Electricity Demand Surge**: Data centers will account for approximately 8%–10% of total global electricity demand by 2035, up from under 2% in 2020, forcing electric utilities and grid regulators to reassess long-term capacity plans.
- **Nuclear Energy and SMR Renaissance**: Hyperscalers' zero-carbon corporate mandates combined with 24/7 baseload reliability requirements make nuclear energy indispensable; the report forecasts rapid commercialization of Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) alongside life-extension and power uprates for existing nuclear fleets.
- **Capital Goods & Industrial Beneficiaries**: Equipment providers in power distribution (turbines, generators, medium/low voltage switchgear, transformers) and thermal engineering (liquid chillers, manifolds, CDUs) command structural multi-year backlogs and margin expansion.
- **Cross-Sector Valuation Implications**: The infrastructure bottleneck redistributes equity market value from software and pure-play tech multiples toward asset-heavy utilities, industrial manufacturers, power generators, and specialized digital infrastructure REITs.
