## Analytical Overview
### The End of the Frictionless Global Operating Model

*Five Rules for Companies Navigating Geopolitical Volatility*, published in *Harvard Business Review* (2024), addresses an existential challenge confronting modern enterprise executives: for three decades following the collapse of the Soviet Union, multinational corporations optimized their global supply chains purely for cost efficiency, just-in-time logistics, and open borders. Today, geopolitical conflict has permanently subordinated corporate economics to national security imperatives.

### Five Strategic Governance Imperatives

The briefing outlines five operational rules:
1. **Stress-Test Critical Choke Points:** Auditing cross-border component dependencies for vulnerability to weaponized export controls, sanctions, and blockades.
2. **Build 'Multi-Local' Supply Chains:** Replacing monolithic global factories with modular, regionalized manufacturing hubs (friend-shoring and near-shoring).
3. **Appoint a Chief Geopolitical Officer:** Integrating intelligence-grade geopolitical risk assessment directly into the executive committee and board of directors.
4. **Decouple Data and Software Architectures:** Establishing compliant data boundaries across competing sovereign jurisdictions (GDPR in Europe, Cyberspace Administration of China).
5. **Prepare for Sudden Forced Market Exits:** Designing operational pre-mortems and legal escrow structures to execute rapid, orderly market withdrawals during wartime crises.
