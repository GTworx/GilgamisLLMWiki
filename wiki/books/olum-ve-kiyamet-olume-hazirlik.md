## Executive Summary

Authored by *Hujjat al-Islam* ("Proof of Islam") **Abu Hamid Muhammad al-Ghazali** (1058–1111 CE) and published in this 394-page Turkish edition (*Ölüm ve Kıyamet: Ölüme Hazırlık*, Sağlam Yayınevi, Istanbul, 1999, pp. 2–3), this devotional-philosophical treatise presents Al-Ghazali's celebrated eschatological meditation on mortality, the grave, resurrection, and moral accountability (rooted in the closing fortieth book of his magnum opus *Ihya Ulum al-Din*, *Kitab Dhikr al-Mawt wa ma Ba'dahu*).

Opening with the prophetic maxim *"Death suffices as an admonisher"* (pp. 4–6), Al-Ghazali treats the continuous remembrance of death (*tefekkür-i mevt*) not as morbid despair, but as a sharp psychological instrument for stripping away worldly delusion (*gaflet*), disciplining the ego (*nefs*), and orienting every ethical action toward eternity.

---

## Three Psychological Tiers of Remembering Death (pp. 7–8)

1. **The Heedless (*Gâfil*)**: Recalls death with resentment and fear solely because it severs sensual pleasure and material accumulation—yet even this recoil begins to loosen blind attachment to the world.
2. **The Penitent (*Tevbe Ehli*)**: Remembers death proactively to awaken moral urgency, fearing only departing unprepared before completing sincere repentance and righteous provision.
3. **The Gnostic / Knower (*Ârif*)**: Anticipates death serenely as the appointed tryst (*vâde*) of reunion with the Beloved Divine—transforming mortal anxiety into spiritual peace.
