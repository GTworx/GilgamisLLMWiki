## Executive Summary
This 13-page pharmacoeconomic and health technology assessment study analyzes the clinical cost-effectiveness, quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and national budget impact of tafamidis (Vyndaqel/Vyndamax) for treating Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) in heart failure patients with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) in Spain.

The study models the clinical efficacy of tafamidis—a first-in-class small molecule that kinetically stabilizes the transthyretin tetramer, preventing misfolding into toxic cardiac amyloid fibrils—against standard palliative heart failure care. While confirming substantial reductions in cardiovascular hospitalizations and all-cause mortality, the health economists highlight the astronomical pricing of the drug (exceeding €100,000 per patient annually), calculating incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) that far exceed conventional European willingness-to-pay thresholds (€30,000–€50,000/QALY), necessitating national risk-sharing agreements and substantial price negotiations.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Mechanism of Transthyretin Tetramer Stabilization**: Details how tafamidis binds to the thyroxine-binding sites of transthyretin, preventing dissociation into amyloidogenic monomers that infiltrate ventricular myocardium.
- **Clinical Mortality and Hospitalization Reductions**: Validates pivotal ATTR-ACT trial data showing a 30% reduction in all-cause mortality and 32% decline in cardiovascular-related hospital admissions.
- **Pharmacoeconomic Pricing Dilemma**: Documents that at current commercial list prices, the drug is not cost-effective under standard international thresholds, straining public health service drug budgets.
- **Value-Based Pricing and Managed Entry Agreements**: Recommends outcome-based reimbursement models where payment is tied to verified clinical stabilization of functional capacity (6-minute walk distance) and NT-proBNP levels.
