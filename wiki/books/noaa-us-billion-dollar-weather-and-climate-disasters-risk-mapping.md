## Executive Summary

Authored by renowned applied climatologist Adam B. Smith from the NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI), this authoritative 34-page presentation monograph analyzes the historical trends, geographic concentration, and macroeconomic escalation of U.S. billion-dollar weather and climate disasters between 1980 and 2021. Presented at the American Meteorological Society (AMS) Washington Forum, the study represents the gold-standard empirical dataset for climate hazard risk mapping.

The report tracks how the annual frequency of billion-dollar weather disasters has increased four-fold—from an average of 3.1 events per year in the 1980s to over 13 events per year between 2000 and 2020, culminating in 20 distinct billion-dollar events in 2021 alone. Smith integrates county-level socioeconomic data, asset density, and social vulnerability indices (SoVI) with physical hazard footprints (tropical cyclones, severe convective storms, inland floods, wildfires, droughts), demonstrating that escalating catastrophe losses are driven by the deadly confluence of climate change and rapid asset accumulation in vulnerable coastal and wildland-urban interface (WUI) corridors.

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Exponential Escalation of Billion-Dollar Disasters**: Documents cumulative U.S. disaster losses exceeding $2.15 trillion across 310 separate billion-dollar events since 1980, with tropical cyclones causing over 50% of aggregate damage.
- **Dual Drivers: Climate Physics and Asset Accumulation**: Proves that disaster losses are accelerating due to both shifts in meteorological extremes (frequency and intensity) and massive demographic migration into vulnerable hazard zones.
- **County-Level Socioeconomic Risk Mapping**: Integrates the CDC Social Vulnerability Index and FEMA National Risk Index, identifying high-risk counties across the Gulf Coast, Florida, and the inland South where low-income populations face extreme physical exposure.
- **Implications for Reinsurance and Capital Markets**: Explores the solvency strain on property-casualty (P&C) insurers, highlighting the migration of uninsurable risks toward state-backed residual insurance pools (e.g., Florida Citizens).
