## Summary
Proposes a gigaton-scale, highly economical carbon sequestration architecture: harvesting agricultural biomass residues and preserving them in anaerobic engineered subterranean vaults.

## Analytical Overview
### An Engineering Solution to Gigaton Carbon Removal

In *Scalable, Economical, and Stable Sequestration of Agricultural Fixed Carbon*, published on SSRN, renowned Caltech and UC Berkeley physicists Eli Yablonovitch, Harry A. Atwater, and collaborators tackle the grand challenge of climate change: gigaton-scale carbon dioxide removal (CDR). While commercial Direct Air Capture (DAC) facilities require immense energy and capital expenditures ($400-$1000 per ton of $CO_2$), the authors note that terrestrial plants already capture over 100 gigatons of $CO_2$ annually via photosynthesis at zero industrial cost.

### The Biomass Burial Paradigm

The fundamental thermodynamic insight of the paper is **Biomass Carbon Removal and Storage (BiCRS)**:
- Agriculture generates billions of tons of crop residues (corn stover, sugarcane bagasse, forestry thinning) that naturally decompose within two to three years, releasing their fixed carbon back into the atmosphere as $CO_2$ and methane.
- The authors design engineered subterranean storage vaults lined with impermeable geopolymer membranes, maintained under strictly anaerobic and desiccated conditions.
- By burying densified biomass dry and oxygen-free, microbial decomposition is halted indefinitely, sequestering carbon for thousands of years.

### Economics and Scalability

The authors present detailed engineering economics proving that biomass burial can sequester carbon at **under $60 per ton of $CO_2$**, establishing a scalable, immediately deployable planetary carbon drawdown technology that requires no unproven chemical innovations.
