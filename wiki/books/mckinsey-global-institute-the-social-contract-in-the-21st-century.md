## Summary
Monumental 176-page empirical investigation evaluating how economic risk, pensions, healthcare costs, housing, and labor protections have shifted from governments and corporations onto individuals across 22 advanced economies.

## Analytical Overview
Examines the fraying of post-war social contracts from 2000 to 2020. Details how work opportunities have expanded alongside growing job polarization, wage stagnation for middle-income tiers, soaring costs of essential goods (housing, education, healthcare), and the erosion of collective safety nets, outlining policy imperatives for a revitalized 21st-century social contract.
