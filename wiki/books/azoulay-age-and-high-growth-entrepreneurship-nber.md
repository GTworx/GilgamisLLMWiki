## Analytical Overview
### The Empirical Demolition of Silicon Valley's Youth Myth

In *Age and High-Growth Entrepreneurship*, published in the *American Economic Review: Insights* (2019/2020), Pierre Azoulay, Benjamin F. Jones, J. Daniel Kim, and Javier Miranda utilize confidential, comprehensive U.S. Census Bureau administrative microdata on 2.7 million business founders to resolve a defining question in entrepreneurship: does youth confer a special advantage in building high-growth venture companies?

### The Average Successful Founder Is 45 Years Old

The authors uncover decisive, incontrovertible empirical findings:
- Across all U.S. businesses founded between 2007 and 2014, the mean founder age at inception was **42.0 years**.
- When filtering for high-tech, venture-backed startups, the mean age remains **43.2 years**.
- Most critically, when filtering for the **top 0.1% fastest-growing firms** by employment and sales growth, the average founder age rises to **45.0 years**.
- Successful founders in their 50s are **twice as likely** to produce a top-tier hyper-growth company or achieve a blockbuster acquisition or IPO as founders in their 20s.

### The Role of Domain Experience and Social Capital

The authors prove that deep industry experience, operational knowledge, and mature executive networks dramatically outweigh youthful stamina, exposing Silicon Valley's ageist funding bias as an economically irrational distortion.
