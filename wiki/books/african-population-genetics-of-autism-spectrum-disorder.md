## Executive Summary
Published in 2024, this 18-page scoping review comprehensively audits the clinical and molecular genomic landscape of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) across the African continent. The authors expose a severe Eurocentric bias in global neuropsychiatric databases: over 90% of all published autism genomics studies analyze cohorts of European ancestry, despite African genomes possessing the highest genetic diversity, shortest linkage disequilibrium blocks, and highest number of novel rare variants on Earth. The paper synthesizes identified de novo copy number variants (CNVs), point mutations in synaptic genes, and environmental co-factors across sub-Saharan cohorts, demonstrating that studying African genomic diversity is essential for resolving universal autism biology.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Extreme Genomic Underrepresentation**: African individuals represent less than 2% of participants in global autism genetic discovery cohorts, severely limiting the clinical utility of standard Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) for non-European patients.
- **Short Linkage Disequilibrium Leverage**: Because African ancestral populations have existed longer without evolutionary population bottlenecks, their shorter linkage disequilibrium allows ultra-fine mapping of causative disease mutations that cannot be disentangled in European genomes.
- **Novel Candidate Synaptic Genes**: Reviews emerging whole-exome sequencing data identifying novel rare variants in chromatin remodeling and synaptic cell adhesion genes unique to African probands.
- **Global Health Equity and Infrastructure**: Calls for urgent investment in local African biobanking, bioinformatic capacity building, and accessible diagnostic tools tailored to low-resource pediatric clinical settings.
