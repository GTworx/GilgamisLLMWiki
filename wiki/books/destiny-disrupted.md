## Executive Summary

Written by Afghan-American historian and storyteller **Tamim Ansary** (PublicAffairs, 2009, Copyright p. 7; 418 pp.), *Destiny Disrupted* retells global history from the inside out—reconstructing how fifteen centuries of world events look when **the Islamic "Middle World"** (linking the Mediterranean, Mesopotamia, Iran, Central Asia, and India) stands at the center of the narrative rather than as a sidebar to European history.

Across seventeen vivid chapters (Outline, p. 55)—from **The Middle World Before Islam**, **The Hijra**, **Birth of the Khalifate**, and the Sunni-Shi'a **Schism** (Ch. 1–4) through the **Umayyad** and **Abbasid** golden ages, **Scholars, Philosophers, and Sufis** (Ch. 5–7), **Enter the Turks**, **Crusader & Mongol Havoc**, and the **Rebirth** of the three great gunpowder empires (**Ottomans, Safavids, Mughals**, Ch. 8–10) to **West Comes East**, **Wahhabism vs. Secular & Islamist Modernism**, **Constitutions & Nationalism**, and **The Crisis of Modernity** (Ch. 11–17)—Ansary explains why the Muslim world and the West often inhabit parallel historical narratives that talk right past one another.

## Core Historical Insights

1. **Two Intersecting Historical Narratives**: Shows how European history remembers the Crusades as a pivotal awakening and the Renaissance as rebirth, whereas the Middle World viewed the Crusades as a peripheral nuisance compared to the existential cataclysm of the Mongol invasions—before experiencing nineteenth-century European colonialism as an unprecedented civilizational "disruption."
2. **Pluralistic Intellectual Legacy (Ch. 6–8)**: Celebrates Baghdad's translation movement, al-Khwarizmi, Ibn Sina, al-Ghazali, and Sufi mysticism alongside the decisive political revitalization brought by Turkic dynasties.
3. **Three Responses to Modernity (Ch. 13–17)**: Clearly differentiates puritanical revivalism (Wahhabism), secular nationalist modernization (Atatürk, Nasser, Aligarh), and political Islamism (al-Afghani, Qutb, Khomeini) as competing attempts to heal the trauma of colonial subordination.
