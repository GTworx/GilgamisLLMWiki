## Executive Summary

Part of **Ali Çimen**'s bestselling Timaş popular-history series (1st ed. Jan. 2008, ISBN 978-975-263-486-9, p. 3; 272 pp.), *Tarihi Değiştiren Bilginler* presents thirty-seven biographical portraits of foundational scientists, mathematicians, physicians, and inventors whose breakthroughs transformed human civilization. Notably, Çimen bridges classical antiquity and the European Scientific Revolution by giving prominent, detailed chapters to the **Islamic Golden Age polymaths** who preserved, critiqued, and expanded scientific method.

The chronological arc spans **Pythagoras**, **Hippocrates**, **Euclid**, and **Archimedes**; moves through **Al-Khwarizmi**, **Al-Kindi**, **Al-Battani**, **Ibn al-Haytham**, **Al-Biruni**, **Ibn Sina**, **Omar Khayyam**, and **Al-Jazari** (pp. 37–85); continues with **Copernicus**, **Paracelsus**, **Kepler**, **Harvey**, **Newton**, **Lavoisier**, **Jenner**, **Faraday**, **Carnot**, **Darwin**, **Mendel**, **Pasteur**, **Maxwell**, **Mendeleev**, **Röntgen**, **Edison**, **Pavlov**, and **Freud**; and culminates in modern physics with **Max Planck**, **Marie Curie**, **Ernest Rutherford**, and **Albert Einstein** (TOC, pp. 5–7, 237–272).

## Core Historical Themes

1. **Cross-Civilizational Continuity of Science**: Highlights the direct intellectual lineage linking Greek geometry, Baghdad/Nishapur/Diyarbakır breakthroughs (algebra, optics, clinical medicine, cybernetic automata), and early modern European experimental science.
2. **Epistemic Courage Against Dogma**: Chronicles how figures like Ibn al-Haytham, Copernicus, Paracelsus, Darwin, and Marie Curie risked institutional hostility, poverty, or persecution to test reality empirically.
3. **Humanizing Genius**: Balances technical discoveries with intimate biographical anecdotes—from Lavoisier's execution during the French Revolution and Röntgen's refusal to patent X-rays to Faraday's self-taught rise from bookbinder's apprentice.
