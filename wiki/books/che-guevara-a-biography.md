## Executive Summary

*Che Guevara: A Biography* (Greenwood Biographies / ABC-CLIO, 2011, 267 pp.) by Latin American studies scholar [[richard-l-harris]] delivers an accessible, balanced scholarly biography of Argentine physician, Marxist guerrilla theorist, and Cuban revolutionary leader **Ernesto "Che" Guevara** (1928–1967) (source: BOOKS/Biography_/Che Guevara_ A Biography (Greenwood Biographies)   ( PDFDrive ).pdf, Title Page, Copyright & Table of Contents, pp. 4–8).

Moving systematically from Guevara's asthmatic youth in Argentina and formative 1951–1952 motorcycle journey across South America to the 1954 CIA-backed coup in Guatemala, the Sierra Maestra guerrilla campaign alongside Fidel Castro, ministerial governance in revolutionary Cuba, ill-fated internationalist expeditions in the Congo and Bolivia, and his execution at La Higuera in October 1967, Harris separates historical reality from both Cold War demonization and pop-culture commodification—analyzing Guevara's *foco* theory of guerrilla warfare, his critique of Soviet bureaucracy, and his enduring symbolic resonance across Latin America (pp. 12–243).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Chapters 1–3: Political Awakening Across Latin America (pp. 28–95)
- **From Medical Student to Revolutionary**: Chronicles Guevara's privileged yet bohemian upbringing in Argentina, his transformative encounters with indigenous poverty and leper colonies during *The Motorcycle Diaries* odyssey, and his radicalization after witnessing the 1954 overthrow of Guatemala's elected Arbenz government before joining Fidel Castro's Granma expedition in Mexico (pp. 28–95).

### Chapters 4–7: Guerrilla Commander, Minister & Theorist (pp. 96–157)
- **Cuba & *Foco* Warfare**: Details Guevara's decisive tactical leadership in the Sierra Maestra and the Battle of Santa Clara, his controversial post-revolution tenure directing La Cabaña tribunals, the National Bank of Cuba, and the Ministry of Industries, and his influential treatise *Guerrilla Warfare*—arguing that a small revolutionary vanguard (*foco*) could catalyze insurrectionary conditions without waiting for classical Marxist industrial maturation (pp. 96–145).
- **Anti-Imperialism & the "New Man"**: Explores Guevara's vision of moral over material incentives, voluntary labor, and Third World anti-imperialist solidarity—a stance that increasingly strained ties with Moscow's orthodox economic model (pp. 146–157).

### Chapters 8–13: Congo, Bolivia, Execution & Enduring Iconography (pp. 158–243)
- **Limits of Exported Revolution**: Analyzes why Guevara's clandestine 1965 Congo mission and 1966–1967 Bolivian campaign failed due to local political isolation, fractured communist support, and U.S.-assisted counterinsurgency—culminating in his capture and execution in October 1967, the international circulation of his Bolivian diary, and the posthumous transformation of "El Che" into a global symbol of rebellious idealism (pp. 158–243).
