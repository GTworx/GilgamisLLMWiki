## Executive Summary
Comprising the landmark 2018 U.S. National Defense Strategy (NDS, drafted under Secretary James Mattis) and RAND Corporation's comprehensive 2025 research report *Stabilizing the U.S.-China Rivalry*, this strategic defense dossier analyzes the return of great power competition. The 2018 NDS formalizes the historic pivot from two decades of post-9/11 counterinsurgency in the Middle East toward strategic deterrence of peer competitors (China and Russia). The RAND study evaluates crisis stability in the Western Pacific (Taiwan Strait, South China Sea), modeling deterrence architecture, redlines, diplomatic guardrails, and confidence-building measures to prevent great power military escalation from spiraling into catastrophic nuclear conflict.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Pivot to Great Power Competition**: The 2018 NDS officially codifies that inter-state strategic competition, not terrorism, is the primary concern in U.S. national security.
- **The Indo-Pacific Maritime Theater**: Identifies Chinese anti-access/area-denial (A2/AD) capabilities as the primary military challenge to forward-deployed allied forces.
- **Escalation Control and Redline Ambiguity**: RAND proves that deliberate strategic ambiguity over redlines increases miscalculation risks during cyber, space, or maritime gray-zone incidents.
- **Bilateral Crisis De-Escalation Architecture**: Recommends establishing resilient military-to-military communication hotlines, AI military safety protocols, and mutual restraints on counter-space operations.
