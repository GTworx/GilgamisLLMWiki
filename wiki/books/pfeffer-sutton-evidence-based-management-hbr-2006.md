## Analytical Overview
### Bringing Scientific Method to Executive Suites

In *Evidence-Based Management*, published in *Harvard Business Review* (2006), Stanford organizational scholars Jeffrey Pfeffer and Robert I. Sutton issue a devastating indictment of standard corporate decision-making. Observing that modern medicine experienced a revolution when doctors abandoned bloodletting and tradition in favor of randomized controlled trials, the authors ask why business executives continue to manage based on obsolete fads, consulting hype, unexamined dogma, and flawed personal ideology.

### Five Warning Signs of Un-Evidenced Management

Pfeffer and Sutton identify pervasive cognitive traps in corporate boardrooms:
1. Relying on casual benchmarking of superstar companies without understanding context.
2. Promoting unproven management panaceas sold by charismatic consultants.
3. Over-relying on personal dogmatic beliefs ('this is how we've always done it').
4. Systematically ignoring or suppressing negative performance feedback.
5. Rewarding consultants for telling executives what they want to hear.

### The Six Principles of Evidence-Based Decision-Making

The authors formulate practical guidelines: treating the organization as an unfinished prototype, encouraging experimentation, demanding hard quantitative and qualitative evidence before major reorganizations, and cultivating intellectual humility in executive leadership.
