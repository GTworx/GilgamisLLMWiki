## Executive Summary

Authored by Charlotte Martial, Steven Laureys, and the Coma Science Group at the University of Liège, this 36-page two-part clinical neuroscience dossier evaluates Near-Death Experiences (NDEs) as a unique scientific paradigm for investigating 'disconnected consciousness'—subjective conscious states that occur during severe cerebral hypoperfusion, cardiac arrest, or coma in the absence of behavioral responsiveness. The authors analyze standardized phenomenological scales (Greyson NDE Scale), neurochemical and neurophysiological triggers (endogenous neuroprotective surges, transient cortical gamma coherence, temporoparietal junction dysfunction generating out-of-body experiences), and propose empirical methodologies combining EEG, functional neuroimaging, and neurophenomenology to map consciousness at the boundary of clinical death.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Defining Disconnected Consciousness**: Conceptualizes NDEs as profound conscious states that are disconnected from external environmental sensory input and physical motor output.
- **Temporoparietal Junction (TPJ) and Out-of-Body Sensations**: Identifies transient focal hypoxia and disrupted multisensory vestibular integration in the right temporoparietal junction as the neuroanatomical trigger for out-of-body experiences (OBEs).
- **Post-Cardiac-Arrest Gamma Synchronization**: Examines electrophysiological recordings showing transient surges of highly organized global gamma oscillations following cardiac arrest, correlating with vivid recall.
- **Standardized Greyson NDE Scale Metrics**: Evaluates clinical phenomenology across cognitive (time distortion, life review), affective (peace, joy), and transcendental components across thousands of documented resuscitations.
- **Methodological Framework for Resuscitation Telemetry**: Outlines non-invasive clinical monitoring protocols to capture continuous quantitative EEG and cerebral oximetry in emergency resuscitation bays.
