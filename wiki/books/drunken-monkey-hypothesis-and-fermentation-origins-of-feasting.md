## Overview
Published in BioScience (2025), this fascinating evolutionary anthropology monograph examines the 'Drunken Monkey Hypothesis'—the evolutionary origin of human alcohol attraction. The authors trace the genetic mutation in alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH4) ~10 million years ago when hominid ancestors adapted to eating overripe, naturally fermented forest floor fruits, laying the metabolic groundwork for human feasting and alcohol consumption.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Identifies the ADH4 gene mutation ~10 Ma allowing hominid ancestors to metabolize ethanol-rich fallen fruits without poisoning.
- **Key Finding**: Links primate fruit-sharing and olfactory ethanol detection directly to the cultural evolution of communal feasts.
- **Key Finding**: Explains contemporary alcohol dependency as an evolutionary mismatch between ancestral fruit consumption and industrial distilled spirits.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Our taste for alcohol is not an accidental modern vice; it is a 10-million-year-old evolutionary legacy inherited from ape ancestors who survived environmental drying by learning to harvest fermented fruits from the forest floor."
