# Triangulation Supports Agricultural Spread of the Transeurasian Languages and the Neolithic Homeland

## Overview
Published in Nature (November 2021) by an international consortium led by Martine Robbeets, this monumental multidisciplinary study resolves the origins of the Transeurasian (Altaic: Turkic, Mongolic, Tungusic, Koreanic, Japonic) language family. Triangulating linguistic Bayesian phylogenetics, archeobotanical millet farming distributions, and ancient Eurasian paleogenomics, the authors trace the ancestral speech community to early Neolithic broomcorn millet farmers in the West Liao River basin (~9,000 BP).

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Triangulates linguistics, archaeology, and archaeogenetics to resolve the centuries-old Transeurasian origin debate.
- **Key Finding**: Demonstrates that early Transeurasian dispersals were driven by Neolithic millet agricultural expansion rather than pastoral nomadism.
- **Key Finding**: Integrates ancient genomes from Korea, Japan, Amur basin, and the Russian Far East with Bayesian linguistic trees.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Our results support an agricultural origin for the Transeurasian language family, rooted in early Neolithic farming communities of the West Liao River basin who subsequently dispersed across Northeast Asia."
