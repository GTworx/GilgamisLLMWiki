## Overview
Published in Frontiers in Virtual Reality by Mel Slater and Maria V. Sanchez-Vives—two of the world's foremost experimental presence researchers—this groundbreaking paper establishes the ethical imperatives necessitated by hyper-realistic VR and AR. The authors analyze 'Place Illusion' and 'Plausibility Illusion', demonstrating that when virtual experiences trigger real autonomic fear and empathy, ethical duties equivalent to physical interventions must apply.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Deconstructs Place Illusion (PI) and Plausibility Illusion (Psi) as cognitive engines of total sensory immersion.
- **Key Finding**: Analyzes psychological risks: post-immersion depersonalization, false memory implantation, and simulated traumatic stress.
- **Key Finding**: Establishes ethical guidelines for developers regarding consent, emotional safety boundaries, and experiential debriefing.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Even when participants know intellectually that an event is a computer simulation, their bodies and brains respond as if it were real. Realism creates genuine moral consequence."
