## Overview
Published in the Journal of Old Turkic Studies (JOTS) by Cem Aydoğan, this massive 114-page philological treatise compiles an exhaustive etymological dictionary of Turkic loanwords in Classical and Middle Mongolian texts (Secret History of the Mongols, Hua-Yi Yiyu). Aydoğan tracks phonological shifts, semantic drift, and military/administrative terminology exchanges between steppe nomads.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Documents hundreds of lexical loans from Old Turkic into Middle Mongolian spanning administrative, military, and pastoral domains.
- **Key Finding**: Provides rigorous phonological criteria distinguishing shared Altaic substrate roots from late historical loanwords.
- **Key Finding**: Illuminates the institutional culture of the 13th-century Mongol Empire and its reliance on Uighur scribal administration.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "The extensive lexical borrowing from Turkic into Middle Mongolian reflects centuries of symbiotic nomadic contact, where Old Turkic served as the primary administrative and diplomatic lingua franca of Inner Asia."
