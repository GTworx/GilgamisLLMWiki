## Executive Summary
Published in 2025 by an international biomaterials and regenerative medicine consortium, this 13-page technical engineering paper details the biochemical synthesis, rheological optimization, and cellular validation of a novel bioink derived from decellularized extracellular matrix (dECM) for 3D bioprinting human lung tissue models.

The researchers developed a detergent-enzymatic perfusion decellularization protocol that eliminates cellular and nuclear DNA while preserving the complex architectural proteins (collagen types I and IV, laminin, elastin, and sulfated glycosaminoglycans) of pulmonary tissue. When solubilized into a cross-linkable hydrogel bioink, the material exhibited ideal shear-thinning properties and print fidelity, supporting the high viability, proliferation, and alveolar epithelial differentiation of human primary lung cells inside bioprinted microfluidic alveolus-capillary barrier models.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Preservation of Native Extracellular Matrix Signals**: Proves that native tissue-specific biochemical cues in dECM bioinks promote cell survival and functional differentiation far superior to generic synthetic polymers.
- **Rheological and Cross-Linking Optimization**: Details temperature-induced and photo-crosslinking mechanisms that provide mechanical stability to withstand cyclic respiratory pressure gradients without collapsing.
- **Bioprinting Alveolar-Capillary Micro-Architecture**: Successfully fabricates multi-laminar cellular constructs featuring endothelial capillary networks and alveolar type II epithelial cells displaying surfactant protein expression.
- **Drug Testing and Disease Modeling**: Formulates the bioprinted lung construct as an advanced in vitro human testing platform for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) drug screening and respiratory viral infections.
