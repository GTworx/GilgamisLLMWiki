## Executive Summary

Authored by pioneering Turkish intellectual, political thinker, and *Yön*/*Devrim* movement leader **Doğan Avcıoğlu** (1926–1983), *Millî Kurtuluş Tarihi: 1838'den 1995'e* is a monumental, four-volume, 1,729-page anti-imperialist socioeconomic and political history of modern Turkey. Beginning with the catastrophic 1838 Anglo-Ottoman Treaty of Balta Limanı (which dismantled Ottoman protective tariffs and turned the empire into an open semicolonial market for Western industrial powers), Avcıoğlu provides a devastating structural analysis of how bureaucratic comprador elites succumbed to foreign debt, how Mustafa Kemal Atatürk mobilized the Anatolian peasantry and national bourgeoisie in an armed anti-imperialist liberation war, and the ongoing struggle for complete economic and political sovereignty.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The 1838 Semicolonial Turning Point**: Proves that the fall of the Ottoman Empire was fundamentally economic: the 1838 Balta Limanı treaty subordinated Ottoman handicrafts to British factory imports, leading directly to bankruptcy (1875) and the loss of sovereignty under the Public Debt Administration (*Düyûn-ı Umûmiye*).
2. **The Turkish War of Independence as the First Anti-Imperialist Revolution**: Frames Atatürk's national struggle not merely as a Greco-Turkish war, but as the world's first victorious twentieth-century anti-imperialist national liberation war, inspiring subsequent decolonization movements across Africa and Asia.
3. **The Imperative of Economic Independence (*Tam Bağımsızlık*)**: Reiterates Atatürk's famous principle that political and military victories are hollow unless crowned by domestic industrialization, national banking, and agrarian reform.
