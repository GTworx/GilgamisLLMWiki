## Overview
Published in Information Systems Journal by Thomas Chesney and collaborators, this pioneering criminology and social computing paper investigates 'griefing', harassment, and digital crime within Second Life. The authors categorize disruptive in-world behaviors, assess user coping mechanisms, and analyze the limits of self-policing versus corporate platform intervention.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Taxonomizes griefing behaviors ranging from spatial vandalism and chat spamming to sexual harassment and financial fraud.
- **Key Finding**: Examines the psychological and economic costs imposed on virtual community members and land owners.
- **Key Finding**: Critiques top-down platform bans versus decentralized community moderation and technical permissions.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Griefing in 3D virtual spaces is not merely vandalism; it is an asymmetrical assertion of power that exploits spatial immersion to generate acute psychological distress."
