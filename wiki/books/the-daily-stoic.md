## Executive Summary

Co-authored by modern Stoicism popularizer **Ryan Holiday** and Harvard Divinity–trained translator and literary agent **Stephen Hanselman** (Portfolio/Penguin, 2016; 406 pp.), *The Daily Stoic* distills the surviving ethical wisdom of **Epictetus**, **Seneca**, **Marcus Aurelius**, Musonius Rufus, Zeno, and Cleanthes into **366 freshly translated daily meditations** accompanied by concise contemporary commentary.

Following Pierre Hadot's scholarly reconstruction of Stoic spiritual exercises, the book organizes the calendar year into three four-month philosophical disciplines (TOC, pp. 7–13+): **Part I: The Discipline of Perception** (January–April: Clarity, Passions & Emotions, Awareness, Unbiased Thought), **Part II: The Discipline of Action** (May–August: Right Action, Problem Solving, Duty, Pragmatism), and **Part III: The Discipline of Will** (September–December: Fortitude & Resilience, Virtue & Kindness, Acceptance / *Amor Fati*, Meditation on Mortality / *Memento Mori*).

---

## Core Stoic Disciplines

1. **Perception (Jan–Apr)**: Trains the **Dichotomy of Control** (Jan 1 & 13), strips away emotional catastrophizing and unexamined impressions, and cultivates objective self-assessment before reacting.
2. **Action (May–Aug)**: Grounds ethics in civic duty (*sympatheia*), justice, deliberate craftsmanship, and transforming obstacles into raw material for virtuous work.
3. **Will (Sep–Dec)**: Builds psychological invulnerability through premeditation of adversity (*premeditatio malorum*), radical acceptance (*amor fati*), and *memento mori*—using mortality to prioritize what genuinely matters today.
