## Overview
Published in Rosetta by epigrapher Frank Simons, this 26-page monograph investigates the Proto-Sinaitic inscriptions discovered at Serabit el-Khadim in the Sinai Peninsula. Simons details how Semitic turquoise mine workers in the Middle Bronze Age adapted Egyptian hieroglyphs acrophonically to invent the world's first consonantal alphabet (abjad), the ultimate ancestor of Phoenician, Greek, Latin, Aramaic, and Arabic scripts.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Explains the acrophonic principle: borrowing Egyptian pictograms to represent only the initial consonant of the Semitic word (e.g., 'house' *bayt* -> 'b').
- **Key Finding**: Dates the birth of alphabetic script to circa 1850–1700 BCE in mining communities under Egyptian cultural contact.
- **Key Finding**: Traces the evolutionary genealogy from Proto-Sinaitic through Proto-Canaanite, Phoenician, and the Greek alphabet.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "The invention of the alphabet was not the achievement of elite scribes, but a brilliant act of subaltern cultural appropriation by Semitic workers who democratized writing forever."
