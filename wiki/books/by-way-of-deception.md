## Executive Summary

*By Way of Deception: The Making and Unmaking of a Mossad Officer* (originally published by St. Martin's Press, 1990; 1994 electronic/print edition, 378 pp.) by former Israeli intelligence trainee [[victor-ostrovsky]] and Canadian political journalist [[claire-hoy]] is a controversial insider exposé of the recruitment, tradecraft, institutional culture, and covert operations of Israel's foreign intelligence agency, the Mossad (source: BOOKS/Politics_/1994 By Way of Deception- The Making and Unmaking of a Mossad Officer.pdf, Title Page & Author's Foreword, pp. iii, ix–xii).

Taking its title from the agency's traditional motto drawn from Proverbs, the book combines a personal narrative of Ostrovsky's training as a *katsa* (case officer) with sweeping—and fiercely contested—reconstructions of major covert missions from the late 1970s through the mid-1980s. Its central normative thesis is democratic oversight: unlike the CIA, which faces congressional intelligence committees and public budgets, an unaccountable shadow intelligence apparatus risks manipulating its own elected leaders and betraying the long-term interests of the nation it exists to protect (Foreword, pp. ix–x).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Author's Foreword & Institutional Structure (pp. vii–xii)
- **Accountability Deficit**: Hoy frames Ostrovsky not as an enemy of Israel, but as a disillusioned insider arguing that every intelligence service—including the Mossad, established by David Ben-Gurion on September 1, 1951—must remain accountable to elected civilian oversight (p. ix).
- **Lean Structure & the *Sayanim* Network**: Contrasts the Mossad's small formal roster (roughly 1,200 total personnel and only 30–35 active *katsas* globally) with the much larger CIA and KGB, attributing its operational reach to *sayanim*—volunteer diaspora helpers providing logistics, cover, and local access (pp. xi–xii).

### Prologue: Operation Sphinx (pp. 1–15)
- **Targeting Iraq's Osirak Reactor**: Opens with a detailed narrative of how Mossad's Paris station and *Tsomet* recruiting division cultivated an Iraqi nuclear technician working on the French-built Tamuze 17 (Osirak) reactor in Sarcelles during 1978–1981, culminating in Israel's June 7, 1981 airstrike destroying the Tuwaitha facility outside Baghdad (pp. 1–15).

### Part I: Cadet (Chs. 1–5, pp. 16–115)
- **Recruitment & Academy Tradecraft**: Details psychometric testing, polygraph vetting, surveillance detection, legend-building, dead-drops, agent handling, and computerized operational case-study archives at the Mossad training academy ("The Academy" / *Midrasha*) (pp. 31–115).

### Part II: Inside and Out (Chs. 6–8, pp. 117–175)
- **The Belgian Desk & Institutional Disillusionment**: Recounts Ostrovsky's posting to the Belgian desk, internal rivalries between desks and stations, operational turf wars, and the bureaucratic scapegoating that led to his departure from the service (pp. 117–175).

### Part III: By Way of Deception (Chs. 9–17 & Epilogue, pp. 177–362)
- **Covert Operations & Intelligence Failures**: Reconstructs alleged operations spanning European arms interdiction (*Strella*, *Exocet*), counterterrorism (*Carlos*), PLO affairs (*Helping Arafat*), intelligence-sharing tensions with Washington (*Only in America*), the Ethiopian rescue (*Operation Moses*), and the 1983 *Beirut* Marine barracks bombing—arguing that compartmentalization and ideological hubris caused catastrophic strategic blind spots (pp. 177–336).
