## Executive Summary

*Saatleri Ayarlama Enstitüsü* (*The Time Regulation Institute*) is Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar's masterpiece of modernist satire and one of the towering achievements of 20th-century world literature. Narrated in the deadpan, self-deprecating memoir voice of **Hayri İrdal**—a hapless son of late-Ottoman Istanbul who drifts between traditional watchmakers, coffeehouse eccentrics, spiritists, psychoanalysts, and modern technocrats—the novel chronicles the meteoric rise and absurd institutional sprawl of the fictional **Time Regulation Institute**, founded by the charismatic, Nietzschean-bureaucratic impresario **Halit Ayarcı**.

Framed by an epigraph from Ottoman poet İzzet Molla invoking divine rescue from the "affliction of reason" (p. 4), Tanpınar uses clocks, calendars, and synchronized timepieces as an overarching allegory for Turkish modernization: a society suspended uncomfortably between the organic, sacred, *alaturka* temporality of its Ottoman past and the hyper-rationalized, performative *alafranga* institutions of the modern Republic.

## Four-Part Narrative Structure

1. **Birinci Bölüm: Büyük Ümitler ("Great Expectations," Parts I–XII, pp. 6–293)**: Establishes Hayri İrdal's childhood under his father's brooding grandfather clock (*Mübarek*), his apprenticeship with the old-world master watchmaker **Nuri Efendi** (who views timepieces as moral organisms), and the comic misadventures of eccentric tutor **Seyit Lütfullah** and the Abdüsselam Bey household.
2. **İkinci Bölüm: Küçük Hakikatler ("Small Truths," Parts I–X, pp. 294–705)**: Follows Hayri through World War I, poverty, spiritist salons, an accidental brush with the law, and psychiatric observation under **Dr. Ramiz**—who enthusiastically diagnoses Hayri with a textbook Freudian father complex regardless of reality—before Hayri meets the decisive catalyst of his life, **Halit Ayarcı**.
3. **Üçüncü Bölüm: Sabaha Doğru ("Toward Dawn," Parts I–IX, pp. 706–1347)**: Traces the surreal creation and bureaucratic hypertrophy of the **Time Regulation Institute**, complete with fabricated traditions, invented historical clockmaker **Şeyh Ahmet Zamanî**, circular regulations, fine-collection schemes for out-of-sync watches, nepotistic staffing, and a monumental clock-shaped architectural headquarters.
4. **Dördüncü Bölüm: Her Mevsimin Bir Sonu Vardır ("Every Season Has an End," Parts I–II, pp. 1348–1455)**: Recounts the quiet unraveling of the Institute's illusion after a visiting American delegation triggers its liquidation—yet leaves its employees permanently absorbed into comfortable liquidation commissions—culminating in Halit Ayarcı's disillusioned confession ("I realized I had deceived myself," p. 1454) and fatal car crash (p. 1455).

## Key Philosophical & Satirical Themes

1. **Temporal Split & Cultural Schizophrenia**: Watches serve as the central metaphor for Turkey's dual identity—neither wholly anchored in ancestral rhythm nor organically inhabiting imported Western rationalism, resulting in anxious imitation (*taklit*).
2. **Institutional Absurdism & Bureaucratic Self-Perpetuation**: Through Halit Ayarcı's genius for inventing prestigious, entirely useless bureaucracies that justify themselves through sheer rhetorical confidence, Tanpınar anticipates Kafkaesque and Parkinson's-Law critiques of modern managerial states.
3. **Invented Tradition**: Hayri's bestselling fabricated biography of the nonexistent 17th-century clockmaker *Şeyh Ahmet Zamanî* lampoons both nationalist myth-making and uncritical intellectual gullibility.
4. **Unreliable Compliance**: Hayri İrdal is neither heroic rebel nor pure villain; his passive complicity exposes how ordinary citizens accommodate absurdity once it pays a salary and confers social respectability.
