## Executive Summary

Commissioned by the U.S. Army and prepared by the RAND Arroyo Center, **Lessons from Others for Future U.S. Army Operations in and Through the Information Environment** is an exhaustive, 318-page multi-theater comparative study of information warfare, influence operations, and cyber-electromagnetic activities. Analyzing contemporary operations conducted by state adversaries (Russia, China, Iran) and non-state actors (ISIS, Hezbollah), the authors demonstrate that modern military overmatch no longer relies solely on kinetic firepower. Instead, operations in the **information environment (IE)**—combining cyber intrusions, algorithmic social media weaponization, tactical deception, psychological operations (PSYOP), and civil-military media manipulation—shape the cognitive terrain and strategic political outcomes long before conventional forces clash.

## Key Strategic & Architectural Takeaways

- **Holistic Information Confrontation**: Russian military doctrine (*informatsionnoye protivoborstvo*) integrates technical cyber attacks (grid disabling, wiper malware) seamlessly with psychological disorganization and narrative warfare, viewing information confrontation as continuous rather than bounded by formal war declarations.
- **China's Three Warfares Doctrine**: Deconstructs the PLA's integrated framework: (1) **Psychological Warfare** (demoralizing foreign leadership and populations), (2) **Media/Public Opinion Warfare** (shaping global perceptions and controlling information channels), and (3) **Legal Warfare / Lawfare** (exploiting international law and domestic statues to constrain adversary action).
- **ISIS and the Industrialization of Terror Propaganda**: Analyzes how non-state actors utilized decentralized social media networks (Twitter, Telegram), localized digital brand fragmentation, and high-production visual media to crowdsource insurgent mobilization across continents.
- **The U.S. Military's Institutional Deficits**: Identifies acute legal, organizational, and bureaucratic impediments in Western military structures—such as stovepiped authorities between Title 10 (military operations) and Title 50 (covert intelligence), sluggish narrative approval cycles, and inadequate linguistic/cultural expertise.
- **Doctrinal Recommendations for the U.S. Army**: Urges the operational integration of Cyber Electromagnetic Activities (CEMA) with public affairs, civil affairs, and military information support operations (MISO) under unified information warfare commanders at corps and division levels.
