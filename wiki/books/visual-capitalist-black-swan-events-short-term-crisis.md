## Summary
Visual Capitalist historical compendium analyzing market drawdowns and subsequent multi-year recoveries across historical black swan shocks from 1918 to the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Anatomy of Market Pandemonium

In *Black Swan Events: Short-Term Crisis, Long-Term Opportunity*, Visual Capitalist researcher Jenna Ross provides a quantitative and visual post-mortem of catastrophic market shocks over a century of financial history. Published during the acute panic of the March 2020 COVID-19 liquidity collapse, the work synthesizes Nassim Nicholas Taleb's Black Swan framework to help investors maintain long-term perspective.

### Historical Shocks: From 1918 Spanish Flu to the GFC

The report traces the market drawdown and recovery trajectories of monumental unexpected crises:
- The 1918 Spanish Flu and post-WWI demobilization.
- The 1929 Wall Street Crash and the Great Depression.
- The 1973 Oil Embargo and stagflation crisis.
- The 1987 Black Monday crash (-22.6% in a single trading session).
- The 2000 Dot-Com bust and the 2008 Global Financial Crisis.

### The Compounding Asymmetry of Patient Capital

The analysis proves that while black swan shocks induce violent short-term panic and severe wealth destruction for leveraged actors, markets historically recover and surpass prior highs. For patient, non-leveraged allocators, extreme panic creates the highest-expected-value entry points in financial history.
