## Executive Summary

*What the Dog Saw* (2009, Little, Brown, pp. 5–6) collects nineteen of **Malcolm Gladwell's** finest *New Yorker* essays, unified by a single investigative impulse: stepping inside the perceptual worldview of unusual specialists—whether dog whisperer Cesar Millan, infomercial inventor Ron Popeil, food psychophysicist Howard Moskowitz, or Wall Street short-sellers—to uncover hidden systems of expertise and failure.

---

## Three-Part Essay Structure (Contents, p. 6)

1. **Part One — Obsessives, Pioneers, and Other Varieties of Minor Genius**: Includes *The Pitchman* (Ron Popeil), *The Ketchup Conundrum* (why Heinz owns ketchup while mustard fragmented), *Blowing Up* (profiling Nassim Taleb's tail-risk trading philosophy!), and *What the Dog Saw* (Cesar Millan's nonverbal mastery).
2. **Part Two — Theories, Predictions, and Diagnoses**: Contrasts **puzzles** (solved by gathering more missing facts) against **mysteries** (like Enron's public financial disclosures in *Open Secrets*, where all facts are already visible but judgment fails), alongside essays on choking vs. panicking (*The Art of Failure*) and mammography/profiling limits.
3. **Part Three — Personality, Character, and Intelligence**: Examines late bloomers vs. child prodigies, hiring illusions (*The New-Boy Network*), and criminal-profiling myths.
