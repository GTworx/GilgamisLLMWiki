## Executive Summary
This monumental 608-page global reference volume published by the World Health Organization represents the most comprehensive planetary audit of medical devices, health technologies, and regulatory policies ever assembled. Surveying 177 member states, the Atlas analyzes national device procurement policies, biomedical engineering infrastructures, donation guidelines, and health technology assessment (HTA) systems.

The compendium documents profound global inequalities in medical technology density, mapping per-capita availability of life-saving equipment—such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) units, computed tomography (CT) scanners, linear accelerators for cancer radiotherapy, mammography machines, and oxygen generation plants—between high-income nations and low-and-middle-income countries (LMICs). It establishes international standards for nomenclature, unique device identification (UDI), safe maintenance, and decommissioning.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Planetary Health Technology Disparities**: Documents that high-income nations possess over 30 times more advanced diagnostic imaging units per million inhabitants than low-income nations, where millions die from lack of basic ultrasound and radiography.
- **Biomedical Engineering Gaps**: Identifies the severe shortage of trained clinical engineers in LMICs, resulting in over 40% of donated medical equipment remaining out of service due to lack of spare parts and voltage mismatch.
- **Unique Device Identification (UDI)**: Formulates global standards for barcode tracking, traceability, post-market adverse event reporting, and counterfeit prevention across international medical supply chains.
- **Priority Medical Devices Lists**: Details WHO essential device packages tailored for specific clinical challenges, including maternal-child health, cancer management, and infectious disease emergencies.
