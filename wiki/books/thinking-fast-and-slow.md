## Executive Summary

Dedicated *"In memory of Amos Tversky"* (p. 3), Nobel laureate **Daniel Kahneman**'s *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011, 533 pp.) is the capstone synthesis of four decades of behavioral economics and cognitive psychology. Writing to enrich the vocabulary of "informed office watercooler gossip" with precise diagnostic labels for systematic errors of judgment (Introduction, p. 7), Kahneman structures the mind around two metaphorical agents: **System 1** (fast, automatic, associative, effortless, emotionally driven) and **System 2** (slow, effortful, deliberative, statistically capable, yet chronically lazy, Part I, Ch. 1–9).

Across five monumental parts—**Two Systems** (Part I), **Heuristics and Biases** (Part II), **Overconfidence** (Part III), **Choices** (Part IV), and **Two Selves** (Part V), plus the classic *Science* (1974) and *American Psychologist* (1984) appendices—Kahneman demolishes the neoclassical *Econ* model of expected-utility maximization and replaces it with **Prospect Theory** and an empirically grounded psychology of decision-making and well-being.

## Core Frameworks & Insights

1. **WYSIATI & Cognitive Heuristics (Parts I–II, Ch. 1–18)**: Explains *"What You See Is All There Is"* (associative coherence jumping to conclusions), anchoring, availability cascades, the conjunction fallacy (*Linda the bank teller*, Ch. 15), base-rate neglect, and regression to the mean.
2. **Illusions of Understanding & The Outside View (Part III, Ch. 19–24)**: Exposes hindsight bias, the illusion of validity in stock picking and interviews, why simple algorithms beat clinical intuition in low-validity environments (Ch. 21–22), and how Reference Class Forecasting ("The Outside View") and **Premortems** curb planning-fallacy overconfidence.
3. **Prospect Theory & The Two Selves (Parts IV–V, Ch. 25–38)**: Demonstrates reference-dependent value, **Loss Aversion** (losses loom roughly twice as large as gains), the endowment effect, the fourfold pattern of risk attitudes (Ch. 29), and the tension between the **Experiencing Self** and the **Remembering Self** (peak-end rule and duration neglect).
