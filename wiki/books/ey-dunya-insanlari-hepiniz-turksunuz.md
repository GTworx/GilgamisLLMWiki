## Executive Summary

Written by American alternative historian, educator, and independent researcher **Gene D. Matlock**, *Ey Dünya İnsanları Hepiniz Türksünüz* (*What Strange Mystery Unites the Greeks, the Turks, the Hebrews, and the Mexicans? / You Are All Turks*) is an expansive, highly controversial alternative-history treatise. Drawing on cross-continental comparative mythology, linguistics, and diffusionism, Matlock argues that an advanced, forgotten global seafaring and horse-riding civilization originating in Central Eurasia—which he identifies with the Proto-Turks / Turanians—seeded ancient civilizations across India, Sumer, ancient Greece, Egypt, and Pre-Columbian Mesoamerica.

## Structural & Chapter Breakdown

| Section | Cross-Cultural Comparative Focus |
|---|---|
| **I. Unutulan Evrensel Miras** | Challenging conventional isolationist anthropology; the worldwide distribution of flood myths, pyramids, and serpent motifs |
| **II. Hindistan ve Türk Dünyası** | Tracing ancient Indian epics (*Mahabharata*, *Rigveda*); Sanskrit terms analyzed through Turkic; the *Kurus* and *Pandavas* as steppe nomads |
| **III. Mezopotamya, İbraniler ve Türkler** | Abraham (*Brahma* / *Abram*); linguistic parallels between Hebrew, Sumerian, and Turkic religious vocabulary |
| **IV. Antik Yunan ve Anadolu Bağlantısı** | Etymology of Greek gods (Dionysus, Apollo, Zeus) linked to ancient Asian and Anatolian roots; the Pelasgians as pre-Greek Turanians |
| **V. Amerika Yerlileri ve Mayalar** | Mayan, Incan, and Aztec myths; comparative analysis of Native American languages and rituals with Siberian Turkic shamanism |
| **VI. Bütün İnsanlığın Kardeşliği** | Matlock's conclusion: humanity's deep genealogical unity through a common linguistic and mythological root |

---

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Global Diffusionist Hyper-Synthesis**: Proposes that a single, highly advanced prehistoric culture radiated across the globe following glacial meltwaters, carrying agriculture, astronomy, and pyramidal construction.
2. **Native American Linguistic Parallels**: Re-examines affinities between Amerindian languages and Siberian Turkic/Altaic tongues, arguing for sustained trans-Pacific contacts in antiquity.
3. **Humanity as One Family**: Despite its sensationalist title, Matlock's overarching thesis is humanistic—arguing that beneath superficial ethnic and religious divisions lies a shared prehistoric ancestry.
