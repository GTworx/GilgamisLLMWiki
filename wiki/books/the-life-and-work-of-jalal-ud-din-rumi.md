## Executive Summary

First published in 1956 with a warm Foreword by Cambridge Persianist **Prof. A. J. Arberry** and here presented in its 6th edition (1991, pp. 4–5), Pakistani diplomat-scholar **Afzal Iqbal's** *The Life and Work of Jalal-ud-din Rumi* is a classic comprehensive intellectual biography situating Rumi squarely inside the cataclysmic geopolitical, theological, and cultural crucible of the 13th-century Islamic world (1207–1273).

Tracing Rumi's life across three distinct periods—from his family's flight from Balkh ahead of Genghis Khan's Mongol onslaught and his formation as an orthodox scholar, through the volcanic mystical crisis triggered by Shams-i Tabrizi, to his final twelve years as mature philosopher-poet in Seljuk Konya—Iqbal treats Rumi not merely as an ecstatic lyricist, but as a systematic thinker responding to civilizational collapse with a dynamic philosophy of spiritual evolution, moral responsibility, and universal love (pp. 8–10).

---

## Structural Overview (Contents, pp. 8–10)

1. **Chapter 1 — The Age of Rumi (1207–1273)**: Maps the dual external shocks fracturing the 13th-century Muslim world—the Crusades from the West and the devastating Mongol invasions culminating in the 1258 Sack of Baghdad and the 1260 Mamluk victory at Ain Jalut (p. 8).
2. **Chapters 2–4 — Intellectual Formation & Sufi Lineage**: Charts Rumi's formidable scholarly horizon by age thirty-four, detailing his debts to **al-Ghazali**, Persian mystical pioneers **Sana'i** and **Farid al-Din Attar**, early Sufi saints (Dhu'l-Nun al-Misri, Bayazid Bistami, Ibrahim ibn Adham, and Rumi's bold defense of Mansur al-Hallaj), and classical manuals such as Hujwiri's *Kashf al-Mahjub* (p. 9).
3. **Chapter 5 — The Mystical Revolution**: Chronicles the transformative relationship with Shams-i Tabrizi, Salah al-Din Zarkub, and Husam al-Din Chalabi, showing how lyric fire in the *Divan-i Shams* matured into the didactic architecture of the *Masnavi*.
4. **Chapter 6 — The Poet as a Thinker (1261–1273, p. 10)**: Systematically analyzes Rumi's mature metaphysics: poetic intuition vs. dry scholastic philosophy, the interplay of love (*ishq*) and intellect (*aql*), spiritual evolution (from mineral to plant, animal, human, and angelic realization), free will vs. determinism, revelation, and the *Insan-i Kamil* ("Ideal Man").
5. **Chapter 7 — Global Reception**: Surveys European translation history including Latin renderings of the *Masnavi* and extensive bibliography (p. 10).

---

## Key Analytical Takeaways

1. **Geopolitical Trauma & Spiritual Renewal**: Iqbal illuminates how Konya—sheltered under Seljuk Anatolia while Khorasan and Baghdad burned—became a refugee sanctuary of scholars, artisans, and mystics, making Rumi's message of inner resilience a direct historical answer to apocalyptic chaos.
2. **Synthesis of Love and Reason**: Rejecting caricatures of Rumi as anti-intellectual, Iqbal demonstrates that Rumi critiques only *partial, ego-serving intellect* (*aql-i juz'i*), while elevating *universal intellect* (*aql-i kulli*) harmonized with love.
