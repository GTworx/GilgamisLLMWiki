## Summary
Empirical study investigating why Chief Marketing Officers suffer the shortest tenure in the C-suite, modeling the '80/20 rule' of external hiring versus internal promotions.

## Analytical Overview
### The Perilous Tenure of the Chief Marketing Officer

In *Does the 80/20 Rule of CMO Promotions Explain 'Jumpy' Careers?*, executive search and marketing leadership scholars investigate one of the most glaring anomalies in modern corporate governance: the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO) consistently suffers the shortest average tenure of any C-suite role—averaging roughly 40 months compared to over 80 months for CEOs and CFOs.

### The 80/20 External Hiring Paradox

The authors analyze the mathematical and institutional dynamics behind this rapid executive turnover:
- Over 80% of open CMO roles are filled through external recruitment rather than internal promotions, creating an unstable executive musical-chairs dynamic.
- When an enterprise experiences a revenue slowdown, the CMO is frequently the first executive sacrificed as a corporate scapegoat, because marketing spend is easily cut in the short term without immediate operational collapse.
- This creates an incentive for CMOs to execute 'jumpy' career hops every two to three years, prioritizing short-term flashy campaigns over enduring, long-term brand equity development.

### Rebuilding the CMO-CFO-CEO Strategic Alliance

The study outlines structural solutions: aligning marketing metrics with GAAP financial revenue definitions, establishing clear board-level expectations, and building robust internal marketing succession pipelines.
