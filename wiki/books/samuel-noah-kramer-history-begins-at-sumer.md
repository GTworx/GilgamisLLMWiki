## Summary
Foundational classic of Assyriology and Sumerology documenting thirty-nine cultural, institutional, and intellectual 'firsts' in recorded human history, from schools and legal codes to bicameral assemblies and epic literature.

## Analytical Overview
Kramer translates and analyzes cuneiform tablets from Nippur, Ur, and Uruk, demonstrating that the Sumerians pioneered the earliest recorded schools, bicameral political assemblies, historical writing, pharmacopoeias, agricultural manuals, philosophical disputations, and literary elegies. The work reconstructs third-millennium BCE urban civilization in southern Mesopotamia.
