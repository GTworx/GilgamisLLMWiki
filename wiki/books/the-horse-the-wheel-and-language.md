## Executive Summary

Winner of the Society for American Archaeology Book Award, Hartwick College archaeologist **David W. Anthony**'s *The Horse, the Wheel, and Language* (Princeton University Press, 2007, Copyright p. 4; 565 pp.; consolidated from two full 565-pp. library copies) is the landmark synthesis solving the two-century-old mystery of the **Proto-Indo-European (PIE) homeland**—a model dramatically corroborated a decade later by ancient DNA (aDNA) genomics.

In **Part One: Language and Archaeology** (Ch. 1–6, pp. 1–120), Anthony uses linguistic paleontology—specifically reconstructed PIE vocabulary for **wool textiles and wheeled wagons** (*kwekwlos*, axle, thill, hub, Ch. 4, pp. 59–82)—to date late PIE firmly to **c. 4000–3500 BCE** (refuting Colin Renfrew's earlier Neolithic Anatolian farming hypothesis). In **Part Two: The Opening of the Eurasian Steppes** (Ch. 7–17, pp. 121–466), he synthesizes Pontic-Caspian archaeology—horse-bit tooth wear at Botai and Dereivka (Ch. 10), the collapse of Old Europe's copper cultures (Ch. 11), the **Yamnaya** wagon-pastoralist horizon (Ch. 13), and the **Sintashta** invention of the spoked-wheel **chariot** (Ch. 15)—showing how steppe riders transmitted Indo-European languages from Ireland to India.

---

## Core Scientific & Historical Breakthroughs

1. **Wagon-Wheel Dating & Ecological Localization (Ch. 4–5)**: Proves that PIE speakers knew wheeled vehicles and domesticated horses/honeybees, pinpointing the late-PIE homeland squarely on the **Pontic-Caspian steppes** north of the Black and Caspian Seas between 4500 and 2500 BCE.
2. **Horse Domestication & Mobile Pastoralism (Ch. 10 & 13)**: Demonstrates how combining horseback riding with ox-drawn bulk wagons unlocked the deep Eurasian grassland interior for the first time, creating the highly mobile Yamnaya pastoral economy.
3. **Elite Patron–Client Hospitality & Language Shift (Ch. 6 & 14–16)**: Explains *how* steppe languages spread without genocidal replacement—through charismatic oath-bound warrior brotherhoods, lavish feasting prestige, and spoked-wheel chariot innovations (Sintashta–Andronovo) linking the steppes to Central Asia, Iran, and South Asia.
