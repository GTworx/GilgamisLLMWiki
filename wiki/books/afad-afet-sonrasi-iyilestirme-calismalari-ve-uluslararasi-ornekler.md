## Executive Summary

This monumental 270-page comparative public policy and civil engineering compendium by AFAD's Department of Disaster Recovery (İyileştirme Dairesi Başkanlığı) provides an exhaustive global survey of post-disaster recovery, reconstruction, and community rehabilitation strategies. It investigates how leading disaster-prone nations have planned, financed, and executed large-scale post-catastrophe recovery, extracting transferable lessons for the Turkish context.

The volume synthesizes institutional governance models, funding mechanisms, spatial planning protocols, and socioeconomic restitution programs across major international benchmarks, including the 1995 Kobe and 2011 Tohoku earthquakes in Japan, the 2005 Hurricane Katrina and 2012 Hurricane Sandy in the United States, the 2010 Canterbury earthquakes in New Zealand, and the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake in China. It analyzes temporary container cities, permanent housing procurement, debris recycling, and psychological trauma restoration.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Build Back Better (BBB) Governance**: Articulates the implementation of Sendai Framework Priority 4, ensuring that reconstruction does not replicate pre-disaster vulnerabilities but enhances structural, ecological, and economic resilience.
- **Temporary to Permanent Shelter Transition**: Compares temporary housing trajectories (tents, prefabricated container modular cities, subsidized rental vouchers) and their socioeconomic impacts on displaced communities.
- **Debris Management and Environmental Remediation**: Details protocols for the safe removal, sorting, recycling, and disposal of millions of tons of earthquake debris, addressing asbestos and heavy metal contamination risks.
- **Economic and Livelihood Rehabilitation**: Examines business continuity grants, subsidized agricultural credit, tax moratoria, and workforce retention programs to revitalize devastated local economies.
