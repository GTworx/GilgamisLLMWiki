## Summary
Global macroeconomic report by WIPO and Luiss Business School tracking investment in intangible assets (R&D, software, design, patents, and brand equity) relative to tangible capital across major economies.

## Analytical Overview
### The Ascendance of the Intangible Economy

*World Intangible Investment Highlights 2024*, co-published by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and Luiss Business School, provides definitive macroeconomic benchmarks on corporate capital allocation. In modern post-industrial economies, investment in intangible assets—comprising software, proprietary data, R&D, organizational architecture, and intellectual property—now outpaces physical capital investment (factories, machinery, real estate) across all OECD nations.

### Key Structural Findings

The 2024 edition highlights several transformative shifts:
- **Resilience Against Inflation and Macro Shocks:** Intangible investments demonstrated remarkable resilience through post-pandemic inflation, continuing to expand while physical capex stalled.
- **Divergence Across Geographies:** The United States, Northern Europe, and parts of East Asia maintain high intangible-to-tangible capital ratios (>1.5x), driving structural productivity premiums, while developing economies remain constrained by physical capital dependencies.
- **The Generative AI Capex Wave:** R&D in foundational software algorithms and proprietary enterprise training corpora represents the fastest-growing intangible asset class in history.

### Strategic Implications for Valuation and Corporate Debt

The report explores the severe challenges traditional accounting and banking systems face when attempting to collateralize and value intangible assets on balance sheets.
