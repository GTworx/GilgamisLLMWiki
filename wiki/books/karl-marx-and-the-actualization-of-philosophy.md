## Executive Summary

Written by Northeastern University London philosopher and *Hegel Bulletin* editor **Christoph Schuringa** (Cambridge University Press, 2025, pp. 2–5), *Karl Marx and the Actualization of Philosophy* overturns the standard narrative that Marx abandoned philosophy in favor of pure economics or political journalism after 1845. Tracing a rigorous textual arc from Marx's 1841 doctoral dissertation on **Democritus and Epicurus** (Ch. 1) through his **Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right** and logic (Ch. 2–3), the **1844 Paris Manuscripts** (*Gattungswesen*, humanism = naturalism, Ch. 4), the critique of Feuerbach and Young Hegelians (Ch. 5), and the dialectical architecture of **Capital** (Ch. 6–7), Schuringa demonstrates that Marx pursued a single lifelong ambition: overcoming isolated *"self-sufficient philosophy"* by **actualizing philosophy** as a living practical force inside historical reality (Ch. 8, pp. 180–192).

---

## Core Textual & Philosophical Contributions

1. **Continuity Across the Young and Mature Marx**: Rejects Althusser's rigid "epistemological break," showing how Marx's Aristotelian, Kantian, and Hegelian philosophical commitments deepen—rather than vanish—inside *Capital*'s analysis of commodities, labor-power, and crisis.
2. **Dialectic in *Capital* (Ch. 6–7, pp. 136–179)**: Clarifies the exact structural difference between Hegel's self-generating logical contradiction and Marx's materialist critique of real social contradictions in political economy.
3. **Beyond "Applied Philosophy" (Ch. 8)**: Argues that actualizing philosophy does not mean mechanically applying abstract academic doctrines to politics, but dissolving the sterile wall between theoretical contemplation and transformative praxis.
