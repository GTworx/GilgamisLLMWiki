## Analytical Overview
### The Frontiers of Post-Silicon Computation

The November/December 2021 issue of *MIT Technology Review* (Volume 124, Number 6, 92 pages) is a landmark thematic edition devoted entirely to **The Future of Computing**. As traditional Dennard scaling and Moore's Law reach fundamental atomic and thermodynamic limits, computer scientists, physicists, and chip architects are reinventing the computational stack from basic materials to quantum algorithms.

### Deep Architectural Explorations

The volume features authoritative investigative analyses:
- **The End of Moore's Law and Beyond Silicon:** Exploring 2D materials (graphene, transition metal dichalcogenides), carbon nanotube transistors, and optical interconnects.
- **Quantum Simulators and Co-Processors:** How noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) computers simulate molecular chemistry long before general fault-tolerant quantum computing arrives.
- **Neuromorphic Computing:** Designing silicon that mimics biological synaptic firing architectures to achieve ultra-low-power event-driven artificial intelligence.
- **Geopolitics of the Global Chip Supply Chain:** The extreme fragility of advanced semiconductor lithography and the geopolitical tensions surrounding Taiwan.

### Essential Reading for Technologists

The issue provides a comprehensive technological roadmap for the hardware and software systems that will power 21st-century civilization.
