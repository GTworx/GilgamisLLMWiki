## Executive Summary

Published in March 1776, Scottish moral philosopher **Adam Smith's** *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* is the foundational text of modern political economy. Challenging two centuries of European **mercantilism**—which equated national wealth with hoarded bullion and state-granted trading monopolies—Smith argues that true prosperity flows from the annual productive labor of a nation, multiplied dramatically by the **division of labor**, capital accumulation, market competition, and institutional security of property and justice (Book I, pp. 11–49).

Far from advocating naive corporate laissez-faire, Smith pairs his famous analysis of decentralized price coordination ("the invisible hand") with blistering skepticism of merchant cartels, East India Company rent-seeking, and stagnant monopolies—while outlining explicit public duties for the sovereign in national defense, impartial justice, public infrastructure, and basic popular education to counteract the mental numbing of factory specialization.

## Five-Book Architecture (Contents, pp. 3–8)

1. **Book I — Of the Causes of Improvement in the Productive Powers of Labour**: Introduces the pin-factory **division of labor**, demonstrates that specialization is bounded by the **extent of the market** (pp. 16–39), traces the origin of money, and decomposes commodity prices into wages, profit, and rent.
2. **Book II — Of the Nature, Accumulation, and Employment of Stock**: Distinguishes productive from unproductive labor, analyzes fixed vs. circulating capital, and examines money, credit, and banking.
3. **Book III — Of the Different Progress of Opulence in Different Nations**: Traces Europe's economic transformation from feudal estates to commercial towns and agrarian productivity.
4. **Book IV — Of Systems of Political Economy**: Mounts Smith's comprehensive dismantling of mercantilism and colonial trade monopolies (including his celebrated digression on the Bank of Amsterdam, p. 625) alongside a nuanced critique of French Physiocracy.
5. **Book V — Of the Revenue of the Sovereign or Commonwealth**: Articulates Smith's four canons of taxation (equity, certainty, convenience, economy), public debt risks, and the proper scope of public works and education.
