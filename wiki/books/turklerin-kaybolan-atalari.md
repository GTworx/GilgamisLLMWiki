## Executive Summary

In *Türklerin Kaybolan Ataları* (*The Lost Ancestors of the Turks*), **Kâzım Mirşan** sets out to recover the forgotten historical memory and tribal confederations of ancient Eurasia that have been mislabeled or obscured in Greco-Roman, Chinese, and modern Eurocentric historiography. Analyzing ancient inscriptions, petroglyphs, and Chinese dynastic annals (*Shiji*, *Hanshu*), Mirşan traces forgotten ancestral groups—such as the **Dingling, Yuezhi, Wusun, Gutians, and Scythians**—demonstrating their deep cultural, linguistic, and political continuities with later historical Turkic qağanates.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **De-sinicizing and De-hellenizing Steppe Records**: Demonstrates that many tribal names appearing in Chinese or Greek records (e.g., Ting-ling, Wu-sun) were phonetic approximations of early Turkic tribal titles.
2. **Technological Innovations of the Steppe Ancestors**: Attributes key ancient Eurasian technological leaps—such as advanced iron metallurgy, horse-drawn carts, and the compound recurve bow—to early northern nomadic ancestors.
3. **Cultural Continuity Across Millennia**: Rejects catastrophic break models in Eurasian history, highlighting the continuous transmission of burial rites, tamga scripts, and state organization.
