## Analytical Overview
### The Autopsy of Corporate Extinction

*50 Examples of Corporations That Failed to Innovate* compiles 108 pages of rigorous post-mortem case studies detailing the downfall of market-leading global corporations that succumbed to technological disruption. From Kodak inventing the digital camera only to shelve it for film margins, to Blockbuster rejecting the opportunity to purchase Netflix for $50 million, the compendium serves as a definitive catalog of incumbent blindness.

### Typology of Incumbent Failure Modes

The report synthesizes 50 corporate autopsies into recurring organizational pathologies:
- **Cannibalization Terror:** Refusing to commercialize disruptive internal inventions because doing so would jeopardize lucrative legacy revenue streams (e.g. Xerox PARC, Kodak, Polaroid).
- **Ecosystem Blindness:** Focusing exclusively on hardware specs while failing to recognize that platform operating systems and app developer ecosystems constitute the true competitive moat (e.g. Nokia, Blackberry, Palm).
- **Customer Feedback Myopia:** Mistaking the satisfaction of conservative current enterprise clients for immunity against cheaper, lower-fidelity upstart technologies (Christensen's classic Innovator's Dilemma).
- **Bureaucratic Committee Gridlock:** Suffocating novel ideas through multi-layered executive approvals, discounted cash flow (DCF) hurdles, and risk-averse legal vetting.

### Strategic Lessons for Executive Survival

The book establishes essential governance principles to help contemporary boards identify early warning signs of cultural stagnation.
