## Overview
Presented at ACM CHI 2017, this foundational treatise argues that the ethical challenges of immersive human-computer interaction, algorithms, and virtual spaces cannot be resolved by deontological rules or utilitarian calculus alone. Instead, the authors construct an Aristotelian virtue ethics framework for HCI designers, emphasizing human flourishing (eudaimonia), moral character cultivation, and intentional technological restraint.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Critiques persuasive technology and dark patterns that optimize engagement at the expense of user moral agency.
- **Key Finding**: Formulates eudaimonic design principles for immersive environments that nurture reflection, empathy, and wisdom.
- **Key Finding**: Provides an actionable design rubric translating classical virtues (temperance, courage, phronesis) into software interface patterns.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "The question for HCI is not merely 'Does this interface function efficiently?', but 'What kind of human being does prolonged interaction with this system foster?'"
