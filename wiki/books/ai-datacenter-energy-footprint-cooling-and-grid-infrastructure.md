## Summary
Engineering, energy economics, and industrial supply chain compendium addressing the physical constraints of the AI revolution. Compiles MIT Technology Review's 'AI's Energy Footprint' report, Brookings Institution case studies on electricity grid capacity, BCG executive perspectives on AI in supply chains, and novel physical paradigms including probabilistic photonic computing with chaotic light.

## Analytical Overview
This volume confronts the physical reality underpinning the virtual intelligence revolution: megawatts, water cooling, transmission lines, and silicon supply chains.

Key infrastructure dimensions:
1. **Datacenter Power Surges & Grid Interconnection Delays**: Features MIT Technology Review and Brookings reports modeling the exponential increase in datacenter power demand—projected to consume up to 8–10% of US electricity by 2030—driving tech giants (Microsoft, Amazon, Google) to secure dedicated nuclear power plant PPAs (Three Mile Island, SMRs) and geothermal generation to meet round-the-clock clean energy mandates.
2. **Liquid Cooling & Thermal Engineering**: Evaluates direct-to-chip liquid cooling, rear-door heat exchangers, and immersion cooling required for next-generation 100kW+ server racks housing Nvidia Blackwell GPUs.
3. **Photonic Computing Frontiers**: Incorporates research into probabilistic photonic computing utilizing chaotic light, exploring non-silicon optical hardware that performs matrix multiplication at the speed of light with a fraction of electronic energy consumption.
