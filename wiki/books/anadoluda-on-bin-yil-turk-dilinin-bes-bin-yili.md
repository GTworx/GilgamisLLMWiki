## Executive Summary

Authored by independent philologist and epigrapher **Selâhi Diker**, *Anadolu'da On Bin Yıl: Türk Dili'nin Beş Bin Yılı* (*Ten Thousand Years in Anatolia: Five Thousand Years of the Turkish Language*) is a massive, 574-page comparative epigraphic and linguistic treatise that seeks to decipher the 'lost languages' of antiquity—including **Sumerian, Etruscan, Parthian, Phrygian, Hurrian, Urartian, Scythian, Achaemenid Elamite, and Aramaic**—through the morphological rules, root vocabulary, and runic scripts of early Turkic languages.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Decipherment of Etruscan via Turkic**: Aligns with Mario Alinei and Adile Ayda, arguing that the undeciphered non-Indo-European Etruscan language of ancient Italy exhibits regular agglutinative grammar and vocabulary solvable through Turkic.
2. **Scythian and Issyk Kul Inscription**: Reads the mysterious 26-character runiform inscription on the silver cup from the Issyk Kurgan (Kazakhstan, 5th c. BCE) as an archaic Turkic dialect, pushing written Turkic back a millennium prior to the Orkhon stones.
3. **Anatolian Indigenous Continuity**: Contends that the ancestors of the Turks did not arrive in Anatolia for the first time in 1071 CE, but inhabited the Anatolian plateau continuously since the Neolithic era.
