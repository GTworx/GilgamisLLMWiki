## Executive Summary

Edited by world-renowned executive coach **Marshall Goldsmith** and leadership scholar **Laurence S. Lyons** (Pfeiffer/Wiley, 2nd ed., 2006, Title Page p. 2, Contents p. 10; 284 pp.), *Coaching for Leadership* assembles the canonical methodologies of the field's top practitioners—including Edgar Schein, Paul Hersey, Warren Bennis, Beverly Kaye, Jim Kouzes, Barry Posner, and Judith Glaser—to establish professional standards amid a booming, unregulated coaching industry (p. 4).

Organized across **Foundations of Coaching** (Part I, p. 28), **Building Blocks** (Part II, p. 70), **Leading Change** (Part III, p. 112), and advanced strategic interventions, the anthology bridges organizational psychology, situational leadership, and measurable behavioral change for senior executives.

---

## Core Frameworks & Insights

1. **Coaching vs. Consultation & Situational Executive Coaching (Ch. 2–3, pp. 44–62)**: Clarifies role boundaries (who initiates, who is coached, and what organizational system is served) and pairs Hersey's Situational Leadership with gap-and-cause diagnostics and leading with questions.
2. **Pay-for-Results Behavioral Coaching & Feedforward (Ch. 4–5, pp. 64–76)**: Introduces Goldsmith's stakeholder-centered "Pay for Results" model alongside his signature **Feedforward** technique—replacing defensive past-focused feedback with forward-looking, constructive suggestions for future behavior.
3. **Feedback Acceptance, Upward Influence & Strategic Coaching (Ch. 6–11, pp. 77–125)**: Details a four-step feedback-acceptance ladder (Hearing → Accepting → Prioritizing → Changing), "Selling Up Is Leading Up" (Ch. 10), and positioning coaching directly at the heart of corporate strategy (Ch. 11).
