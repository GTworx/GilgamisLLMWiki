## Executive Summary

Edited by pioneering world historian **Peter N. Stearns** with thirty specialist historians over a ten-year revision (Houghton Mifflin, 6th Edition, 2001, p. 1; 3,540 pp.), *The Encyclopedia of World History: Ancient, Medieval, and Modern* continues and radically globalizes William L. Langer's classic chronological reference tradition. Spanning more than **20,000 structured chronological entries** from human origins (4 million BCE) to the year 2000 CE, the volume is organized systematically by era, region, and institutional domain rather than alphabetical fragments.

Opening with a rigorous prehistoric and archaeological foundation (Part I: *Prehistoric Times*, Outline p. 87—covering *Homo erectus*, *Out of Africa*, Holocene hunter-gatherers, Old and New World agricultural transitions, and early state-organized webs of relations), Stearns advances through Classical Antiquity, the Post-Classical/Medieval era, Early Modern globalization, the Long Nineteenth Century, and the Twentieth-Century world system, integrating social, demographic, scientific, and cultural history alongside diplomacy and warfare.
