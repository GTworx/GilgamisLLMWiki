## Summary
Two-volume neuro-evolutionary synthesis linking the adaptive functional architecture of human social anxiety and social exclusion monitoring to mammalian neocortical field expansion and phenotypic plasticity.

## Analytical Overview
Maner explores social anxiety not merely as clinical dysfunction, but as an evolved adaptive behavioral guidance system designed to prevent ostracism, maintain hierarchy status, and navigate coalitional perils in small ancestral bands. Krubitzer reviews cortical field evolution across mammals, demonstrating how genetic constraints and sensory-driven activity interact to forge specialized neocortical processing architectures.
