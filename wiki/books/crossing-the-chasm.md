## Executive Summary

High-tech marketing strategist **Geoffrey A. Moore** reshaped how Silicon Valley commercializes discontinuous innovations with *Crossing the Chasm* (first published 1991; refreshed in 1999 and 2014 to update case studies and incorporate mobile/cloud consumer IT—both the 3rd edition [288 pp.] and the 1999/2001 revised edition [174 pp.] are preserved in our library). Moore's core insight corrects the classic bell-curve **Technology Adoption Life Cycle**: rather than flowing smoothly across five psychographic cohorts (*Innovators, Early Adopters, Early Majority, Late Majority, Laggards*), discontinuous technologies face a treacherous **chasm** between visionary **Early Adopters** (who seek breakthrough change agents) and pragmatic **Early Majority** buyers (who demand proven productivity improvements and peer references, Part I).

Startups routinely stall or die in the chasm because Early Majority pragmatists refuse to accept visionary Early Adopters as credible references. Moore's **D-Day beachhead strategy** (Part II) prescribes concentrating overwhelming resources on a single, tightly bounded vertical niche until the venture achieves dominant referenceability, then expanding sequentially like pins in a **bowling alley** toward mainstream hypergrowth (*the Tornado*).

---

## Core Strategic Toolkit

1. **Beachhead Niche Selection**: Pick one underserved mainstream segment with an urgent, painful problem ("compelling reason to buy") small enough to win decisively without diluting sales and engineering capacity.
2. **The Whole Product Concept**: Pragmatists never buy raw generic technology; winning the chasm requires assembling the complete **Whole Product** (core product + integrations, support, training, standards, and partner ecosystem, 3rd ed. pp. 130–137).
3. **Competitive Positioning Compass**: Anchor value propositions against a recognizable market alternative and a clear product alternative so conservative buyers can categorize and justify the purchase.
4. **Post-Chasm Expansion (3rd Ed. Appendices)**: Maps the full arc from Early Market → Chasm → Bowling Alley → Tornado → Main Street, plus how mobile/cloud consumerization inverted traditional B2B-first IT adoption (3rd ed., Author's Note pp. 4–5).
