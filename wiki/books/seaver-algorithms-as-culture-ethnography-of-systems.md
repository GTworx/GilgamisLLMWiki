## Overview
Published in Big Data & Society by anthropologist Nick Seaver (Tufts University), this seminal paper provides an ethnographic methodology for researching computational algorithms. Seaver argues that algorithms are not self-contained mathematical black boxes, but messy, emergent cultural phenomena enacted by software engineers, corporate metrics, server maintenance, and user adaptations.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Reconceptualizes algorithms: from sterile mathematical formulas to dynamic 'heterogeneous cultural systems'.
- **Key Finding**: Outlines field tactics for studying secret corporate algorithms: interviewing engineers, tracking edge cases, and parsing system logs.
- **Key Finding**: Demystifies the 'black box' metaphor by treating algorithmic code as human sociotechnical practice.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Algorithms are not simply technical objects that affect culture from the outside; they are composed of human culture through and through—stitched together by coffee-fueled debugging, corporate politics, and cultural assumptions."
