## Executive Summary

*A Brief History of Artificial Intelligence: What It Is, Where We Are, and Where We Are Going* (published by Flatiron Books / Macmillan, 2020, ISBN 978-1-250-77073-8) by Oxford Computer Science Head [[michael-wooldridge]] is an authoritative nine-chapter intellectual history and philosophical assessment of artificial intelligence written by one of the field's foremost multi-agent theorists (source: BOOKS/AI/a-brief-history-of-artificial-intelligence-what-it-is-where-we-are-and-where-we-are-going.epub, package.opf, Introduction, Contents). Written to strip away both Hollywood dystopian sensationalism and Silicon Valley silver-bullet hype, Wooldridge traces how AI repeatedly swung between euphoric over-promising and devastating 'AI Winters' whenever a single paradigm—heuristic search, symbolic logic, expert systems, or connectionist perceptrons—was mistaken for the complete recipe for human intelligence (source: BOOKS/AI/a-brief-history-of-artificial-intelligence-what-it-is-where-we-are-and-where-we-are-going.epub, ch. 1–5, 9).

Across nine chapters, Wooldridge examines Alan Turing's 1936 universal machine and 1950 'Computing Machinery and Intelligence' (**Chapter 1: Turing's Electronic Brains**); the 1956 Dartmouth Workshop, Newell and Simon's Logic Theorist/GPS, and the combinatorial explosion that prompted the 1973 UK **Lighthill Report** (**Chapter 2: The Golden Age**); Ed Feigenbaum's MYCIN/DENDRAL expert systems, Douglas Lenat's Cyc common-sense knowledge base, and the second AI winter (**Chapter 3: Knowledge Is Power**); Rodney Brooks's subsumption robotics and the 1990s shift to decision-theoretic and game-theoretic **Rational Agents** (**Chapter 4: Robots and Rationality**); the deep learning breakthroughs from Geoffrey Hinton to Google's $650M acquisition of DeepMind and AlphaGo (**Chapters 5–6: Deep Breakthroughs & AI Today**); debunking 'Terminator/Singularity' myths vs. real-world algorithmic bias, autonomous weapons, deepfakes, and technological unemployment (**Chapters 7–8: How We Imagine Things Might Go Wrong vs. How Things Might Actually Go Wrong**); and why deep learning lacks out-of-distribution common sense, intentionality, and phenomenal consciousness (**Chapter 9: Conscious Machines?**) (source: BOOKS/AI/a-brief-history-of-artificial-intelligence-what-it-is-where-we-are-and-where-we-are-going.epub, ch. 1–9).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Chapters 1–3: Turing's Electronic Brains, The Golden Age & Knowledge Is Power**:
  * *Symbolic AI, Combinatorial Explosion & The Lighthill Report*: Traces AI from Bletchley Park and Dartmouth (1956) through early theorem provers and Shakey the Robot, showing how exponential state-space growth triggered Sir James Lighthill's 1973 critique and how 1980s rule-based Expert Systems and Cyc collided with the 'knowledge acquisition bottleneck' and brittle common-sense reasoning (source: BOOKS/AI/a-brief-history-of-artificial-intelligence-what-it-is-where-we-are-and-where-we-are-going.epub, ch. 1–3).

* **Chapters 4–6: Robots and Rationality, Deep Breakthroughs & AI Today**:
  * *Embodied Subsumption, Game-Theoretic Agents & DeepMind*: Contrasts Rodney Brooks's reactive insectoid robots ('the world is its own best model') with the utility-maximizing **Rational Agent** paradigm (Markov Decision Processes and multi-agent game theory), then reconstructs how GPUs, big data, and convolutional/recurrent networks fueled DeepMind's DQN Atari and AlphaGo triumphs (source: BOOKS/AI/a-brief-history-of-artificial-intelligence-what-it-is-where-we-are-and-where-we-are-going.epub, ch. 4–6).

* **Chapters 7–9: Sci-Fi Myths vs. Real Risks & Conscious Machines?**:
  * *Why Deep Learning Is Not a Silver Bullet for General or Conscious AI*: Debunks media panics (such as the 2017 Facebook negotiation-bot 'secret language' story, Ch. 7) to focus on genuine socio-technical hazards—Frey and Osborne's automation exposure, opaque credit/judicial scoring, lethal autonomous weapons, and deepfake disinformation (Ch. 8)—concluding in Ch. 9 that current AI possesses zero phenomenal consciousness ('what it is like to be') or semantic understanding (source: BOOKS/AI/a-brief-history-of-artificial-intelligence-what-it-is-where-we-are-and-where-we-are-going.epub, ch. 7–9).

## Key Quotes & Citations

Many academics started to treat AI as a pseudoscience—the field has really only recently recovered from the reputational damage it suffered during the AI winter." * "If I have succeeded in doing one thing in this book, I hope it is to have convinced you that, while the recent breakthroughs in AI and machine learning are real and exciting, they are not a silver bullet for general AI.
