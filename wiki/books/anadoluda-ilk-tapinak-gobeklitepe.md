## Executive Summary

Published by historian of religions **Prof. Dr. Ali Osman Kurt**, *Anadolu'da İlk Tapınak: Göbeklitepe* (*The First Temple in Anatolia: Göbeklitepe*) provides a comprehensive archaeological and religious analysis of the monumental Pre-Pottery Neolithic (PPN) sanctuary of **Göbeklitepe** near Şanlıurfa (dating to c. 9600–8200 BCE). Incorporating Klaus Schmidt's seminal excavations, Kurt evaluates how Göbeklitepe fundamentally overturned V. Gordon Childe's classic 'Neolithic Revolution' thesis by proving that **monumental religious architecture and ritual assembly preceded settled agriculture**, acting as the primary catalyst for farming and domestication rather than its byproduct.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Religion Preceded Agriculture**: Inverts the materialist archaeological paradigm: human beings did not build temples because they farmed; they domesticated wild einkorn wheat in the Karacadağ slopes to feed thousands of hunter-gatherers assembling for sacred rituals.
2. **Anthropomorphic Architecture**: Interprets the T-pillars as stylized depictions of supernatural ancestors or deities—represented with arms, belts, and loincloths, but deliberately faceless.
3. **The Center of Neolithic Anatolia**: Connects Göbeklitepe with the wider **Taş Tepeler** network (Karahantepe, Sayburç, Çakmaktepe), establishing southeastern Anatolia as the epicenter of global human civilization 11,500 years ago.
