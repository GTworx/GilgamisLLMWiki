## Executive Summary

Written by Foundry Group co-founders **Brad Feld** (serial entrepreneur/Techstars co-founder) and **Jason Mendelson** (former Cooley VC attorney turned managing director)—with an enthusiastic Foreword by then-Twitter CEO **Dick Costolo** (pp. 12–13)—*Venture Deals* (Wiley, 2011, copyright p. 9) is universally recognized as the gold-standard field manual on U.S. startup financing and term-sheet negotiation. Drawing on 17+ years across hundreds of venture transactions (Preface p. 14), Feld and Mendelson strip away legal obfuscation so founders understand what truly matters—and what is mere noise—when negotiating with VCs and lawyers.

Structured across 13 lucid chapters plus full sample **Term Sheet** and **M&A Letter of Intent (LOI)** appendices (pp. 188–203), the book distills every venture financing down to just **two overarching dimensions**: **Economics** (who gets how much money at exit) and **Control** (who makes which binding decisions along the way, Ch. 3, p. 45).

## Core Term-Sheet & Negotiation Playbook

1. **Economic Terms (Ch. 4, pp. 47–71)**: Explains pre-money vs. post-money valuation, the hidden "option pool shuffle," **liquidation preferences** (1x non-participating vs. participating "double-dip"), pay-to-play, 4-year founder vesting with 1-year cliff, and weighted-average vs. full-ratchet anti-dilution.
2. **Control Terms (Ch. 5, pp. 72–81)**: Demystifies board composition (founder, VC, and mutually agreed independent seats), stockholder **protective provisions** (vetoes over sale, new stock, debt), drag-along rights, and mandatory IPO conversion triggers.
3. **How VC Funds Actually Work (Ch. 8, pp. 110–123)**: Reveals the 2-and-20 management fee/carry structure, 10-year fund lifecycles, follow-on reserve allocation, cross-fund conflicts, and GP fiduciary duties so founders grasp why VCs behave as they do.
4. **Game-Theoretic Negotiation & M&A LOIs (Ch. 9–13, pp. 124–186)**: Applies collaborative negotiation and repeated-game leverage (warning against NDA requests, email carpet-bombing, and bluffing walk-aways), decodes acquisition **Letters of Intent** (asset vs. stock deals, escrows, earn-outs), and highlights mandatory legal traps (83(b) elections, 409A valuations, Delaware C-corp incorporation).
