## Executive Summary
Published in the *Journal of Modern Transportation*, this foundational 16-page technical survey provides a comprehensive review of the engineering, sensing, and infrastructural challenges facing autonomous vehicles (AVs). The authors evaluate the complementary trade-offs of LiDAR, radar, ultrasonic sensors, and optical cameras for 360-degree environmental perception. The paper explores the algorithmic intricacies of simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), high-definition 3D vector maps, Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) communication, edge computing architectures, and the long tail of adverse weather and sensor blinding.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Sensor Fusion Modalities**: Demonstrates that optical cameras provide rich semantics but fail in heavy fog or glare, while radar offers robust velocity tracking but poor resolution, requiring deep sensor fusion.
- **Localization and HD Mapping**: Shows that centimeter-level GNSS positioning must be continuously reconciled against local LiDAR point-cloud feature matching.
- **The Long-Tail Problem**: Highlights that achieving 99% autonomy requires modest effort, but solving the remaining 1% of edge cases (unusual debris, hand signals) demands orders of magnitude more validation.
- **V2X Communication**: Details how vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication mitigates blind intersections and optimizes intersection throughput without traffic signals.
