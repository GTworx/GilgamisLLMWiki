## Summary
Deep philological and morphological compendium investigating Turkish historical grammar, Persian lexical borrowings, folk etymologies, and proverbial semantics. Features Ahmet Bican Ercilasun's treatises on folk etymology, morphological evolution of past-tense suffixes in Old Anatolian Turkish (-dUk-), Persian compound verbs and Ilkhanid-era derivational affixes, and thematic classifications of proverbs across Divan poetry and colloquial vernaculars.

## Analytical Overview
This volume gathers specialized linguistic research tracing the internal structural logic and intense historical contact zones of the Turkish language.

Key philological investigations:
1. **The Structural Logic of Agglutinative Morphology**: Explores *Türkçenin Mantığı* (The Logic of Turkish) and Old Anatolian Turkish morphosyntax, examining participle transformations, copular enclitics, and the diachronic evolution of the `-dUk-` past participle.
2. **Perso-Turkic Lexical and Morphological Synthesis**: Documents the linguistic interchange between Persian and Turkish during the Seljuk, Ilkhanid, and Ottoman eras. Analyzes Ilkhanid-era Persian derivational affixes naturalized into Turkish grammar, 100 high-frequency Persian compound verbs, and the false etymological assumptions (Halk Etimolojisi) analyzed by Ahmet Bican Ercilasun.
3. **Proverbial Semantics and Folkloric Register**: Features linguistic corpora evaluating the affective resonance of Turkish proverbs—contrasting the refined, courtly metaphors of Divan poetry (*olumlu duygular*) with earthy, transgressive vernacular expressions (*kaba sözler*) across historical centuries.
