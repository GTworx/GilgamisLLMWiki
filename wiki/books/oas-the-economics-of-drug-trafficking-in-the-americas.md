## Analytical Overview
### The Microeconomics of Illicit Transnational Cartels

*The Economics of Drug Trafficking*, published by the Organization of American States (OAS) as part of its landmark hemispheric study *The Drug Problem in the Americas*, applies rigorous microeconomic theory, industrial organization, and supply-chain logistics to transnational criminal syndicates. Stripping away sensationalist media tropes, the report evaluates drug trafficking organizations (DTOs) as rational, profit-maximizing multinational corporate enterprises operating in high-risk, unregulated markets.

### The Escalation of Price Markups and Risk Premiums

The report documents the staggering economic markup that occurs along illicit supply chains:
- A kilogram of coca leaf worth pennies in rural Bolivia or Colombia transforms into wholesale cocaine in Mexico worth thousands of dollars, and ultimately retails on street corners in New York or London for over $100,000.
- Over 90% of the final retail price represents pure **enforcement risk premium**: compensating smugglers and dealers for the risk of interdiction, violence, and incarceration.

### Cartel Monopolies and Violence as Business Strategy

The OAS proves that violence is not irrational savagery, but an economic enforcement mechanism: cartels deploy violent coercion to secure territorial smuggling corridors ('plazas'), enforce debt collection, and barrier-block new market entrants in the absence of legal contract courts.
