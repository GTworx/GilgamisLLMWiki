## Summary
Methodological treatise explaining the scientometric and bibliometric algorithm developed by Thomson Reuters to forecast Nobel Prize laureates using Web of Science citation data.

## Analytical Overview
### Scientometric Forecasting of Scientific Breakthroughs

*Identifying Researchers of Nobel Class: The Citation Laureates Quantitative Methodology*, published by Thomson Reuters (now Clarivate), details the mathematical and bibliometric methodology used to identify researchers whose scientific papers have achieved peer influence of "Nobel class." Since its inception in 2002, the Citation Laureates method has successfully predicted dozens of Nobel Prize winners in Physiology or Medicine, Physics, Chemistry, and Economics years before their official recognition by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences.

### The Mathematics of Bibliometric Impact

The methodology relies on rigorous quantitative filters applied across the Web of Science database:
- **Total Citation Count and Highly Cited Papers:** Identifying the top 0.1% of scientists whose publications rank in the highest tier of historical citations.
- **Citation Acceleration & Hub Influence:** Measuring the velocity and interdisciplinary diffusion of citations, demonstrating that a paper has opened up an entirely new research subfield.
- **Peer Recognition & Crucial Discoveries:** Pairing citation data with qualitative assessment of whether the researcher made a discrete, foundational discovery (such as the Higgs boson, carbon nanotubes, or CRISPR).

### Epistemic Significance

The document serves as a prime case study in the sociology of science, proving that mathematical citation networks mirror the collective epistemic consensus of global scientific communities.
