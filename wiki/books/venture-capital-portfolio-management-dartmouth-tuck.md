## Executive Summary

Prepared by Andy Waldeck under the supervision of Professor Fred Wainwright and Professor Colin Blaydon at the **Center for Private Equity and Entrepreneurship at Dartmouth College's Tuck School of Business**, this case monograph provides an inside look at **Venture Capital Portfolio Management and Follow-On Capital Allocation**. The case follows a leading institutional venture firm as it manages a $250 million early-stage fund reaching its third year of deployment. Facing macroeconomic shifts and divergent portfolio company trajectories, the partners must make critical decisions regarding reserve sizing, follow-on pro-rata execution, bridging struggling investments, writing down non-performing assets, and balancing partner time allocation between promising winners and troubled turnaround situations.

---

## Key Strategic & Financial Takeaways

- **The Follow-On Capital Allocation Dilemma**: General partners face difficult strategic trade-offs: should follow-on capital reserves be concentrated in breakout companies raising at high valuations, or used to sustain promising companies experiencing temporary cash runway crises?
- **Reserve Allocation Mathematical Modeling**: Demonstrates how failure to properly reserve follow-on capital causes severe dilution in later funding rounds, destroying the fund's ownership stake and ultimate return multiple in eventual breakout acquisitions.
- **The Sunk Cost Fallacy in Startup Turnarounds**: Analyzes the psychological and operational trap of 'throwing good money after bad'—where partners spend disproportionate time and capital trying to rescue underperforming portfolio companies rather than supporting top-performing breakout assets.
- **Syndicate Dynamics and Internal Rounds**: Examines the delicate negotiations between syndicate investors when an insider bridge round or pay-to-play recapitalization is required, managing conflicts of interest between early and late-stage co-investors.
- **GP Time Management and Board Stewardship**: Highlights that a venture partner's most scarce asset is not capital, but time: managing more than 8 to 10 active corporate board seats severely degrades a partner's ability to provide high-value strategic stewardship.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations

- "In venture capital, your first check buys you an option; your follow-on checks buy you the return. A firm that runs out of reserves right when its best companies hit their growth inflection is doomed to mediocre performance." (source: Dartmouth Tuck Case, p. 5)
- "The hardest discipline in venture partnership meetings is having the courage to say no to follow-on bridge financing for a company you love, acknowledging when the thesis has failed." (source: Dartmouth Tuck Case, p. 14)
