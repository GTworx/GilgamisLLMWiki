## Executive Summary

Written in Ankara in September 2007—shortly before Osman Pamukoğlu founded the Rights and Equality Party (HEPAR) in 2008—*İnsan ve Devlet: Arada Sıkışıp Kalanlar* ("The Human Being and the State: Those Caught in Between") articulates Pamukoğlu's comprehensive political philosophy, state theory, and national-security diagnosis for early-21st-century Turkey (pp. 3, 7, 9). Framed by an epigraph from Jean-Paul Sartre lamenting humanity's unchanging persistence in war, hunger, and injustice (p. 5), the book examines the perennial friction between individual human nature, state sovereignty, political leadership, and democratic institutions.

## Key Analytical Arguments

1. **Human Nature as the Pivot of Statecraft**: Argues that institutional blueprints fail when they ignore psychological constants—ambition, fear, tribalism, and self-interest—making leadership character decisive for state integrity (pp. 13–58).
2. **Four-Vector Threat Assessment (2007)**: Maps the convergence of armed/political insurgency (PKK), regional superpower realignment in Iraq, European Union leverage, and the politicization of religion (*dinin siyasallaşması*) as simultaneous structural challenges to the secular Republic (pp. 133–206).
3. **Republican Civic Sovereignty**: Grounds Turkish democracy in the historical continuity of Turkish statecraft and Kemalist republicanism, warning against clientelism and partisan capture of public institutions.
