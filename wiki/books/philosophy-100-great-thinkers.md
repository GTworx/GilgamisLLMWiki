## Executive Summary

Written by historian and popular-reference author **Jeremy Harwood** (Quercus, 2010, copyright p. 3), *Philosophy: A Beginner's Guide to the Ideas of 100 Great Thinkers* is a richly structured chronological handbook mapping 2,600 years of global philosophical thought across five major eras: **The Ancient World** (pp. 10–60), **The Middle Ages** (pp. 61–85), **The Early Modern Era** (pp. 86–139), **The Modern Era** (pp. 140–211), and **The Post-Modern Era** (pp. 212–283), capped with a concise philosophical glossary (pp. 284–289).

Notably inclusive of Eastern, Islamic, and Jewish medieval bridges (*Buddha, Confucius, Lao-tzu, Avicenna, Averroes, Maimonides*) alongside the canonical Western trajectory from Thales and Plato through Kant, Hegel, Marx, Nietzsche, Wittgenstein, Popper, Lacan, Turing, Rawls, Foucault, Chomsky, and Saul Kripke (TOC pp. 4–7), the volume serves as a rapid cross-referencing compass across our entire philosophical corpus.

## Structural Highlights

1. **Five-Epoch Intellectual Cartography**: Pairs biographical timelines with crisp distillations of each thinker's signature problem, core argument, and lasting influence.
2. **Bridging Analytic, Continental & Scientific Traditions**: Places Frege, Russell, Carnap, Quine, Alan Turing, Thomas Kuhn, and Kripke directly alongside Heidegger, Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir, Simone Weil, Jacques Lacan, and Jacques Derrida.
