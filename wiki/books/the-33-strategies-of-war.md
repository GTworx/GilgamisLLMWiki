## Executive Summary

Third major strategic treatise by **Robert Greene** (2006, Viking, pp. 3–6), *The 33 Strategies of War* distills three millennia of military history—drawing on Sun Tzu, Alexander the Great, Hannibal, Napoleon, Clausewitz, Admiral Nelson, Mao Zedong, T. E. Lawrence, and Erwin Rommel alongside modern corporate and political battles—into thirty-three actionable frameworks for navigating everyday competition, organizational conflict, and psychological warfare.

---

## Five-Part Strategic Taxonomy

1. **Self-Directed Warfare**: Declaring war on internal inertia, refusing to fight the last war, cultivating presence of mind under pressure, and creating controlled urgency (*burning your boats*).
2. **Organizational (Team) Warfare**: Avoiding groupthink, segmenting forces for decentralized agility (Napoleon's corps system), and aligning morale around a unifying crusade.
3. **Defensive Warfare**: Picking battles selectively, trading space for time (Fabian strategy), building credible deterrence, and knowing when strategic retreat preserves strength.
4. **Offensive Warfare**: Mastering intelligence before engaging, exploiting morale weaknesses, overwhelming centers of gravity, and executing rapid blitzkriegs of surprise.
5. **Unconventional (Dirty) Warfare**: Controlling perception, passive-aggressive jujitsu, asymmetric guerrilla endurance, and knowing how to conclude conflict cleanly without planting seeds of future revenge.
