## Executive Summary
This 23-page comprehensive clinical review investigates the complex, high-mortality clinical intersection between Lung Cancer and Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD), particularly Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF).

The review analyzes shared pathophysiological mechanisms connecting pulmonary fibrosis with malignant transformation, including repetitive epithelial cell micro-injury, telomere shortening, aberrant myofibroblast activation, epigenetic dysregulation, and persistent oxidative inflammation. The authors explore the severe diagnostic and therapeutic dilemmas that emerge when cancer develops within a fibrotic lung: high-resolution CT (HRCT) distortion of tumor margins, high risk of lethal acute exacerbation of ILD triggered by thoracic surgery or stereotactic body radiation (SBRT), and the safety profile of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in ILD patients.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Shared Pathophysiological Pathways**: Details how chronic alveolar micro-injury, myofibroblast senescence, and TGF-beta signaling drive both progressive fibrotic remodeling and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) toward cancer.
- **High Incidence of Cancer in IPF**: Documents that patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis have a 7- to 14-fold increased relative risk of developing lung cancer, predominantly peripheral squamous cell and adenocarcinoma.
- **The Deadly Threat of Acute Exacerbations**: Highlights that lung resection surgery, radiation therapy, and cytotoxic chemotherapy carry a 10% to 20% risk of triggering a lethal acute exacerbation of ILD (AE-ILD).
- **Targeted and Immunotherapy Considerations**: Examines emerging antifibrotic agents (nintedanib, pirfenidone) co-administered with antineoplastic regimens to preserve pulmonary functional reserve.
