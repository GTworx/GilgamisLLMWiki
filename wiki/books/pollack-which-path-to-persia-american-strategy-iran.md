## Summary
Monumental 170-page Brookings Institution grand strategy report evaluating six policy options for the United States toward Iran: diplomatic engagement, economic sanctions, internal regime change, popular revolution, surgical airstrikes, and full-scale invasion.

## Analytical Overview
### The Grand Strategic Calculus on Iran

*Which Path to Persia? Options for a New American Strategy toward Iran*, authored by Kenneth M. Pollack, Daniel L. Byman, Martin S. Indyk, Suzanne Maloney, Michael E. O'Hanlon, and Bruce Riedel (Brookings Institution, 2009, 170 pp.), stands as the definitive policy roadmap governing Washington's geopolitical confrontation with the Islamic Republic of Iran.

### Systematic Evaluation of Six Strategic Tracks

The Brookings task force methodically assesses the feasibility, costs, and regional blowback of six mutually exclusive policy paths:
1. **The Diplomatic Track:** Comprehensive engagement and grand bargaining to exchange economic integration and security guarantees for nuclear dismantling.
2. **Coercive Containment:** Unilateral and multilateral economic sanctions, oil embargoes, and financial blacklisting to strain the Iranian economy.
3. **Internal Regime Subversion:** Covert action, funding dissident media, and intelligence operations to foster military coups or internal unrest.
4. **Fostering Popular Revolution:** Supporting grassroots democratic civil society to repeat the 1979 revolution in reverse (The Velvet Revolution option).
5. **Targeted Military Strikes:** Surgical airstrikes against Natanz, Fordow, and IRGC command nodes (analyzing the risk of retaliatory asymmetric mine warfare in the Strait of Hormuz).
6. **Full-Scale Military Invasion:** Outright regime decapitation, modeled on Operation Iraqi Freedom, analyzing the staggering troop requirements and protracted counterinsurgency costs.

### Enduring Influence

The report served as the core analytical framework for the Obama administration's twin-track strategy of crippling sanctions combined with P5+1 nuclear negotiations (the JCPOA).
