## Executive Summary

This monumental 48-page target review article in the Review of General Psychology (2001) by Roy F. Baumeister and colleagues establishes the universal psychological principle that 'Bad Is Stronger Than Good'. Surveying evidence across every major subfield of psychology—learning theory, social relationships, emotion, memory, trauma, cognitive processing, and feedback reception—the authors prove that negative events, negative emotions, negative information, and negative relationships exert a far more powerful, enduring, and pervasive influence on human thought and behavior than positive ones of equal magnitude. The paper provides an evolutionary adaptive rationale: an organism that overlooks an opportunity for pleasure loses a meal, but an organism that overlooks a mortal threat loses its life.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Universality Across All Psychological Domains**: Documents the dominance of bad over good across learning (avoidance conditioning), memory (negative events remembered with greater sensory detail), and relationships (one negative interaction requires 5 positive interactions to neutralize).
- **Evolutionary Adaptive Asymmetry**: Explains the asymmetric survival payoffs: survival requires urgent, priority processing of lethal dangers, predators, and social ostracism, whereas positive opportunities can be pursued leisurely.
- **Relationship Dissolution Dynamics**: Proves that the presence of negative behaviors (contempt, criticism, defensiveness) predicts marital dissolution with over 90% accuracy, whereas the presence of positive behaviors has far less predictive power.
- **Loss Aversion & Financial Cognition**: Connects the negativity bias to Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory, where the pain of losing $100 is psychophysiologically twice as intense as the joy of gaining $100.
- **Depression, Rumination, and Counteracting the Bias**: Details why humans naturally ruminate over criticisms rather than compliments, emphasizing the need for intentional, structured institutional praise and psychological reframing.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations

- "The psychological superiority of bad over good is one of the most robust and pervasive principles in human nature. Bad emotions, bad parents, and bad feedback have more impact than good ones, and bad information is processed more thoroughly than good." (source: Baumeister et al., 2001, Abstract, p. 323)
- "Survival comes first. An organism that fails to respond to a desirable opportunity may miss dinner, but an organism that fails to respond to a predator will become dinner. Evolution selected brains that treat bad as stronger than good." (source: Baumeister et al., 2001, p. 325)
