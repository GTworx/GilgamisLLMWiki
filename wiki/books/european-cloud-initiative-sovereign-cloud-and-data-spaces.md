## Executive Summary

Published by the European Cloud Initiative (ECI), this 32-page policy and systems architecture monograph addresses the geopolitical, legal, and technical dimensions of European cloud sovereignty and common data spaces (Gaia-X, European Data Act). Confronting Europe's overwhelming dependence on American hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud), the paper outlines architectural standards for trusted, federated, and interoperable cloud infrastructures. The authors evaluate confidential computing, hardware-enforced cryptographic boundaries, data localization mandates, and open-source distributed cloud middleware designed to protect European sovereign data from extraterritorial surveillance (e.g., US CLOUD Act) while preserving edge-to-cloud scalability.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Geopolitical Chokepoints in Cloud Infrastructure**: Documents that non-European hyperscalers control over 75% of European enterprise cloud workloads, creating profound strategic vulnerabilities in data sovereignty and regulatory compliance.
- **Federated Common Data Spaces**: Formulates the architectural standards for European data spaces (health, manufacturing, energy) that enable multi-party data sharing without centralizing data into proprietary silos.
- **Confidential Computing & Hardware Enclaves**: Analyzes secure enclaves (AMD SEV, Intel SGX) and cryptographic zero-trust architectures that guarantee data remains encrypted in transit, at rest, and in use during computation.
- **Regulatory Compliance Alignment**: Maps compliance strategies across the EU Data Act, General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and European Cybersecurity Certification Scheme for Cloud Services (EUCS).
- **Interoperability and Cloud Switching**: Recommends mandatory open API standards and portable container runtimes to eliminate vendor lock-in and enable dynamic multi-cloud workload migration.
