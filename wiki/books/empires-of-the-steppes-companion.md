## Executive Summary

*(Bibliographic Verification Note: This 143-page Hanover Square Press companion PDF [ISBN 978-1-335-42927-8, Copyright ©2023, pp. 1–2] contains the complete scholarly apparatus—**Glossary** [pp. 3–18], **Select Bibliography** [pp. 19–45], and **Annotated Chapter Endnotes** [pp. 46–143]—for Tulane University historian **Kenneth W. Harl**'s 2023 hardcover synthesis *Empires of the Steppes: A History of the Nomadic Tribes Who Shaped Civilization*.)*

Functioning as a dense, standalone reference compendium on Eurasian nomadic history from the Afanasievo culture (3300–2500 BCE) through the Abbasids, Xiongnu, Huns, Göktürks, Seljuks, Golden Horde, Timurids, and Ottomans (pp. 3, 60, 100, 142–143), this scholarly apparatus maps primary sources (*Hanshu*, *Shiji*, Byzantine and Islamic chronicles) and leading secondary literature across four millennia of steppe–sedentary warfare, statecraft, and trade.

---

## Core Reference Value

1. **Comprehensive Steppe Glossary (pp. 3–18)**: Concisely defines archaeological horizons, nomadic confederations, imperial titles, and dynastic chronologies spanning the Pontic steppes to Mongolia.
2. **Cross-Regional Bibliographic Map (pp. 19–45)**: Integrates Cambridge Histories of Iran, Inner Asia, China, and Turkey with specialist monographs on Xiongnu, Ghaznavids, Seljuks, Mongols, and Crusaders.
3. **Annotated Endnote Synthesis (pp. 46–143)**: Provides compact historical commentary on key hinge episodes—from Han–Xiongnu wars and the Anarchy of Samarra (861–870 CE) to Tokhtamysh, Temür Qutlugh, and late medieval Anatolian art and warfare.
