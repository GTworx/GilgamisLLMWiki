## Executive Summary

Translated and edited with an interpretive introduction by Turkish researcher **Kaya Ataberk** (T&K Yayınları, 1st ed. Nov. 2015 / 3rd ed. April 2016, pp. 3–7; 128 pp.), *Ermenilerin Kaleminden Kürtleşen Ermeniler* brings into Turkish two rare wartime Armenian eyewitness memoirs originally published in Cairo/London immediately after World War I: **Thomas K. Mugerditchian**'s *The Diyarbekir Massacres and Kurdish Atrocities* (*Diyarbakır Katliamları ve Kürt Zulümleri*, Book I, p. 33)—written by the former Armenian dragoman and acting vice-consul at the British Consulate in Diyarbakır up to 1914 (p. 7)—and **Esther Mugerditchian**'s companion family escape chronicle (*From Turkish Toils*).

Focusing on the complex local social ecology of Ottoman Diyarbakır, Harput, and Dersim during 1915–1918, the memoirs document both violent predation by armed tribal chieftains and irregulars and survival pathways involving forced or protective conversion, adoption into Kurdish households, and identity assimilation—framed in Ataberk's editorial introduction within contemporary Turkish debates over Eastern Anatolian identity and historical responsibility.

---

## Core Primary-Source Dimensions

1. **Local Micro-History of 1915 Diyarbakır & Eastern Anatolia**: Complements macro-diplomatic studies by preserving ground-level testimony from a bilingual consular insider familiar with Ottoman officials, British diplomats, urban Armenians, and rural Kurdish tribes.
2. **Assimilation, Conversion & Survival ("Kürtleşen Ermeniler")**: Illuminates a sensitive demographic phenomenon—crypto-Armenians and Islamized/Kurdified Armenian survivors in Eastern Anatolia—that entered broader Turkish public discourse in the 2000s–2010s.
3. **Editorial Framing Awareness**: Readers should distinguish the 1918–1919 primary eyewitness narratives of Thomas and Esther Mugerditchian from Kaya Ataberk's modern nationalist-polemical introductory commentary (pp. 7–32).
