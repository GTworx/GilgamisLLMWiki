## Executive Summary

Harvard Business School professor **Tom Eisenmann**—who has led HBS's required MBA course *The Entrepreneurial Manager* for over two decades and advised thousands of founders—wrote *Why Startups Fail* (Currency, 2021, copyright p. 3; Introduction pp. 6–7) after watching two promising student ventures (*Triangulate* and *Quincy*) collapse despite diligent execution of the Lean Startup playbook. Combining deep multi-year case forensics (including Fab.com, Quincy Apparel, Triangulate, and high-profile VC blowups) with an empirical survey of 470+ startups (Appendix, pp. 314–334), Eisenmann overturns the simplistic myth that startups fail merely because founders lack grit or "didn't validate."

Instead, Eisenmann demonstrates that startup failure follows **six distinct, recurring structural patterns**—three during the **Early Launch** phase (Part I, pp. 40–144) and three during the **Late Scaling** phase (Part II, pp. 145–262)—concluding with a compassionate operational guide to shutting down responsibly and bouncing back as a wiser founder (Part III, pp. 263–308).

## Six Recurring Startup Failure Archetypes

1. **Good Idea, Bad Bedfellows (Ch. 3, pp. 72–94)**: Ventures undermined by misaligned co-founders, toxic early hires, mismatched investors, or unreliable supply-chain/strategic partners.
2. **False Starts (Ch. 4, pp. 95–125)**: Impatient "ready-fire-aim" founders who skip upfront customer research and engineer products before validating core problem-solution hypotheses.
3. **False Positives (Ch. 5, pp. 126–144)**: A subtle trap of Lean validation—mistaking enthusiastic feedback from atypical early adopters for mainstream demand, causing ventures to stall right at the chasm (Ch. 2–5).
4. **Speed Trap (Ch. 7, pp. 179–215)**: Aggressive blitzscaling fueled by cheap VC capital when unit economics are upside-down (CAC > LTV), exemplified by Fab.com's catastrophic $330M burn.
5. **Help Wanted (Ch. 8, pp. 216–231)**: Growth-stage leadership gaps when first-time founders fail to recruit seasoned functional executives—or hire big-company executives who clash with startup DNA.
6. **Cascading Miracles (Ch. 9, pp. 232–262)**: Moonshot ventures requiring multiple independent technical, regulatory, and market breakthroughs to succeed simultaneously, where a single broken link topples the entire enterprise.
