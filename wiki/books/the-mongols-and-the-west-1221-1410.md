## Executive Summary

Authored by renowned medievalist **Peter Jackson** (Emeritus Professor of Medieval History at Keele University), *The Mongols and the West: 1221–1410* is the definitive academic monograph examining the two-century encounter between Western Latin Christendom and the **Mongol World Empire**. Analyzing papal diplomatic embassies (John of Plano Carpini, William of Rubruck), missionary friars, commercial treaties with Genoa and Venice, and proposed Franco-Mongol military alliances against the Mamluk Sultanate of Egypt, Jackson reconstructs how medieval Europe's apocalyptic terror of 'Gog and Magog' transformed into pragmatic diplomatic collaboration and global trade.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Inevitable Clash of Universalisms**: Shows that early diplomacy stalled because both the Roman Papacy (claiming universal vicariate of Christ) and the Chinggisid Great Khans (claiming a Tengrist mandate to rule all nations under heaven) claimed absolute, non-negotiable global sovereignty.
2. **The Ilkhanid-Western Flirtation**: Demonstrates that between 1260 and 1305, the Buddhist/Christian Ilkhanid rulers of Iran sent over sixteen diplomatic embassies to the Kings of England, France, and the Popes seeking a joint amphibious invasion to crush Mamluk Egypt and return Jerusalem to Christian custody.
3. **Trade and Biological Interconnection**: Details how the Pax Mongolica created the commercial highway that enabled Italian merchants (Polo family) to reach Beijing, but tragically also served as the vector transmitting the *Yersinia pestis* Black Death from Central Asia into the Mediterranean and Europe in 1347.
