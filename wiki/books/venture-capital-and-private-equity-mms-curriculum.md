## Executive Summary

*Venture Capital and Private Equity (Master in Management Studies, Semester IV CBCS Curriculum, Subject Code `00000NMMSS4S2`)* (published by the Institute of Distance and Open Learning [IDOL], University of Mumbai Press, September 2023, 131 pp., coordinated by Nilesh Nandkumar Manore and Dr. Rajashree Pandit) written by Assistant Professor [[tushar-balkrishna-raut]] (CMA, PhD) is a structured eleven-unit graduate finance textbook integrating global venture capital and private equity mechanics with **Indian regulatory, taxation, and developing-market governance frameworks** (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, pp. 1–6).

Progressing systematically across the full fund and deal lifecycle, Raut covers the historical evolution of VC/PE from 1940s ARDC and 1970s Silicon Valley to modern global unicorns and impact investing (Unit 1), the GP–LP limited-partnership funding cycle (Unit 2), capital-raising challenges and fallacies (Unit 3), deal valuation and dilution modeling (Unit 4), **term-sheet structuring** (Unit 5), legal/financial due diligence documentation (Unit 6), and multi-stakeholder liquidity **exit strategies** spanning IPOs, M&A, mezzanine financing, and secondary buyouts (Unit 7). Uniquely, Units 8 through 11 ground global PE theory in **SEBI Alternative Investment Funds (AIF) Regulations**, **Indian Income Tax Act provisions** (Sections `10(23FB)` and `10(47)`, STT, STCG/LTCG, non-resident taxation), **developing-market investment dynamics**, and **board fiduciary ethics and community engagement** (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Contents & Syllabus, pp. 3–6, 85–125).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Units 1–3: Global Evolution, The VC/PE Cycle & Fundraising Challenges (pp. 1–33 / PDF pp. 7–39)**:
  * *Capital Formation & GP–LP Architecture*: Traces VC from ARDC and J.H. Whitney (1940s–1950s) through NVCA, Silicon Valley's Kleiner Perkins/Sequoia era, the dot-com cycle, global expansion into Asia/India/Israel, and the rise of unicorns and impact investing; breaks down 10-year LP/GP fund structures, sector specialization, and first-time fund credibility barriers (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Units 1–3, PDF pp. 7–34).

* **Units 4–7: Valuation, Term Sheets, Due Diligence & Exit Strategies (pp. 34–84 / PDF pp. 40–90)**:
  * *Deal Engineering & Liquidity*: Teaches quantitative valuation and equity-dilution math (Unit 4), critical economic and control clauses in venture **term sheets** (Unit 5), comprehensive legal/IP/compliance **due diligence** checklists (Unit 6), and structuring **exit pathways**—IPOs, strategic acquisitions, later-stage mezzanine rounds, and buyouts—across competing founder and investor incentives (Unit 7) (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Units 4–7, PDF pp. 5, 73, 85).

* **Units 8–9: Regulatory Governance (SEBI AIF) & Tax Architecture (pp. 85–104 / PDF pp. 91–110)**:
  * *Emerging-Market Legal & Fiscal Design*: Analyzes India's **SEBI Alternative Investment Funds (AIF) Regulations** governing Category I, II, and III funds alongside fiscal pass-through and capital-gains regimes under Sections `10(23FB)` and `10(47)` of the Income Tax Act, 1961, Securities Transaction Tax (STT), STCG/LTCG, and cross-border non-resident taxation (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Units 8–9, PDF p. 6).

* **Units 10–11: Developing Markets, Corporate Governance & Ethics (pp. 105–125 / PDF pp. 111–131)**:
  * *Fiduciary Duty & Community Impact*: Explores macroeconomic and institutional realities of PE investing in developing economies, emphasizing local supply-chain development, community engagement, consumer protection, board fiduciary duties to shareholders, and ethical governance frameworks (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Units 10–11, PDF pp. 6, 118).

## Key Quotes & Citations

* "Venture capitalists are the financiers who assume the disproportionate financial risk and aid entrepreneurs in achieving their goals. In return, the investors receive a stake in the company as well as various returns if and when the business succeeds." (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Unit 1, p. 1 / PDF p. 7)
* "Private equity and venture capital firms provide a vital source of capital for startups, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and companies in need of expansion or restructuring. They bridge the funding gap, allowing these companies to grow, create jobs, and drive innovation." (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Unit 1, pp. 4–5 / PDF pp. 10–11)
* "Developing markets often have close-knit communities that can be directly affected by investment activities. Private equity investors should engage with local communities, respect their rights, and contribute to local development initiatives." (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/Private Equity & Venture Capital MS Thesis.pdf, Unit 10, p. 112 / PDF p. 118)
