## Overview
Authored by historical linguist Mustafa Koç and literary pedagogy scholars, this two-part linguistic monograph explores the morphosyntax of Old Anatolian Turkish (13th–15th centuries) alongside structural syntactic coherence in Modern Turkish. Koç analyzes the archaic participle suffix /-dUk/ functioning as a finite past-tense marker in early Anatolian manuscripts, illuminating syntactic transitions between Seljuk and classical Ottoman prose.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Examines the functional shift of the participial /-dUk/ suffix into an indicative past-tense finite predicate.
- **Key Finding**: Cites early manuscript evidence from Yunus Emre, Aşık Paşa, and early Ottoman hagiographies.
- **Key Finding**: Provides a comprehensive normative taxonomy of sentence-level syntactic and semantic incoherence (anlatım bozuklukları).

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Eski Anadolu Türkçesi metinlerinde /-dUk/ ekinin şahıs ekleriyle birleşerek asli bir geçmiş zaman kipi olarak kullanılması, dilin morfolojik esnekliğinin ve tarihî dönüşümünün en canlı şahididir."
