## Executive Summary

Written by physician, political theorist, and one of twentieth-century Turkey's most original Marxist thinkers **Dr. Hikmet Kıvılcımlı** (1902–1971; orig. 1965, Title Page p. 1, Contents pp. 3–7; 296 pp.), *Tarih, Devrim, Sosyalizm* ("History, Revolution, Socialism") presents Kıvılcımlı's signature **Tarih Tezi** ("History Thesis")—a bold synthesis bridging **Lewis Henry Morgan**, **Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels**, and fourteenth-century North African sociologist **Ibn Khaldun** (*İbni Haldun'un Gerçek Diyalektiği*, p. 6 [185]).

Rather than applying Eurocentric linear class-struggle schemas mechanically to Anatolia and the Middle East, Kıvılcımlı distinguishes **Social Revolutions** (internal class overthrows within modern capitalism) from **Historical Revolutions** (*Tarihsel Devrim*, p. 3 [19])—arguing that throughout ancient and medieval history (from Sumerian Ubaid/Uruk to Rome, early Islam, and the Ottoman state), dynamic, egalitarian **"barbarian" communal-tribal societies** (*barbarlık* possessing vigorous collective cohesion/*asabiyya*) periodically overthrew decadent, class-stratified "civilizations" and injected fresh productive vitality into history (Ch. X: *Tarih ve Barbarlık*, p. 7 [255]).

---

## Core Theoretical Contributions

1. **Synthesizing Ibn Khaldun with Historical Materialism (pp. 185–210)**: Elevates Ibn Khaldun's dialectic of nomadic *umran/asabiyya* vs. sedentary urban decay into a materialist engine explaining ancient Near Eastern, Islamic, and Ottoman state formation.
2. **Revaluing Pre-State Communal Dynamism (*Barbar Neden Önemsenmez*, p. 4 [66])**: Critiques orthodox historiography's disdain for "barbarians," modeling collective solidarity (*kolektif aksiyon*) as a major historical productive force alongside tools and geography.
3. **Distinctively Turkish Left Historiography**: Laid the intellectual groundwork for decades of Turkish debates on the Asiatic Mode of Production, Ottoman land tenure (*dirlik/tımar*), and non-Western revolutionary trajectories.
