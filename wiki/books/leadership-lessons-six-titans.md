## Executive Summary

Published by New Word City (Copyright ©2017, p. 198; 198 pp.), **Will Peters**'s *Leadership Lessons* opens by confronting leadership's central paradox: effective leaders vary so radically—from General Patton's blunt command to Lao-tse's invisible facilitation—that their playbooks are often mutually contradictory (Introduction, pp. 5–6). Rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all template, Peters profiles **six iconic American builders** in depth: **Warren Buffett** (p. 7), **Walt Disney** (p. 48), **Thomas Edison** (p. 78), **Katharine Graham** (p. 102), **Steve Jobs** (p. 127), and **Ray Kroc** (p. 153).

Contrasting Buffett's disciplined "margin of safety" against Disney's compulsive "bet-the-company" gambles, and Katharine Graham's reluctant rise into fearless journalistic courage during the Pentagon Papers and Watergate against Ray Kroc's late-blooming franchising empire at age fifty-two, Peters urges leaders to audit their own temperament honestly while adopting proven crisis tactics.

---

## Core Comparative Leadership Lessons

1. **Temperament-Matched Risk & Capital Stewardship**: Contrasts Buffett's rational patience and decentralized trust (Salomon Brothers crisis rescue) with Disney's relentless creative perfectionism (*Snow White*, Disneyland) and Jobs's uncompromising product aesthetic.
2. **Moral Courage & Late-Stage Persistence**: Demonstrates through Katharine Graham (defying White House threats to publish Watergate reporting) and Ray Kroc (turning a single San Bernardino hamburger stand into McDonald's after 52) that transformative leadership often emerges unexpectedly under crucible pressure.
3. **Industrializing Invention**: Profiles Edison's transformation of solitary tinkering into the systematic Menlo Park R&D laboratory—turning failure and deafness into disciplined experimental iteration.
