## Summary
John Keane's masterwork on the history, transformation, and structural fragility of democratic institutions, introducing the foundational concept of 'Monitory Democracy'. Keane demonstrates that modern democracy has evolved beyond classical assembly democracy (ancient world) and party-centered representative democracy (18th–20th centuries) into an expansive web of extra-parliamentary public watchdogs, citizen juries, whistleblower networks, and digital transparency watchtowers.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
Published following a decade of democratic triumphalism after the Cold War, Keane's 950-page historical treatise appeared as democratic stagnation, voter apathy, and algorithmic polarization began to erode Western parliaments. Keane sought to rescue democratic theory from constitutional complacency by diagnosing the novel mechanisms through which citizens hold power accountable outside traditional periodic elections.

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
Keane outlines a three-phase historical evolution: 1) Assembly Democracy (Athens, Mesopotamia, early Levant); 2) Representative Democracy (electoral parliaments, mass parties, geographical suffrage); and 3) Monitory Democracy (post-1945 emergence of non-electoral scrutiny bodies). In monitory democracy, power is subjected to continuous, permanent public audit via regulatory ombudsmen, international human rights NGOs, consumer advocacy groups, algorithmic watchdogs, and investigative media.

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
Keane documents hundreds of institutional innovations that constrain executive hubris: truth and reconciliation commissions, freedom-of-information statutory disclosures, global climate audits, and decentralized watchdog platforms. However, he warns that monitory democracy faces acute vulnerabilities: 'democide'—the slow, internal decay of democratic norms through demagoguery, executive capture, wealth concentration, and citizen cynicism.

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
Keane provides one of the most innovative and comprehensive re-theorizations of democracy in modern political philosophy. By decoupling democratic accountability from the narrow ritual of periodic voting, his model of monitory scrutiny accurately anticipates modern digital civic activism and independent regulatory oversight.
