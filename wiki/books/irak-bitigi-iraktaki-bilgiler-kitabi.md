## Executive Summary

Prepared and annotated by **Mustafa Turgay Tüfekçioğlu** and **Özden İlteriş Bulca** from the posthumous papers and research manuscripts of **Kâzım Mirşan**, *Irak Bitiği: Irak'taki Bilgiler Kitabı* (*The Iraq Inscription: The Book of Knowledge in Iraq*) examines archaic runic and tamga-like inscriptions discovered in Northern Mesopotamia and Iraq (including Kirkuk, Mosul, and Kurdistan regions), proposing that they represent an early pre-cuneiform Proto-Turkic epigraphic layer linked to Subarian, Gutian, and early Mesopotamian civilizations.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Pre-Cuneiform Steppe Writing**: Claims that writing did not originate solely from clay token accounting in Uruk, but was influenced by migratory runic symbol systems from the northern highlands.
2. **The Gutian and Subarian Connection**: Argues that the Gutians (who conquered Akkad in the 22nd c. BCE) and the Subarians spoke an agglutinative Proto-Turkic tongue.
3. **Tamga Continuity in Northern Iraq**: Highlights the survival of ancient Central Asian tamgas on tribal carpets, gravestones, and fortress walls among Turkmen and regional populations in contemporary Iraq.
