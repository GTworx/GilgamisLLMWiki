## Executive Summary

*(Bibliographic Verification Note: This 7-page scanned PDF preserves the cover, copyright page, complete Table of Contents [pp. v–vi], and complete List of Figures [pp. vii–ix] of **David Christian**'s 2018 Wiley Blackwell monograph *A History of Russia, Central Asia and Mongolia, Volume II: Inner Eurasia from the Mongol Empire to Today, 1260–2000*.)*

Big History pioneer **David Christian** (Macquarie University) frames 740 years of Russian, Central Asian, Xinjiang, and Mongolian history as a single ecological-geopolitical unit—**Inner Eurasia**—divided into two grand energy epochs (Contents, pp. 3–4): **Part I: Inner Eurasia in the Agrarian Era, 1260–1850** (Ch. 1–9, tracing the Mongol zenith, Golden Horde/Timurid unraveling, Muscovy's agrarian *smychka*, and the 1600–1750 Russo-Qing tipping point that subordinated steppe pastoralists) and **Part II: Inner Eurasia in the Era of Fossil Fuels, 1850–2000** (Ch. 10–19, tracking railway industrialization, 1914–1921 collapse, Stalinist modernization, Soviet plateau and 1991 dissolution, and post-Soviet transition across Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Central Asia, and Mongolia).

## Core Structural Architecture (Verified TOC & Figures)

1. **The Agrarian Tipping Point (1600–1750, Ch. 7–9)**: Maps how gunpowder empires (Muscovy/Russia and Qing China) reversed millennia of nomadic military superiority across the Inner Eurasian heartlands.
2. **Energy Regimes & Fossil-Fuel Modernization (Part II, Ch. 10–17)**: Uses quantitative demographic, energy, and GDP curves (List of Figures, pp. 5–7) to track Russia's and the USSR's coal/oil/electricity leap, World War II resilience, and structural stagnation (1950–1991).
3. **Post-Soviet Divergence (Ch. 18–19 & Epilogue)**: Compares post-1989 GNI trajectories, Gini inequality, and state-building across the Russian Federation, Western post-Soviet republics, Central Asia, and Mongolia—asking *"After 2000: The End of Inner Eurasia?"*
