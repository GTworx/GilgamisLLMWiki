## Executive Summary

Prepared by literary scholar and poet **Doç. Dr. Dilâver Cebeci**, *Devrannâme: Seyyah-ı Fakir Evliyâ Çelebi* is an evocative analytical and literary study of the worldview, psychological journey, and cultural universe of seventeenth-century Ottoman traveler **Evliyâ Çelebi** (1611–1682). Cebeci reconstructs Evliyâ's famous spiritual calling (the dream of Ahi Çelebi Mosque where he stammered *"Seyâhat yâ Resûlallah"* instead of *"Şefâat"*), contextualizing his fifty years of restless wanderings across three continents within the broader cosmological and mystic framework (*devrân*) of classical Ottoman civilization.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Travel as Spiritual Pilgrimage (*Devrân*)**: Evliyâ's journey was not mere geographic curiosity but an existential enactment of Ottoman cosmic consciousness.
2. **Humor and Baroque Realism**: Vindicates Evliyâ against naive modern accusations of 'lying', showing how his hyperbolic descriptions represent a deliberate Ottoman rhetorical art form (*mübalağa-i makbul*).
3. **The Mirror of 17th-Century Ottoman Pluralism**: Illustrates how the *Seyahatnâme* captures the multi-ethnic, multilingual vitality of the Ottoman world at its territorial zenith.
