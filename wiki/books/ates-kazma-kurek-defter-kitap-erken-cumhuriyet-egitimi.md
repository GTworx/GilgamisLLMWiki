## Summary
Explores the symbolic and material reality of the Early Republican educational mobilization, where digging shovels and schoolbooks ('kazma-kürek, defter-kitap') jointly forged modern Turkish civic identity.

## Analytical Overview
### The Dual Instruments of Republican Nation-Building

In *Kazma-Kürek, Defter-Kitap: Erken Cumhuriyet Döneminde Halk Eğitimi ve Maarif Seferberliği*, historian Ahmet Emre Ateş examines the physical and symbolic synthesis of physical labor and intellectual literacy during the early decades of the Turkish Republic. The title—'Pickaxe-Shovel, Notebook-Book'—captures the revolutionary ethos of an impoverished post-war nation striving to rebuild its physical infrastructure and intellectual consciousness simultaneously.

### The Nation Schools and People's Houses

Ateş documents the sweeping civic literacy campaigns that followed the 1928 Alphabet Reform:
- The **Millet Mektepleri (Nation Schools)** mobilized adults aged 16 to 45 across every province, teaching millions of citizens to read and write the new Latin-based Turkish script.
- The **Halkevleri (People's Houses)** served as vibrant regional community hubs for theatrical arts, health hygiene, civic debates, and vocational education.

### Education as Total Social Transformation

Ateş shows that early Republican pedagogy viewed education not as a passive consumer service, but as an existential civic mobilization—the construction of roads, bridges, and schools was inseparable from the construction of enlightened, self-governing republican citizens.
