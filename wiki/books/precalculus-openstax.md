## Executive Summary

*Precalculus* (published by OpenStax / Rice University, 2014 / 2017 edition, 1,156 pp.) led by Senior Contributing Author [[jay-abramson]] (Principal Lecturer in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at Arizona State University, with 33 years of precalculus pedagogy and a nationwide peer-review panel) is a flagship open-educational-resource (OER) textbook designed to democratize college-level STEM preparation at zero cost without compromising academic rigor (source: BOOKS/Math/Precalculus-OP_9wwF7YT.pdf, pp. 3–5, 13–15).

Structured into **twelve modular chapters** aligned to explicit learning objectives, worked examples, real-world data-fitting applications, and chapter glossaries, *Precalculus* spans three cohesive blocks: **Algebraic & Transcendental Functions (Ch. 1–4)**—general functions, composition/inverses, linear modeling, complex numbers, polynomials, rational/radical functions, and exponential/logarithmic models; **Trigonometry & Polar/Vector Geometry (Ch. 5–8)**—unit-circle trigonometry, periodic graphs, trigonometric identities, Laws of Sines/Cosines, polar coordinates, parametric curves, and vectors; and **Advanced Algebraic Foundations for Calculus (Ch. 9–12)**—systems of equations and matrices, conic sections, sequences, probability, and an explicit **Introduction to Limits and Derivatives** bridging seamlessly into university calculus (source: BOOKS/Math/Precalculus-OP_9wwF7YT.pdf, Outline & Preface, pp. 13–15).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Chapters 1–4: Functions, Linear Models, Polynomials & Exponential/Logarithmic Growth (pp. 17–454)**:
  * *Functional Thinking & Data Fitting*: Introduces domain/range, rates of change, transformations, and inverse functions (Ch. 1); connects linear equations directly to empirical linear regression (Ch. 2); develops complex numbers, polynomial zeros, and rational asymptotes (Ch. 3); and models compounding growth and decay via exponential and logarithmic functions (Ch. 4) (source: BOOKS/Math/Precalculus-OP_9wwF7YT.pdf, Outline, pp. 17–454).

* **Chapters 5–8: Trigonometry, Periodic Models, Polar Coordinates & Vectors (pp. 455–770)**:
  * *Oscillations & Spatial Geometry*: Builds right-triangle and unit-circle trigonometry (Ch. 5), sinusoidal periodic graphs and inverse trig functions (Ch. 6), sum/difference/double-angle identities (Ch. 7), and applied geometry through non-right triangles, polar complex numbers, parametric equations, and 2D vectors (Ch. 8) (source: BOOKS/Math/Precalculus-OP_9wwF7YT.pdf, Outline, pp. 455–763).

* **Chapters 9–12: Systems, Matrices, Conics, Probability & Calculus Prelude (pp. 771–1156)**:
  * *Launching into Calculus & Linear Algebra*: Equips students with matrix algebra, determinants, conic sections, arithmetic/geometric sequences, combinatorics/probability, and preliminary limits to ensure an effortless transition into differential calculus (source: BOOKS/Math/Precalculus-OP_9wwF7YT.pdf, Preface, p. 14).
