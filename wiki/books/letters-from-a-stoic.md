## Executive Summary

Selected, translated, and introduced by **Robin Campbell** for Penguin Classics (pp. 2–5), *Letters from a Stoic* presents a curated English edition of **Lucius Annaeus Seneca's** *Epistulae Morales ad Lucilium*—124 intimate philosophical letters written during Seneca's tense retirement from Emperor Nero's court between 63 and 65 CE. Rather than delivering dry academic lectures, Seneca writes as a warm, witty, self-aware older friend guiding Lucilius in the everyday art of living well, mastering anxiety, valuing time, treating enslaved people with shared human dignity (Letter XLVII), and confronting mortality without terror.

---

## Enduring Stoic Themes

1. **Mastery of Time & Present Attention**: Warns that we suffer far more in imagination than in reality, and squander life postponing wisdom until tomorrow.
2. **Voluntary Discomfort & Inner Freedom**: Recommends periodically practicing plain food and coarse clothing so poverty loses its psychological terror—building inner independence from fortune's swings.
3. **Human Brotherhood & Philosophical Therapy**: Treats philosophy not as verbal showmanship, but as medicine for the soul aimed at tranquility (*ataraxia*) and virtue (*virtus*).
