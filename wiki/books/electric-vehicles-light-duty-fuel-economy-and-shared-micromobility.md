## Executive Summary

This four-volume synthesis encompasses the complete `BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/` sub-library (totaling 749 pages across four landmark engineering and National Academies policy studies published between 2013 and 2022): (1) *Electric Vehicle Battery Technologies* (2013, 44 pp., Springer, `DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4614-0134-6_2`) by [[kwo-young]], Caisheng Wang, Le Yi Wang, and Kai Strunz; (2) *Assessment of Technologies for Improving Light-Duty Vehicle Fuel Economy—2025–2035* (2021, 457 pp., National Academies Press) chaired by [[gary-marchant]]; (3) *TCRP Research Report 230: Transit and Micromobility* (2021, 111 pp., Transportation Research Board / NASEM) by [[colin-murphy]] et al.; and (4) *NCHRP Synthesis 597: Micromobility Policies, Permits, and Practices* (2022, 137 pp., TRB / NASEM) by [[alexander-kolpakov]], Austin Marie Sipiora, and Jana E. Huss (source: BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/2013 EV-book.pdf, p. 2; 2021 NASEM Fuel Economy, p. 6; 2021 TCRP-230, p. 2; 2022 NCHRP-597, p. 2).

Together, these four volumes trace the full technical and regulatory arc of electrified transportation across scales: from the fundamental electrochemistry and vehicle dynamics of EV battery packs (NiMH vs. Li-ion cathode/anode potentials, State of Charge $SOC$, Depth of Discharge $DOD$, and Level I–III charging limits), to the 2025–2035 macroeconomic transition of light-duty passenger vehicles from downsized/hybridized internal combustion engines (ICE) to zero-emission Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) and Fuel Cell Electric Vehicles (FCEVs), down to urban light-battery **shared micromobility** (docked/dockless e-bikes and e-scooters) solving the public transit 'first-and-last-mile' problem via the open-source **Mobility Data Specification (MDS)** and municipal/state DOT permitting (source: BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/2013 EV-book.pdf, pp. 5–21; 2021 TCRP-230, pp. 11–51; 2022 NCHRP-597, pp. 11–51).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Volume 1: Electric Vehicle Battery Technologies (Kwo Young et al., 2013, 44 pp.)**:
  * *Propulsion Physics, Battery Metrics & Li-ion Trade-offs*: Formulates total vehicle propulsion and regenerative braking power ($186\text{ kW}$ peak braking from $96.6\text{ km/h}$ in 5s, pp. 5–6), defines USABC peak power $P = \frac{2V_{oc}^2}{9R}$, $SOC$, $DOD$, and Level I/II vs. Level III ($>100\text{ kW}$) fast charging (pp. 8–13), and proves the fundamental voltage/safety trade-off between high-abuse-tolerance $\text{LiFePO}_4 + \text{LTO}$ ($1.9\text{ V}$) and high-energy $4.0\text{ V}$ Li-ion cells (source: BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/2013 EV-book.pdf, pp. 5–21).

* **Volume 2: NASEM Assessment of Technologies for Improving Light-Duty Vehicle Fuel Economy 2025–2035 (Gary Marchant et al., 2021, 457 pp.)**:
  * *ICE Efficiency Pathways vs. BEV/FCEV Zero-Emission Transition*: Evaluates 2025–2035 powertrains across downsized/boosted ICE, naturally aspirated/Atkinson-cycle hybrids, mass reduction (10–20% lightweighting), battery pack cost decline ($\text{$/kWh}$), hydrogen fuel cells, connected/automated vehicle eco-driving, and NHTSA/EPA Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) and GHG regulatory stringency (source: BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/2021 National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. Assessment of Technologies for Improving Light-Duty Vehicle Fuel Economy—2025–2035_.pdf, pp. 6–51, 442).

* **Volume 3: TCRP Report 230 — Transit and Micromobility (Colin Murphy et al., 2021, 111 pp.)**:
  * *First/Last-Mile Transit Integration & Mobility Data Specification (MDS)*: Analyzes how docked bikeshare, dockless e-bikes, and motorized e-scooters interact with public transit across dense, medium, and small urban markets. Details integrated fare subsidies, curb-space geofencing, equity access requirements, and the Los Angeles DOT / Open Mobility Foundation **MDS** API standard (source: BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/2021 TCRP-230-Transit-and-Micromobility-Shared-Use-Mobility-Center.pdf, pp. 8–51).

* **Volume 4: NCHRP Synthesis 597 — Micromobility Policies, Permits, and Practices (Alexander Kolpakov et al., 2022, 137 pp.)**:
  * *State DOT Statutory Definitions & Municipal Permitting*: Surveys 39 U.S. State Departments of Transportation and municipal agencies on the 3-tier e-bike classification system, sidewalk vs. bike-lane operating rules, fleet caps, vendor permit fees, helmet/age mandates (23.1% helmet, 28.2% minimum age across surveyed DOTs), and crash data reporting (source: BOOKS/Energy/Light Battery Vehicle including eBike/2022 Micromobility Policies, Permits, Practices_.pdf, pp. 8–51).
