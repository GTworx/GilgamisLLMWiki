## Executive Summary

Curated from the historic 123-year archive of *National Geographic Magazine* (1888–2011) by **Ali Z. Çiftçi**, *Görmediğimiz Türkiye* (*The Turkey We Have Not Seen*) is a stunning photographic and documentary retrospective capturing rare historical images, ethnographic portraits, and editorial dispatches from late Ottoman Constantinople, Anatolian villages, the Battle of Gallipoli, and the dramatic cultural transformation of early Republican Turkey under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk.

---

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Western Eyes on Ottoman-Turkish Transition**: Documents how international photojournalism chronicled the rapid metamorphosis of Turkey from an exoticized oriental empire to a sleek modern secular republic.
2. **Visual Memory of Lost Urban Spaces**: Preserves irreplaceable visual records of wooden Ottoman Istanbul architecture, guild life, and historical landscapes prior to mid-twentieth-century concrete urbanization.
3. **The Dignity of the Anatolian People**: Showcases intimate, unvarnished photographic portraits of ordinary Turkish peasants, students, and soldiers, celebrating their endurance across wars and revolutions.
