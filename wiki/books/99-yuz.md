## Executive Summary

Written by towering Turkish *İkinci Yeni* poet and essayist **Cemal Süreya** (1931–1990) and published here in the canonical Yapı Kredi Yayınları edition (first YKY ed. 2004, 3rd YKY ed. March 2010; orig. Kaynak Yayınları 1991, p. 3; 607 pp.), *99 Yüz: İzdüşümler – Söz Senaryosu* is a dazzling literary gallery of over one hundred incisive, epigrammatic portraits of twentieth-century Turkey's politicians, poets, industrialists, journalists, artists, actors, and cultural icons.

Originally penned for the weekly magazine *2000'e Doğru*, Süreya's sketches dissect figures ranging from **Turgut Özal**, **Süleyman Demirel**, **Deniz Baykal**, **Erdal İnönü**, **Necmettin Erbakan**, and **Sakıp Sabancı** to **Türkan Şoray**, **Zeki Müren**, **Barış Manço**, **Metin Oktay**, **Sezai Karakoç** (p. 391), **Uğur Mumcu** (p. 447), **İlhan Berk**, **Fethi Naci**, **Murat Belge**, and even an ironic self-portrait (*Cemal Süreya*, p. 65; TOC, pp. 6–469).

## Core Literary & Cultural Insights

1. **Poetic Physiognomy & Aphoristic Wit**: Treats the human face, gesture, speech cadence, and biographical contradiction as compressed cultural metaphors—capturing an entire political era in three sparkling pages.
2. **Panorama of Post-1980 Turkey**: Preserves a vivid, fiercely independent social history of the 1980s Turkish transition—from neoliberal transformation (Özal, Banker Kastelli, Sabancı) to literary resistance and popular culture.
3. **Cross-Ideological Literary Empathy**: Even when politically sharp or satirical, Süreya writes with an artist's deep attentiveness to craft and human eccentricity, honoring literary rivals like Sezai Karakoç and investigative journalists like Uğur Mumcu alike.
