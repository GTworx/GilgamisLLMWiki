## Executive Summary

Published by Verso (2014, Copyright p. 4, Contents p. 5; 295 pp.), **Ilan Pappé**'s *The Idea of Israel* shifts from 1948 military archives to a Foucauldian intellectual history of **Zionist, Post-Zionist, and Neo-Zionist knowledge production** inside Israeli universities, cinema, literature, school curricula, and public memory.

In **Part I: The Scholarly and Fictional Idea of Israel** (Ch. 1–3), Pappé examines how early Israeli historians, sociologists, and novelists forged an "objective" foundational myth casting Palestinians as absent, alien, or security threats while framing the 1948 war as pure redemption. **Part II: Israel's Post-Zionist Moment** (Ch. 4–12 & Epilogue: *Brand Israel 2013*) chronicles the remarkable 1990s intellectual opening—driven by the **New Historians** (Simha Flapan, Benny Morris, Avi Shlaim, Pappé), Mizrahi ("Arab Jewish") critical sociologists (Ch. 8), dissenting filmmakers and playwrights (Ch. 9–10), and critical reassessments of Holocaust memory (Ch. 7)—before documenting the post-2000 **Triumph of Neo-Zionism** (Ch. 11–12) that rolled back academic dissent and reconsolidated state consensus.

---

## Core Intellectual-History Themes

1. **Power, Knowledge & Academic Hegemony (Ch. 1–3, 11–12)**: Applies Edward Said's and Michel Foucault's frameworks to show how mainstream academic disciplines actively construct and police national legitimacy.
2. **The 1990s Post-Zionist Opening (Ch. 4–10)**: Archives a brief, vibrant decade when Israeli scholarship, theater, and film openly confronted 1948 expulsions, Mizrahi marginalization, and militarism.
3. **Neo-Zionist Retrenchment & "Brand Israel" (Ch. 11–Epilogue)**: Analyzes how the collapse of the Oslo process and the Second Intifada shifted prominent former New Historians rightward while institutionalizing public-diplomacy (*hasbara*) campaigns.
