## Analytical Overview
### Reinvigorating Platt in the Era of Big Data

In *Strong Inference: The Way of Science*, published in *BioScience*, biologists Thomas B. Kinraide and R. Ford Denison evaluate the contemporary relevance of John R. Platt's classic 1964 thesis. In an era dominated by high-throughput omics, automated machine learning, and multi-terabyte datasets, the authors ask whether big data has rendered deliberate hypothesis testing obsolete, or made it more desperately needed than ever.

### The Seduction of Inductive Data-Mining

The authors demonstrate that modern science suffers from a new failure mode: **inductive data dredging and HARKing (Hypothesizing After the Results are Known)**:
- Running massive correlation matrices across thousands of variables inevitably surfaces statistically significant 'discoveries' that are pure random noise or unreplicable artifacts.
- Without a priori mutually exclusive alternative hypotheses and crucial exclusionary tests, computational biology and medicine risk building elaborate castles of unverified correlation.

### Practical Protocols for Contemporary Labs

Kinraide and Denison provide practical guidelines for modern laboratory directors: formalizing pre-registered alternative hypotheses, implementing blind data analysis, and teaching junior researchers to prioritize falsification over confirmation.
