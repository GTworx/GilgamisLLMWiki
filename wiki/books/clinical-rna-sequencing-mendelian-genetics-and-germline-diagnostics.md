## Summary
Authoritative genomic and transcriptomic compendium detailing the clinical translation of RNA sequencing. Features landmark papers on resolving Mendelian disease diagnostics, detecting non-coding deep-intronic variants that disrupt splicing, and JAMA Oncology clinical trials demonstrating that concurrent DNA and RNA sequencing significantly improves diagnostic accuracy and reclassifies Variants of Uncertain Significance (VUS) in hereditary cancer.

## Analytical Overview
This volume documents the critical transition from DNA-only whole-exome sequencing (WES) to concurrent functional RNA transcriptomics in clinical pathology.

Key clinical genomic insights:
1. **Diagnostic Lift Through Transcriptomics**: Features Yépez et al.’s implementation studies and Byron’s reviews, demonstrating that whole-transcriptome RNA sequencing increases the diagnostic yield for undiagnosed rare Mendelian diseases by 10–35% by functionally proving the pathogenicity of non-coding, deep-intronic, or synonymous genomic variants.
2. **Concurrent DNA and RNA Sequencing in Hereditary Cancer**: Analyzes Horton et al.’s landmark *JAMA Oncology* study evaluating hereditary cancer panels across tens of thousands of patients. Concurrent RNA testing identified pathogenic splicing alterations that standard DNA testing missed, while successfully resolving hundreds of clinically ambiguous Variants of Uncertain Significance (VUS) into benign or pathogenic classifications without waiting for family segregation studies.
3. **Metagenomic and Transcriptomic Methods**: Incorporates comparative studies on DNA vs. RNA extraction protocols, cervical metagenomic microbiome profiling, and bioinformatics workflows (OUTRIDER for aberrant expression, FRASER for splicing) required for CLIA/CAP laboratory validation.
