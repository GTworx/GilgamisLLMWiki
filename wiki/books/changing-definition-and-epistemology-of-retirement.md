## Overview
This two-part sociological and macroeconomic dossier explores the fundamental transformation of 'retirement' in 21st-century aging societies. Moving beyond Bismarckian concepts of sudden, passive withdrawal from productive labor at age 65, the authors examine phased retirement, lifelong learning, un-retirement, and the psychological necessity of purposeful longevity.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Chronicles the historical invention of retirement as an industrial labor management tool rather than a biological imperative.
- **Key Finding**: Examines the rise of 'phased retirement' and fractional consulting keeping senior cognitive capital active.
- **Key Finding**: Demonstrates that psychological health and longevity depend on maintaining purposeful social contribution post-career.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Retirement is no longer a cliff where life ends and waiting begins; in 21st-century longevity societies, it is being reinvented as a dynamic second adulthood of continuous learning, civic engagement, and flexible work."
