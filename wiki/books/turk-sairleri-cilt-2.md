## Executive Summary

*Türk Şairleri, Cilt 2* ("Turkish Poets, Volume 2") is the second volume of literary historian Sadettin Nüzhet Ergun's ambitious, multi-volume biographical encyclopedia of Turkish poets across all regions, centuries, and poetic traditions—uniting classical Divan poets, Sufi *tekke* mystics (Mevlevî, Bektaşî, Hurufî), folk minstrels (*sazşairi*), and modern intellectuals within a single alphabetical archive. Spanning entries #313 through #595+ (continuous pagination ~pp. 475–950, from **Ali Turan [Hüseyinzade Ali]** through **B** and **C** poets including **Bâkî**, **Balım Sultan**, **Bâyezid [Sultan Adlî]**, **Beliğ**, **Bezmî**, **Celâlî**, and **Celîlî**, pp. 2, 475–479), the volume synthesizes classical Ottoman biographical dictionaries (*tezkire*s such as Latîfî, Esad, and Arif Hikmet), archival *sicil* registers, manuscript divan collations, and representative poem selections.

## Scope & Methodological Architecture

- **Inclusive Pan-Turkic & Cross-Genre Coverage**: Opens immediately with a major intellectual biography of Azerbaijani-born polymath, physician, painter, and Turkist pioneer **Hüseyinzade Ali Turan** (1864–1940), tracing his education in Tiflis, St. Petersburg, and the Istanbul Military Medical School (*Mekteb-i Tıbbiye-i Askeriye*) and his co-founding of Baku's pioneering Turkish daily *Hayat* in 1905 (pp. 2–3).
- **Equal Dignity for Folk, Sufi, and Court Poets**: In the volume's index (*İkinci Cildin Fihristi*, pp. 477–479), entries place *Divan şairi*, *Sazşairi*, *Mutasavvıf*, *Bektaşi*, *Hurufî*, and *Mevlevî* figures side by side—for instance distinguishing multiple poets sharing pen names such as *Azmî*, *Bahayî*, *Bâkî*, *Bâlî*, *Beliğ*, *Beyanî*, and *Bezmî*.
- **Manuscript & Tezkire Collation**: For obscure and canonical poets alike (e.g., Ebûbekir Celâleddîn Efendi, d. 1818, and 15th-century Bursalı Celîlî, pp. 475–476), Ergun quotes verbatim from *tezkire* authorities, cites specific Istanbul manuscript library shelfmarks (such as Millet Library *Reşid Efendi* no. 747, noting exact couplet counts like 2,528 *beyit*), and prints full *gazel* and *koşma* texts alongside abbreviated bibliographies.

## Key Historical Significance

1. **Early Republican Canon-Building**: Compiled in the decades following the 1928 Alphabet Reform, Ergun's *Türk Şairleri* undertook the massive rescue operation of transferring centuries of Ottoman-script *tezkire* and *cönk* knowledge into Latinized modern Turkish scholarship.
2. **Transregional Cultural Geography**: Entries map poets born across Kastamonu, Bursa, Edirne, Baghdad, Tabriz, Baku, Priştine, Selânik, Rusçuk, Yanbolu, Vardar Yenicesi, Crimea, and Khorasan (pp. 478–479), illustrating the vast geographic footprint of Turkic literary culture.
3. **Reference Anchor for Literary Research**: Even decades later, Ergun's dense bibliographic citations and primary-poem transcriptions remain a vital starting point for identifying minor Ottoman and folk poets.
