## Executive Summary

In *Blink: The Power of Thinking Without Thinking* (2005, Little, Brown), **Malcolm Gladwell** investigates the cognitive power—and treacherous blind spots—of the **adaptive unconscious**: our brain's ability to make remarkably accurate split-second judgments through **"thin-slicing"** (extracting deep patterns from tiny slivers of experience).

Through vivid case studies—art historians instantly sensing the Getty Museum's multimillion-dollar *kouros* statue was a forgery despite scientific tests, psychologist John Gottman predicting divorce from minutes of marital dialogue, Cook County Hospital improving heart-attack triage by slashing diagnostic variables, and Paul Van Riper outmaneuvering high-tech Pentagon wargames—Gladwell shows that expert intuition can outperform bloated deliberation, *yet* turns catastrophic when distorted by implicit bias (exemplified by blind orchestra auditions and the Warren Harding error) or acute physiological stress (the Amadou Diallo police shooting).

---

## Key Cognitive Insights

1. **Thin-Slicing & Frugal Heuristics**: Under time pressure, isolating a handful of decisive high-signal cues beats drowning decision-makers in excessive irrelevant data.
2. **The Dark Side of Snap Judgments**: Unconscious priming, racial/gender stereotypes, and "mind-reading failure" under extreme heart-rate stress show that intuition must be deliberately trained and structurally shielded (e.g., behind-the-screen orchestra auditions).
