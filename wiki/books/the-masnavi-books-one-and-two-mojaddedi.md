## Executive Summary

Jalal al-Din Rumi's *Masnavi-ye Ma'navi* ("Spiritual Couplets," composed in Konya between c. 1258 and 1273 across six books totaling ~25,000 Persian couplets) is revered across the Islamic world as the "Qur'an in Persian." This consolidated Oxford World's Classics pair presents **Books One and Two** in **Jawid Mojaddedi's** acclaimed modern verse translation—notable as the first full-scale English rendering to preserve the rhymed-couplet poetic form (*masnavi* rhyming *aa bb cc*) while remaining rigorously faithful to the original Persian text and its dense Qur'anic and *hadith* resonances (Book One, pp. 12–32; Book Two, pp. 2–5).

Opening with the immortal eighteen-line *Song of the Reed* (*Neyname*, Book One, p. 37)—in which the reed flute laments its separation from the reed bed as an emblem of the human soul's exile from the Divine—Rumi weaves comic fables, prophetic allegories, animal parables, and piercing theological digressions into a living pedagogical spiral designed to strip away ego (*nafs*) and awaken spiritual discernment.

## Structural Highlights Across Books One & Two

- **Book One (304 pp., 2004)**:
  - *Prose Introduction & The Song of the Reed* (pp. 36–38): Frames the *Masnavi* as "the roots of the roots of the roots of religion" and launches the longing of separation.
  - *The King and the Sick Slave-Girl* (p. 39): Contrasts worldly attachment with the spiritual physician who diagnoses the soul's hidden love.
  - *The Bald Parrot, The Merchant's Parrot, & The Lion and the Hare* (pp. 52, 90, 130): Uses playful animal fables to critique shallow imitation (*taqlid*), spiritual vanity, and the illusion of autonomous power—interwoven with *The Greater Jihad* (p. 119) and *Moses and the Magicians* (p. 134).
- **Book Two (292 pp., 2007)**:
  - Composed after a two-year hiatus following the death of scribe Husam al-Din Chalabi's wife, Book Two deepens Rumi's critique of spiritual pretension and binary egoism—featuring iconic parables such as *Moses and the Shepherd* (affirming that God looks at inner sincerity rather than liturgical polish), *The Four Men Arguing Over Grapes* (differing words for one reality), and *The Sufi's Donkey Sold by Fellow Dervishes*.

## Key Philosophical & Literary Contributions

1. **Verse Fidelity Over Free Adaptation**: Unlike Coleman Barks's free-verse re-creations ([[the-essential-rumi]]), Mojaddedi retains rhyming meter, Islamic scriptural citations, and structural transitions—letting readers experience how Rumi's humor, earthiness, and Qur'anic exegesis work together.
2. **Spiral Pedagogy**: Rather than advancing a linear scholastic syllabus, Rumi interrupts stories mid-flight to draw metaphysical morals, mirroring how mystical realization breaks conventional narrative expectations.
3. **Discernment Between Form (*Surat*) and Meaning (*Ma'na*)**: Across both books, Rumi trains readers to look past outward appearances, sectarian slogans, and literalism toward underlying spiritual truth.
