## Summary
Sophisticated 64-page cliometric study using historical census microdata to prove that the 1882 Chinese Exclusion Act severely slowed manufacturing expansion, reduced capital investment, and depressed wages for Caucasian laborers in the American West.

## Analytical Overview
Evaluates the historical economic impact of America's first major race-based federal immigration restriction. Proves that excluding Chinese laborers did not benefit native-born white workers; instead, manufacturing establishments suffered labor bottlenecks, agricultural productivity stagnated, and total economic activity contracted across western counties with high prior Chinese employment.
