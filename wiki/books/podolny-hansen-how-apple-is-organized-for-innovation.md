## Analytical Overview
### Overturning Conventional Corporate Conglomerate Design

In *How Apple Is Organized for Innovation*, published in *Harvard Business Review* (November–December 2020), Joel M. Podolny and Morten T. Hansen uncover the secret organizational blueprint powering Apple's sustained product excellence: its refusal to adopt the conventional multidivisional (M-form) structure with autonomous business units and siloed general managers.

### The Functional Architecture: Experts Leading Experts

When Steve Jobs returned to Apple in 1997, he fired the general managers of all product business units and reorganized the entire company into a single functional organization:
- **Decision-Making by Technical Depth:** Decisions are made by individuals who possess deep technical domain expertise rather than generalist administrators or finance executives. Hardware engineers manage hardware; software specialists manage software.
- **Unified Corporate P&L:** Apple maintains a single company-wide P&L statement. Individual department heads are freed from short-term unit margin pressures, allowing product teams to focus purely on customer experience, industrial design elegance, and technical integration.
- **Collaborative Tension & Institutional Debate:** Because product releases cross multiple functional lines (hardware, software, operations, design), executives must master collaborative negotiation without bureaucratic gridlock.

### The Leadership Triad at Scale

Podolny and Hansen outline the three essential leadership qualities required of Apple executives: deep domain expertise, intense immersion in technical details, and the willingness to collaboratively debate across peer functions.
