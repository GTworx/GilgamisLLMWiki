## Executive Summary

This 26-page philosophical psychology monograph by Mahon McCann synthesizes contemporary existential psychology, Viktor Frankl's logotherapy, and positive psychology into a coherent framework for discovering, constructing, and sustaining meaning in modern secular life. Addressing the pervasive 'meaning crisis' characterized by nihilism, digital distraction, and existential anxiety, McCann deconstructs meaning into three distinct psychological pillars: Purpose (future-oriented goal directedness), Significance (the subjective feeling that one's life matters), and Coherence (the cognitive sense that life is intelligible and orderly). The work establishes practical self-authoring exercises to build psychological resilience.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **The Tripartite Structure of Meaning**: Formulates the three validated psychological components of meaning: Coherence (making sense of experience), Purpose (having directional goals), and Significance (perceiving inherent value).
- **The Modern Meaning Crisis**: Analyzes how the collapse of traditional religious metanarratives, combined with digital consumerism and social media fragmentation, has plunged individuals into an existential vacuum.
- **Frankl's Logotherapeutic Insights**: Reconstructs Viktor Frankl's core thesis: humans can endure any 'how' if they possess a compelling 'why', finding meaning through creative work, experiential love, and courageous attitude toward suffering.
- **Distinguishing Happiness from Meaning**: Highlights research proving that chasing superficial positive affect (happiness) leads to fragility, whereas pursuing meaningful, responsibility-heavy commitments builds deep psychological well-being.
- **Narrative Identity and Self-Authoring**: Outlines autobiographical writing exercises that help individuals integrate past traumas into coherent stories of personal growth and heroic responsibility.
