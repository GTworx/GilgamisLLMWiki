## Executive Summary

Curated to mark the centenary of *Harvard Business Review* (founded in 1922) and introduced by Editor-in-Chief **Adi Ignatius** (pp. 4, 8), *HBR at 100* (2022) brings together thirty landmark essays that defined modern management science across strategy, leadership, innovation, organizational psychology, data science, and the future of work.

Reading the volume front to back traces a full century of corporate intellectual evolution—from **Peter F. Drucker's** *Managing Oneself*, **Theodore Levitt's** *Marketing Myopia*, **Frederick Herzberg's** *One More Time: How Do You Motivate Employees?*, and **Michael E. Porter's** *How Competitive Forces Shape Strategy*, through **Clayton M. Christensen's** *Disruptive Technologies*, **W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne's** *Blue Ocean Strategy*, **John P. Kotter's** *Leading Change*, and **Daniel Goleman's** *What Makes a Leader?*, all the way to 21st-century frontiers including **Erik Brynjolfsson & Andrew McAfee's** *The Business of Artificial Intelligence*, **Thomas H. Davenport & D. J. Patil's** *Data Scientist: The Sexiest Job of the 21st Century*, **Amy C. Edmondson & Mark Mortensen's** *What Psychological Safety Looks Like in a Hybrid Workplace*, and **Matt Weinzierl & Mehak Sarang's** *The Commercial Space Age Is Here* (pp. 4–7).

---

## Key Centennial Arcs

1. **Strategy Evolution**: Moves from structural industry-moat defense (Porter's Five Forces) to value-curve creation (Blue Ocean) and incumbent vulnerability to low-end disruption (Christensen).
2. **Human-Centered Leadership**: Traces the shift from command-and-control delegation (*Who's Got the Monkey?*) toward emotional intelligence (Goleman), intrinsic motivation (Herzberg, Amabile), authentic inclusion (Tina Opie), and hybrid psychological safety (Edmondson).
3. **Algorithmic & Space Frontiers**: Documents how data science, enterprise AI, and commercial space have transitioned from niche technical curiosities into boardroom core strategy.
