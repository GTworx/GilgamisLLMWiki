## Executive Summary
Published in 2025 by evolutionary anthropologists and primatologists, this 18-page comparative ethology treatise examines the evolutionary origins and functional biology of kissing in humans and non-human animals. Testing rival evolutionary hypotheses—premastication food transfer, social grooming reduction, and olfactory/salivary mate assessment—the authors demonstrate that romantic/sexual kissing is not a human cultural universal, but emerged from primitive tactile and chemical sampling mechanisms. The paper evaluates how kissing facilitates major histocompatibility complex (MHC) immune profile sampling via saliva, oxytocin and dopamine release, and pathogenic oral microbiome transmission.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Maternal Premastication Hypothesis**: Traces the evolutionary origin of mouth-to-mouth contact to ancestral maternal food transfer (pre-masticating tough meat and tubers for infants), which was co-opted into adult bonding.
- **Salivary MHC Chemical Sampling**: Close-contact kissing allows unconscious olfactory and gustatory assessment of a partner's Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) immune diversity, favoring genetically dissimilar mates for immunocompetent offspring.
- **Neuroendocrine Surge**: Salivary contact triggers rapid surges in oxytocin (trust and bonding) and dopamine (anticipatory reward) while significantly suppressing cortisol (stress reduction).
- **Cultural Variation and Pathogen Load**: Cross-cultural ethnographic analysis of 168 societies reveals romantic kissing is present in only 46% of cultures, correlating strongly with social stratification and complex societal hygiene regimes.
