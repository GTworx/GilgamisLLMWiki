## Summary
Exhaustive legal, strategic, and operational compendium of Turkey’s national disaster governance frameworks, analyzing the National Disaster Response Plan (TAMP), the Disaster Risk Reduction Plan 2022–2030 (TARAP), the National Earthquake Strategy (UDSEP 2012–2023), and AFAD institutional performance.

## Analytical Overview
This work provides an authoritative, comprehensive analysis of the institutional architecture governing civil protection, emergency response, and disaster risk reduction in Turkey. The foundation of modern Turkish civil defense is AFAD, established in 2009 to replace fragmented civil protection bodies and integrate emergency governance under a unified central agency. This volume examines the four primary statutory pillars designed by AFAD to mitigate national geohazards.

First, the volume dissects the *Türkiye Afet Müdahale Planı (TAMP)*, which organizes emergency operations across 28 specialized disaster service groups (search-and-rescue, temporary shelter, debris removal, psychosocial support, health, logistics) coordinated via hierarchical incident command centers. Second, it reviews the *Türkiye Afet Risk Azaltma Planı (TARAP 2022–2030)*, which operationalizes the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction through localized Provincial Risk Reduction Plans (İRAP), featuring granular action matrices such as the Hatay İRAP Earthquake Action Table analyzed in this volume.

Third, the work evaluates the *Ulusal Deprem Stratejisi ve Eylem Planı (UDSEP 2012–2023)* and successful research initiatives funded under the National Earthquake Research Program (UDAP). The collection is critically contextualized by academic public administration research from Dr. M. Fatih Şahin and AFAD's internal budgetary and performance reports. The analysis exposes structural operational tensions: while institutional plans on paper exhibit sophisticated inter-agency design, real-world execution during mega-disasters is routinely compromised by extreme administrative centralism, delayed military mobilization, and underfunded municipal enforcement.
