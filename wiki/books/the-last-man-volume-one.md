## Executive Summary

Published in 1826 by the author of *Frankenstein*, Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley's *The Last Man* is a pioneering work of futuristic political and apocalyptic science fiction, set in the late 21st century (2073–2100). This digitized volume contains the complete **Volume I** of the original three-volume first edition (Introduction, pp. i–xii, and Chapters I–X, pp. 1–358 / PDF pp. 14–370), establishing the novel's mythic framing device and its sweeping pre-plague political and romantic drama.

Opening with a haunting **Introduction** in which the narrator and a companion descend into the **Cave of the Cumaean Sibyl** near Naples and gather prophetic leaves inscribed in many languages (PDF pp. 5–12), Volume I follows **Lionel Verney**—an orphaned Cumbrian shepherd transformed by friendship with **Adrian, Earl of Windsor** (an idealistic portrait of Percy Bysshe Shelley)—through the fall of the British monarchy into a 21st-century Republic, the rise of ambitious aristocratic statesman **Lord Raymond** (modeled on Lord Byron), the tangled loves of **Perdita**, **Idris**, and **Evadne**, and Raymond's fateful campaign in the Greco-Turkish war outside Constantinople (pp. 353–357 / PDF pp. 366–370).

## Structural Arc of Volume I

- **Introduction — The Sibyl's Cave (PDF pp. 5–12)**: Constructs a brilliant metafictional frame—positioning the entire futuristic novel as a deciphered translation of scattered Sibylline prophecies recovered from Baiae and Cumae, explicitly meditating on why narrating ruin brings paradoxical imaginative solace (pp. 11–12).
- **Chapters I–III (PDF pp. 14–112)**: Introduces Lionel Verney's wild youth in Cumberland, the abdication of England's last king in favor of a Republic, and Lionel's intellectual awakening through Adrian's compassionate mentorship at Windsor.
- **Chapters IV–VII (PDF pp. 113–261)**: Traces the high-stakes romance and political rivalries connecting Lionel, Adrian, Perdita, Idris, and the charismatic military-political titan **Lord Raymond**, who abandons royal intrigue to become **Lord Protector of England** (Chapter VI, PDF p. 208).
- **Chapters VIII–X (PDF pp. 262–370)**: Depicts Raymond's tragic domestic rupture with Perdita over his lingering obsession with Greek exile Evadne, his resignation from the Protectorate to fight in Greece's war against the Ottomans, his victory on the plains of Thrace along the Hebrus, his subsequent capture, and Lionel and Perdita's May departure from Portsmouth to rescue him in the East (pp. 353–357)—setting the stage for the global plague that dominates Volumes II and III.

## Key Literary & Political Themes

1. **Romantic Elegy as Speculative Fiction**: Written in the wake of Percy Shelley's drowning (1822) and Lord Byron's death in Greece (1824), *The Last Man* transfigures intimate bereavement into a sweeping meditation on republican idealism, aristocratic charisma, and civilizational fragility.
2. **Critique of Revolutionary Ambition & War**: Before the pandemic even arrives, Shelley exposes how geopolitical ambition (Raymond's quest to capture Constantinople) and partisan pride erode domestic happiness and republican harmony.
3. **Foundational Modern Sci-Fi Lineage**: Together with *Frankenstein*, Shelley's 21st-century republican Britain—complete with hot-air balloon travel and globalized geopolitics—helped invent modern future-history speculation.
