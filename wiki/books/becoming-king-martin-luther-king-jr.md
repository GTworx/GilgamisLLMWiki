## Executive Summary

*Becoming King: Martin Luther King Jr. and the Making of a National Leader* (University Press of Kentucky, 2008, 270 pp., with an Introduction by Clayborne Carson) by historian and pastor [[troy-jackson]] re-examines the crucible that transformed a twenty-six-year-old newcomer minister into the global emblem of the American Civil Rights Movement: the **1955–1956 Montgomery Bus Boycott** (source: BOOKS/Biography_/Becoming King_ Martin Luther King Jr. and the Making of a National Leader...pdf, Title Page, Copyright & Contents, pp. 4–8).

Challenging "great man" hagiographies that credit the movement to a lone charismatic messiah, Jackson demonstrates that **Montgomery made King just as much as King made Montgomery**. By foregrounding grassroots organizers—E. D. Nixon, Jo Ann Robinson and the Women's Political Council, Rosa Parks, Ralph Abernathy, and thousands of everyday Black domestic workers who walked for 381 days—Jackson reveals how King forged his distinctive Gandhian–Christian theology of nonviolent direct action, prophetic oratory, and national moral leadership in real-time dialogue with an already-mobilized community (Introduction & Chs. 1–6, pp. 12–207).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Introduction & Prologue: Community Before Charisma (pp. 12–29)
- **Reciprocal Leadership**: Frames King's emergence through the lens of grassroots social-movement historiography, showing how Dexter Avenue Baptist Church and Montgomery's civic infrastructure shaped his political awakening (pp. 12–29).

### Chapters 1–3: Arrival in Montgomery & the Spark of the Boycott (pp. 30–105)
- **Preparation & Reluctant Call**: Traces King's Boston University doctoral training, his pastoral apprenticeship at Dexter Avenue, and his unexpected election as president of the **Montgomery Improvement Association (MIA)** following Rosa Parks's December 1955 arrest—selected in part because his youth and newcomer status placed him outside local factional rivalries (pp. 30–105).

### Chapters 4–6 & Epilogue: Nonviolence, Crisis & National Stature (pp. 106–207)
- **Kitchen-Table Epiphany & Gandhian Synthesis**: Chronicles the bombing of King's home, mass arrests, legal warfare culminating in *Browder v. Gayle*, and the tutelage of Bayard Rustin and Glenn Smiley, through which King synthesized Black Baptist prophetic theology with [[mahatma-gandhi]]'s *satyagraha* (pp. 106–167).
- **From Local Pastor to SCLC Visionary**: Shows how the Montgomery victory launched King onto the national and global stage as founding president of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC), carrying Montgomery's lessons of disciplined nonviolent solidarity into the broader 1960s freedom struggle (pp. 168–207).
