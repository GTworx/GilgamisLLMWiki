## Executive Summary

Bringing together eleven landmark peer-reviewed research monographs by **Peter B. Golden** in the prestigious *Variorum Collected Studies Series*, *Turks and Khazars: Origins, Institutions, and Interactions in Pre-Mongol Eurasia* represents the gold standard of philological and historical scholarship on pre-Mongol Inner Asia. Golden examines the origins of the early Turkic ethnonym, the statecraft and sacred kingship of the **Khazar Qağanate** (including the conversion to Judaism), interaction between nomadic steppe elites and Byzantine diplomacy, the Pechenegs and Cumans/Qıpçaqs, and the transformation of steppe political culture.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Sacral Kingship and Dual Rule**: Unpacks the anthropological mechanics of Khazar sacred dual kingship, proving that the supreme Qağan was a divine lightning rod whose charismatic *Kut* protected the realm, while executive power was wielded by the Bek.
2. **Judaism as Diplomatic Neutrality**: Interprets the Khazar ruling elite's adoption of Judaism (c. 8th–9th c.) not as an eccentric aberration, but as a brilliant diplomatic maneuver to preserve complete ideological independence against both Christian Byzantine and Muslim Abbasid imperial expansion.
3. **Linguistic Pluralism of the Western Steppe**: Demonstrates that the western steppe was a polyglot zone where Oghuric (Bulghar), Common Turkic, Alanic (Iranian), Finno-Ugric, and Slavic tongues coexisted under Khazar suzerainty.
