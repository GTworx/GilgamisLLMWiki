## Executive Summary

Written by Byzantine statesman, polymath, and chief church magistrate **Georges Pachymérès** (Georgios Pachymeres, 1242–c. 1310), *Relations Historiques* (*Bizanslı Gözüyle Türkler*) is the single most vital contemporary Byzantine primary text documenting the final collapse of Byzantine authority in Western Anatolia and the meteoric rise of the Turkmen principalities (Beyliks)—most critically containing the **very first recorded mention in world historiography of Osman Gazi (Atman)** and the foundational **Battle of Bapheus (Koyunhisar, July 27, 1302)**.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The First Historic Record of Osman I**: Pachymeres records Osman (*Atman*) as an aggressive frontier warlord operating near Nicaea and Nicomedia, uniting nomadic gazis and disaffected border populations.
2. **The Dismantling of the Akritai**: Analyzes the catastrophic fiscal mistake of Michael VIII Palaiologos in revoking tax exemptions and grain subsidies for Anatolian frontier guards (*akritai*), leaving the Byzantine border defenseless against Turkish encroachment.
3. **The Battle of Bapheus as Turning Point**: Confirms Halil İnalcık's thesis that Bapheus (1302) marks the true birth of the Ottoman state as an imperial power recognized across Anatolia and the Levant.

## Notable Excerpts & Primary Citations
