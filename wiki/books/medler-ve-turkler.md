## Executive Summary

In this monumental, 601-page academic investigation, prominent philosopher, historian of ideas, and theologian **Prof. Dr. Mehmet Bayrakdar** examines the ethnic, linguistic, mythological, and archaeological origins of the ancient **Medes (Medler)**—who founded the great Median Empire in the first millennium BCE, overthrew the Assyrian Empire at Nineveh in 612 BCE, and ruled the Iranian plateau prior to the Persian Achaemenids. Bayrakdar gathers classical Greek sources (Herodotus, Ctesias, Strabo), Assyrian and Babylonian cuneiform annals, and comparative philology to challenge the standard Indo-Iranian classification, arguing that the Medes were originally an agglutinative-speaking, Turanian/Turkic horse-riding steppe people akin to the Gutians, Kassites, and Scythians.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Turanian Substratum of the Medes**: Argues that the core founding tribes of the Median confederation were not Indo-European Iranians, but belonged to the ancient non-Semitic, non-Indo-European Turanian/Proto-Turkic stratum of the Zagros and Caspian regions.
2. **Medieval Historiographical Equation**: Cites Byzantine chronicles (such as Theophylact Simocatta and Anna Comnena) and Armenian chroniclers (such as Moses Khorenatsi) who routinely and systematically referred to the Seljuk and early Turks as *'Medes'*.
3. **The Magi as Shamanic Priests**: Suggests that the famous priestly caste of the Magi (*Mecusiler*) preserved ancient Ural-Altaic cosmological, astronomical, and fire-reverence rituals later subsumed into Zoroastrianism.
