## Overview
Published in Archaeological Discovery, this 23-page epigraphic study investigates the symbolic glyphs and pictorial low-reliefs carved onto the monumental T-shaped limestone pillars at Göbekli Tepe (~9500 BCE). The authors evaluate arguments that recurring abstract symbols (H-signs, crescents, disks, animals) represent proto-writing or mnemonic astronomical notation pre-dating Sumerian cuneiform by over six millennia.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Analyzes recurring geometric and animal bas-reliefs on Pillar 43 ('Vulture Stone') and Enclosure D as proto-symbolic communication.
- **Key Finding**: Compares Göbekli Tepe iconography with later Anatolian hieroglyphic and Near Eastern proto-pictographic traditions.
- **Key Finding**: Debates the threshold between ritual symbolic art, astronomical calendrical notation, and genuine early writing systems.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Whether interpreted as astronomical markers or ritual narrative emblems, the carvings at Göbekli Tepe demonstrate that complex external symbolic storage existed long before sedentary agricultural civilization."
