## Executive Summary

*The End of Modernism: Elias Canetti's Auto-da-Fé* (published by University of North Carolina Press in 2001; reissued with a new Foreword in 2010, UNC Studies in the Germanic Languages and Literatures No. 124) is [[william-collins-donahue]]'s definitive literary-historical monograph on Nobel laureate [[elias-canetti]]'s 1935 modernist masterpiece *Die Blendung* (*Auto-da-Fé*) (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 4–8, 13–21). Challenging decades of critical orthodoxy that read *Auto-da-Fé* as an ahistorical allegory of timeless madness or pure high-modernist formal experiment, Donahue demonstrates that Canetti crafted an **analytic modernism**—a fiercely historicized satire of Weimar and First Austrian Republic cultural, philosophical, and socio-political pathologies on the eve of Nazi totalitarianism (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 13–34).

Focusing on the reclusive sinologist Peter Kien—who barricades himself inside a 25,000-volume private library ('A Head Without a World') before being dispossessed by his housekeeper Therese Krumbholz, cast into the predatory urban underworld of the hunchbacked chess-playing dwarf Fischerle ('Headless World'), and driven to self-immolation amidst his books despite his psychiatrist brother Georg Kien's intervention ('The World in the Head')—Donahue proves that Canetti indicts high modernism's own escapist inwardness (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 8, 18–160). When scholars and aesthetes retreat into Neo-Kantian idealism, positivist philology, or fashionable Freudian relativism while abandoning the public sphere, they become complicit in the triumph of vulgar materialism, misogyny, anti-Semitism, and fascist crowd hysteria (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 43–145).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Preface, 2010 Foreword & Introduction: Modernism in a Different Key (pp. xi–xxiv, 1–17)**:
  * *Analytic vs. Aesthetic High Modernism*: Distinguishes Canetti from the world-weary aestheticism of Rilke, early Hofmannsthal, and Musil. Canetti's novel deploys 'wicked humor' and socio-analytic precision to diagnose the shrinking democratic public sphere of interwar Vienna and Weimar Germany (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 13–25).

* **Chapter 1: The Novel(s) in the Novel — Modernism as Parody of Popular Realism (pp. 18–42)**:
  * *Subverting Bourgeois Realism*: Shows how Therese's obsession with sentimental serial novels and Willibald Alexis's nationalist historical fiction (*Die Hosen des Herrn von Bredow*) is parodied alongside Kien's elitist textual fetishism, exposing how both popular realism and mandarin scholarship evade social reality (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 26–51).

* **Chapter 2: 'The truth is you're a woman. You live for sensations' — Misogyny as Cultural Critique (pp. 43–75)**:
  * *Otto Weininger on Trial*: Refutes critics who accuse Canetti of unreflective misogyny by proving that Kien's virulent anti-female tirades are a deliberate, grotesque parody of Otto Weininger's *Geschlecht und Charakter* (1903) and Karl Kraus's Vienna, exposing how intellectualized misogyny masks male ascetic impotence and socio-economic greed (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 43–75).

* **Chapter 3: Self-Indulgent Philosophies of the Weimar Period — Neoempiricism and Neo-Kantianism (pp. 76–105)**:
  * *Epistemological Blindness (*Blendung*)*: Analyzes how Kien weaponizes George Berkeley's subjective idealism (*esse est percipi*) and Marburg Neo-Kantianism to literally 'blind' himself to неприятные truths—practicing the art of overlooking real-world violence until fascist brutality breaks down his door (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 76–105).

* **Chapter 4: The Hunchback of 'Heaven' — Anti-Semitism and the Failure of Humanism (pp. 106–138)**:
  * *Fischerle and the Stereotype Mirror*: Examines the Jewish dwarf Siegfried Fischerle not as an anti-Semitic caricature, but as a devastating indictment of how Weimar anti-Semitic tropes (*Der Stürmer*) deform their victims while humanist intellectuals like Peter Kien fail to extend solidarity to the persecuted (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 106–138).

* **Chapter 5: 'An Impudent Choir of Croaking Frogs' — Freud, Crowd Psychology, and the End of Modernism (pp. 139–195)**:
  * *Georg Kien and the Limits of Psychoanalysis*: Critiques brother Georg Kien's romanticization of madness and Freudian ego-psychology, showing how *Auto-da-Fé* anticipates Canetti's later magnum opus *Crowds and Power* (*Masse und Macht*, 1960) by warning that neither ivory-tower rationalism nor irrationalist crowd-worship can save civilization from self-incineration (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, pp. 139–195).

## Key Quotes & Citations

* "The novel's wicked humor, its analytic posture, and above all its concern for the diminishing public sphere set it far apart from what we would come to know as 'aesthetic,' or 'high modernism.'" (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, Preface, p. 13)
* "A shrewd observer of entrenched power, he knew that social change was not a decisionist affair, and his modernist novel satirizes those who think they can simply think away the unpleasantness that surrounds them." (source: BOOKS/Fiction/2010 The End of Modernism.pdf, Foreword, p. 21)
