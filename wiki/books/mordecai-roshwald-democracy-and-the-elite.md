## Summary
Philosophical treatise by Mordecai Roshwald exploring the enduring historical tension between democratic mass egalitarianism and the functional necessity of intellectual and administrative meritocracies.

## Analytical Overview
### The Dilemma of Mass Rule and Elite Competence

In *Democracy and the Elite*, political philosopher Mordecai Roshwald examines the fundamental, unresolved paradox at the heart of democratic theory: democracy proclaims the absolute moral equality and political sovereignty of all citizens, yet the effective administration of complex modern civilization requires specialized technical, legal, and intellectual elites.

### The Tyranny of Either Extreme

Roshwald analyzes the twin pathologies threatening democratic polities:
1. **Oligarchic Hubris:** When technocratic elites become detached from the citizenry, holding mass sentiment in contempt and rigging institutional rules to perpetuate their own caste.
2. **Populist Anti-Intellectualism:** When egalitarian sentiment degenerates into mob rule and hostility toward expertise, electing demagogues and degrading scientific competence.

### Aristotle's Polity Re-Visited

Drawing on Aristotle's *Politics* and the American Founders, Roshwald argues that enduring free societies require a delicate constitutional synthesis: open, permeable meritocracies accountable to periodic popular scrutiny.
