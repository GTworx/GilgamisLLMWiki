## Summary
Pioneering memory studies treatise exploring why modern democratic societies institutionalize collective commemoration, examining the transition from heroic nationalist myths to victim-centered memory cultures.

## Analytical Overview
Uhl analyzes the post-1989 European 'memory boom', investigating how national identities reconfigure their relationship to traumatic historical pasts. Explores Maurice Halbwachs's social frameworks of memory, Jan Assmann's communicative vs. cultural memory, and the political imperatives driving monuments, state apologies, and museum architecture.
