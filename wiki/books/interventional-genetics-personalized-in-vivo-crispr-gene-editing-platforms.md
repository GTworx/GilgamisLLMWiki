## Executive Summary
This forward-looking 4-page perspective paper outlines the scientific, regulatory, and manufacturing roadmap for transitioning gene therapy from rare-disease ex vivo cell engineering into universal, personalized *in vivo* 'interventional genetics' platforms.

The authors detail how next-generation base editors, prime editors, and RNA-guided nucleases formulated in tissue-targeted lipid nanoparticles (tLNPs) can be deployed as point-of-care therapeutics. The paper proposes streamlined regulatory approval tracks (n-of-1 trial frameworks), modular gRNA synthesis pipelines, automated bioinformatic off-target prediction software, and localized non-viral delivery to correct pathogenic single-nucleotide variants in cardiovascular, metabolic, and neurodegenerative disorders.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Transition to In Vivo Interventional Genetics**: Details the shift from labor-intensive ex vivo cell engineering toward direct, intravenous or local infusion of targeted gene-editing complexes.
- **Targeted Lipid Nanoparticles (tLNPs)**: Examines antibody- and peptide-conjugated LNPs capable of bypassing the liver to deliver Cas-mRNA and guide RNAs specifically to cardiac myocytes, lung epithelium, or brain microglia.
- **Precision of Base and Prime Editing**: Highlights how base editors (C-to-T, A-to-G) and prime editors correct transition mutations without generating double-stranded DNA breaks, drastically reducing chromosomal translocation risks.
- **Regulatory Pathways for N-of-1 Therapies**: Proposes standardized pre-clinical safety frameworks to accelerate clinical approval for patient-tailored guide RNAs correcting private familial mutations.
