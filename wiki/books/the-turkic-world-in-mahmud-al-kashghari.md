## Executive Summary

Delivered as the inaugural Omeljan Pritsak Memorial Lecture by **Peter B. Golden**, *The Turkic World in Mahmûd al-Kâshgharî* (2010, 68 pp.) is an extraordinary philological and historical exploration of the earliest encyclopedia of the Turkic world: the **Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk** (Compendium of the Turkic Dialects), completed in Baghdad in 1074 CE by Qarakhanid prince and lexicographer **Mahmûd al-Kâshgharî**. Golden analyzes Kâshgharî's famous round world map (centering on Balasagun and Lake Issyk-Kul), his dialectological classifications of 20+ Turkic tribes (Oğuz, Qıpçaq, Karluk, Basmıl, Yabaku), and the ideological worldview that celebrated the Turks as God's designated rulers of the Islamic world.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Divine Destiny of the Turks**: Cites Kâshgharî's famous prophetic *hadith*: *"God Almighty said: 'I have a host whom I have called Türk and stationed in the East. When I am angered with a people, I send them against them.'"*
2. **Pioneering Comparative Dialectology**: Recognizes Kâshgharî as a medieval linguist eight centuries ahead of his time, applying rigorous phonetic rules and morphological comparisons across dozens of disparate nomadic dialects.
3. **The Seljuk-Qarakhanid Cultural Synthesis**: Illustrates how the Dīwān served as the cultural bridge reconciling proud Inner Asian steppe martial heritage with Islamic high literacy and Arab-Persian cosmopolitanism.
