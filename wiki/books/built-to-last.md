## Executive Summary

Drawing on a six-year Stanford Graduate School of Business research project comparing eighteen enduring **"visionary companies"** (3M, Hewlett-Packard, Johnson & Johnson, Boeing, Disney, Sony, Merck, Walmart, IBM, GE, Procter & Gamble, Marriott, Motorola, Nordstrom, Citicorp, American Express, Philip Morris, Ford) against direct industry comparison peers, **Jim Collins** and **Jerry I. Porras** overturn twelve pervasive corporate myths in *Built to Last* (1994, p. 3).

Their unifying discovery is the **"Genius of the AND"**: truly enduring institutions refuse the "Tyranny of the OR," simultaneously **preserving a near-sacred core ideology** (core values and purpose beyond mere profit maximization) while relentlessly **stimulating progress** through audacious goals, evolutionary experimentation, and self-imposed discomfort.

---

## Signature Management Concepts

1. **Clock Building, Not Time Telling**: Visionary founders focus on designing self-renewing organizational architectures that outlive any single charismatic CEO rather than relying on one brilliant product idea or personality.
2. **BHAGs (Big Hairy Audacious Goals)**: Clear, compelling, long-term challenges—like Boeing betting the company on the 707 or Sony vowing to change Japan's global reputation for poor quality—that galvanize collective energy.
3. **Cult-Like Cultures & Home-Grown Management**: Enduring companies hold uncompromising standards that rapidly attract true believers and eject misfits, while cultivating deep internal leadership benches rather than chasing savior outsiders.
4. **Try a Lot of Stuff and Keep What Works**: Treats strategic evolution (exemplified by 3M's Post-it Notes) like Darwinian branching variation rather than omniscient master planning.
