## Executive Summary

**Malcolm Gladwell's** debut bestseller *The Tipping Point* (2000, Little, Brown, pp. 3–5) applies epidemiological modeling to cultural trends, consumer fads, crime waves, and social change—explaining how ideas, products, and behaviors suddenly cross a critical nonlinear threshold ("tip") and spread like contagious epidemics. From the sudden revival of Hush Puppies shoes and Paul Revere's midnight ride to New York City's 1990s subway crime drop and *Sesame Street*'s educational retention, Gladwell argues that tiny targeted levers can produce disproportionate macro-level shifts.

## The Three Rules of Social Epidemics (Contents, p. 5)

1. **The Law of the Few (Connectors, Mavens, and Salesmen)**: Social contagion is driven not by average citizens, but by three rare personality archetypes—**Connectors** (bridging disparate social worlds), **Mavens** (obsessive information specialists eager to educate), and **Salesmen** (charismatic emotional persuaders).
2. **The Stickiness Factor**: Subtle structural tweaks in presentation and pacing (discovered empirically by *Sesame Street* and *Blue's Clues*) determine whether a message actually lodges in memory and compels action.
3. **The Power of Context**: Human behavior is far more sensitive to immediate environmental cues and group-size limits (including **Dunbar's Number**, ~150 people for cohesive social groups, and the "Broken Windows" policing debate) than to fixed internal personality traits.
