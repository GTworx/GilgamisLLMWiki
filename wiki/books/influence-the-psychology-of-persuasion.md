## Executive Summary

Combining rigorous experimental social psychology with three years of undercover participant-observation inside car dealerships, direct-sales training programs, telemarketing rooms, and fundraising agencies (pp. 7–8), Arizona State University professor **Robert B. Cialdini's** *Influence* (1984/2007) is the definitive scientific anatomy of human compliance—exposing the six universal psychological "click, whirr" shortcuts that trigger automatic **"yes"** responses, along with practical defenses to spot when those triggers are weaponized against you.

---

## Six Universal Weapons of Influence (Contents, p. 4)

1. **Reciprocation**: Humans feel intense social obligation to repay uninvited favors, free samples, or strategic concessions (the *rejection-then-retreat* / door-in-the-face technique).
2. **Commitment and Consistency**: Once people make a small, active, public commitment ("foot-in-the-door"), internal pressure to appear consistent pulls them toward vastly larger follow-on actions.
3. **Social Proof**: Under uncertainty, we determine appropriate behavior by watching what similar peers are doing (explaining both viral crowds and bystander apathy).
4. **Liking**: We say yes to people we like—amplified by physical attractiveness, similarity, sincere compliments, cooperative contact, and positive association.
5. **Authority**: Deep evolutionary deference to legitimate expertise—and its superficial symbols (titles, uniforms, luxury trappings, echoing Milgram's obedience experiments).
6. **Scarcity**: Opportunities appear exponentially more valuable as their availability shrinks (loss aversion, limited deadlines, and psychological reactance against censorship).
