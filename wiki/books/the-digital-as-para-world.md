## Executive Summary

*The Digital as Para-world: Design, Anthropology, and Information Technologies* (Chapter 14, pp. 202–211, 10 pp., in Alison J. Clarke, ed., *Design Anthropology: Object Culture in the 21st Century*, SpringerWienNewYork, 2011) by anthropologist and STS scholar [[lane-denicola]] examines the historical lacuna between classical cultural anthropology and technology—and charts how a new generation of **digital and design anthropologists** investigates ubiquitous computing, social networking, and interaction design as a **"para-world"** interwoven with pre-existing material cultures (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/denicola-in-clarke The Digital as Para-world.pdf, pp. 1–2).

Drawing on François Sigaut's maxim that *"because technical facts are facts of human activity, [technology] is a human science, a branch of anthropology"* and Christina Wasson's history of corporate ethnography, DeNicola contrasts earlier usability testing—dominated by cognitive psychology and laboratory models of what happens "in the head" of the user—with **participant-observation ethnography**, which emerged in industrial design labs during the late 1980s and 1990s to capture what real people *actually* do with information technologies in situated social worlds (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/denicola-in-clarke The Digital as Para-world.pdf, pp. 1–2).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Anthropology's Undertheorized Relationship with Technology (pp. 1–2 / book pp. 202–203)**:
  * *From Homo Faber to STS*: Notes how classical anthropology treated tools as mere cultural artifacts while Science and Technology Studies (STS) adopted ethnographic and postcolonial methods (Sharon Traweek, Gyan Prakash) to interrogate how technological infrastructures reconfigure global social order (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/denicola-in-clarke The Digital as Para-world.pdf, pp. 1–2).

* **From Cognitive Usability to Design Ethnography & The Digital Para-world (pp. 2–10 / book pp. 203–211)**:
  * *Participant-Observation in Interaction Design*: Traces the corporate transition from surveys and cognitive human-factors models toward situated participant-observation; frames the digital as a **"para-world"** where software interfaces, ubiquitous sensors, and cultural imaginaries co-constitute physical environments rather than replacing them (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/denicola-in-clarke The Digital as Para-world.pdf, pp. 1–10).
