## Summary
Landmark NBER study calculating that innovators capture only a minuscule fraction (~2.2%) of the total economic surplus generated by technological innovations, with 97.8% passing to consumers in the form of lower prices and higher quality.

## Analytical Overview
Develops theoretical and econometric models of Schumpeterian creative destruction and technological diffusion across non-farm business sectors in the United States from 1948 to 2001. Demonstrates that rapid technological progress generates massive societal consumer surplus while competitive imitation and obsolescence quickly erode entrepreneurial profit margins.
