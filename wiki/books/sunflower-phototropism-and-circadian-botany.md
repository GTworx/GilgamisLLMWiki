## Executive Summary
This botanical and physiological monograph analyzes the circadian mechanics of solar tracking (heliotropism) in the common sunflower (*Helianthus annuus*). The study investigates the cellular hormonal mechanisms—specifically asymmetric stem elongation mediated by auxin redistribution, photoreceptor phytochromes, and phototropins—that allow young sunflower heads to track the sun eastward to westward during the day, and actively reorient back toward the east overnight in anticipation of dawn. The paper addresses linguistic and evolutionary nuances (e.g., Turkish naming conventions comparing *Ayçiçeği* / *Günebakan*), while exploring why mature sunflowers permanently face east to warm morning flower heads and attract pollinating bees.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Endogenous Circadian Clock Drive**: Heliotropism is not a passive response to light, but an active endogenous circadian rhythm; sunflowers transplanted into 24-hour constant light continue rhythmic east-west oscillations for several days.
- **Differential Stem Growth**: During the day, the east side of the stem grows faster than the west side, bending the stem westward; at night, the west side grows faster, reorienting the flower eastward before sunrise.
- **Pollination Advantage of Eastward Orientation**: Mature sunflowers stop tracking and lock facing eastward; thermal imaging proves east-facing heads heat up 5x faster in morning sunlight, dramatically increasing bee visits and pollination efficiency.
- **Agronomic Yield Applications**: Understanding the auxin-signaling cascade behind heliotropic light capture informs breeding programs optimizing canopy leaf angles for maximum photosynthetic efficiency.
