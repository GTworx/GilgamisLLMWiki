## Summary

## Analytical Overview
### Mathematical Formalization of Parkinson's Law

In *The Parkinson's Model of Management: Weak Middle Managers in Consulting Companies*, economist Kaushik Bhattacharya provides a formal mathematical and game-theoretic model of C. Northcote Parkinson's famous 1955 satirical observation: *'Work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion.'* Moving beyond satire, Bhattacharya investigates Parkinson's lesser-known second law: **'An official wants to multiply subordinates, not rivals.'**

### The Incompetence Preservation Equilibrium

Bhattacharya constructs an asymmetric-information matching model analyzing hiring dynamics:
- An insecure, marginally competent middle manager faces an existential dilemma when hiring: hiring a brilliant, highly capable subordinate creates an immediate internal rival who may outshine and replace them.
- Consequently, the manager's optimal self-preservation strategy is to recruit two or more subordinates who are demonstrably weaker and less competent than themselves.
- Over successive managerial generations, this generates a cascading pyramidal collapse of organizational competence (the 'Bozo Explosion' famously described by Steve Jobs).

### Counteracting Bureaucratic Degeneracy

Bhattacharya demonstrates that without external meritocratic hiring panels and objective performance benchmarking, bureaucratic enterprises naturally converge on parasitic administrative bloat.
