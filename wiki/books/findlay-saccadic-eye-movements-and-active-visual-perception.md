## Executive Summary

This 105-page 3-part visual neuroscience and computational perception treatise investigates the physiological, computational, and clinical dimensions of human saccadic eye movements. Anchored by Findlay & Walker's landmark Behavioral and Brain Sciences target article on the dual-pathway 'Find-and-Fix' model, the collection explores how active vision constructs a continuous, high-resolution perception of the world despite the eye executing ballistic saccadic jumps 3 to 4 times per second. Contemporary Bayesian models formulate transsaccadic integration as an active inference process where the visual cortex predicts post-saccadic sensations through efference copies and corollary discharge, while clinical studies demonstrate how micro-saccades and fixation dwell times serve as objective diagnostic biomarkers for neurodegenerative and cognitive disorders.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Findlay & Walker's Dual-Pathway Saccade Architecture**: Distinguishes the parallel cortical and subcortical pathways governing 'WHERE' (spatial motor targeting in the superior colliculus) and 'WHEN' (temporal initiation in the frontal eye fields).
- **Transsaccadic Visual Continuity**: Resolves the paradox of visual stability: despite rapid retinal image shifts and saccadic suppression (transient blindness during eye jumps), corollary discharge signals allow the brain to align pre-saccadic predictions with post-saccadic sensory inputs.
- **Bayesian Active Perception**: Models eye movements not as passive cameras, but as active information-gathering sensors directed to minimize Bayesian uncertainty over visual hypothesis spaces.
- **Eye Tracking as Clinical Cognitive Biomarkers**: Demonstrates that gaze entropy, fixation dispersion, and pupil dilation during cognitive puzzle-solving detect early executive function decline and mild cognitive impairment (MCI).
- **Reading, Dyslexia, and Visual Foraging**: Details how saccadic landing positions and regression frequencies reflect instantaneous linguistic parsing and working memory constraints during reading.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations
