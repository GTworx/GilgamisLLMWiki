## Overview
Presented at the IEEE ICEST conference, this technical survey examines the acute security and forensic risks arising from photogrammetric and AI-driven 3D reconstruction of human bodies and faces. The authors investigate how high-fidelity 3D meshes can be harvested, manipulated for non-consensual deepfakes, or reverse-engineered to unlock facial biometric access control systems.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Dissects vulnerability vectors in photogrammetric 3D body scanning and mobile neural reconstruction.
- **Key Finding**: Demonstrates that 3D meshes contain sufficient topological detail to fool physical biometric authentication sensors.
- **Key Finding**: Proposes cryptographic watermarking and differential privacy noise injection for 3D point cloud distribution.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "When users generate high-fidelity 3D digital twins of their physical bodies, they generate an indelible biometric blueprint that, once leaked, can never be revoked or changed."
