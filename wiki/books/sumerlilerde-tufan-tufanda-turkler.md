## Executive Summary

Focusing specifically on the universal myth of the Cataclysmic Deluge, **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ's** *Sümerlilerde Tufan, Tufanda Türkler* (*The Flood among the Sumerians, The Turks in the Flood*) traces the evolution of the flood narrative from its original third-millennium BCE Sumerian cuneiform account (featuring King **Ziusudra** of Shuruppak) through Babylonian **Atrahasis** and **Utnapishtim** (Epic of Gilgamesh), biblical **Noah's Ark** (Genesis), the Qur'an (Surah Hud and Nuh), and crucially compares them with Central Asian Turkic and Altay flood myths recorded by Vasily Radlov and Bahaeddin Ögel (featuring hero **Nama**).

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Cuneiform Priority**: Unmistakably establishes that the biblical and Qur'anic accounts of Noah are direct descendants of the Sumerian Ziusudra deluge text inscribed over 1,500 years earlier.
2. **Parallel Altay Flood Mythology**: Demonstrates remarkable motif parallels in Altay Turkic myths (sending forth birds, ark resting on a sacred summit), suggesting either ancient diffusion or shared memories of post-glacial catastrophic mega-floods (such as the Black Sea deluge).
3. **From Caprice to Morality**: Shows how the original Sumerian story (where gods drown humanity due to overpopulation and noise) was gradually re-theologized into a moral judgment on human wickedness in Abrahamic scriptures.
