## Executive Summary

Recorded and authored by journalist **Turhan Gürkan** from the twelve-year eyewitness memoirs (1927–1938) of Atatürk's personal valet and steward **Cemal (Çelebi) Granda** (Fer Yayınları, İstanbul, 1971, Title Page p. 3; originally serialized in *Şehir Gazetesi* from 4 March to 31 May 1959, p. 4; 246 pp.), *Atatürk'ün Uşağının Gizli Defteri* offers an unmatched ground-level portrait of *"Citizen Mustafa Kemal"* behind the marble curtains of Çankaya, Dolmabahçe, Florya, and the yacht *Savarona*.

As the preface observes (pp. 7–8), great figures are distorted equally by hostile cynics and uncritical idolaters; true greatness is *"ordinary like everyone else up close, yet unmistakable from afar."* Granda's affectionate, candid recollections capture Atatürk's humor, sudden tempers, generous tipping of ordinary staff, late-night dining-table symposia, musical devotion to Rumelian folk songs, insomnia, profound inner solitude, and stoic endurance during his final illness.

---

## Core Biographical Contributions

1. **Humanizing the Founder**: Replaces bronze-statue hagiography with vivid daily vignettes of domestic life, protocol breaches, intellectual debates with scholars and politicians, and unguarded kindness toward servants and villagers.
2. **Oral-History Micro-Witness**: Preserves dialogue, culinary habits, travel episodes, and interpersonal dynamics among Atatürk's inner circle (İsmet İnönü, Fevzi Çakmak, Kılıç Ali, Salih Bozok, Afet İnan) rarely documented in official state archives.
3. **Psychological Cost of State-Building**: Illuminates the exhausting emotional toll and personal loneliness borne by a revolutionary leader racing against declining health to secure a young republic's future.
