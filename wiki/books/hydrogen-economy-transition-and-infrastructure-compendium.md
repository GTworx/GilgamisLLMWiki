## Executive Summary
Spanning five foundational treatises across two decades (2001–2023) and over 480 pages, this compendium unites UNEP's early baseline assessments, Ulf Bossel and Baldur Eliasson's seminal thermodynamic critique of hydrogen round-trip efficiencies, the International Energy Agency's (IEA) landmark 2019 *The Future of Hydrogen* report prepared for the G20, and modern 2023 infrastructure transition blueprints. The research examines the complete hydrogen value chain: green electrolysis (PEM and alkaline), blue hydrogen with carbon capture (SMR-CCS), geological salt cavern storage, liquid organic hydrogen carriers (LOHC), ammonia synthesis for maritime shipping, and fuel-cell heavy transport. The compendium balances techno-optimism with unforgiving second-law thermodynamic realities regarding energy conversion losses.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Thermodynamic Realities of Round-Trip Efficiency**: Bossel & Eliasson demonstrate that the well-to-wheel round-trip efficiency of hydrogen (electricity to hydrogen, compression, transport, and back to electricity via fuel cell) hovers at 25–35%, compared to 75–85% for direct battery electrification.
- **Strategic Hard-to-Abate Use Cases**: Rather than passenger vehicles or residential heating, green hydrogen delivers maximum decarbonization leverage in chemical feedstocks (ammonia, methanol), steelmaking (direct reduced iron), and long-duration seasonal grid storage.
- **The Levelized Cost Horizon**: Reaching cost parity with fossil-derived gray hydrogen ($1.50–$2.00/kg) requires electrolyzer capital cost reductions of 70% and continuous access to ultra-cheap renewable electricity (<$20/MWh).
- **Global Transport & Ammonia Vectors**: Liquid hydrogen requires cryogenic cooling to -253°C; transforming hydrogen into green ammonia (NH3) offers superior volumetric energy density and leverages existing global fertilizer maritime infrastructure.
