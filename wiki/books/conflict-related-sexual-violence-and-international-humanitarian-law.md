## Executive Summary
This 18-page human rights and international humanitarian law (IHL) monograph examines the systematic deployment of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) as a deliberate, strategic weapon of modern warfare. Grounded in jurisprudence from the International Criminal Tribunals for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) and Rwanda (ICTR) and Rome Statute Article 7/8 classifications, the paper analyzes how military and paramilitary commanders weaponize sexual assault to inflict demographic terror, force ethnic displacement, destroy social cohesion, and humiliate civilian populations. The treatise audits evidentiary standards, survivor-centered medical protocols, and the legal codification of sexual violence as a war crime and crime against humanity.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Sexual Violence as Strategic Military Doctrine**: Dispels the myth of sexual violence as an accidental 'byproduct' of undisciplined troops; demonstrates it is frequently an orchestrated tactic ordered by military leadership to terrorize populations.
- **Jurisprudential Evolution (Akayesu to Rome Statute)**: Traces the landmark 1998 Jean-Paul Akayesu ruling establishing that sexual violence constitutes an act of genocide when committed with intent to destroy a group.
- **Trauma, Stigma and Social Fragmentation**: Examines the multi-generational psychological, physical, and socio-economic trauma inflicted on survivors, including forced pregnancies and familial ostracization.
- **Command Responsibility and Accountability**: Highlights the urgent need to prosecute commanding officers under the doctrine of command responsibility for failing to prevent or punish sexual violence committed by subordinates.
