## Executive Summary

First written while Robert L. Heilbroner was a graduate student in 1953 and revised across forty-six years through this definitive **Seventh Edition (1999)** (pp. 4, 6–8), *The Worldly Philosophers* is the most widely read intellectual history of economic thought ever published. Coining the phrase "worldly philosophers" to describe thinkers who sought not cosmic metaphysics but the hidden laws governing everyday material survival, Heilbroner brings economic ideas to life through vivid biographical portraits—showing how each paradigm emerged from the concrete crises of its own historical era.

## Historical Progression of Economic Thought (Contents, p. 8)

1. **The Economic Revolution (Chapter II)**: Explains why economics did not exist before the emergence of the modern market system—when **tradition** and **command** gave way to land, labor, and capital priced as commodities.
2. **Classical Foundations (Chapters III–IV)**: **Adam Smith** (dynamic harmony of self-interest and competition), **Thomas Malthus** (population vs. subsistence), and **David Ricardo** (comparative advantage and class distribution of rent).
3. **Critics & Utopians (Chapters V–VII)**: Utopian reformers (Robert Owen, Saint-Simon, Fourier, J. S. Mill), **Karl Marx's** dialectical anatomy of capitalist crisis, and the Victorian "underworld" (Henry George, Hobson, Edgeworth).
4. **Twentieth-Century Transformations (Chapters VIII–X)**: **Thorstein Veblen's** satirical *Theory of the Leisure Class* ("conspicuous consumption"), **John Maynard Keynes's** macroeconomic revolution rescuing capitalism from the Great Depression, and **Joseph Schumpeter's** dynamic vision of entrepreneurial **creative destruction**.
5. **The End of Worldly Philosophy? (Chapter XI)**: Warns in the 7th edition that hyper-mathematized modern economics risks severing itself from historical, political, and moral vision.
