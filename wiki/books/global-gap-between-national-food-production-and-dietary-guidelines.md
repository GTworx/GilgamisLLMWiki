## Executive Summary
Published in 2025 by an international team of public health nutritionists and agricultural economists, this comprehensive 28-page planetary health study quantifies the systemic misalignment between current national food production outputs and nutritional dietary guidelines (such as the EAT-Lancet Planetary Health Diet). Analyzing food balance sheets across 180 countries, the authors demonstrate that global agriculture drastically overproduces refined grains, sugars, and red meat, while underproducing fruits, vegetables, legumes, and nuts by 30–50% relative to human nutritional needs. The paper models the ecological and land-use consequences of rebalancing national food production to match healthy dietary standards.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Global Agronomic Maladaptation**: The global food system currently produces enough calories to feed 10 billion people, but produces a composition that drives skyrocketing rates of metabolic syndrome, diabetes, and cardiovascular mortality.
- **The Fruit and Vegetable Deficit**: Global production of fruits and vegetables falls 34% short of minimum dietary guidelines; meeting nutritional targets requires converting millions of acres from livestock feed crops to horticulture.
- **Land and Water Synergies**: Shifting diets toward whole grains, legumes, and plant proteins would free up over 1 billion hectares of global pastureland and reduce agricultural freshwater withdrawals by 20%.
- **Policy and Agricultural Subsidy Distortions**: Massive government agricultural subsidies disproportionately artificially cheapen grain monocultures and feed crops, disincentivizing diversified horticultural farming.
