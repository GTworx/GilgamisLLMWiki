## Overview
This 4-study philological compendium—featuring critical monographs by renowned Turkologists Ahmet Bican Ercilasun, Tuncer Gülensoy, and DTCF scholars—investigates the principles, pitfalls, and pseudo-scientific aberrations in Turkish etymological research. The authors deconstruct famous linguistic disputes (such as whether 'okul' derives from Old Turkic *okı-* or French *école*, and the Byzantine Greek origin of 'İstanbul' from *eis tēn polin*), while warning against nationalist folk etymologies.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Distinguishes scientific historical phonology from naive 'folk etymology' (halk etimolojisi) based on accidental phonetic resemblances.
- **Key Finding**: Conducts a comprehensive review of the -ay suffix in Old and Modern Turkic word formation.
- **Key Finding**: Resolves classic cultural debates regarding Ottoman administrative loans, Greek toponyms, and 1930s Language Reform neologisms.
