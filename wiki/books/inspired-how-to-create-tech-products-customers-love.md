## Executive Summary

Written by Silicon Valley Product Group founder and former eBay/Netscape product executive **Marty Cagan** (2nd revised edition, 2018, Wiley, pp. 11–12), *Inspired* is widely regarded as the bible of modern technology product management. Contrasting top-tier product companies (Amazon, Apple, Google, Netflix, Tesla) against struggling traditional enterprises, Cagan diagnoses why most corporate software initiatives fail: rigid **feature-roadmap "feature factories"** that hand pre-specified requirements down to engineering teams and measure output rather than validated customer outcomes (Part I, Chapters 1–8, TOC).

Instead, Cagan lays out a comprehensive blueprint for **empowered, cross-functional product teams** (Product Manager, Product Designer, and Engineers working side by side) practicing **continuous product discovery** alongside continuous delivery—rapidly testing prototypes to retire the **four big product risks** (*value, usability, feasibility, and business viability*) *before* writing expensive production code.

---

## Core Product Operating Model

1. **Product Discovery vs. Delivery**: Separates *figuring out what is worth building* (cheap, rapid customer prototypes and experiments) from *building scalable production software* (reliable engineering execution).
2. **Empowered Product Teams vs. Feature Teams**: Gives durable missionary teams hard business/customer problems to solve (via OKRs) rather than lists of stakeholder-mandated features to ship.
3. **The Four Risks**: Requires collaborative validation across **Value** (will customers buy/choose it?), **Usability** (can users figure it out?), **Feasibility** (can engineers build it?), and **Viability** (does it work for legal, sales, finance, and strategy?).
4. **Beyond Waterfall-Disguised-as-Agile**: Exposes how most corporate "Agile" transformations merely wrap Scrum sprints around legacy waterfall roadmaps without empowering product discovery.
