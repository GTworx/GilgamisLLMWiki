## Summary
Rigorous chronological investigation of U.S.–Mexico territorial confrontations, from early Spanish-American colonial buffer zones and the Texas Revolution (1836) through the Mexican-American War and Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo.

## Analytical Overview
Details the geopolitical annexation of Texas, President Polk's border provocations along the Rio Grande, General Winfield Scott's Mexico City campaign, and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) which severed 55% of Mexico's national territory. Follows border security dynamics through the Gadsden Purchase, Pancho Villa's 1916 Columbus raid, and Pershing's Punitive Expedition.
