Foundational 1988 economic paper in Review of Economic Studies mathematically modeling political budget cycles under rational expectations, proving that incumbent politicians manipulate fiscal transfers before elections to signal administrative competency.

## Analytical Overview
### Game-Theoretic Foundations of the Political Business Cycle

In *Elections and Macroeconomic Policy Cycles*, published in *The Review of Economic Studies* (1988), prominent macroeconomists Kenneth Rogoff and Anne Sibert overturn William Nordhaus's traditional opportunistic political business cycle model by demonstrating that electoral fiscal cycles persist even when voters have **rational expectations**.

### The Asymmetric Information Signaling Game

Rogoff and Sibert construct a micro-founded, game-theoretic signaling model:
- **Competency Asymmetry:** Incumbent politicians possess private information regarding their own administrative competency—specifically, their efficiency in delivering public goods per unit of tax revenue.
- **Pre-Electoral Fiscal Distortion:** To signal high competency to voters before an election, the incumbent cuts taxes or expands visible transfer payments, financing expenditures through hidden post-electoral debt or inflationary borrowing.
- **Rational Voter Dilemma:** Because voters cannot instantly distinguish between genuine administrative competency and temporary deficit-financed distortion, they rationally vote for the incumbent, generating systematic political budget cycles.

### Academic Legacy

Rogoff and Sibert's model established the modern neoclassical political economy of public finance, providing the theoretical benchmark for analyzing pre-electoral stimulus across global democracies.
