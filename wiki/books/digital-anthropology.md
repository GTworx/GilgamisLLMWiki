## Executive Summary

*Digital Anthropology* (published by Berg / Bloomsbury Publishing, London & New York, 2012, 327 pp., ISBN `978-0-85785-290-8`; paired in `BOOKS/Anthropology_/` with the 27-page standalone offprint of Chapter 1, *The Digital and the Human: A Prospectus for Digital Anthropology*, `DOI: 10.4324/9781003085201-2`) edited by anthropologists [[heather-a-horst]] and [[daniel-miller]] is the foundational manifesto establishing **digital anthropology** as a rigorous global sub-discipline (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, pp. 4–7, 14–17; BOOKS/Anthropology_/MillerHorst.2012.DigitaltheHumanDigitalAnthropology.pdf, p. 1). Defining the "digital" strictly as **everything reducible to binary code (0s and 1s)**—analogous to Keith Hart's anthropological treatment of **money** as both universalized abstraction and differentiated cultural particularity—Miller and Horst demolish the romantic myth that pre-digital or face-to-face life was somehow "more authentic" or "more real" than digitally mediated humanity (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, ch. 1, pp. 14–17).

Organized across five parts and fourteen chapters by leading scholars—including Tom Boellstorff, [[lane-denicola]], Faye Ginsburg, Stefana Broadbent, John Postill, Jelena Karanović, Bart Barendregt, and Jo Tacchi—the volume anchors digital research on **six foundational principles**: (1) the digital intensifies the dialectical nature of culture; (2) humanity is not one iota less mediated before the digital; (3) commitment to **anthropological holism**; (4) **cultural relativism** across global digital worlds (from Islamic pop media to Trinidadian Facebook); (5) essential **ambiguity** of openness vs. closure (free software, surveillance, governance); and (6) **materiality** of digital infrastructure, devices, and physical space (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, Contents & Ch. 1–14, pp. 6–7, 14–317).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Part I — Introduction: Six Principles for Digital Anthropology (Ch. 1, pp. 14–47)**:
  * *The Digital and the Human*: Proposes the six foundational principles; shows how digital platforms expose the already-framed nature of analog culture, draws Keith Hart's structural parallel between binary code and money, and insists on studying digital artifacts as deeply material, localized cultural practices rather than disembodied cyberspace (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, ch. 1, pp. 14–17).

* **Part II — Positioning Digital Anthropology (Ch. 2–4, pp. 48–109)**:
  * *Virtuality, Everyday Media & Geomedia*: Tom Boellstorff rethinks the virtual/actual boundary (Ch. 2); Heather Horst examines new media embedded in domestic everyday life (Ch. 3); and [[lane-denicola]] analyzes **Geomedia**—how GPS, GIS, and location-based networks reassert physical geography within digital culture (Ch. 4) (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, Contents, p. 6).

* **Part III — Socializing Digital Anthropology (Ch. 5–7, pp. 110–173)**:
  * *Disability, Intimate Communication & Social Networks*: Faye Ginsburg explores digital accessibility and disability activism (Ch. 5); Stefana Broadbent maps intimate personal-communication channels (Ch. 6); and Daniel Miller analyzes cross-cultural appropriations of Social Networking Sites (Ch. 7) (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, Contents, p. 6).

* **Parts IV–V — Politicizing & Designing Digital Anthropology (Ch. 8–14, pp. 174–317)**:
  * *Digital Politics, Free Software, Design & Gaming*: Examines digital activism (John Postill, Ch. 8), **Free Software and the politics of sharing** (Jelena Karanović, Ch. 9), diverse non-Western digital worlds (Bart Barendregt, Ch. 10), development voice (Jo Tacchi, Ch. 11), **Design Anthropology**, digital museums, and video-game design precursors (Ch. 12–14) (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, Contents, pp. 6–7).

## Key Quotes & Citations

* "Digital anthropology will progress to the degree that the digital enables us to understand and exposes the framed nature of analogue or predigital life as culture and fails when we fall victim to a broader and romanticized discourse that presupposes a greater authenticity or reality to the predigital." (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, ch. 1, p. 4 / PDF p. 15)
* "The commitment to holism, the foundation of anthropological perspectives on humanity, represents a third principle." (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, ch. 1, p. 4 / PDF p. 15)
* "This introduction will propose six basic principles as the foundation for a new sub-discipline: digital anthropology... its larger purpose is to spread the widest possible canvas upon which to begin the creative work of new research and thinking." (source: BOOKS/Anthropology_/Digital-Anthropology-edited-by-Heather-A.-Horst-and-Daniel-Miller.pdf, ch. 1, p. 3 / PDF p. 14)
