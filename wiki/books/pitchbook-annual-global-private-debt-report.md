## Executive Summary

PitchBook's **Annual Global Private Debt Report** documents the historic expansion of the **private credit asset class into a $1.7+ trillion global powerhouse**. Catalyzed by regional banking stress (Silicon Valley Bank, Signature Bank) and regulatory capital constraints (Basel III Endgame) that caused traditional commercial banks to retreat from leveraged lending, non-bank direct lenders (Ares, Blue Owl, Blackstone Credit, Oaktree) stepped in to finance multi-billion-dollar sponsor-backed buyouts. The report benchmarks fundraising, direct lending terms, unitranche structures, interest coverage ratios, and default rates, evaluating whether private debt's customized covenants provide genuine systemic stability or obscure latent credit risk through Payment-in-Kind (PIK) forbearance.

---

## Key Strategic & Financial Takeaways

- **Explosive Growth of Direct Lending**: Direct lending cemented its position as the largest sub-strategy within private debt, capturing over 60% of total capital raised; direct lenders routinely underwrote single-tranche unitranche loans exceeding $2 billion to finance PE buyouts without bank syndication syndicates.
- **Yield Attractiveness and LP Demand**: Floating-rate senior secured direct loans delivered net yields between 11% and 13% (SOFR + 550–650 bps), attracting massive inflows from institutional LPs and high-net-worth retail platforms seeking equity-like returns with senior debt protection.
- **Interest Coverage Compression**: Higher SOFR rates compressed portfolio company interest coverage ratios (ICRs) toward 1.5x–1.8x, eroding free cash flow margins and testing borrower debt-service capacities.
- **The Rise of Payment-in-Kind (PIK) Amendments**: Direct lenders increasingly agreed to synthetic amendments allowing stressed borrowers to pay interest via additional debt (PIK) rather than cash, suppressing headline default rates while increasing terminal leverage.
- **Regulatory Scrutiny of Non-Bank Financial Intermediation (NBFI)**: Examines regulatory concerns from the IMF, Fed, and Bank of England that private credit lacks transparent secondary market mark-to-market pricing, creating potential systemic opacity during severe macro downturns.
