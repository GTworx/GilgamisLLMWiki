## Executive Summary

*Global Ethics for Leadership: Values and Virtues for Life* (published by Globethics.net in Geneva as *Globethics.net Global No. 13*, 2016, 443 pp., ISBN 978-2-88931-123-1) edited by Globethics.net Founder [[christoph-stuckelberger]], Ambassador [[walter-fust]], and Executive Director [[obiora-ike]] is a 32-chapter cross-cultural anthology authored by scholars and practitioners from four continents addressing a central dilemma of the 21st century: how to reconcile **universal global values** (such as the UN 2030 Sustainable Development Goals and Universal Declaration of Human Rights) with **contextual cultural, religious, and national identities** without collapsing into either neo-colonial uniformity or fundamentalist/relativist fragmentation (source: BOOKS/Ethics/2016_Globalethics_Global_13_web.pdf, pp. 1–11).

Structured into four parts, the volume first establishes the relational paradigm of **'Globally Responsible and Contextually Rooted'** ethics (Part 1, Ch. 1–3). Part 2 examines **Twelve Core Values in Leadership**—*Justice, Non-Violence, Sustainability, Responsibility, Freedom, Liberation, Empowerment, Equal Rights, Participation, Peace in Diversity, Sharing, Solidarity,* and *Trust* (Ch. 4–17). Part 3 explores **Twelve Personal Virtues in Leadership**—*Integrity, Openness, Vision, Reliability, Compassion ('The Mother of All Virtues'), Gratitude, Modesty, Forgiveness, Patience, Courage, Love,* and *Hope* (Ch. 18–29). Part 4 applies these values and virtues to corporate governance, religious organizations, and international multilateral institutions (Ch. 30–32) (source: BOOKS/Ethics/2016_Globalethics_Global_13_web.pdf, pp. 6–11, 15–428).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Part 1 (Chapters 1–3): Balancing Global and Contextual Ethics & The UN SDGs 2030 (pp. 15–60)**:
  * *Relational Global Ethics*: [[walter-fust]] details how value-based knowledge management transforms institutional culture (pp. 15–24); [[christoph-stuckelberger]] maps future global scenarios between nationalism and relativism via the 'Global and Contextual' tree metaphor (roots in local culture, crown in shared global sky, pp. 25–42); and [[obiora-ike]] links core ethical values to the 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals (pp. 43–60) (source: BOOKS/Ethics/2016_Globalethics_Global_13_web.pdf, pp. 6, 15–60).

* **Part 2 (Chapters 4–17): Twelve Core Societal Values in Leadership (pp. 61–224)**:
  * *From Justice and Non-Violence to Solidarity and Trust*: Contributions from South Africa, India, Russia, Latin America, Great Britain, Pakistan, China, and Switzerland examine how societal benchmarks—Justice, Non-Violence, Sustainability, Empowerment, Participation, Peace in Diversity, Sharing, Solidarity, and Trust—operate as institutional compasses across business and statecraft (source: BOOKS/Ethics/2016_Globalethics_Global_13_web.pdf, pp. 6–7, 61–224).

* **Part 3 (Chapters 18–29): Twelve Character Virtues in Leadership (pp. 225–386)**:
  * *Virtue Ethics as the Engine of Value Realization*: Distinguishes institutional *values* (shared societal orientation points) from personal *virtues* (internalized character dispositions that give leaders the strength to live those values under pressure), analyzing Integrity, Vision, Reliability, Compassion, Gratitude, Modesty, Forgiveness, Courage, and Agape Love (source: BOOKS/Ethics/2016_Globalethics_Global_13_web.pdf, pp. 8, 11, 225–386).

* **Part 4 (Chapters 30–32): Implementation in Business, Religious & International Organizations (pp. 387–428)**:
  * *Institutionalizing Ethics*: Examines anti-corruption compliance and stakeholder ethics in multinational corporations, interfaith peacebuilding in religious networks (Katherine Marshall), and ethical accountability in international organizations (Joan Elise Dubinsky) (source: BOOKS/Ethics/2016_Globalethics_Global_13_web.pdf, pp. 9, 387–428).
