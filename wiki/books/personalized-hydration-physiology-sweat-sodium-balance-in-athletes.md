## Executive Summary
Published in *Nutrients* (2024; 16:1341), this 13-page comprehensive sports medicine and exercise physiology study investigates the science and clinical application of personalized hydration protocols for endurance athletes and occupational tactical personnel.

The study demonstrates that monolithic hydration guidelines ('drink 8 glasses a day' or fixed hourly fluid targets) are physiologically obsolete and dangerous. The authors present evidence-based methodologies for quantifying individual sweat rate (L/hour) and sweat sodium concentration ($[Na^+]$ ranging widely from 20 to over 80 mmol/L). They model how sweat rate and electrolyte loss vary based on ambient temperature, humidity, exercise intensity, and individual genetic CFTR/ENaC channel expression, formulating guidelines to prevent both performance-degrading hypohydration (>2% body mass loss) and lethal exercise-associated hyponatremia (EAH) caused by water overconsumption.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **High Inter-Individual Electrolyte Variability**: Documents that sweat sodium loss varies by over 400% between athletes under identical thermal conditions, necessitating individualized sodium replacement plans.
- **The Lethal Risk of Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia (EAH)**: Explains the physiological etiology of EAH, where over-drinking plain water during prolonged exercise dilutes plasma sodium, causing cerebral edema, seizures, and death.
- **Wearable Microfluidic Sweat Sensing**: Explores emerging skin-interfaced microfluidic patches that analyze sweat rate and chloride/sodium concentrations in real time via colorimetric or electrochemical telemetry.
- **Carbohydrate-Electrolyte Solution Optimization**: Details ideal osmolarity, glucose/fructose ratios, and sodium concentrations for intestinal SGLT-1 co-transporter water absorption during extreme physical exertion.
