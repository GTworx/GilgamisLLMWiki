## Summary
Compendium on the new space economy, detailing commercial space launch economics, private venture capital flows into orbital infrastructure, heavy-lift launch cadence, and international aerospace cooperation under the Kármán Project.

## Analytical Overview
The space industry is undergoing a historic structural revolution driven by reusable rocket architectures, dramatic reductions in launch costs to low-Earth orbit (LEO), and the proliferation of private capital. This volume compiles strategic venture market analyses and diplomatic aerospace frameworks mapping the emerging orbital economy. Anchoring the collection are PitchBook’s *Aerospace & Defense Launch Reports* and *Commercial Space Launch Briefs*, tracking private venture investment across launch providers (SpaceX, Rocket Lab, Relativity Space), commercial Earth observation constellations, and direct-to-cell satellite networks.

The PitchBook reports demonstrate how the radical collapse in cost-per-kilogram to orbit—falling from over $10,000/kg during the Space Shuttle era to sub-$1,500/kg on Falcon 9 and heading toward sub-$100/kg on next-generation super-heavy architectures—has fundamentally altered space business economics. The report models the emergence of secondary in-space markets that were previously economically unviable: orbital microgravity pharmaceutical crystallization, high-purity ZBLAN optical fiber manufacturing in vacuum, active debris removal, and on-orbit satellite servicing and refueling.

These commercial dynamics are paired with the diplomatic and governance frameworks of The Kármán Project. As commercial mega-constellations crowd orbital shells and geopolitical rivalry intensifies between the U.S. Artemis Accords and the Sino-Russian International Lunar Research Station (ILRS), the Kármán Fellowship program brings together global aerospace leaders, satellite operators, and policymakers to establish international consensus on space situational awareness (SSA), space traffic management, orbital debris mitigation, and the peaceful stewardship of the extraterrestrial commons.
