## Executive Summary

Conducted by the Open Source Security Foundation (OpenSSF) and the Linux Foundation, this comprehensive 64-page global survey delivers a sobering empirical audit of **secure software development education across academia and industry**. Despite software supply-chain attacks increasing exponentially (exemplified by SolarWinds, Log4j, and the XZ Utils backdoor), the report reveals that the vast majority of university computer science and software engineering programs still mandate zero coursework in cybersecurity or secure coding practices. The survey benchmarks current developer training deficiencies, corporate onboarding failures, and the adoption of secure software development frameworks (such as NIST SSDF and OpenSSF Scorecards), proposing systemic reforms to embed security into foundational computer science education.

---

## Key Strategic & Architectural Takeaways

- **Academic Curricular Failure**: Fewer than 30% of surveyed computer science graduates were required to take a single secure coding course; universities prioritize algorithm efficiency and rapid feature development over defensive design and vulnerability prevention.
- **The Open-Source Supply Chain Blindspot**: Modern enterprise software stacks consist of 80% to 90% open-source components; developers routinely ingest unvetted third-party npm, PyPI, and GitHub packages without understanding upstream dependency risks.
- **The XZ Utils and Log4j Wake-Up Call**: Examines how sophisticated nation-state actors exploit maintainer burnout and lack of automated security tooling to inject multi-year latent backdoors into foundational open-source utilities.
- **Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) Adoption**: Benchmarks industry progress in generating and enforcing machine-readable SBOMs (SPDX, CycloneDX) to achieve real-time visibility into transitive software dependencies.
- **OpenSSF Educational Frameworks**: Highlights the impact of free, community-driven training (such as OpenSSF's Developing Secure Software course) and automated GitHub CI/CD integration tools in elevating developer security hygiene.
