## Executive Summary
Authored by Jeremy Lockwood and leading vertebrate paleontologists in 2025, this comprehensive 36-page anatomical monograph investigates the evolutionary origins, biomechanics, and functional morphology of extreme hyper-elongated neural spines in Cretaceous iguanodontian dinosaurs (such as *Ouranosaurus nigeriensis* and *Brighstoneus simmondsi*). Using 3D micro-computed tomography, bone histology, and comparative biomechanical finite element modeling, the authors evaluate competing hypotheses for the 'sail' or 'hump': thermoregulation, mechanical muscle attachment for dorsal stiffness, fat/energy storage, and sexual/social display. Histological vascular patterns prove the structures functioned primarily as visual signaling billboards for species recognition and sexual selection.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Bone Histology of Neural Spines**: Cross-sectional vascular histology shows sparse, dense cortical bone with low vascularity at spine tips, refuting active thermoregulatory heat-exchange hypotheses (unlike *Spinosaurus* or *Dimetrodon*).
- **Sexual Selection and Visual Display**: The massive dorsal sails served primarily as prominent visual signaling structures for intra-specific display, mating dominance, and species recognition in densely populated Early Cretaceous river deltas.
- **Biomechanical Load Distribution**: Finite element analysis indicates that baseline neural spine elongation provided enhanced mechanical anchorage for epaxial musculature, stabilizing long vertebral columns during bipedal and quadrupedal transitions.
- **Convergent Evolution Across Archosaurs**: Documents repeated independent evolutionary origins of dorsal neural sails across multiple disparate dinosaurian and synapsid clades during warm greenhouse climates.
