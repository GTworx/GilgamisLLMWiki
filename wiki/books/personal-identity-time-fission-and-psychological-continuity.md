## Overview
This two-part philosophical treatise on personal identity brings together John Perry's cutting-edge work on time, fission, and branching persons alongside Marya Schechtman's definitive Routledge Encyclopedia monograph on narrative identity. The authors evaluate Lockean memory continuity, Parfit's Bundle Theory, and narrative self-constitution under radical hypothetical thought experiments.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Deconstructs the classic teletransportation and brain-fission paradoxes: what survives when psychological continuity branches?
- **Key Finding**: Contrasts psychological reductionism (Parfit) with Schechtman's 'Narrative Self-Constitution View'.
- **Key Finding**: Explores the ethical stakes of personal identity: moral accountability, anticipatory fear of death, and long-term commitments.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Personal identity is not merely a biological fact; it is a normative narrative structure through which a person organizes their memories, desires, and responsibilities into a unified life."
