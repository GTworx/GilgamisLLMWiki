## Analytical Overview
### The Architectural Invention of the Pure-Play Foundry

*He Turned 55, Then Started the World's Most Important Company*, published in the *Wall Street Journal's* Science of Success series, delivers a profound biographical and business case study of Morris Chang, the visionary founder of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC).

### Late-Career Entrepreneurship and Structural Insight

Chang's founding of TSMC in 1987 at the age of 55 decisively challenges modern startup mythologies:
- After a stellar 25-year career rising to Group Vice President at Texas Instruments, Chang possessed deep technical and operational mastery of semiconductor physics, manufacturing yield curves, and corporate politics.
- When invited by the Taiwanese government to build a domestic technology industry, Chang realized that Taiwan had no competitive advantage in semiconductor design, sales, or marketing.
- His radical insight was to create a **Pure-Play Foundry** that would *never design its own chips*, guaranteeing customers (such as Nvidia, Apple, Qualcomm, and AMD) that TSMC would never compete with them.

### Enabling the Fabless Semiconductor Revolution

By decoupling chip design from the staggering capital expenditure of manufacturing fabrication, TSMC enabled the entire modern fabless semiconductor ecosystem. Chang's relentless focus on manufacturing yield, extreme capital reinvestment, and customer trust transformed a sleepy Taiwanese startup into the indispensable physical linchpin of global civilization.
