## Executive Summary
This 9-page critical oncological review separates popular metabolic myth from rigorous scientific reality regarding the use of periodic fasting, water-only fasting, and fasting-mimicking diets (FMD) in clinical cancer treatment.

The paper investigates the molecular mechanisms of 'Differential Stress Resistance' (DSR) and 'Differential Stress Sensitization' (DSS) discovered by Valter Longo and colleagues. During fasting, normal healthy somatic cells downregulate nutrient-sensing pathways (IGF-1, mTOR, PKA) and redirect energy toward cellular maintenance, DNA repair, and autophagy, rendering them resistant to chemotherapy toxicity; in contrast, cancer cells carrying oncogenic mutations (PI3K, RAS, MYC) fail to downregulate proliferation, starving of glucose and becoming hyper-vulnerable to oxidative damage and chemotherapeutic death. The authors review emerging Phase II clinical trials combining FMD with standard platinum and taxane chemotherapy, while strictly warning against unmonitored fasting causing cachexia in advanced patients.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Differential Stress Resistance (DSR)**: Explains the evolutionary biochemical divergence where nutrient deprivation shields normal tissues from cytotoxic therapy while selectively sensitizing malignant cells.
- **Downregulation of the IGF-1/mTOR Axis**: Documents dramatic drops in circulating glucose, insulin, and IGF-1 levels during 48-to-72-hour fasting cycles, stripping tumors of primary glycolytic fuel.
- **Immune Rejuvenation and Tumor Clearance**: Details how periodic fasting cycles induce apoptosis in damaged lymphocytes followed by hematopoietic stem cell activation, boosting T-cell-mediated tumor infiltration.
- **Clinical Contraindications and Cachexia**: Strictly differentiates between monitored, cyclical fasting-mimicking diets and lethal unmonitored caloric deprivation that accelerates sarcopenia in frail oncology patients.
