## Executive Summary
Produced by the MIT Energy Initiative in collaboration with the Institute for Research in Technology (IIT-Comillas), this monumental 376-page treatise serves as the international benchmark on electrical utility reform. Led by Ignacio Pérez-Arriaga and Christopher Knittel, the study investigates how distributed energy resources (DERs)—rooftop solar, behind-the-meter batteries, flexible smart appliances, and EV chargers—disrupt traditional centralized utility cost-recovery models. The authors formulate a comprehensive framework for cost-reflective network tariffs, locational marginal pricing (LMP), and performance-based regulation (PBR) to prevent cross-subsidies between solar adopters and low-income rate-payers.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Death Spiral of Volumetric Tariffs**: Traditional per-kWh volumetric tariffs create economic distortion; as affluent consumers adopt rooftop solar, fixed distribution network costs are shifted onto non-solar customers, driving further grid defection.
- **Cost-Reflective Tariff Architecture**: Advocates for three-part electricity billing: a fixed customer service charge, a capacity demand charge (peak kW contribution), and a dynamic, location-specific volumetric charge reflecting hourly wholesale energy costs.
- **Distribution System Operator (DSO) Evolution**: Distribution utilities must transition from passive wire maintainers into active market coordinators (DSOs) orchestrating bilateral DER dispatch and congestion relief.
- **Locational Value of DERs**: The economic value of distributed solar and storage varies by orders of magnitude depending on local substation transformer capacity and feeder line congestion.
