## Summary
The critical edition and English translation by Max Meyerhof and Joseph Schacht of the 13th-century philosophical and theological novel *Risalat Fadil ibn Natiq* (*Theologus Autodidactus*) by Syrian polymath Ibn al-Nafis. Written in response to Ibn Tufayl's *Hayy ibn Yaqzan*, the work explores spontaneous generation, autonomous rational epistemology, Islamic eschatology, and contains Ibn al-Nafis's historic medical discovery of pulmonary transit (lesser circulation) centuries before William Harvey and Michael Servetus.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
Ala al-Din Abu al-Hassan Ali ibn Abi al-Hazm al-Qarshi al-Dimashqi, known as Ibn al-Nafis (1213–1288 CE), was Chief Physician of the Nasiri Hospital in Cairo during the Mamluk Sultanate. A master of Galenic medicine, jurisprudence, and theology, he wrote *Theologus Autodidactus* between 1268 and 1277 CE. The manuscript was rediscovered and edited in the 20th century by orientalists Max Meyerhof and Joseph Schacht, confirming Ibn al-Nafis as the true discoverer of pulmonary circulation.

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
The narrative follows Kamil, a boy spontaneously generated on an isolated desert island, who through pure unaided reason deduces the laws of physics, biology, theology, and astronomy. Unlike Ibn Tufayl's mystical solitude, Kamil eventually encounters human society, deduces the necessity of prophecy and divine law for civil order, and rationally analyzes the physical resurrection of the body. Within his anatomical arguments, Ibn al-Nafis refutes Galen and Avicenna, demonstrating that the interventricular septum of the heart is completely solid and devoid of invisible pores; consequently, blood must pass from the right ventricle to the lungs via the pulmonary artery, mix with air, and return to the left ventricle via the pulmonary vein.

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
The edition documents Ibn al-Nafis's explicit anatomical descriptions: 1) The absolute impermeability of the cardiac septum; 2) The pulmonary capillary bed transit where venous blood transforms into spirituous arterial blood; and 3) The coronary circulation supplying nourishment to the heart muscle itself. These anatomical discoveries, formulated in Cairo around 1242–1270 CE, antedated Michael Servetus (*Christianismi Restitutio*, 1553) and Realdo Colombo (1559) by nearly three centuries.

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
Ibn al-Nafis's work is an intellectual triumph that combines visionary physiological observation with philosophical science-fiction storytelling. The Meyerhof-Schacht edition provides definitive philological and historical proof of one of the greatest medical breakthroughs of Islamic Golden Age science.
