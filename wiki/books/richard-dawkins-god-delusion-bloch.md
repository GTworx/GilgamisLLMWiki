## Executive Summary

*Richard Dawkins' God Delusion* (Polish original 2011; English translation 2014, Flavius Publishing House, Warsaw, pp. 3–4) by Polish philosopher-apologist **Paweł Bloch** is a systematic, point-by-point philosophical, ethical, and historical rebuttal to Richard Dawkins's *The God Delusion*. Entering the timeless dialogue between theism and atheism (p. 8), Bloch argues that Dawkins repeatedly commits category errors—treating philosophical metaphysics as if it were empirical biology, misrepresenting classical Thomistic arguments (such as confusing finite biological complexity with divine simplicity in the "infinite regress" critique), and applying inconsistent moral standards when blaming Christianity for historical violence while overlooking the implications of strict "moral Darwinism" (pp. 5–7).

---

## Five-Chapter Rebuttal Structure (Contents, pp. 5–7)

1. **Chapter 1 — Science and God (pp. 14–45)**: Interrogates Dawkins's epistemological claims, dissecting Russell's Teapot, the burden of proving non-existence, the infinite-regress/Boeing 747 argument, and the methodological boundaries of natural science (p. 5).
2. **Chapter 2 — Morality: Atheistic Darwinism vs. Christian Ethics (pp. 46–85)**: Contrasts moral absolutism against evolutionary relativism across bioethical flashpoints including abortion and euthanasia, questioning whether strict Darwinian naturalism can ground objective human dignity—and distinguishing "inconsistent atheism" (which borrows Judeo-Christian moral intuitions) from "inconsistent Christianity" (p. 6).
3. **Chapters 3–4 — Religion, Violence & Historical Crimes (through p. 126)**: Responds to Dawkins's indictment of religious wars and ecclesiastical abuses, arguing that crimes attributed to the Church stem from human power corruption violating Christian teaching rather than from core Gospel ethics (p. 7).
4. **Chapter 5 — Historical Sources of Christianity (pp. 127–174)**: Defends the historical reliability of the New Testament canon against skeptical dismissals—examining Gospel transmission, Jesus's genealogies, and Roman census chronology under Quirinius (p. 7).
