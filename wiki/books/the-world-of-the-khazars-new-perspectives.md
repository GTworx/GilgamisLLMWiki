## Executive Summary

Co-edited by international academic authorities **Peter B. Golden**, **Haggai Ben-Shammai**, and **András Róna-Tas**, *The World of the Khazars: New Perspectives* (Brill 2007, 469 pp.) is the preeminent, multidisciplinary peer-reviewed compendium on the history, archaeology, linguistics, and religion of the **Khazar Khaganate** (c. 650–965 CE). Gathering contributions from 18 leading world specialists from Russia, Israel, Hungary, the US, and Ukraine, the volume presents cutting-edge archaeological excavations from the lower Don and Volga (Sarkel, Semikarakory), numismatic analyses of Khazar silver coins (*Ard al-Khazar*), Hebrew epigraphy, and critical evaluations of Arthur Koestler's controversial Khazar-Ashkenazi ancestry thesis.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Saltovo-Mayaki Material Culture**: Establishes the Saltovo-Mayaki archaeological culture across the Pontic steppes as the definitive physical manifestation of the multiethnic Khazar Khaganate.
2. **Economic Primacy via Trans-Eurasian Commerce**: Details how Khazar control of the Volga-Don portage enabled them to levy a 10% tithe on immense Viking (Rus') and Arab trade networks, accumulating vast hoards of Islamic silver dirhams.
3. **Genetic and Historiographical Demystification**: Rigorously debunks Arthur Koestler's political thesis that Ashkenazi Jewry descended primarily from converted Khazars, showing that while the Khazar court and military elite embraced Judaism, the demographic contribution to modern European Jewry was negligible compared to Mediterranean-Rhineland migrations.
