## Executive Summary

Composed in Greek between 60 and 30 BCE by Sicilian historian **Diodorus Siculus** and preserved here in two landmark bilingual Greek–English **Loeb Classical Library** volumes—**Volume II** (Books II.35–IV.58, translated by **C. H. Oldfather**, 1935/1967, 562 pp.) and **Volume VIII** (Books XVI.66–95 & XVII, translated by Yale historian **C. Bradford Welles**, 1963, 260 pp.)—the *Bibliotheca Historica* was classical antiquity's most ambitious universal history, uniting myth, ethnography, and political-military narrative across the known Mediterranean and Asian world.

**Volume II** preserves indispensable fragments of otherwise lost Hellenistic sources: **Megasthenes** on Mauryan India (II.35–42), accounts of **Scythians, Amazons, Hyperboreans, and Arabia**, **Iambulus**'s utopian Sun Island (Introduction, pp. 5–6), **Agatharchides** on Nubia/Ethiopia and Red Sea gold mines (Book III), and the comprehensive mythography of Dionysus, Heracles, and the Argonauts (Book IV). **Volume VIII** delivers the culmination of Philip II of Macedon's rise (Chaeronea and assassination, Book XVI.66–95) and the entire **Book XVII**—the earliest surviving continuous Greek history of **Alexander the Great**'s conquest of Persia, Bactria, and India (drawing heavily on Cleitarchus).

---

## Core Primary-Source Value

1. **Preserving Lost Hellenistic Ethnography (Vol. II)**: Serves as our chief window into Megasthenes' *Indica*, Posidonius of Apamea, and ancient Greek geographic imagination regarding India, Scythia, Ethiopia, and North Africa.
2. **Book XVII — The "Vulgate" Tradition of Alexander the Great (Vol. VIII)**: Balances Arrian's official military account with vivid dramatic detail on Granicus, Issus, Tyre, Gaugamela, Persepolis's burning, Central Asian guerrilla warfare, and Alexander's final years.
3. **Universal-History Ideal**: Models Stoic-inflected cosmopolitan historiography—treating humanity across continents as citizens of one shared historical narrative.
