## Summary
Interdisciplinary paleoanthropological, primatological, and material culture compendium. Synthesizes Nature excavations at Tolbor-16 (Mongolia) proving the northern dispersal route of modern humans into Northeast Asia ~45,000 years ago, Late Pleistocene hominin systematics, the dietary evolution of cooked meat and fermented fruit consumption as the biocultural origin of human feasting and alcohol, and Alfred Gell's seminal 'Technology of Enchantment'.

## Analytical Overview
This volume bridges deep prehistoric paleoanthropology with contemporary cultural and algorithmic anthropology.

Key scientific arcs:
1. **The Northern Dispersal Route into Eurasia**: Compiles archaeological evidence from the Tolbor-16 site in Mongolia (*Nature*, 2019), demonstrating that anatomically modern humans migrated along a northern steppe/riverine corridor through Central and Northeast Asia between 45,000 and 40,000 BP, intersecting with archaic hominin populations (Denisovans and Neanderthals) in ecological niches previously considered uninhabitable.
2. **The Biocultural Evolution of Feasting**: Features recent primatological and archaeological investigations into 'scrumping' and fermented wild fruits, demonstrating that primate ethanol metabolism (ADH4 enzyme mutation) provided an adaptive foraging mechanism that later evolved into the foundational human institution of shared feasting, social solidarity, and ritual alcohol consumption.
3. **The Technology of Enchantment to Algorithmic Culture**: Bridges material anthropology with digital modernity, pairing Alfred Gell’s classic anthropological analysis of technical virtuosity as psychological capture (*The Technology of Enchantment*) with Nick Seaver’s contemporary ethnographic tactics for researching algorithmic systems as cultural practices.
