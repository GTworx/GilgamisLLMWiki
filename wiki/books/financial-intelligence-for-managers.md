## Executive Summary

Written by Business Literacy Institute co-founders **Karen Berman** and **Joe Knight** with veteran business writer **John Case** (2nd revised edition, February 2013, Harvard Business Review Press, pp. 3–4), *Financial Intelligence* demystifies corporate accounting for non-financial managers—anchored in one liberating core premise: **finance is as much an art as a science** (Part One, p. 6). Because revenue recognition, depreciation schedules, reserves, and goodwill valuations all rely on managerial assumptions, estimates, and biases, financially intelligent leaders never take numbers at face value; they interrogate the assumptions underneath.

---

## Core Financial Literacy Framework

1. **The Art of Finance (Part One, p. 6)**: Exposes why accounting numbers are structured estimates under GAAP/IFRS—and how spotting aggressive revenue recognition or capitalization tricks protects managers from Enron- or WorldCom-style blind spots.
2. **Decoding the Three Statements (Parts Two–Four)**: Translates the **Income Statement** (profit ≠ cash), **Balance Sheet** (assets, liabilities, equity, goodwill), and **Cash Flow Statement** (operating cash flow and owner earnings) into plain operational intuition.
3. **Ratios, ROI & Working Capital (Parts Five–Six)**: Equips operating leaders to manage DSO/DPO/inventory cash-conversion cycles, evaluate capital expenditures via NPV/IRR/payback, and build a financially literate, open-book organizational culture.
