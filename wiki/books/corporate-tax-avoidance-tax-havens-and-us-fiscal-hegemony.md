## Summary
Exhaustive four-part University of Chicago Booth corporate finance compendium examining transfer pricing shelters, multinational profit shifting, economic nationalism blocking foreign cross-border M&A, and how non-CRS compliance positioned the US as the premier global tax haven.

## Analytical Overview
Details the empirical mechanisms multinational corporations utilize to shift profits into low-tax jurisdictions (Bermuda, Ireland, Luxembourg) without shifting real operational activity. Examines Dinç's landmark findings on government economic nationalism favoring domestic bidders in corporate acquisitions, and explores how Delaware, Nevada, and South Dakota trust laws turned the US into the world's most secure secrecy jurisdiction.
