## Summary
Master technical compendium on structural earthquake engineering and building inspection governance, centering on the 416-page Turkish Building Earthquake Code (TBDY 2018), statutory liabilities under Law No. 4708, international defect auditing benchmarks (BSCI), and OECD regulatory inspection reforms.

## Analytical Overview
This treatise constitutes an exhaustive technical, legal, and operational analysis of the built environment's seismic safety. The core document is the massive 416-page *Türkiye Bina Deprem Yönetmeliği (TBDY 2018)*, edited by Nazan Kılıç and the national engineering commission. Replacing the outdated 2007 code, TBDY 2018 revolutionized Turkish structural design by transitioning from uniform regional acceleration zones to site-specific, coordinate-based seismic spectra derived from the interactive Turkish Earthquake Hazard Map. It mandates non-linear performance-based seismic design for tall and mission-critical buildings, strict ductility detailing for reinforced concrete shear walls, beam-column joint confinements, and soil-structure interaction (SSI) modeling.

Crucially, the technical code is linked directly to real-world construction enforcement failures through in-depth empirical field studies. Research by Seda Yağız examining private building inspection firms under Building Inspection Law No. 4708 across Bursa uncovers chronic conflicts of interest, where contractors historically selected and compensated the very inspection firms tasked with auditing them. Legal analyses by Barış Duman and TMMOB President Mehmet Soğancı detail statutory liabilities, occupational safety hazards on construction sites, and the widespread practice of 'ghost engineering'—where certified engineers sign inspection affidavits without visiting pouring sites.

To elevate these national findings into a global comparative context, the volume integrates Daniel C. W. Ho and Y. Yau’s Hong Kong Building Safety and Conditions Index (BSCI), Funmilayo Rotimi’s defect reporting audits in New Zealand residential developments, Scandinavian comparative construction labor inspections (Denmark vs. Sweden), and Florentin Blanc’s landmark OECD treatise *Inspection Reforms: Why, How, and with What Results*. Together, these works establish that building codes are only as strong as the institutional independence and technical competence of the inspection regime enforcing them.
