## Executive Summary

Edited by University of Exeter Persian-Sufism specialist **Leonard Lewisohn** (2014, World Wisdom, pp. 4–5), *The Philosophy of Ecstasy: Rumi and the Sufi Tradition* gathers twelve authoritative essays by leading international Rumi scholars—including **Husayn Ilahi-Ghomshei**, **Franklin Lewis**, **Carl W. Ernst**, **Alan Williams**, **James W. Morris**, **Simon Weightman**, **Kabir Helminski**, **Roderick Grierson**, **Shahram Pazouki**, **Iraj Anvar**, and **Peter Chelkowski** (pp. 5–7).

Bridging philology, comparative theology, performance studies, and structural poetics, the anthology examines how Rumi forged a rigorous *philosophy of ecstasy* (*wajd*, *sama'*, and *ishq*) rooted simultaneously in Sunni-Sufi prophetic tradition (*Sunna*) and a remarkably inclusive interfaith ecumenism capable of speaking across religious and modern cultural boundaries.

## Key Scholarly Contributions

1. **Structural Intentionality in the *Masnavi***: Essays by Simon Weightman, Alan Williams, and Carl Ernst overturn the old Orientalist assumption that the *Masnavi* is a chaotic miscellany, uncovering intricate ring-compositional architectures and deliberate spiritual curricula embedded across Books I–IV (pp. 6–7).
2. **Ecumenism Rooted in Tradition**: Grierson and Pazouki show that Rumi's openness to Christians, Jews, and seekers of all creeds did not arise from modern secular relativism, but directly from the interior depth of Islamic *tawhid* and prophetic mercy.
3. **Ecstasy as Disciplined Knowledge**: Lewisohn demonstrates that *ecstasy* in Sufism is not irrational emotionalism, but a disciplined epistemological state transcending subject–object dualism.
