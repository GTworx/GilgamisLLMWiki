## Analytical Overview
### The Mathematics of Present Bias in Strategic Decisions

In *Intertemporal Choice, Hyperbolic Discounting, and Short-Termism in Corporate Strategy*, behavioral economists model why corporate executives and public policymakers routinely sacrifice massive long-term value for immediate short-term quarterly gains. While classical discounted cash flow (DCF) models assume exponential discounting (where future value decays at a constant rate $e^{-rt}$), real human decision-makers exhibit **Hyperbolic Discounting**.

### The Hyperbolic Decay Function and Reversal of Preferences

The treatise formalizes the cognitive failure:
- In hyperbolic discounting, the subjective discount rate between 'today' and 'tomorrow' is enormous, while the discount rate between 'one year from now' and 'one year and a day' is negligible.
- This produces dynamic inconsistency: an executive today rationally plans to invest heavily in long-term R&D five years out; but when year five arrives, the immediate temptation to boost quarterly EPS forces an R&D cut.

### Institutional Mechanisms to Enforce Long-Term Discipline

The dossier details architectural remedies: multi-year executive compensation clawbacks, tenure-weighted voting shares (loyalty shares), sovereign wealth fund capital lockups, and independent capital allocation committees that shield visionary investments from quarterly earnings pressure.
