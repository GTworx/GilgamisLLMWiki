## Executive Summary

Published in 1973 by German-born British economist and longtime Chief Economic Adviser to the UK National Coal Board **E. F. Schumacher** (with an incisive Introduction by Theodore Roszak, pp. 2–5), *Small Is Beautiful: Economics as if People Mattered* is the seminal manifesto of **human-scale ecological economics**. Challenging orthodox GDP-growth obsession and giant centralized bureaucracy, Schumacher argues that modern industrial society commits a fatal accounting error: treating **irreplaceable natural capital** (fossil fuels, topsoil, ecological tolerance) as if it were repeatable current income (Part I, p. 3).

Drawing inspiration from **Mahatma Gandhi** and his landmark essay **"Buddhist Economics"** (Chapter 4, p. 3), Schumacher champions **intermediate (appropriate) technology**—tools affordable, decentralized, repairable, and compatible with human dignity—so that economic organization serves meaningful work and community flourishing rather than reducing people to cogs in mega-scale machinery.

---

## Four-Part Architecture (Contents, p. 3)

- **Part I — The Modern World**: *The Problem of Production*, *Peace and Permanence*, *The Role of Economics*, *Buddhist Economics*, and *A Question of Size*—critiquing blind growth idolatry and proposing "Right Livelihood" as an economic design criterion.
- **Part II — Resources**: Evaluates education as the greatest resource, land stewardship, nuclear energy hazards, and technology "with a human face."
- **Part III — The Third World**: Makes the case for **intermediate technology** tailored to capital-scarce, labor-abundant societies rather than inappropriate Western capital-intensive mega-projects.
- **Part IV — Organization and Ownership**: Articulates principles of subsidiarity, decentralized business units inside large enterprises, and democratic ownership experiments (such as the Scott Bader Commonwealth).
