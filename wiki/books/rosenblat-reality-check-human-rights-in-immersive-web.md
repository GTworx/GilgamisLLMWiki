## Overview
Published by the NYU Stern Center for Business and Human Rights by legal scholar Mariana Olaizola Rosenblat, this incisive 28-page policy report outlines the urgent human rights obligations incumbent upon spatial computing platforms. Rosenblat analyzes threats to bodily integrity, harassment of marginalized groups, biometric tracking of religious/political minorities, and calls on hardware vendors to adhere to UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Applies international human rights frameworks (UDHR, ICCPR) to 3D spatial computing architectures.
- **Key Finding**: Documents the psychological severity of in-world virtual assault and spatial harassment compared to text comments.
- **Key Finding**: Demands human-rights-by-design: default personal safety bubbles, granular telemetry permissions, and independent human rights impact assessments.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Because spatial immersion triggers somatic nervous system responses, virtual violations feel viscerally real. Protecting human rights in the 3D web requires recognizing virtual bodily autonomy as an extension of physical rights."
