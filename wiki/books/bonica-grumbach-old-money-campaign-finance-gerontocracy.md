Monumental 57-page empirical paper by Stanford and Berkeley political scientists Adam Bonica and Jacob Grumbach demonstrating that private campaign finance networks systematically tilt American politics toward a billionaire gerontocracy.

## Analytical Overview
### Campaign Finance and the Rise of the American Gerontocracy

In *Old Money: Campaign Finance and Gerontocracy in the United States* (2023, 57 pp.), Stanford political scientist Adam Bonica and UC Berkeley public policy scholar Jacob M. Grumbach investigate an urgent institutional dysfunction: why the United States Congress and presidency are older than at any point in American history, with average lawmaker ages reaching unprecedented heights.

### The Mechanism of Donors' Age Bias

Utilizing comprehensive FEC transaction records across millions of campaign contributions, the authors uncover the financial engine of gerontocracy:
- **Donor Wealth Concentration:** Over 70% of individual campaign contributions originate from donors over the age of 60, who hold a disproportionate share of national wealth.
- **Affinity Bias:** Elderly high-net-worth donors overwhelmingly funnel primary campaign cash to elderly, established incumbent candidates whom they perceive as safe, moderate, and culturally familiar.
- **The Liquidity Chasm for Young Candidates:** Younger, non-wealthy challengers face insurmountable early-stage fundraising deficits, effectively shutting working-class and young talent out of public office.

### Structural Exclusion

The paper proves that American gerontocracy is not merely a biological accident, but a direct structural byproduct of an unregulated private campaign finance system.
