## Executive Summary

*Ethics in Higher Education: Values-driven Leaders for the Future* (published by Globethics.net in Geneva as *Globethics.net Education Ethics No. 1*, 2017, 370 pp.) edited by Professor [[divya-singh]] (Vice Principal, University of South Africa) and Professor [[christoph-stuckelberger]] (with contributions by [[obiora-ike]], N. Barney Pityana, and Mandla Makhanya) is a 19-chapter treatise examining how universities must reclaim their ethical mission to form values-driven global citizens rather than merely credentialing technical specialists (source: BOOKS/Ethics/Ethics in Higher Education_ Values-driven Leaders for the Future ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 1–9, 21). Originating from the 26th International Council for Open and Distance Education (ICDE) World Conference in Sun City, South Africa, the volume addresses both brick-and-mortar campuses and large-scale distance e-learning institutions.

Structured across four sections, the book investigates **Values in Higher Education** (Section 1: [[obiora-ike]] on ethics as a tool for human destiny; [[christoph-stuckelberger]]'s eight recommendations for strengthening university ethical culture across teaching, research integrity, procurement, and interreligious campus tolerance; and Paulachan Kochappilly on harmony as the horizon of higher education), **Academic & Leadership Ethics** (Section 2: N. Barney Pityana on university vice-chancellor leadership and epistemological responsibility in African universities), **Global Citizenship & Curriculum Transformation** (Section 3: Mandla Makhanya on universities in transformation), and **Digital & Distance Learning Ethics** (Section 4: academic dishonesty, plagiarism, and cognitive digital balance) (source: BOOKS/Ethics/Ethics in Higher Education_ Values-driven Leaders for the Future ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 6–13, 17–348).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Section 1 (Chapters 1–6): Values in Higher Education & Strengthening Campus Ethical Culture (pp. 17–100)**:
  * *Teachers as Leaders of Future Leaders*: [[obiora-ike]] argues that because university faculty train the leaders of tomorrow, higher education without ethics produces 'clever devils' (pp. 17–26). [[christoph-stuckelberger]] formulates **Eight Recommendations** for university governance—including anti-plagiarism, transparent funding/procurement, gender equity, and managing multi-faith campus prayer rooms as instruments of peace rather than fundamentalism (pp. 27–52) (source: BOOKS/Ethics/Ethics in Higher Education_ Values-driven Leaders for the Future ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 6–8, 21, 51).

* **Section 2 (Chapters 7–11): Leadership, Governance & Epistemological Responsibility in Universities (pp. 101–204)**:
  * *African Higher Education & Epistemological Justice*: Former UNISA Vice-Chancellor N. Barney Pityana and colleagues analyze the moral dilemmas of university governance amidst student protests, funding crises, and postcolonial curriculum decolonization, emphasizing epistemological responsibility and institutional autonomy (source: BOOKS/Ethics/Ethics in Higher Education_ Values-driven Leaders for the Future ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 9, 133–204).

* **Sections 3 & 4 (Chapters 12–19): Global Citizenship, Open/Distance e-Learning & Digital Balance (pp. 205–348)**:
  * *Ethics of Distance Education & Screen Addiction*: Examines how mega-universities prepare graduates for ethical global citizenship (Mandla Makhanya, Ch. 15, pp. 261–268), combat contract cheating and digital plagiarism in online assessment, and cultivate mindful analog/digital balance in hyper-connected learning environments (Ch. 19, pp. 330–348) (source: BOOKS/Ethics/Ethics in Higher Education_ Values-driven Leaders for the Future ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 11–13, 261–348).

## Key Quotes & Citations
