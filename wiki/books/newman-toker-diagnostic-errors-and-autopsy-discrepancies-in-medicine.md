## Executive Summary
This 16-page two-part clinical safety and epistemic compendium pairs Dr. David E. Newman-Toker's landmark BMJ Quality & Safety 2023 epidemiological study on diagnostic error harms with the classic 1995 medical treatise on autopsy decline and clinical overconfidence (*Failure to Perform Autopsies Means Some Physicians are Walking in a Fog of Misplaced Optimism*).

Newman-Toker et al. estimate that 795,000 Americans suffer permanent disability or death each year due to diagnostic errors across vascular events, infections, and cancers (the 'Big Three'). The historical companion paper demonstrates that clinical diagnoses are contradicted by post-mortem autopsy findings in 15% to 30% of cases, revealing how the collapse of routine autopsy rates blinds the medical profession to persistent cognitive biases and systemic diagnostic blind spots.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The 'Big Three' Diagnostic Categories**: Demonstrates that stroke, sepsis, and lung cancer account for the vast majority of lethal and permanently disabling diagnostic errors in emergency departments and outpatient clinics.
- **Quantification of Clinical Harm**: Calculates that approximately 371,000 deaths and 424,000 permanent disabilities occur annually in the United States alone directly attributable to misdiagnosis.
- **The Autopsy as Epistemic Truth Standard**: Details how post-mortem examination historically served as the supreme quality control mechanism, disproving arrogant clinical assumptions and correcting erroneous therapeutic paths.
- **Cognitive Biases in Diagnostic Triage**: Analyzes anchoring bias, premature closure, and availability heuristics among emergency physicians, urging algorithmic decision-support and rapid biomarker deployment.
