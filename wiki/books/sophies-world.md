## Executive Summary

Written by former Norwegian high-school philosophy teacher **Jostein Gaarder** (*Sofies verden*, 1991; translated by Paulette Møller, Berkley Books, 1996, copyright p. 8), *Sophie's World* is an international literary phenomenon that fuses a complete 3,000-year survey of Western philosophy with a playful metafictional thriller. When fourteen-year-old **Sophie Amundsen** finds two enigmatic anonymous notes in her mailbox—*"Who are you?"* and *"Where does the world come from?"*—she is drawn into a secret correspondence course led by enigmatic mentor **Alberto Knox**.

Guiding Sophie from the Pre-Socratic natural philosophers (*Democritus, Heraclitus, Parmenides*) through Athens (*Socrates, Plato, Aristotle*), Hellenism, the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, Rationalism and Empiricism (*Descartes, Spinoza, Locke, Hume, Berkeley*), Enlightenment (*Kant*), Romanticism (*Hegel, Kierkegaard*), and modern thought (*Marx, Darwin, Freud, Sartre*), the novel executes a brilliant Berkeleyan plot twist: Sophie and Alberto gradually discover they are fictional characters inside a birthday novel written by UN Major Albert Knag for his daughter Hilde—staging a rebellion for narrative free will.

## Pedagogical & Literary Craft

1. **Philosophy as Radical Wonder**: Uses the metaphor of the white rabbit pulled from the magician's top hat to show how adults sink comfortably into the rabbit's fur while philosophers stubbornly cling to the tips of the hairs, refusing to lose childlike astonishment at existence.
2. **Metafiction as Philosophical Demonstration**: Enacts George Berkeley's subjective idealism (*esse est percipi*) and Sartre's existential freedom directly within the plot structure rather than merely summarizing them.
