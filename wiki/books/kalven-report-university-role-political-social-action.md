## Summary
The landmark 1967 University of Chicago statement establishing the definitive doctrine of institutional neutrality: a university must provide the forum for dissent, not be an institutional participant in political conflicts.

## Analytical Overview
### The Magna Carta of Institutional Neutrality

The *Kalven Committee Report on the University's Role in Political and Social Action*, authored in 1967 at the University of Chicago under the chairmanship of legal scholar Harry Kalven Jr., stands as the modern world's definitive philosophical charter on the purpose of higher education and academic freedom. Drafted amidst the intense campus turmoil of the Vietnam War and the Civil Rights movement, the report established the doctrine of **Institutional Neutrality**.

### The Mission of the University: The Forum, Not the Actor

The report's central philosophical axiom is uncompromising:
> "The university is the home and sponsor of critics; it is not itself the critic. It is to be prized for the freedom of its faculty and students to argue with each other, but it must be distinct from them. To perform its mission in the society, a university must sustain an extraordinary environment of freedom of inquiry and tolerate the widest diversity of views. It cannot insist on an orthodoxy of conduct or belief."

### The Epistemic Necessity of Neutrality

The Kalven Report demonstrates that when a university as a corporate body takes collective political or moral stances, it inevitably chills internal dissent, silences minority viewpoints among faculty and students, and invites external political retaliation. The university can fulfill its unique role as an engine of intellectual discovery only by remaining an open, non-combatant forum dedicated to unfettered inquiry.
