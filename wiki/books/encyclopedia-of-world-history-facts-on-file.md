## Executive Summary

Edited by a five-historian editorial team led by **Marsha E. Ackermann**, **Michael J. Schroeder**, **Janice J. Terry**, **Jiu-Hwa Lo Upshur**, and **Mark F. Whitters** (Facts On File / Infobase Publishing, 2008, Copyright p. 5, Contents p. 6; 3,756 pp. combining the complete **7-Volume Set**), the *Encyclopedia of World History* is an exhaustive, chronologically segmented A–Z reference architecture explicitly designed around modern global-history standards (AP World History / comparative periodization) rather than Western-centric surveys.

Organizing thousands of signed specialist articles, historical atlases, thematic essays, and primary-source chronologies across seven era-defined volumes (p. 3)—**Vol. I: The Ancient World (Prehistoric–600 CE)**, **Vol. II: The Expanding World (600–1450)**, **Vol. III: The First Global Age (1450–1750)**, **Vol. IV: Age of Revolution and Empire (1750–1900)**, **Vol. V: Crisis and Achievement (1900–1950)**, **Vol. VI: The Contemporary World (1950–Present)**, and **Vol. VII: Primary Documents & Master Index**—the compendium gives equal analytical weight to Asian, African, Islamic, Mesoamerican, Oceanic, and European civilizations.

---

## Core Architectural Features

1. **Periodized Comparative Structure**: Grouping A–Z entries within six historical epochs allows readers to compare simultaneous phenomena (e.g., Axial Age thought, Silk Road trade, early modern gunpowder empires, decolonization) without anachronism.
2. **Global Non-Eurocentric Coverage**: Integrates African city-states, Andean Neolithic cultures, Indian Mauryan/Mughal dynasties, Chinese imperial institutions, Islamic caliphates, and European developments within a unified framework.
3. **Atlas, Chronology & Primary-Document Apparatus**: Each volume opens with tailored regional maps, major thematic essays, and synchronized timelines, making the 3,756-page set an authoritative reference anchor for the entire wiki.
