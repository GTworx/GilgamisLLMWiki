## Executive Summary

Published in Oxford's Brain: A Journal of Neurology (2009) by British physician and former Foreign Secretary David Owen alongside psychiatrist Jonathan Davidson (Duke University), this 11-page clinical paper defines 'Hubris Syndrome' as an acquired, non-clinical personality disorder triggered by the possession of unchecked political power. Examining US Presidents and UK Prime Ministers over a 100-year period (including George W. Bush, Tony Blair, Margaret Thatcher, Neville Chamberlain, and Lyndon B. Johnson), the authors establish 14 specific diagnostic criteria for Hubris Syndrome—including narcissistic arrogance, contempt for advice, conflation of personal identity with the nation, and reckless impulsivity. The study demonstrates that Hubris Syndrome is a toxic psychiatric consequence of power that subsides once power is relinquished.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **14 Diagnostic Criteria of Hubris Syndrome**: Codifies distinct symptoms: narcissistic propensity to see the world as an arena for self-glorification, messianic zeal, loss of touch with reality, restless recklessness, and profound contempt for criticism.
- **Acquired and Reversible Condition**: Unlike lifelong genetic personality disorders, Hubris Syndrome develops specifically as an intoxication of sustained executive power and typically remits once the individual leaves office.
- **Historical Case Studies**: Delivers forensic clinical analyses of Tony Blair and George W. Bush leading up to the 2003 Iraq War, Margaret Thatcher during her third term, and Lyndon Johnson during the Vietnam escalation.
- **Power as a Neurochemical Intoxicant**: Explores the neurobiological hypothesis that prolonged absolute power alters dopaminergic and serotonergic executive reward pathways, impairing prefrontal risk evaluation.
- **Constitutional Countermeasures**: Concludes that individual self-discipline is insufficient to ward off hubris; constitutional democracies require strict term limits, independent cabinet oversight, and institutional checks and balances.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations

- "Hubris Syndrome is an acquired disorder of power. It develops in political leaders who hold great power over years with minimal accountability, transforming confident statesmanship into reckless, messianic folly." (source: Owen & Davidson, Brain 2009, p. 1396)
- "The hubristic leader increasingly conflates themselves with the nation or state, believing that they answer not to parliament or the electorate, but only to history or God." (source: Owen & Davidson, Brain 2009, p. 1401)
