## Overview
Authored by historical linguist Alexander Savelyev, this 25-page chapter applies Bayesian computational phylogenetic algorithms to basic 100-word and 110-word Swadesh-Starostin basic vocabulary lists across dozens of Turkic languages. Savelyev provides robust quantitative date estimates for the primary bifurcation of Proto-Turkic (Oghur vs Common Turkic at ~100 BCE) and subsequent medieval dialect radiations across Eurasia.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Applies Markov Chain Monte Carlo Bayesian methods to lexical cognate matrices of modern and historical Turkic tongues.
- **Key Finding**: Dates the initial Oghur-Common Turkic split to around 100 BCE, coinciding with early Xiongnu steppe empire dynamics.
- **Key Finding**: Clarifies internal genealogical topologies within Kipchak, Karluk, Oghuz, and Siberian Turkic branches.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Bayesian phylogenetic methods provide an objective, falsifiable chronological framework that anchors Turkic linguistic splits directly to archaeological and historical events on the Eurasian steppe."
