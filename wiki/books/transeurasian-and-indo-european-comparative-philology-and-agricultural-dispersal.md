## Summary
Master philological, genomic, and archaeological treatise establishing the agricultural dispersal and deep genetic divergence of Transeurasian and Indo-European languages. Anchored by Robbeets et al.'s landmark Nature paper 'Triangulation supports agricultural spread of the Transeurasian languages', Bayesian phylolinguistic modeling, and ancient genomic evidence for the deep split between Eastern and Western Mediterranean Indo-European lineages.

## Analytical Overview
This volume documents a paradigm shift in historical linguistics and prehistoric human dispersal: the methodological unification of linguistics, archaeology, and archaeogenetics (**triangulation**).

Key analytical components:
1. **The Transeurasian Agricultural Hypothesis**: Led by Martine Robbeets and published in *Nature* (2021), this study demonstrates that the Transeurasian language family (comprising Turkic, Mongolic, Tungusic, Koreanic, and Japonic) originated among Early Neolithic millet farmers in the West Liao River basin in Northeast China (~9,000 BP). Combining an unprecedented dataset of agricultural terminology, Bayesian linguistic trees, archaeological site density, and ancient human DNA, the authors prove that agrarian demic expansion drove the initial diaspora, later overlaid by pastoralist nomadic expansions across the Eurasian steppe.
2. **Indo-European Genomic Divergence**: Synthesizes 2024–2025 ancient genomic data demonstrating a deep divergence between Eastern and Western Mediterranean Indo-European speakers, challenging single-wave Steppe pastoralist dogmas with complex multi-vector interactions spanning the Caucasus, Anatolia, and the Balkans.
3. **Epigraphic Origins & Writing Systems**: Features epigraphic analyses tracing early script evolution—from the pictographic symbolic lexicon of Göbekli Tepe to Proto-Sinaitic alphabetic genesis and early Orkhon Turkic runic scripts, alongside the linguistic transmission of tea vocabulary (*cha* vs. *chai*) along the Silk Road.
