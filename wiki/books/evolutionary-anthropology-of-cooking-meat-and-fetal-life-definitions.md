## Executive Summary
This multidisciplinary evolutionary anthropology, bioethics, and systems biology dossier examines foundational questions of human physiology and cultural development. Grounded in Richard Wrangham's 'Cooking Hypothesis', the first component explores how ancestral control of fire and thermal cooking of animal meat denatured tough protein fibers, gelatinized collagen, and dramatically increased caloric absorption, providing the surplus metabolic energy required to fuel the metabolically expensive expansion of the hominin brain while shrinking the human gut. The second component provides a rigorous embryological analysis of fetal biological milestones, mapping neural tube closure, cortical thalamic connectivity, and viability thresholds to inform bioethical definitions of life. The final component maps cross-domain analogies between biological cellular mechanisms and modern computing architectures.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Cooking Hypothesis (Wrangham)**: Thermal cooking effectively acts as external pre-digestion; raw meat requires immense mechanical and enzymatic energy to digest, whereas cooked meat yields 30–50% higher net net caloric intake.
- **The Expensive-Tissue Trade-Off**: Brain tissue consumes 20% of resting human metabolic energy despite weighing only 2% of body mass; shifting to cooked, nutrient-dense meat permitted hominins to evolve smaller, less energy-intensive digestive tracts.
- **Biological Milestones of Fetal Life**: Rigorously delineates embryological milestones—fertilization, gastrulation (day 14, indivisibility), neural tube formation, cardiac spontaneous contraction, cortical synaptogenesis, and EEG brain wave emergence (week 24–28).
- **Biology-Technology Isomorphism**: Formulates structural mappings: DNA as read-only memory (ROM), mRNA as ephemeral message queues, ribosomes as 3D biological assemblers, and immune antibodies as signature-based intrusion detection systems.
