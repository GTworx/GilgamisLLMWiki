## Executive Summary

*Görmediğimiz Türkiye: 123 Yıllık National Geographic Arşivi* is a curated 50-page visual and ethnographic chronicle drawn from over a century of the *National Geographic* photographic archives, documenting everyday social life, urban transformation, maritime commerce, and rural landscapes across early Republican Türkiye between the 1920s and 1950s (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, pp. 1–50). Through rare archival plates captured by international photojournalists including [[kurt-and-margot-lubinski]] and [[bernard-f-rogers-jr]], the volume preserves an intimate, unvarnished portrait of a society navigating the transition from late Ottoman multi-religious cosmopolitanism to Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's modern secular Republic.

Moving between Ankara on the eve of the Republic's tenth anniversary in September 1933, the bustling ferries and quays of Galata, Karaköy, Bebek, and Büyükada in the late 1920s and 1930s, and the snow-clad slopes of Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı) and Eskişehir in the 1950s, the archival captions reveal how material modernization coexisted with deep-rooted Anatolian and Levantine guild traditions (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, pp. 2–21, 50). Rather than focusing on state monuments alone, the photographs foreground dockworkers resting against their woven *küfe* baskets, dapper Istanbul watermelon vendors, Bosphorus net fishermen, and smiling citizens where fedoras and caps mingle with lingering fezzes on Galata Bridge (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, pp. 5–13).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Republican Ankara & The Tenth Anniversary Ethos (p. 2)**:
  * *Sidewalk Culture in 1933 Ankara*: Captures citizens enjoying iced beer at a sidewalk stand in Ankara in September 1933, one month before the 10th anniversary of the Republic, recorded by [[kurt-and-margot-lubinski]] with the archival note that Turks enjoy social drinking 'without excess' (*aşırıya kaçmadan*) (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, p. 2).

* **Maritime Istanbul, Galata Bridge & Haliç Guilds (pp. 3–13)**:
  * *Galata Bridge & Linguistic Transition (1925–1937)*: Documents the cobblestone crossing of Galata Bridge in 1925 where women and men in fedoras, caps, and fezzes walk past flower peddlers (p. 6), alongside [[bernard-f-rogers-jr]]'s 1937 ferry terminal photograph showing 1st and 2nd class fares to Moda–Kalamış and Adalar–Pendik, where signs read *'Girilir'* and *'Çıkılır'* prior to modern noun standardization (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, pp. 3, 6).
  * *Haliç Sailboats, Küfe Porters & Cosmopolitan Büyükada (1928–1937)*: Depicts cargo sailboats drying their wet sails in the Golden Horn between Süleymaniye and Fatih mosques in 1928 (p. 4), dockworkers resting against their *küfe* baskets gazing toward Galata Tower (p. 5), cosmopolitan Friday afternoons on Büyükada in the late 1920s (p. 8), unposed dapper watermelon sellers (*Janti Karpuzcu*) in 1937 (p. 9), and Bosphorus fishermen hauling full nets at Bebek in 1930 and drying *çiroz* mackerel in the 1950s (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, pp. 4–5, 8–13).

* **Anatolian Landscapes, Mount Ararat & Provincial Modernization (pp. 14–50)**:
  * *Majesty of Mount Ararat (1954) & Eskişehir*: Chronicles pastoral and geographic vistas across Anatolia, culminating in a May 1954 Doğubayazıt morning study of Greater Mount Ararat (*Büyük Ağrı*)—whose snowpack descends to 2,000 meters and whose photographer confessed uncertainty as to whether any lens could capture its full grandeur—and mid-century civic scenes from Eskişehir (source: BOOKS/Türkiye_/Görmediğimiz Türkiye.pdf, pp. 21, 50).
