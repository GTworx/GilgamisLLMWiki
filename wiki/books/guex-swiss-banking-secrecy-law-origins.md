## Analytical Overview
### Demystifying the Legend of Swiss Banking Secrecy

In *The Origins of the Swiss Banking Secrecy Law and Its Repercussions for Swiss Federal Policy*, published in the prestigious *Business History Review* (Harvard Business School), University of Lausanne historian Sébastien Guex conducts a rigorous archival investigation that shatters one of the most enduring public-relations myths of 20th-century finance. For decades, the Swiss Banking Association propagated the noble narrative that Article 47 of the 1934 Swiss Federal Banking Law was created to protect the savings of German Jews persecuted by the Nazi regime.

### The Real Historical Motivation: Capital Flight and Tax Evasion

Drawing from confidential Swiss federal archives and bank board minutes, Guex demonstrates that:
- Article 47 was drafted in response to the **1932 French tax evasion scandal**, when French police raided the Paris branch of Banque Commerciale de Bâle, uncovering records of over 1,000 prominent French politicians, generals, and industrialists hiding illicit fortunes in Switzerland.
- Facing aggressive international investigations and domestic socialist calls for bank transparency, Swiss bankers and federal officials criminalized the disclosure of bank client identities to foreign authorities.

### The Engine of Swiss Post-War Financial Hegemony

Guex maps how banking secrecy transformed Switzerland into the world's premier offshore financial haven, allowing Swiss banks to absorb massive capital flight from interwar Europe while providing the federal government with immense diplomatic leverage throughout the 20th century.
