# No Evidence for Nudging After Adjusting for Publication Bias: PNAS Re-Evaluation and Behavioral Economics Dilemmas

## Summary
Landmark PNAS meta-scientific re-evaluation analyzing 212 published nudge interventions, revealing that after correcting for severe publication bias and selective reporting, the average effect size of behavioral nudges shrinks to statistically indistinguishable from zero.

## Analytical Overview
Applies advanced meta-regression techniques (p-uniform*, PET-PEESE) to the premier dataset supporting behavioral nudges (Thaler & Sunstein). Demonstrates that while individual large-scale government RCTs occasionally show modest effects, the broader academic literature on behavioral choice architecture is heavily contaminated by small-sample publication bias, challenging the empirical foundation of nudge-based public policy.
