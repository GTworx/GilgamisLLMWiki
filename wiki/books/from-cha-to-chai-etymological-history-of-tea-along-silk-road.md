## Overview
Presented at the 9th Asian Food Study Conference, this delightful linguistic and historical monograph explores the global bifurcation of the word for 'tea'. Tracing maritime trade routes from coastal Min Chinese (yielding European 'tea', 'thé', 'tee') versus overland Silk Road caravans from Northern Mandarin 'chá' (yielding Persian 'chāy', Turkic 'çay', Russian 'chay', and Hindi 'chai'), the paper maps centuries of Eurasian trade networks.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Traces global trade geography directly through linguistic phonology: coastal ocean trade versus overland camel caravans.
- **Key Finding**: Documents the social and ritual culture of the çayhane (teahouse) across Central Asia, Iran, and Anatolia.
- **Key Finding**: Illustrates how botanical diffusion leaves indelible lexicographical footprints across disparate language families.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Virtually every language on Earth calls tea either a variation of 'tea' or 'cha'. That single phonetic bifurcation tells the entire geopolitical story of global trade over the last five hundred years."
