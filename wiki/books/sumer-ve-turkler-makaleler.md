## Executive Summary

Consolidating both the concise monograph (51 pp.) and the massive 1,027-page collected papers compendium, *Sümer ve Türkler: Makaleler* (*Sumerians and the Turks: Articles*) represents **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ's** lifetime academic defense of the cultural, religious, linguistic, and mythological parallels connecting ancient Mesopotamian **Sumerians** with the early **Turkic peoples of Eurasia**. Synthesizing comparative linguistics (agglutinative grammar, vowel harmony, and lexical cognates), flood mythologies, religious rituals, textile motifs, and archaeological discoveries, Çığ evaluates the historical hypotheses linking Sumerian origins with pre-historic migrations from Central Asia and the Caspian basin.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Agglutinative Linguistic Affinities**: Highlights that Sumerian, unlike Semitic or Indo-European, is an isolate agglutinative tongue sharing fundamental structural grammar (suffixes, root stability) with Ural-Altaic language families.
2. **Cosmological Cognates (Dingir / Tengri)**: Investigates the cuneiform sign for deity/heaven (*Dingir*) as a potential phonological and semantic cognate to the Proto-Turkic sky-god (*Tengri / Tanrı*).
3. **Scholarly Balance on the Turkish Historical Thesis**: Defends Atatürk's original visionary impetus to explore pre-Islamic Near Eastern connections, while advocating rigorous, evidence-based archaeological testing against romantic chauvinism.
