## Executive Summary
This two-part Financial Times investigative series (22 pp.) audits the rise, capital boom, and subsequent economic restructuring of indoor vertical farming and Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA). Reporting from hyper-automated indoor facilities across the United States (AeroFarms, Plenty, Bowery, AppHarvest), the investigation examines the clash between biological promises (95% less water, zero pesticides, 365-day harvesting) and brutal economic realities (soaring electricity bills, high capital expenditures, LED lighting depreciation, and restricted crop varieties limited to leafy greens and herbs). The reports detail how the sector is pivoting toward hybrid greenhouse CEA, gene-edited dwarf fruiting crops, and renewable co-location.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Unit Economics Dilemma**: While vertical farms eliminate weather risks and achieve 300x higher yield per acre, electricity for LED lighting and HVAC dehumidification can represent over 50% of total operating expenses.
- **The Salad Bowl Trap**: Indoor farming has succeeded commercially almost exclusively with high-margin, perishable leafy greens (lettuce, arugula, basil); expanding to caloric staple crops (wheat, soy, potatoes) remains economically unfeasible under current power costs.
- **Hybrid Greenhouse Convergence**: Capital is shifting away from fully enclosed, windowless warehouse farms toward automated glass greenhouses that utilize free natural sunlight supplemented by targeted dynamic LED top-lighting.
- **Biological Adaptation to CEA**: Plant breeders are using CRISPR gene editing to create compact, fast-growing dwarf tomato and strawberry cultivars optimized for multi-layer indoor racking systems.

## Key Data Points & Excerpts
