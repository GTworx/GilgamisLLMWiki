## Executive Summary

Penned by celebrated Turkish Sumerologist **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ** (1914–2024), *Gilgameş: Tarihte İlk Kral Kahraman* (*Gilgamesh: History's First Hero King*) is an accessible, literary retelling and philological reconstruction of the world's oldest epic poem: the **Epic of Gilgamesh**, recorded on third-millennium BCE Sumerian cuneiform clay tablets excavated in Mesopotamia and preserved in Istanbul and European museums. Çığ breathes life into King Gilgamesh of Uruk, his deep companionship with wild man Enkidu, their battle against the monster Humbaba and the Bull of Heaven, Enkidu's tragic mortality, and Gilgamesh's quest across the Waters of Death to find Utnapishtim and the secret of immortality.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Human Condition and Mortality**: Emphasizes that Gilgamesh's journey is the eternal universal human confrontation with death, showing that immortality is attained not through physical longevity, but through enduring civic and cultural deeds (the walls of Uruk).
2. **Cuneiform Tablet Translation for General Readers**: Çığ bridges dense academic Assyriology and public education, demonstrating the direct literary ancestry of Homeric epics and biblical narratives in Sumerian prototypes.
3. **Friendship as Civilizing Catalyst**: Highlights how friendship between the urban king and the wild creature humanizes both, serving as an ancient allegory for the synthesis of nature and civilization.
