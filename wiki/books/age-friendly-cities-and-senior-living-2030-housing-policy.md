## Executive Summary
This comprehensive 50-page urban planning and social gerontology compendium explores the societal, architectural, and policy transformations required to accommodate unprecedented planetary population aging. It unites Joost van Hoof et al.'s empirical field study on *Age-Friendly Cities in the Netherlands* (21 pp., Indoor and Built Environment), the strategic future analysis *Senior Living in 2030* (17 pp.), and the UNECE *Policy Brief on the Participation of Older Persons in Society* (12 pp.).

The volume investigates how the built environment, municipal transportation, and residential senior housing must evolve to facilitate aging in place. It tackles systemic architectural ageism, barrier-free design, intergenerational co-housing models, smart home ambient assistive telemetry, and the legal/political mechanisms necessary to ensure active civic and economic participation of older citizens.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **WHO Age-Friendly Cities Architecture**: Evaluates the eight WHO operational domains: housing, transportation, outdoor spaces, social participation, respect/social inclusion, civic participation, communication, and community health services.
- **Combatting Built Environment Ageism**: Identifies physical urban hindrances (uneven sidewalks, high transit steps, poor lighting, absent public seating) that unintentionally confine older adults to social isolation.
- **Senior Living in 2030 Housing Models**: Reimagines senior housing moving away from institutional nursing homes toward active, wellness-oriented, digitally integrated communities offering continuum-of-care.
- **UNECE Active Ageing Policy**: Outlines policy frameworks empowering older adults to remain active participants in civil society, volunteer networks, and multi-generational workplaces.
