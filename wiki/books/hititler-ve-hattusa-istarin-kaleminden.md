## Executive Summary

In *Hititler ve Hattuşa: İştar'ın Kaleminden* (*The Hittites and Hattusa: Through the Pen of Ishtar*), **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ** adopts an imaginative, evocative first-person narrative technique—speaking through the voice of ancient Mesopotamian goddess of love and warfare **İştar (Inanna / Šawuška)**, patron protector of the Hittite imperial capital **Hattusa (Boğazkale / Çorum)**. Blending primary cuneiform archival records excavated by Hugo Winckler and Ekrem Akurgal with vivid storytelling, Çığ reconstructs the rise, laws, religion, gender equality, and international diplomacy of the great **Hittite Empire** of Bronze Age Anatolia.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Humanitarian Maturity of Hittite Law**: Emphasizes that unlike harsh Babylonian Hammurabi codes demanding bodily mutilation (*eye for an eye*), Hittite law substituted restorative justice, restitution, and civil damages.
2. **The Diplomatic Sovereignty of Queen Puduhepa**: Demonstrates how Queen Puduhepa conducted sovereign correspondence with Pharaoh Ramesses II, co-signing the Treaty of Kadesh as an equal head of state.
3. **Anatolia as the Crucible of Tolerance**: Reconstructs Hattusa as a multilingual, multi-faith metropolis that absorbed Hatti, Luwian, Palaic, Hurrian, and Sumerian cultures into a harmonious imperial synthesis.
