## Executive Summary

Written by MIT mathematician and founder of cybernetics **Norbert Wiener** (1894–1964)—preserved in our library across two copies of the 1989 Free Association Books reprint featuring **Steve J. Heims**'s critical introduction and Wiener's famous 1947 *Atlantic Monthly* anti-militarist open letter *"A Scientist Rebels"* (pp. 1–7, TOC p. 7)—*The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950; revised 1954) translates the mathematical science of **"control and communication in the animal and machine"** into a prophetic social and ethical warning for the automation age.

Framing the twentieth century as *"the age of communication and control"* following the ages of clocks and steam engines (p. 1), Wiener grounds society itself in **information flow and feedback loops** fighting local pockets of thermodynamic **entropy** (Ch. I–II). With uncanny prescience decades before modern AI, he warns that treating automated machines as literal-minded servants without clear human ethical goals risks catastrophic displacement of routine labor and dehumanizing **ant-hill totalitarianism** (Ch. IX–X).

---

## Prophetic Cybernetic Principles

1. **Entropy, Information & Feedback (Ch. I–III)**: Unites Gibbsian statistical mechanics with communication engineering: living organisms and learning machines preserve order against universal entropy by sensing environmental feedback and adapting behavior.
2. **Organization as Message & Law as Communication (Ch. V–VII)**: Conceives organisms as persistent informational patterns ("whirlpools in a river") rather than static matter, warning that excessive Cold War scientific secrecy poisons societal homeostasis.
3. **The Second Industrial Revolution & Ethical Automation (Ch. IX–X)**: Predicts that computing machines will devalue routine human intellectual labor just as steam engines devalued muscle—demanding a society built around creative dignity rather than reducing workers to cogs in rigid mechanical hierarchies.
