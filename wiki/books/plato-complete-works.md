## Executive Summary

Edited with a comprehensive introduction and notes by Princeton philosopher **John M. Cooper** (with Associate Editor **D. S. Hutchinson**, title p. 2; Contents p. 4), the 1997 Hackett *Plato: Complete Works* is the standard one-volume scholarly edition of the entire surviving Platonic corpus in English. Gathering fresh, authoritative translations by leading classicists—including G. M. A. Grube (*Euthyphro, Apology, Crito, Phaedo, Republic*), C. D. C. Reeve (*Cratylus*), M. J. Levett & Myles Burnyeat (*Theaetetus*), Alexander Nehamas & Paul Woodruff (*Symposium, Phaedrus*), Mary Louise Gill (*Parmenides*), Nicholas P. White (*Sophist*), C. J. Rowe (*Statesman*), and Trevor J. Saunders (*Laws*)—this 1,837-page volume brings together all dialogues, letters, and traditional apocrypha.

Spanning Socratic elenchus, the Theory of Forms, epistemological inquiry, aesthetics, cosmology (*Timaeus*), and utopian/constitutional political theory (*Republic, Statesman, Laws*), Plato established the foundational architecture of Western philosophy—prompting A. N. Whitehead's celebrated observation that the European philosophical tradition consists of a series of footnotes to Plato.

## Core Philosophical Pillars

1. **Socratic Ethics & Method (*Apology, Crito, Euthyphro, Protagoras, Gorgias*)**: Interrogates virtue, piety, justice, and civic duty through dialectical questioning (*elenchus*), asserting that the unexamined life is not worth living.
2. **Metaphysics, Epistemology & Eros (*Phaedo, Symposium, Phaedrus, Theaetetus, Parmenides*)**: Contrasts the fluctuating sensory world with eternal intelligible Forms (the Allegory of the Cave), maps erotic ascent toward the Good, and pioneers formal epistemology ("justified true belief").
3. **Political Philosophy (*Republic, Statesman, Laws*)**: Constructs the tripartite soul-city analogy (reason, spirit, appetite), critiques democratic degeneration into tyranny, and proposes philosopher-kingship—later subjected to searing modern critique by Karl Popper.
