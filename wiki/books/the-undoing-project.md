## Executive Summary

Master narrative journalist **Michael Lewis** wrote *The Undoing Project* (W. W. Norton, 2016, 276 pp.) after discovering that the cognitive blind spots behind his 2003 baseball classic *Moneyball* had been uncovered decades earlier by two Israeli psychologists whose intense intellectual romance birthed **behavioral economics** (Introduction pp. 8–11). Profiling the starkly opposite temperaments of anxious, self-doubting Holocaust survivor **Daniel Kahneman** and dashing, fearless paratrooper-mathematician **Amos Tversky**, Lewis weaves dual biography with the dramatic history of how their collaboration dismantled the neoclassical myth of *Homo economicus*.

From Hebrew University seminar breakthroughs in 1969 through wartime Israel, Stanford, and eventual painful estrangement before Tversky's premature death in 1996 (six years before Kahneman won the 2002 Nobel Prize in Economics), *The Undoing Project* illuminates how human minds systematically misjudge probability, frame risk, and mentally "undo" tragic outcomes.

## Breakthroughs of the Kahneman–Tversky Revolution

1. **Heuristics & Cognitive Biases (Ch. 6–8)**: Uncovered how intuitive mental shortcuts—**Availability**, **Representativeness** (the "Linda problem" conjunction fallacy), and **Anchoring**—lead even trained statisticians, doctors, and NBA scouts into systematic prediction errors.
2. **Prospect Theory & Loss Aversion (Ch. 9–10)**: Demonstrated empirically that humans evaluate outcomes relative to reference points rather than absolute wealth, feeling **losses roughly twice as painfully as equivalent gains** and flipping from risk-aversion in gains to risk-seeking in losses.
3. **The Rules of Undoing (Ch. 11)**: Explores counterfactual regret—why humans agonize more over "near misses" (missing a flight by 5 minutes vs. 30 minutes) that the mind can easily imagine undoing.
