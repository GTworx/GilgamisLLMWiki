## Executive Summary

*Financial Analysis using Excel* (Volume 6 of the *Excel for Professionals* series, VJ Books Inc., 2002, 254 pp.) by [[vj-books-editorial]] is a technical reference and modeling handbook dedicated to Excel's quantitative financial function library—spanning discounted cash flow (DCF), loan amortization, capital budgeting, fixed-income bond pricing, yield-to-maturity, duration, and accrued-interest conventions (source: BOOKS/Investment_/Financial Modeling in Excel.pdf, Function Matrix & Title Page, pp. 1–2).

Opening with a master input-requirements matrix mapping date, par-value, settlement, maturity, coupon-frequency, and day-count basis arguments across Excel's fixed-income functions (`PRICE`, `YIELD`, `DURATION`, `MDURATION`, `ACCRINT`, `COUPDAYS`, `PRICEDISC`, `INTRATE`), the manual provides rigorous mathematical transparency so analysts avoid black-box spreadsheet errors in valuation and treasury modeling (pp. 1–254).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Fixed-Income & Treasury Function Architecture (p. 1)
- **Complete Parameter Taxonomy**: Systematically maps required inputs—issue/settlement/maturity dates, coupon frequency, day-count basis, redemption par, and discount rate—across eighteen core bond and money-market Excel functions (`COUPDAYBS`, `COUPDAYSNC`, `DURATION`, `MDURATION`, `PRICE`, `YIELD`, `ACCRINT`, `DISC`) (p. 1).

### Capital Budgeting, Cash Flows & Sensitivity Analysis
- **Time Value of Money & Project Appraisal**: Covers `NPV`, `XNPV`, `IRR`, `XIRR`, `MIRR`, `PMT`, and depreciation functions alongside scenario and sensitivity tables for corporate investment decisions and portfolio risk evaluation.
