## Executive Summary

Investigating the pre-Islamic history of the Zagros and upper Mesopotamian highlands, **Kâzım Mirşan's** *İslam Öncesi Kürt Tarihi* (*Pre-Islamic Kurdish History*) cross-references ancient cuneiform records (Assyrian, Babylonian, Elamite), classical Greek histories (Xenophon's *Carduchi* in the *Anabasis*), and regional rock inscriptions. Mirşan examines the ancient ethnonyms *Gutium*, *Subartu*, *Kardu*, and *Medes*, proposing that early highland tribal groups were part of a wider Turanian and Eurasian nomadic migratory complex that merged with subsequent Iranian and Semitic waves over millennia.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Carduchi Puzzle**: Re-evaluates Xenophon's *Carduchi*, demonstrating that ancient mountain tribes were warlike pastoralists independent of both the Persian Achaemenid Empire and Mesopotamian lowlands.
2. **The Newroz / Nevruz Continuum**: Traces the spring festival across Eurasia, showing that the legend of Kawa the Blacksmith and Demirci Korgan share deep metallurgical liberation motifs found in the Ergenekon epic.
3. **Rejection of Modern Balkanization**: Argues historically against modern attempts to partition Middle Eastern highland peoples into mutually hostile ethnic enclaves, emphasizing shared anti-imperialist destinies.
