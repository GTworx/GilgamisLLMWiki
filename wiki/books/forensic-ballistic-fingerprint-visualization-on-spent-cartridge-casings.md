## Executive Summary
Published in 2025 in forensic science and electrochemistry, this 22-page research treatise solves one of the most stubborn bottlenecks in criminal ballistics: recovering identifiable latent friction-ridge fingerprints from fired brass cartridge casings. Firing a firearm subjects brass casings to extreme thermal friction, expanding propellant gases, and mechanical abrasion inside the chamber, destroying organic sebaceous fingerprint residues. The authors develop a novel electrochemical deposition technique using conductive redox polymers (polypyrrole and polyaniline) that deposit selectively onto bare brass, creating a high-contrast negative image of the microscopic ridge sweat patterns that survived weapon discharge.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Overcoming Ballistic Thermal Degradation**: Chamber temperatures (>600°C) and violent mechanical ejection typically vaporize sebaceous fingerprint oils, but microscopic fatty acid corrosion pits remain etched into the brass surface.
- **Selective Electrodeposition Physics**: Applying a controlled electrical potential causes conductive polymers to deposit exclusively on bare metal surfaces while insulating corrosion pits remain uncoated, yielding sharp ridge contrast.
- **Homicide and Ballistics Investigation Breakthrough**: Enables forensic ballistics teams to link fired shell casings recovered from crime scenes directly to individual shooters with fingerprint clarity admissible in court.
- **Substrate Versatility**: Successfully visualizes latent prints on diverse alloys, including brass, copper, nickel-plated steel, and oxidized ammunition.
