## Summary
Monumental international compendium on electric bicycle adoption and urban micro-mobility, synthesizing 21 field studies, engineering reports, and market analyses—from NASA ultracapacitor baseline tests and the Asian Development Bank's study of China's 120-million e-bike fleet, to European State of the Nation surveys (Shimano/Velco) and comparative Dutch-Chinese modal shifts.

## Analytical Overview
Electric bicycles represent the most rapidly adopted and energy-efficient electric transport technology on earth, outpacing electric passenger cars in unit sales while delivering immediate urban air quality improvements, congestion reduction, and transport decarbonization. This comprehensive volume compiles 21 key international studies, field surveys, and engineering evaluations tracing the two-decade evolution of e-bikes from experimental niche vehicles into the backbone of urban micro-mobility.

The technical foundation begins with NASA Glenn Research Center's rigorous early testing (*NASA/TM-2001-211077*) evaluating electric bicycles powered by novel ultracapacitors and lead-acid/lithium chemistries, transitioning into the Asian Development Bank's landmark 94-page monograph on China (*Electric Bikes in the People’s Republic of China*). The ADB study documents how Chinese municipal motorcycle bans combined with local battery advancements drove an unprecedented surge to over 120 million e-bikes, providing affordable mobility for low-income commuters while eliminating millions of tons of transport emissions.

In Western markets, the volume incorporates foundational survey research by John MacArthur and Christopher Muhs at Portland State University (*E-Bikes in North America*), proving that e-bikes successfully replace car trips—with owners reporting that over 46% of their e-bike mileage directly substituted for solo passenger vehicle travel. This is complemented by European market studies from PwC Italy, Deloitte, Shimano’s 12-nation European *State of the Nation* report, Velco’s connected e-bike IoT frameworks, comparative academic research on e-bike transition trajectories in China versus the Netherlands, and Turkish shared micro-mobility feasibility studies across Konya and Istanbul.
