## Executive Summary

Launching the Pulitzer Prize–winning eleven-volume *The Story of Civilization* (1935–1975)—represented in this library by the full 1,150-page PDF of **Volume I: *Our Oriental Heritage*** (1942 Simon & Schuster printing, p. 1) alongside Internet Archive BookReader HTML references for all eleven volumes (*Our Oriental Heritage*, *The Life of Greece*, *Caesar and Christ*, *The Age of Faith*, *The Renaissance*, *The Reformation*, *The Age of Reason Begins*, *The Age of Louis XIV*, *The Age of Voltaire*, *Rousseau and Revolution*, and *The Age of Napoleon*)—**Will Durant** (with lifelong collaborator **Ariel Durant**) pioneers **"integral history"**: narrating economic life, politics, science, religion, philosophy, literature, and visual art together rather than as separate silos (Preface, pp. 5–6).

Deliberately beginning *outside* Europe in order to humble Western provincialism, *Our Oriental Heritage* opens with a foundational treatise on the **Elements of Civilization** (economic, political, moral, and mental foundations) before surveying **The Near East** (Sumer, Egypt, Babylonia, Assyria, Judea, Persia), **India and Her Neighbors** (Vedic, Buddhist, Mauryan, Gupta, Mughal, and colonial heritage), and **The Far East** (classical China and Japan).

---

## Core Historiographical Contributions

1. **Integral Cultural History**: Replaces narrow king-and-battle chronicles with a panoramic portrait of how daily labor, law codes (Hammurabi), poetry (*Gilgamesh*, *Mahabharata*, Li Bai), sculpture, and ethics co-create civilizations.
2. **Decentering Eurocentrism (1935)**: Insists generations before modern global history that Greece and Europe inherited vast foundations in mathematics, astronomy, architecture, statecraft, and ethical reflection from Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, and China.
3. **Literary Philosophy of History**: Celebrates the "brave stupidity" of attempting a synoptic view of human achievement (p. 6), making vast scholarship accessible and vivid for general readers.
