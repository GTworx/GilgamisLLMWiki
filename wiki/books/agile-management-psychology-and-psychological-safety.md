## Summary
Examines the psychological underpinnings of agile software and business teams, demonstrating that agile rituals fail without Amy Edmondson's psychological safety culture.

## Analytical Overview
### The Human Core of Agile Transformation

In *Çeviklik Psikolojisi: Organizasyonel Çeviklik İçin Psikolojik Güvenlik Kültürü*, organizational psychologists address why thousands of corporate agile transformations collapse into superficial 'cargo cult' ceremonies. While enterprises enthusiastically adopt Scrum sprints, daily standups, and Kanban boards, they routinely fail to achieve meaningful speed or innovation because they neglect the underlying social and psychological foundation: **Psychological Safety**.

### The Edmondson Paradigm Applied to Cross-Functional Teams

Drawing upon Harvard Business School professor Amy Edmondson's foundational models, the work demonstrates that:
- In the absence of psychological safety, employees will not admit mistakes, challenge flawed executive hypotheses, or offer novel, untested ideas during retrospectives.
- Agile rituals without interpersonal trust become instruments of micromanagement and performative compliance.
- High-performing agile squads treat failure as necessary diagnostic data rather than personal deficiency.

### Culture Architecture and Executive Coaching

The manual provides actionable leadership protocols for team leaders and Scrum masters: modeling vulnerability, replacing blame with curiosity during post-mortems, and engineering decentralized autonomous decision rights.
