## Executive Summary
This 124-page analytical compendium bridges macro-level global energy statistics with granular lithium-ion battery manufacturing forecasts. The first part features the Energy Institute's 72nd edition of the *Statistical Review of World Energy* (formerly BP Statistical Review), providing definitive historical data on planetary oil, gas, coal, nuclear, and renewable consumption, showing fossil fuels still accounted for 82% of primary energy in 2022. The second part presents Rethink Energy's *Global Battery Forecast 2023–2040*, projecting a 15-fold expansion in global battery manufacturing capacity (reaching 9 TWh annually by 2035), auditing cell chemistries (LFP, NMC, sodium-ion, solid-state) and cell-level cost deflation toward $50/kWh.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Primary Energy Stagnation**: Despite record solar and wind capacity additions, total global primary energy consumption grew faster, keeping overall fossil share above 80% through 2022.
- **Battery Gigafactory Expansion**: Global battery manufacturing capacity is expanding exponentially from 1.5 TWh in 2023 toward 9 TWh by 2035, driven by massive manufacturing clusters in China, North America, and Europe.
- **LFP Chemistry Dominance**: Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) has captured over 65% of global EV and stationary storage markets due to zero cobalt/nickel exposure, superior thermal stability, and longer cycle life.
- **Stationary Grid Storage Tipping Point**: As battery pack costs decline toward $60–$80/kWh, four-hour utility-scale battery storage becomes cheaper than building new open-cycle gas turbine peakers in nearly every major power market.
