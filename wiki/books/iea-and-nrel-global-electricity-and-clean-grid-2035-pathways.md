## Executive Summary
Uniting the International Energy Agency's (IEA) comprehensive *Electricity 2024* report (172 pp.) with the National Renewable Energy Laboratory's (NREL) landmark *100% Clean Electricity by 2035* study (210 pp.), this 382-page compendium provides the definitive technical and macroeconomic roadmap for global power sector decarbonization. The IEA analyzes soaring global electricity demand driven by AI data centers, industrial heat pumps, and electric vehicles, showing that low-emissions generation (renewables, nuclear) will meet all additional global demand growth through 2026. NREL models multiple capacity-expansion scenarios for achieving a zero-carbon US electric grid by 2035, detailing the requisite deployment rates of wind, solar, high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission, seasonal hydrogen storage, and clean firm capacity (nuclear, geothermal).

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Surging Global Power Demand**: Global electricity consumption is accelerating at 3.4% annually, with data centers, artificial intelligence, and cryptocurrency projected to double electricity consumption by 2026, reaching over 1,000 TWh.
- **NREL 2035 Deployment Velocity**: Reaching 100% clean electricity requires quadrupling the historical annual installation rate of solar and wind, deploying 40–90 GW of solar and 30–50 GW of wind every year through 2035.
- **The Critical Transmission Buildout**: Interconnecting geographically dispersed renewable generation demands expanding national inter-regional transmission capacity by 200–300%, requiring radical modernization of federal permitting.
- **Clean Firm Capacity Mandate**: While wind and solar provide 60–80% of generation, the final 10–20% of grid decarbonization requires dispatchable clean firm capacity (advanced nuclear, enhanced geothermal, hydrogen combustion turbines) to maintain adequacy during multi-day renewable lulls (dunkelflaute).
