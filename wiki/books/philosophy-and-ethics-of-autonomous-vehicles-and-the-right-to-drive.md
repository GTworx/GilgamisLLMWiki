## Executive Summary
Published in *Philosophy & Technology* (2021 and 2022), this two-part philosophical treatise dissects the moral, legal, and political philosophy of autonomous mobility. The first paper critiques the popular fascination with abstract 'trolley problems', demonstrating that real-world AV ethics centers instead on probabilistic risk distribution, fault allocation, and 'moral crumple zones' (where human safety drivers are unfairly blamed for algorithmic systemic failures). The second paper addresses the fundamental political question: In an era where autonomous fleets statistically surpass human drivers in safety, do citizens retain a moral 'right to drive', or should manual human driving be restricted or banned on public roads as an impermissible negative externality?

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Critique of Trolley Problem Fallacies**: Autonomous vehicle software does not encounter discrete binary kill-choices; it continuously evaluates continuous probabilistic risk fields across pedestrians, cyclists, and occupants.
- **Moral Crumple Zones**: Highlights the ethical injustice of holding human backup drivers criminally liable for sudden system takeovers that human reaction times cannot biologically resolve.
- **The Political Right to Drive**: Evaluates whether human driving is a fundamental liberty of self-determination or a recreational activity that must yield to collective public safety once AVs prove statistically superior.
- **Algorithmic Risk Allocation**: Shows that programming an AV to avoid large trucks may inherently shift collision risks toward vulnerable road users (cyclists, pedestrians), requiring explicit public ethical frameworks.

## Key Data Points & Excerpts

> "The ethical challenge of autonomous driving is not deciding who to kill in an unavoidable crash, but deciding how to distribute background risk across society before crashes occur."

> "If autonomous vehicles eliminate 90% of highway fatalities, manual human driving will shift from being perceived as a fundamental right of mobility to being viewed as an intolerable public hazard."
