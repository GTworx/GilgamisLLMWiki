## Summary
Authoritative research collection by MIT chemical engineer Emre Gençer establishing the SESAME life cycle emissions modeling environment, round-the-clock solar/biomass hybrid power systems, and empirical evaluations of marginal grid variations from electric vehicle charging patterns.

## Analytical Overview
This volume brings together the foundational peer-reviewed contributions of Emre Gençer and his research group at the MIT Energy Initiative, advancing the rigorous engineering science of full life cycle assessment (LCA) and multi-sector energy modeling. The cornerstone of the work is the development of the Sustainable Energy System Analysis Modeling Environment (SESAME), an open-source, modular, bottom-up life cycle analysis platform that dynamically calculates greenhouse gas footprints across electricity generation, industrial manufacturing, synthetic transportation fuels, and carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS) pathways.

In seminal papers published in *PNAS* and *Energy Policy*, Gençer and Rakesh Agrawal model synergistic hybrid configurations capable of solving the core intermittency hurdle of solar energy: providing 100% round-the-clock baseload electrical power and liquid transportation fuels through the co-utilization of concentrated solar thermal energy, high-temperature electrolysis, biomass gasification, and closed-loop carbon recycling. The system completely bypasses massive battery requirements while capturing and reusing biogenic CO2.

Furthermore, in a landmark empirical study published in *Environmental Science & Technology*, Gençer and colleagues analyze hourly power grid variations across regional transmission organizations (such as PJM and CAISO) to assess the true operating emissions generated by electric vehicle (EV) fleet charging. The study conclusively demonstrates that charging an EV during late-evening coal-heavy marginal baseload hours generates significantly higher carbon emissions than smart midday charging aligned with peak solar generation, providing essential quantitative data for dynamic EV charging rate designs.
