## Summary
Two-volume 103-page comparative study by 1776 and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation ranking 25 major American cities on their capacity to cultivate startups in regulated sectors (healthcare, energy, civic tech, smart cities).

## Analytical Overview
### Regulated Tech and Urban Innovation Ecosystems

*Innovation That Matters (2016–2017)*, published collaboratively by global incubator 1776 and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation, provides an in-depth comparative benchmark of how 25 major U.S. metropolitan areas nurture startups tackling heavily regulated, institutional industries: healthcare, clean energy, public education, and smart urban infrastructure.

### Moving Beyond Consumer Software Dogma

The report highlights that while pure software thrives in unregulated spaces, the greatest future economic value resides in transforming complex, regulated legacy systems:
- Cities like Boston, Austin, Denver, and Washington, D.C. excel in regulated sectors because they combine technical talent with deep proximity to regulatory agencies, hospital systems, and municipal utilities.
- The index evaluates cities across five metrics: talent availability, capital density, regulatory hospitality, municipal government partnership, and local corporate engagement.

### Blueprints for Civic Innovation

The volumes offer municipal leaders and corporate executives concrete playbooks for establishing civic sandboxes and open government procurement pipelines.
