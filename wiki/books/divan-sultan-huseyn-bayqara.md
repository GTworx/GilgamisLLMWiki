## Executive Summary

Published under the auspices of **TürkSOY** and the Azerbaijan National Academy of Sciences (AMEA) Institute of Folklore—transposed into Azerbaijani Turkic with an extensive historical introduction by **Ramiz Asker** and edited by Prof. Dr. Rafael Hüseynov (Baku, MBM, 2011, 248 pp., p. 2)—this critical literary edition gathers the complete **Chagatai Turkic *Divan*** (ghazals, takhmis, mukhammas, rubaiyat, and qit'as) and prose **Risala** of Timurid sovereign and poet **Sultan Hüseyn Bayqara** (1438–1506, pen name *Hüseyni*).

Asker's historical essay *"Qılınc və Qələm Sultanı Hüseyn Bayqara"* (pp. 3–25) situates Bayqara as the patron-ruler of the **Timurid Renaissance in Herat** (reigning 1469–1506), whose court fostered his lifelong friend and vizier **Ali-Shir Nava'i**, Persian master **Jami**, and miniature painter **Bihzad**—championing Turkic as a literary language of supreme refinement alongside Persian.

---

## Cultural & Literary Significance

1. **The Timurid Herat Renaissance**: Chronicles how Bayqara—praised even by his rival Zahir al-Din Babur in the *Baburnama* for unmatched swordsmanship and cultural brilliance (p. 4)—turned Herat into the artistic, philosophical, and architectural capital of late fifteenth-century Central Asia.
2. **Turkic Literary Sovereignty**: Alongside Ali-Shir Nava'i, Bayqara's Chagatai ghazals and his *Risala* defended and exemplified the expressive musicality of classical Turkic verse across love mysticism, royal ethics, and elegiac reflection.
3. **Pan-Turkic Philological Adaptation**: Prepared following the 2009 Istanbul TürkSOY international symposium on cross-dialectal Turkic adaptation (p. 2), preserving classical Chagatai heritage for contemporary Azerbaijani and Turkish readers.
