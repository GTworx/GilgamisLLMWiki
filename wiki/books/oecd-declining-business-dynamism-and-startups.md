## Analytical Overview
### The Global Paradox of Declining Startup Dynamism

*Declining Business Dynamism: Structural Barriers, Market Concentration, and the Global Startup Deficit*, authored by the OECD Directorate for Science, Technology and Innovation, investigates an alarming structural trend across North America, Europe, and Asia: despite unprecedented technological advances, startup creation rates and business dynamism have steadily declined for over three decades.

### Macroeconomic Symptoms of Stagnation

The report documents four mutually reinforcing structural headwinds:
1. **Falling Startup Rates:** The proportion of new firms within national economies has fallen by over 30% since the late 1980s, creating an aging corporate stock.
2. **Rising Market Concentration:** Dominant superstar firms have expanded their market share and profit markups, erecting insurmountable barriers to entry for newcomers.
3. **Slower Technology Diffusion:** Frontier technological innovations are increasingly trapped within a small cluster of elite firms, failing to diffuse to median enterprises.
4. **Labor Mobility Calcification:** Reduced rates of job-to-job worker reallocation, trapping human capital in low-productivity incumbents.

### Policy Prescriptions for Revitalization

The OECD provides structural recommendations, including modernizing competition policy, eliminating non-compete agreements, and dismantling occupational licensing restrictions.
