## Executive Summary

Published in 2003 by HarperCollins (p. 233), *Rumi: The Book of Love* is Coleman Barks's focused thematic anthology gathering Rumi's ecstatic love lyrics (*ishq*)—drawn from *Divan-i Shams-i Tabrizi*, the *Masnavi*, and quatrains translated in collaboration with John Moyne, Nevit Ergin, A. J. Arberry, Reynold Nicholson, and M. G. Gupta (p. 3)—interleaved with Barks's reflective prose commentaries on the "magnificent regions of the heart" (p. 5).

Where *The Essential Rumi* spans Rumi's full didactic and parabolic range, *The Book of Love* narrows its lens to spiritual longing, *sohbet* (transformative mystical conversation), surrender of ego, and the paradox of tavern madness—placing Rumi in explicit poetic dialogue with global contemplatives cited in the credits including Rainer Maria Rilke, Bashō, W. H. Auden, Sappho, and Thich Nhat Hanh (pp. 231–232).

## Thematic Progression (Contents, pp. 5–7)

1. **Spontaneous Wandering (pp. 1–9)**: Opens with the disorientation of falling out of calculative habit into radical presence (*The Many Wines*, *In your light I learn how to love*).
2. **Sohbet: Who You Talking To? (pp. 10–18)**: Explores mystical companionship (*sohbet*)—the intimate relational alchemy forged between Rumi and Shams of Tabriz.
3. **Sudden Wholeness & Escaping into Silence (pp. 25–40)**: Contrasts fragmented "half-loves" against total absorption in the Beloved (*The clear bead at the center changes everything*, *The Waterwheel*, *Two Days of Silence*).
4. **The Allure of Love & Tavern Madness (pp. 49–60+)**: Celebrates emotional honesty (*The Purpose of Emotion*, *The ground's generosity takes in our compost*) and the holy folly that transcends narrow rationalism (*Real value comes with madness*, *A Cap to Wear in Both Worlds*).

---

## Key Mystical Themes

- **Love as Ontological Engine**: Across Barks's selections, *ishq* is never mere sentimental romance; it is an all-consuming force that burns away egoic defense mechanisms and reveals interconnectedness.
- **Dialogic Cosmopolitanism**: By juxtaposing Rumi's 13th-century Anatolian lyrics with Zen haiku, Rilke's *Duino Elegies*, and Buddhist mindfulness (pp. 231–232), the volume illustrates how Rumi entered late-20th-century global contemplative culture.
