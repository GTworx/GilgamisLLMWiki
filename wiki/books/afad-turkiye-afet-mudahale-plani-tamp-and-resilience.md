## Executive Summary

This 95-page research compendium pairs the foundational statutory document of the Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency—the Türkiye Disaster Response Plan (TAMP 2014, 65 pp.)—with the critical scholarly evaluation by Dr. Ali Utku Şahin published in the *Resilience Journal* (2020, 30 pp.). TAMP defines the principles of emergency coordination, operational command structures, and service group logistics required across all phases of disaster response.

The operational architecture of TAMP is organized around 28 Disaster Service Groups (e.g., Search and Rescue, Health, Debris Removal, Shelter, Nutrition, Psychosocial Support) distributed across national and local levels. Dr. Şahin's academic critique evaluates TAMP through public administration and systems resilience theories, analyzing inter-agency coordination frictions, information bottlenecks, operational agility, and decentralized decision-making during catastrophic multi-provincial emergencies.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Modular Incident Command Architecture**: Details the activation criteria for national emergency levels (Level 1 local to Level 4 international assistance), establishing clear command hierarchies under AFAD leadership.
- **Service Group Specialization**: Structures disaster intervention into specialized functional groups, establishing standard operating procedures (SOPs) for mutual logistics, communication networks, and personnel mobilization.
- **Resilience and Coordination Pathologies**: Identifies critical vulnerabilities in communication redundancy, horizontal coordination between central ministries and local municipalities, and volunteer spontaneous surge management.
- **Transition to Recovery**: Outlines mechanisms for transitioning from initial acute search-and-rescue (first 72 hours) to temporary shelter, infrastructure restitution, and economic recovery.
