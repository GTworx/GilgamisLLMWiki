## Executive Summary

Produced by **Dorling Kindersley (DK)** in partnership with the **Smithsonian Institution** and authored by specialist academic consultants including **Jane McIntosh** (Cambridge, 8 MYA–700 BCE), **Neville Morley** (Bristol, 700 BCE–599 CE), **Roger Collins** (Edinburgh, 600–1449), and **R. G. Grant** (modern era, pp. 6–7; 514 pp.), *Timelines of History* offers a richly illustrated, globally synchronized chronological atlas spanning human origins to the twenty-first century.

Divided into seven overarching epochs (Outline, p. 46)—**Human Origins**, **Early Civilizations**, **The Classical Age**, **Trade & Invention** (600–1450), **Reformation & Exploration** (1450–1750), **The Age of Revolution** (1750–1914), and **Technology & Superpowers** (1914–present)—the book weaves parallel regional timelines with thematic visual dossiers (*The Story of Writing, Metalworking, Money, Printing, Astronomy, Arms and Armor, Navigation, Agriculture, Steam Power, Medicine, Electricity, Flight*).

---

## Core Reference Value

1. **Synchronized Global Chronology**: Allows readers to see simultaneous developments across Africa, the Americas, Asia, the Islamic World, and Europe side by side—countering isolated national histories.
2. **Material & Technological Threads**: Highlights general-purpose technologies (metallurgy, coinage, printing, steam, electricity, aviation) as recurring inflection points reshaping empires and everyday life.
3. **Visual Artifact Integration**: Pairs concise dates with museum artifacts, maps, and architectural spreads, serving as a rapid cross-checking reference alongside deeper narrative histories.
