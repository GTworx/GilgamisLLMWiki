## Executive Summary

First published in 1923 by financial journalist **Edwin Lefèvre** (p. 7) as a thinly fictionalized first-person biography of legendary Wall Street speculator **Jesse Lauriston Livermore** ("Larry Livingston"), *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* remains the undisputed classic of market psychology, tape-reading, and speculative risk management a century later. This illustrated edition opens with archival photographs tracing Livermore's gilded peak and tragic 1940 suicide (pp. 2–5), framing the timeless lessons of speculative mastery against the personal fragility of unchecked leverage.

Tracing Livingston's evolution from a teenage quotation-board boy beating New England **bucket shops** to executing historic short campaigns during the **Panic of 1907** and navigating commodity corners in cotton and wheat, the book distills an enduring truth: Wall Street never changes because human emotions—hope, fear, greed, and impatience—never change.

## Timeless Market-Psychology Principles

1. **The Big Money Is in the Sitting**: Livingston learns that rapid day-trading scalps pale beside identifying major secular market trends and holding winning positions patiently through routine pullbacks ("being right and sitting tight").
2. **Never Average Down on a Loser**: Cut losses swiftly when market action contradicts your thesis, and scale *into* confirmed winners ("pyramiding") rather than fighting the tape out of ego.
3. **Beware Inside Tips & Crowds**: Tips from insiders—even sincere ones—bypass independent judgment and leave traders paralyzed when conditions shift; price action and general market conditions supersede stories.
4. **Liquidity & Market Manipulation**: Vividly exposes early-20th-century syndicate pools, brokerage front-running, and the critical difference between trading small lots and managing massive institutional size without moving the market against yourself.
