## Executive Summary

Written by Université Laval experimental psychologist **Simon Grondin**—former editor of the *Canadian Journal of Experimental Psychology* (Springer, 2016, 162 pp., pp. 4–12)—*Psychology of Perception* is a crisp, scientifically rigorous textbook surveying how physical energy (light waves and sound pressure) is transduced, organized, and interpreted into conscious sensory experience.

Moving systematically from classical **Psychophysics** through auditory and visual neuroscience to cognitive **Attention** (TOC pp. 8–11), Grondin anchors perceptual psychology in formal quantitative laws (**Weber's Law**, **Fechner's Law**, **Stevens's Power Law**, and **Signal Detection Theory** with ROC curves, Ch. 1 & Appendices A–B), clinical sensory deficits (agnosia, deafness, blindness), and experimental illusions.

---

## Eight-Chapter Scientific Architecture

1. **Psychophysics & Signal Detection (Ch. 1, pp. 13–28)**: Separates pure sensory sensitivity ($d'$) from observer decision criterion ($eta$), detailing constant stimuli, limits, adjustment, and adaptive staircase thresholds.
2. **Auditory Physics, Biology & Speech/Music Perception (Ch. 2–3, pp. 29–62)**: Traces sound waves through the cochlea into auditory streaming, directional/distance localization, musical pitch/timbre, and phonetic speech perception.
3. **Visual Pathways, Color, Form & Depth (Ch. 4–7, pp. 63–129)**: Covers retinal photoreceptors, visual cortex receptive fields, trichromatic vs. opponent-process color theory, lateral inhibition (Mach bands), Gestalt grouping, spatial frequency channels, binocular disparity, perceptual constancy (Gibson's ecological view), and optical illusions (the Moon Illusion).
4. **Attention & Selective Filtering (Ch. 8, pp. 130–142)**: Analyzes inattentional/change blindness, spatial-temporal orienting, visual search, and clinical neglect.
