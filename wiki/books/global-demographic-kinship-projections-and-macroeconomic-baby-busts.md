## Summary
Groundbreaking PNAS and macroeconomic study projecting global human kinship networks to 2100 across all countries, modeling how the rapid contraction of family size ('kinship loss') and declining fertility reshape capital-labor ratios.

## Analytical Overview
Models the global transition from horizontal kinship networks (siblings, cousins) to narrow vertical structures ('beanpole families'). Demonstrates that while falling fertility initially creates a transitory 'growth boom' by lowering child dependency ratios and boosting capital per worker, prolonged sub-replacement fertility leads to acute fiscal crises in elderly care and sovereign debt dynamics.
