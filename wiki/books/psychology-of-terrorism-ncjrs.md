## Executive Summary

Directed by University of South Florida behavioral scientist **Dr. Randy Borum**—with senior project advisors Robert Fein & Bryan Vossekuil and international terrorism scholars Martha Crenshaw, John Horgan, and Andrew Silke (pp. 2–3)—*Psychology of Terrorism* (2004, NCJ 208551, 221 pp.) is a rigorous U.S. federal social-science synthesis and annotated bibliography auditing peer-reviewed psychological and behavioral research on non-state political violence across seven major academic databases (*PsychInfo, Medline, SocioFile, NCJRS, CJ Abstracts, PAIS, CJPI*, pp. 2–3).

Overturning popular stereotypes that terrorists suffer from clinical psychosis or singular "terrorist personalities," the synthesis documents a striking empirical consensus: most militants are psychologically normal individuals drawn into political violence through gradual **radicalization pathways**, group-identity dynamics, perceived grievances, and ideological moral disengagement.

---

## Core Empirical & Operational Findings

1. **Preconditions vs. Precipitants (pp. 4–5)**: Draws on the 2003 Oslo international expert panel to separate long-term structural **preconditions** (alienation, state repression, cultural humiliation) from immediate **precipitants** (provocative events or charismatic recruitment triggers) that ignite violent mobilization.
2. **Pathways & Group Dynamics Over Trait Profiles**: Shifts counterterrorism analysis away from failed "terrorist profiling" toward studying how underground cells enforce conformity, isolate members from outside feedback, and dehumanize out-group targets.
3. **Structured Research Reference**: Pairs author abstracts, editor annotations, and key-quote summaries across hundreds of foundational studies to ground policy and threat assessment in empirical evidence rather than conjecture.
