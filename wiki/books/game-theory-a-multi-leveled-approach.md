## Executive Summary

Written by Maastricht University quantitative-economics professor **Hans Peters** (2nd revised edition, Springer, 2015, 493 pp., pp. 1–5), *Game Theory: A Multi-Leveled Approach* is a crystal-clear, mathematically rigorous textbook designed to serve both introductory economics students (**Part I: Thinking Strategically**) and graduate researchers (**Part II** advanced formal proofs and extensions).

Bridging **noncooperative** and **cooperative** game theory in one unified volume (TOC pp. 12–18), Peters walks step by step through zero-sum matrix games, Nash equilibrium, extensive-form backward induction, **Subgame Perfect** and **Perfect Bayesian Equilibria**, Bayesian incomplete-information signaling games, **Cournot/Bertrand/Stackelberg** oligopoly models, auction theory, **Rubinstein sequential bargaining**, repeated-game Folk Theorems, **Evolutionarily Stable Strategies (ESS)** & replicator dynamics, and cooperative **Transferable Utility (TU)** solution concepts (**the Core**, **the Shapley Value**, and **the Nucleolus**).

---

## Comprehensive Strategic Toolkit

1. **Noncooperative Foundations (Ch. 2–6)**: Moves seamlessly from $2 	imes 2$ dominance and mixed-strategy equilibria to dynamic entry deterrence, Spence-style signaling games (Intuitive Criterion), double auctions, and alternating-offer bargaining.
2. **Repeated & Evolutionary Games (Ch. 7–8)**: Proves Folk Theorems supporting cooperation in infinitely repeated Prisoner's Dilemmas and connects Maynard Smith's ESS directly to continuous replicator dynamics.
3. **Cooperative Allocation & Fair Division (Ch. 9+)**: Formalizes coalition formation and fair payoff distribution using the Core, Shapley Value, and Nucleolus—providing rigorous mathematical tools for cost-sharing, voting power, and multi-party bargaining.
