## Executive Summary

Authored by renowned financial economist **Ludovic Phalippou** (Professor of Financial Economics at Saïd Business School, University of Oxford, writing in *Financial Times Alphaville*), this scathing, mathematically rigorous critique exposes how **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**—the primary marketing and compensation metric of the private equity industry—is systematically distorted, manipulated, and misunderstood. Phalippou demonstrates that IRR inherently presumes that all intermediate cash flows can be reinvested at the same high rate of return (the reinvestment rate fallacy) and reveals how private equity sponsors artificially juice headline IRRs by utilizing **Subscription Credit Lines (capital call facilities)** to delay capital drawdowns from LPs, creating the optical illusion of stellar returns without creating a single extra dollar of actual wealth.

---

## Key Strategic & Financial Takeaways

- **The Mathematical Reinvestment Flaw of IRR**: IRR is not an annualized return equivalent to a public equity compound annual growth rate (CAGR); it mathematically assumes that interim distributions are continuously reinvested at the fund's IRR (e.g., 25% compounded for ten years), an assumption that is economically impossible in real markets.
- **The Subscription Line IRR Hack**: By borrowing against LP capital commitments from banks (subscription credit lines) to fund investments instead of calling LP capital immediately, GPs compress the holding period from the LP's perspective from 3 years to 1 year, artificially doubling or tripling the calculated IRR while leaving the actual cash-on-cash return (MOIC) unchanged.
- **Total Value to Paid-In (TVPI) vs. Net IRR**: Proves that Total Value to Paid-In (TVPI / Multiple on Invested Capital, MOIC) and Public Market Equivalent (PME) metrics provide vastly more honest reflections of true economic value creation than IRR.
- **Distortion of Compensation and Carry Incentives**: Because carried interest hurdles are typically set at an 8% IRR, gaming the IRR timing allows fund managers to clear hurdle rates faster and extract carried interest fees prematurely on unrealized interim paper gains.
- **Institutional LP Governance Failures**: Critiques pension fund trustees, investment consultants, and allocators for relying on headline IRRs in marketing materials to justify high management fees (2%) and carry (20%) when net-of-fee returns of median PE funds have converged with public index benchmarks.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations

- "IRR is the dumbest performance metric in modern finance, yet it remains the supreme metric that bamboozles institutional investors. It is easily gamed, economically illiterate, and tells you almost nothing about how much wealth was actually generated." (source: The Delusion of Private Equity IRRs, p. 1)
- "By borrowing from a bank for six months instead of calling money from an LP, a private equity firm can turn a 15% investment into a 35% headline IRR. No extra value was created; the only thing manufactured was an optical illusion designed to trigger carried interest." (source: The Delusion of Private Equity IRRs, p. 6)
