## Summary
ITIF public policy study proposing the creation of 'Innovation Orchards'—shared physical prototyping facilities, wet labs, and scale-up manufacturing testbeds to de-risk hard-tech and hardware startups.

## Analytical Overview
### Overcoming the Hardware and Deep-Tech Financing Chasm

In *Innovation Orchards: Producing the Startups of the Future*, the Information Technology and Innovation Foundation (ITIF) addresses a critical blind spot in American innovation policy: while digital software and internet startups flourish under standard venture capital models, hard-tech startups—developing advanced hardware, robotics, clean energy, and materials science—face an almost insurmountable "Valley of Death."

### The Concept of the "Innovation Orchard"

ITIF proposes establishing publicly and privately co-funded **Innovation Orchards**:
- **Shared Capital-Intensive Equipment:** Providing early-stage founders with subsidized access to cleanrooms, 5-axis CNC machining, gene synthesizers, and electron microscopes.
- **De-Risking Pre-Commercial Prototypes:** Enabling hardware startups to advance from Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 3 (laboratory proof-of-concept) to TRL 7 (operational prototype demonstration) without burning millions in early dilutive venture equity.
- **Mentorship in Manufacturing Scaling:** Supplying veteran manufacturing engineers to help academic scientists design for manufacturability (DFM).

### Federal and State Policy Agenda

The report establishes concrete legislative recommendations for the U.S. Congress and state governments to fund shared physical infrastructure.
