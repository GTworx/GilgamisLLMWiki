## Executive Summary

Part of Harvard Business Review Press's flagship curated anthology series, *HBR's 10 Must Reads on Teams* (2013, pp. 2–6) compiles ten landmark research articles dissecting why most working groups underperform—and what differentiates truly high-performing, innovative, and collaborative teams across corporate, technical, and global settings.

Uniting behavioral sensor research, organizational sociology, and motivational psychology, the anthology pairs **Jon R. Katzenbach & Douglas K. Smith's** canonical *The Discipline of Teams* (distinguishing real teams with mutual accountability from mere working groups) with **Alex "Sandy" Pentland's** sociometric data-science study *The New Science of Building Great Teams* (showing that communication energy, engagement, and balanced turn-taking predict team performance far better than individual IQ), **J. Richard Hackman's** candid warnings on when *not* to use teams (*Why Teams Don't Work*), **Lynda Gratton & Tamara J. Erickson's** *Eight Ways to Build Collaborative Teams*, and **Teresa M. Amabile & Steven J. Kramer's** *The Power of Small Wins* (p. 6).

---

## Core Operational Takeaways

1. **Communication Geometry Beats Individual Pedigree**: Pentland's empirical badges demonstrate that equal speaking distribution, energetic peer-to-peer dialogue, and external exploration outpredict raw credentials.
2. **Mutual Accountability & Clear Boundaries**: Katzenbach & Smith and Hackman converge on the same structural prerequisite: small size, compelling shared purpose, explicit performance metrics, and psychological safety.
3. **The Progress Principle**: Amabile & Kramer prove that daily incremental progress on meaningful work is the single strongest catalyst of inner work life and team morale.
