## Executive Summary

Curated and introduced by **Frances Hazlitt** and noted libertarian economist-philosopher **Henry Hazlitt** (1984, University Press of America, pp. 1–3), *The Wisdom of the Stoics* distills the three great Roman Stoic voices—**Seneca**, **Epictetus** (including the complete *Enchiridion*), and **Marcus Aurelius**—into a single thematic handbook designed for modern readers seeking moral clarity and psychological steadiness.

---

## Anthology Architecture (Contents, p. 3)

1. **Introduction**: Explains Stoicism's enduring appeal across two millennia—uniting an imperial minister (Seneca), a freed slave (Epictetus), and a Roman emperor (Marcus Aurelius) in one egalitarian ethic where external rank is utterly irrelevant to virtue.
2. **Seneca & Epictetus (*Discourses* + Complete *Enchiridion*)**: Contrasts Seneca's literary elegance with Epictetus's bracing directness on the Dichotomy of Control.
3. **Marcus Aurelius**: Closes with curated meditations on duty, impermanence, and cosmopolitan fellowship.
