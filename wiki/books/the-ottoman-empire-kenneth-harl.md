## Executive Summary

Taught by Tulane University Professor of Classical & Byzantine History **Kenneth W. Harl**, *The Ottoman Empire* (The Great Courses 36-lecture master course guidebook, 303 pp.) delivers an authoritative collegiate survey of Ottoman statecraft, institutions, and civilization from the nomadic frontiers of fourteenth-century Anatolia to the empire's collapse in 1922, emphasizing how the Ottomans inherited and synthesized Roman/Byzantine administrative bureaucracy, Persian imperial court traditions, and Islamic sacred law (*sharia*).

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Third Rome Thesis**: Harl frames the Ottoman Empire as the direct political and geographical successor to the Eastern Roman Empire, demonstrating how Mehmed II consciously adopted *Kayser-i Rum* (Caesar of Rome).
2. **Institutional Flexibility**: The empire's longevity stemmed not from rigid religious fanaticism, but from pragmatic legal dualism—balancing divine *Sharia* with sultanic statutory law (*Kanun*).
3. **The Janissary Meritorious Elite**: Details how the *devshirme* system insulated the throne from aristocratic bloodlines, creating a hyper-capable military and administrative caste unique in early modern statecraft.
