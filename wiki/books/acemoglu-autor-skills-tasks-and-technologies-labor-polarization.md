## Summary
Daron Acemoglu and David Autor's monumental 154-page NBER Handbook of Labor Economics treatise establishing the 'Task-Based Framework' of technological change. Dismantling the standard Skill-Biased Technological Change (SBTC) model, the authors formalize how automation replaces workers in routine cognitive and manual tasks, hollowing out middle-class jobs and driving severe wage polarization.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
Throughout the late 20th century, labor economists explained rising wage inequality via the simple SBTC model: computers complement college-educated workers and substitute for uneducated workers. However, by the 2000s, empirical data revealed an unexpected phenomenon: 'job polarization'—employment expanded rapidly in high-wage analytical jobs and low-wage service jobs, while crashing in middle-skill manufacturing and clerical jobs. Acemoglu and Autor formulated a new macroeconomic foundation to explain this reality.

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
The Acemoglu-Autor framework establishes a critical conceptual distinction: 'skills' are worker endowments, whereas 'tasks' are units of work production. A production process requires a continuum of tasks. Capital/software and labor of different skills compete to perform these tasks based on comparative advantage. The model incorporates three forces: 1) The Displacement Effect (capital takes over tasks previously performed by labor); 2) The Productivity Effect (automation reduces costs, expanding overall economic demand); and 3) The Reinstatement Effect (creation of entirely new tasks where labor has a comparative advantage).

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
The authors analyze four decades of US Census and CPS data, proving that computerization specifically displaced routine tasks (bookkeeping, machine operation, clerical coordination), while leaving manual non-routine tasks (food service, home health) and non-routine analytical tasks (engineering, management) intact. This structural dynamic decimated median manufacturing wages while creating intense wage dispersion across educational cohorts.

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
The Acemoglu-Autor task framework is universally recognized as the single most influential paradigm in modern labor economics. It forms the indispensable intellectual benchmark for analyzing the labor market disruptions of contemporary artificial intelligence and agentic automation.
