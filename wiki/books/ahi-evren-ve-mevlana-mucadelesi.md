## Executive Summary

In this pioneering, intensely researched, and controversial revisionist study, Seljuk historian **Prof. Dr. Mikâil Bayram** unearths the profound political, social, and ideological conflict in thirteenth-century Seljuk Anatolia between the artisan guild leader **Ahi Evren (Şeyh Nasîrüddin Mahmûd el-Hôyî)** and mystic poet **Mevlânâ Celâleddîn-i Rûmî**. Bayram demonstrates that behind the spiritual and literary hagiographies lay a fierce socio-political struggle during the Mongol invasions: Ahi Evren leading the patriotic Turkmen artisans, crafts-guilds (*Ahilik*), and Bâbâî dervishes in anti-Mongol armed resistance, while Mevlânâ and the aristocratic palace establishment accommodated Mongol suzerainty and Mu'înüddin Süleyman Pervâne.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Class and Geopolitical Fault Line in Seljuk Anatolia**: Contrasts the urban-artisan Turkmen populism of the Ahis (demanding independence and social solidarity) against the Persianate palace aristocracy's pragmatic appeasement of Mongol imperial overlords.
2. **Nasreddin Hoca as Historical Persona**: Presents revolutionary archival evidence equating Nasreddin Hoca with Ahi Evren, whose humorous folk tales served as coded political satire against oppressive Mongol tax collectors and collaborating judges.
3. **The Martyrdom of Anatolian Craftsmen**: Reconstructs the brutal 1261 punitive expedition led by Mongol-backed Pervâne against Kırşehir, where Ahi Evren and hundreds of guild leaders were slaughtered.
