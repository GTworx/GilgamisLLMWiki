## Executive Summary

*Fusûl-ı Âşıkân* ("Chapters/Gatherings of the Minstrels") is a monumental 880-page critical transcription and scholarly study edited by Prof. Dr. Eyüp Akman and published by the Atatürk Culture Center (AKM) in 2024 (pp. 4–5). Preserved for over a century by descendants of its 19th-century compiler, **Kastamonulu İshak-zâde Hasan Fevzî** (1857–1917), the manuscript *mecmua* (compiled between 1877 and 1906) documents the thriving minstrel (*âşık*), classical Divan, Sufi (*tasavvuf*), and musical *fasıl* culture of late-Ottoman Kastamonu—a city hailed by folklorist İhsan Ozanoğlu as the "Kaaba of the *âşık*s" (pp. 4, 15, 17).

Because Hasan Fevzî carefully noted the specific musical compositions and *makam* modes accompanying individual poems, Akman observes that *Fusûl-ı Âşıkân* functions simultaneously as an *âşık* anthology, a previously untapped repository of unpublished classical poems, and a *güfte mecmuası* (lyric-music repertoire book) enriching Turkish musicology (pp. 14–15).

## Structural Overview

- **Giriş (pp. 1–4)**: Surveys 19th-century Kastamonu minstrel lineages, including Erzurumlu Emrah's formative sojourn in Kastamonu and his disciples Kemâlî, Meydânî, and the Dertli–Figânî–Nâilî–Yorgansız Hakkı tradition (pp. 17–18).
- **Bölüm I — Âşık Fevzi'nin Hayatı, Eğitimi, Tarikatı, Şairliği ve Eserleri (pp. 5–18)**: Reconstructs Hasan Fevzî's biography, Sufi affiliation, poetic craft, and broader manuscript oeuvre (including his *Hâtırât-ı Fevzi*).
- **Bölüm II — Nüsha, Dil ve İmla Özellikleri & Âşık Fasılları / Beste-Makam Kayıtları (pp. 19–46)**: Describes physical codicology, orthography, performance structure of *âşık fasılları*, and musical *makam* notations (pp. 7, 14–15).
- **Bölüm III — Fihrist, Tam Metin, Kaynaklar, Ekler ve Dizin (pp. 47–861)**: Provides an indexed table of contents (pp. 47–94), the complete Latinized transcription of the manuscript with red-ink rubrics rendered in bold and cross-references (*krş.* / *Bkz.*) to published parallels (pp. 14, 95–818), bibliography, facsimiles/appendices, and comprehensive index (pp. 819–861).

## Key Scholarly Contributions

1. **Bridging Folk, Divan, and Sufi Worlds**: Rather than treating *halk edebiyatı* (folk minstrelsy), *divan edebiyatı* (court/literate verse), and *tasavvuf* (mystical poetry) as airtight silos, *Fusûl-ı Âşıkân* reveals how 19th-century provincial literati seamlessly inhabited and performed all three registers together.
2. **Musicological Preservation**: Recording *beste* and *makam* cues above lyric entries preserves crucial evidence of how *âşık* gatherings intersected with Ottoman classical music traditions (p. 15).
3. **Text-Centered Philology**: Akman defends manuscript-first (*cönk* and *mecmua*) fieldwork as the indispensable bedrock for Anatolian literary historiography, rescuing provincial voices that national canons routinely overlook (pp. 15, 18).
