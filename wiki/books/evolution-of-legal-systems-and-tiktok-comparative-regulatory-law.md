## Executive Summary
This two-part legal philosophy and comparative regulatory dossier traces the broad evolutionary arc of legal traditions alongside modern digital sovereignty conflicts. The first component charts the historical divergence between Roman Civil Law (codified statutes, inquisitorial procedure) and English Common Law (judicial precedent, adversarial trials, equity), examining how legal origins shape modern corporate governance, investor protection, and economic growth. The second component delivers a comparative legal analysis of global regulatory crackdowns on ByteDance's TikTok—contrasting the United States' national security divestiture mandates (Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act) with EU Digital Services Act (DSA) algorithmic transparency audits and India's outright sovereign ban under Section 69A of the IT Act.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Civil Law vs. Common Law Evolutionary Divergence**: Analyzes how English common law's reliance on judicial precedent offers superior flexibility for financial innovation, while civil law codification provides greater regulatory predictability.
- **Legal Origins and Economic Growth**: Reviews Rafael La Porta's seminal 'legal origins' theory showing that common law countries historically fostered deeper capital markets and stronger minority shareholder rights than French civil law systems.
- **TikTok National Security vs. Free Speech Jurisprudence**: Dissects the constitutional battle in U.S. federal courts pitting First Amendment protection of speech and editorial discretion against presidential and congressional national security determinations regarding foreign adversary data access.
- **Comparative Digital Sovereignty Paradigms**: Contrasts the U.S. foreign ownership focus, the European systemic risk and minor protection audits under the DSA, and the Indian complete national security ban.
