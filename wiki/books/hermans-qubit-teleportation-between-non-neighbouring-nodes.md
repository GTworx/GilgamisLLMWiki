## Executive Summary

Published in Nature (May 2022) by Sophie L. N. Hermans, Ronald Hanson, and the QuTech team at Delft University of Technology, this 7-page experimental physics paper reports the first realization of deterministic quantum state teleportation between non-neighbouring nodes across a multinode quantum network. Using nitrogen-vacancy (NV) diamond spin qubits located in distinct optical laboratories, the researchers establish a three-node network (Alice, Bob, Charlie). By entangling Alice with Bob, swapping entanglement at the intermediate node Bob, and executing a real-time feedforward Bell-state measurement, the team teleports arbitrary quantum states directly from Alice to Charlie with fidelity exceeding the classical limit, establishing a critical foundational building block for a future Quantum Internet and distributed quantum computing clusters.

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Non-Neighbouring Teleportation Breakthrough**: Demonstrates quantum state teleportation across a multi-node quantum network where the sending and receiving nodes share no direct physical link, routing through an intermediate quantum repeater node.
- **Diamond NV-Center Architecture**: Implements communication qubits (electron spins coupled to optical photons) and long-lived memory qubits (nuclear carbon-13 spins) within single diamond defect centers.
- **Real-Time Classical Feedforward**: Implements high-speed real-time FPGA control logic that measures the Bell-state outcome at the sender and applies conditional single-qubit Pauli correction rotations at the distant receiver within the qubit coherence window.
- **Surpassing the Classical Teleportation Bound**: Achieves average state teleportation fidelities of 70.2%, comfortably surpassing the fundamental classical limit of 2/3 (66.7%) and proving genuine quantum entanglement transfer.
- **Foundations for the Quantum Internet**: Establishes the core physical protocols required to build quantum repeaters, secure quantum key distribution (QKD) over continental distances, and distributed modular quantum computer networks.
