## Executive Summary

Drawing on primary Sumerian literary tablets translated alongside Samuel Noah Kramer, **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ's** *İnanna'nın Aşkı* (*The Love of Inanna: Belief and Sacred Marriage in Sumer*) examines the profound theological, erotic, agricultural, and cosmic significance of **Inanna (Ishtar)**, Queen of Heaven and Earth, and the ancient fertility rite of the **Sacred Marriage (*Hieros Gamos*)** with shepherd god **Dumuzi (Tammuz)**. Çığ reveals how these third-millennium BCE Sumerian liturgies and love lyrics directly gave birth to the biblical *Song of Songs* (Solomon's Song), Middle Eastern New Year celebrations (*Nevruz / Hıdırellez*), and regional mother-goddess cults.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Origin of the Dying-and-Rising God**: Proves that the myth of Dumuzi spending half the year in the underworld to bring back agricultural life is the direct ancestor of Tammuz, Adonis, Attis, Osiris, and spring resurrection holidays.
2. **Sacred Eroticism and Female Autonomy**: Analyzes Inanna as an empowered, self-directed female sovereign deity who actively chooses her partner, commands cosmic decrees, and descends into the underworld, contrasting sharply with later patriarchal monotheism.
3. **Cuneiform Genesis of the Song of Songs**: Demonstrates verbatim phraseological parallels between Sumerian love poetry (e.g., tablet *Ni 2461* in Istanbul) and biblical bridal poetry.
