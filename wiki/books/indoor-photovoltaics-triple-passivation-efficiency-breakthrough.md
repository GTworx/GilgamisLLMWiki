## Executive Summary
Published in 2025 by leading materials scientists, this 14-page research paper reports a world-record indoor power conversion efficiency of 37.6% in wide-bandgap perovskite indoor photovoltaic (IPV) cells under 1,000-lux fluorescent and LED lighting. By engineering a triple-passivation molecular surface treatment that eliminates non-radiative trap-assisted recombination at grain boundaries, the authors tailor the perovskite absorption spectrum (1.9 eV bandgap) to match the narrow emission wavelengths of indoor commercial lighting. The breakthrough enables self-powered IoT ambient sensors, smart tags, and wearable electronics that operate indefinitely without battery replacement.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Wide-Bandgap Perovskite Engineering**: Unlike terrestrial solar cells optimized for the broad 1.1–1.3 eV solar spectrum, indoor photovoltaics require 1.8–2.0 eV bandgaps to maximize photovoltage under narrow-spectrum indoor artificial lighting.
- **Triple-Passivation Mechanism**: Sequentially passivates under-coordinated lead ions, halide vacancies, and interfacial defect states, driving open-circuit voltage (Voc) to 92% of the thermodynamic radiative limit.
- **Eliminating IoT Battery Waste**: Harvesting 30–50 microwatts per square centimeter under standard office lighting provides continuous power for Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) transmitters, eliminating billions of disposable lithium coin-cell batteries.
- **Commercial Ambient Intelligence**: Establishes indoor energy harvesting as a core enabler for autonomous smart retail price tags, building sensors, and medical telemetry.
