## Summary
Spiritual philosophy, comparative mysticism, and psychological compendium bridging classical Sufism, modern developmental psychology, and Balkan syncretism. Examines the structural parallels between Imam al-Ghazali's stages of the soul (*Nafs*) and Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs (*Sakinah* to Self-Actualization), Said Nursi's spiritual psychology, Bektashi-Christian supraconfessional relations in the Balkans, Yezidi proto-Indo-Iranian archaic roots, and Tolstoy's engagement with Islam.

## Analytical Overview
This volume examines the psychological architecture, mystical discipline, and inter-religious syncretism of the Islamic spiritual tradition.

Key thematic arcs:
1. **Ghazalian Psychology & Maslow’s Hierarchy**: Bridges classical Islamic theology with modern humanistic psychology, demonstrating how Imam al-Ghazali’s tripartite schema of the self—*Nafs al-Ammarah* (the commanding soul / physiological deficiency needs), *Nafs al-Lawwamah* (the reproachful soul / moral self-regulation), and *Nafs al-Mutma'innah* (the tranquil soul / *sakinah* / self-transcendence)—prefigures and deepens Abraham Maslow’s pyramid of needs.
2. **Said Nursi & Modern Economic Sufism**: Investigates Bediüzzaman Said Nursi’s spiritual phenomenology in the *Risale-i Nur*, exploring how traditional Sufi disciplines (*tasawwuf*) adapt to secular modernity, economic agency, and digital connectivity without succumbing to materialism.
3. **Balkan Bektashi Syncretism & Comparative Faiths**: Features archival research on Christian-Bektashi coexistence in the Ottoman Balkans, documenting unique 'supraconfessional' shared shrines, saints (St. George / Hızır), and ecumenical tolerance, alongside historical analyses of Yezidi religious roots and Tolstoy’s spiritual writings on Islam.
