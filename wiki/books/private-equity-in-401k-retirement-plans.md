## Executive Summary

This sharp, insightful compendium combines financial journalist **Matt Levine's** celebrated *Money Stuff* analysis with PitchBook's quantitative research to examine the push by the alternative asset management industry to open **401(k) defined-contribution retirement plans to private equity and private credit**. With institutional DB pension plans in structural decline and PE managers sitting on massive fundraising targets, retail retirement accounts ($10+ trillion pool) represent the ultimate capital frontier. The authors analyze the profound structural, legal, and operational dilemmas of inserting illiquid, high-fee, mark-to-model private assets into daily-liquid, low-cost index-driven 401(k) structures (such as target-date funds), dissecting fee-layering, liquidity buffers, and fiduciary liability under ERISA.

---

## Key Strategic & Financial Takeaways

- **The Demographic Push into Defined Contribution Accounts**: Traditional corporate defined-benefit pension plans (which historically funded 30%+ of private equity commitments) are vanishing; sponsors must tap 401(k) retail retirement accounts to sustain multi-trillion-dollar industry asset growth.
- **The Inherent Illiquidity-Daily Liquidity Mismatch**: 401(k) participants expect daily redemption liquidity and transparent daily pricing; private equity assets are illiquid 10-year commitments with quarterly lagged mark-to-model valuations, requiring complex hybrid fund packaging and cash liquidity sleeves.
- **The Fee-Layering Problem**: Traditional 401(k) plans have spent two decades driving fees down toward zero via passive index funds (Vanguard, BlackRock target-date funds costing 5–10 bps); injecting private equity adds 1.5%–2% management fees and 20% carry, dramatically increasing participant costs.
- **The Matt Levine Paradox**: As companies remain private for 12–15 years (Uber, Airbnb, Stripe) and private markets capture the hyper-growth phase of modern capitalism, ordinary retail workers invested only in public index funds miss out on early wealth generation—yet retail investors are least equipped to evaluate complex private market fees and illiquidity traps.
- **ERISA Litigation Risk for Plan Sponsors**: Explains why corporate employers and plan fiduciaries remain terrified of class-action litigation from participants over excessive fees and underperformance, slowing widespread adoption.
