## Executive Summary

Concluding volume of the *Incerto*, **Nassim Nicholas Taleb's** *Skin in the Game* (2018, pp. 3–4) elevates symmetry of risk into a foundational law of ethics, epistemology, corporate governance, and evolutionary learning. Tracing the principle back to **Hammurabi's Code** (if a house collapses and kills the owner, the builder bears direct liability), Taleb indicts modern **"Intellectuals Yet Idiots" (IYIs)**, bailed-out bankers ("the Bob Rubin Trade," p. 4), and interventionist pundits who pocket personal upside while transferring catastrophic tail risk onto the public.

Without **skin in the game**—direct personal exposure to the consequences of one's advice, policies, or engineering—complex systems lose their primary error-correction mechanism ("systems learn by removing," p. 4) and inevitably decay into systemic fragility.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Asymmetry & Agency Failure**: Never trust advice from anyone who bears zero downside if they are wrong; real competence requires downside exposure.
2. **The Minority Rule (Intransigent Minority)**: A tiny, fiercely committed minority (3–4%) can shift the norms of an entire flexible majority (e.g., kosher/halal food standards, peanut-free schools, moral taboos)—demonstrating non-linear renormalization across society.
3. **Ergodicity & Ruin Prevention**: Ensemble averages across 100 gamblers playing once differ radically from time averages of one person playing 100 times; avoiding absorbing barriers of **ruin** takes absolute priority over maximizing expected return.
4. **Soul in the Game**: Craftsmen, entrepreneurs, and warriors who put honor and identity on the line operate on an ethical plane categorically distinct from salaried risk-transferring bureaucrats.
