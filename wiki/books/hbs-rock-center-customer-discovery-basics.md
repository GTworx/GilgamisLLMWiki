HBS Arthur Rock Center's definitive curriculum guide on customer discovery interviews, problem validation, and avoiding false-positive bias in new product design.

## Analytical Overview
### The Discipline of Market Validation

*Customer Discovery Basics*, developed by the Arthur Rock Center for Entrepreneurship at Harvard Business School, establishes the foundational methodology for early-stage customer research. Grounded in Steve Blank's Customer Development methodology and Clayton Christensen's Jobs-to-Be-Done theory, the playbook instructs founders on how to systematically interrogate market demand before writing code or manufacturing inventory.

### Overcoming the False-Positive Interview Trap

The guide exposes the primary mistake novice entrepreneurs make: pitching their idea to potential customers and mistaking polite validation for real market demand. The playbook details:
- Structuring interviews around past behavioral facts rather than hypothetical future promises ('Tell me about the last time you dealt with problem X' rather than 'Would you buy a product that did Y?').
- Identifying acute, budget-backed pain points versus minor inconveniences.
- Mapping stakeholder decision networks in B2B enterprise procurement.

### Synthesizing Feedback into Product Hypotheses

The manual illustrates how to translate raw customer qualitative interview notes into structured user personas, value proposition canvases, and minimum viable product (MVP) feature priorities.
