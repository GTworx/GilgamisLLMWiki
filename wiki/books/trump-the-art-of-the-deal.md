## Executive Summary

*Trump: The Art of the Deal* (Random House / Ballantine Books, 1987, 418 pp., ghostwritten with Tony Schwartz) is real-estate developer and later U.S. President [[donald-j-trump]]'s bestselling memoir and dealmaking manifesto. Blending a week-in-the-life diary of 1980s Manhattan deal-brokering with an eleven-point negotiation playbook and autobiographical case studies—from the Grand Hyatt redevelopment and Trump Tower to Atlantic City casinos, the USFL, and Central Park's Wollman Rink—the book crystallized Trump's enduring public persona around audacious leverage, "truthful hyperbole," and media-amplified branding (source: BOOKS/Biography_/1987 Trump_ The Art of the Deal.pdf, Outline & Chs. 1–14, pp. 2–417).

As a primary historical artifact inside this wiki, the 1987 volume offers an unfiltered baseline of Trump's instinct-driven, leverage-seeking worldview three decades before his presidency—illuminating the exact psychological, commercial, and rhetorical patterns later analyzed critically in [[enchanted-america]], [[the-room-where-it-happened]], and [[house-of-trump-house-of-putin]].

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Chapters 1–2: A Week in the Life & The Eleven Elements of the Deal (pp. 8–76)
- **Dealmaking as Art Form**: Opens with a minute-by-minute chronicle of phone negotiations across real estate, television, and litigation, presenting dealmaking not as spreadsheet finance but as creative performance (Ch. 1, pp. 8–52).
- **Core Principles (Ch. 2)**: Codifies eleven heuristics: *Think Big*, *Protect the Downside and the Upside Will Take Care of Itself*, *Maximize Your Options*, *Know Your Market*, *Use Your Leverage*, *Enhance Your Location*, *Get the Word Out* (celebrating press controversy as free publicity), *Fight Back*, *Deliver the Goods*, *Contain the Costs*, and *Have Fun* (pp. 53–76).

### Chapters 3–7: Queens Roots to Manhattan Flagships (pp. 77–205)
- **Apprenticeship & Manhattan Breakthrough**: Traces Trump's formative years under builder Fred Trump in Brooklyn and Queens, his early turnaround of Swifton Village in Cincinnati, and his high-stakes entry into 1970s Manhattan via the Commodore Hotel / Grand Hyatt tax-abatement deal and the flagship construction of **Trump Tower** on Fifth Avenue (pp. 77–205).

### Chapters 8–14: Casinos, Sports, Public Works & Brand Warfare (pp. 232–417)
- **High-Stakes Expansion**: Recounts Atlantic City gaming deals, corporate battles with Hilton and Steve Wynn, tenant disputes on Central Park South, the ill-fated USFL antitrust suit against the NFL, and his signature PR triumph rebuilding Central Park's stalled **Wollman Rink** ahead of schedule and under budget—casting private-sector speed against municipal bureaucratic inertia (pp. 232–417).
