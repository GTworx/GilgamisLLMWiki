## Summary
Rigorous NBER empirical study debunking the popular narrative that rapid GDP growth melted away the post-WWII US debt-to-GDP ratio (106% in 1946 down to 23% in 1974), proving that negative real interest rates and unexpected inflation did the heavy lifting.

## Analytical Overview
Decomposes the historical reduction in the US debt ratio between primary budget surpluses, real output growth, and interest-rate differentials ($r - g$). Demonstrates that institutional interest rate caps (1942–1951 Treasury-Fed Accord) and post-war inflation bursts transferred vast wealth from bondholders to the federal government, warning modern policymakers that financial repression, not painless growth, historically solved massive debt burdens.
