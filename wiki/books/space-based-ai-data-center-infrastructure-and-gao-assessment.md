## Summary
Visionary aerospace and computing systems treatise exploring the thermodynamic feasibility of space-based, gigawatt-scale orbital AI data center clusters powered by continuous solar irradiance, paired with the U.S. Government Accountability Office’s (GAO) comprehensive 2025 AI Technology Assessment.

## Analytical Overview
As terrestrial AI data centers exhaust local electrical grid capacities, consume millions of gallons of municipal cooling water, and face fierce land-use zoning pushback, leading technologists are looking beyond Earth's atmosphere. This groundbreaking work compiles the revolutionary 2025 design proposal led by Google Fellow Blaise Agüera y Arcas, Travis Beals, and colleagues (*Towards a future space-based, highly scalable AI infrastructure system design*), presenting an exhaustive physical, thermodynamic, and economic model for deploying gigawatt-scale AI computing clusters in low-Earth orbit (LEO) and sun-synchronous orbits.

The authors demonstrate that space offers three unparalleled thermodynamic advantages for compute: continuous, un-attenuated solar irradiance (1,361 W/m² with zero diurnal interruption or cloud cover), an infinite cryogenic 3-Kelvin heat sink for passive radiative dissipation, and the elimination of expensive terrestrial land and power purchasing agreements. Utilizing reusable super-heavy launch vehicles (Starship) projected at sub-$100/kg launch costs, the study designs modular satellite constellations carrying silicon photonic interconnects, high-efficiency perovskite solar blankets, and ultra-thin graphene thermal radiators capable of training frontier foundation models in vacuum with zero terrestrial carbon footprint.

To ground this visionary orbital paradigm within federal policy, the volume is paired with the U.S. Government Accountability Office’s (GAO) authoritative 47-page 2025 Technology Assessment on Artificial Intelligence (*GAO-25-107172*). The GAO audits civil and defense AI deployments, analyzing computational hardware bottlenecks, algorithmic bias, model hallucinations, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and formulating concrete policy recommendations for federal oversight of national compute capacity.
