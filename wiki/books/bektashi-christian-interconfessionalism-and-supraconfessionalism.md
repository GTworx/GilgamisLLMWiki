## Overview
Published in the Kyoto Bulletin of Islamic Area Studies (Vol. 14) by anthropologist Albert Doja, this profound scholarly treatise investigates centuries of religious syncretism, shared shrines, and shared spiritual devotions between Christian communities and Bektashi Sufi orders in Albania, the Balkans, and Anatolia. Doja conceptualizes this peaceful coexistence not as mere syncretism, but as sophisticated 'supraconfessionalism'.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Examines shared sacred pilgrimage sites (St. Naum / Sari Saltik) venerated simultaneously by Orthodox Christians and Muslims.
- **Key Finding**: Distinguishes superficial syncretism from structural 'supraconfessionalism'—a shared spiritual worldview transcending dogmatic boundaries.
- **Key Finding**: Illuminates the historical role of the Bektashi order in bridging Ottoman Islamic governance with indigenous Christian populations.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Bektashi supraconfessionalism did not ask believers to abandon their Christian or Muslim identity, but created a shared spiritual architecture where the love of God dissolved dogmatic hostility."
