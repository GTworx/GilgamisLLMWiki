## Executive Summary

*The Buyout of America: How Private Equity Will Cause the Next Great Credit Crisis* (published by Portfolio / Penguin Group, 2009, 304 pp., eISBN `978-1-101-15238-6`) by longtime *Buyouts Newsletter*, *The Deal*, and *Mergermarket* investigative financial journalist [[josh-kosman]] delivers a blistering insider indictment of the 2001–2007 leveraged-buyout boom and its structural risks to American workers, customers, and credit markets (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, pp. 6, 9–15). Sparked by his own 1995 layoff as a young journalist after Warburg Pincus–backed Journal Register Co. acquired the 111-year-old *Middletown Press*, Kosman dismantles the industry's claim that private equity firms act as patient operational stewards—arguing instead that LBOs operate as a **"legal shell game"** in which PE sponsors put down 30–40% equity and **saddle the acquired company itself with the mortgage**, extracting management fees and debt-funded **dividend recapitalizations** while cutting staff, service, and capital expenditure (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, Prologue, pp. 9–14).

Organized across three parts, eleven chapters, and an empirical appendix auditing the 1990s LBO track record, Kosman traces the industry's evolution from 1980s corporate raiders to Washington-connected mega-funds employing ~10% of the U.S. private-sector workforce (Part I); dissects the six-step **LBO Playbook**—*Doctoring Customer Service, Lifting Prices, Starving Capital, Plunder and Profit* (examining cases such as Thomas H. Lee's Warner Music dividend recap, Bain Capital, and Mitt Romney), *Leaving Little to Chance*, and rare operational exceptions (*A Different Approach*, Part II); and warns of the impending 2012–2015 U.S. and European leveraged-loan maturity wall alongside government bailout dilemmas such as Cerberus's GMAC rescue (Part III) (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, Contents & Ch. 1–11, pp. 3, 12–15, 108, 172, 186).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Prologue, Introduction & Part One: The Buyout of America (Chapters 1–2, pp. 9–57)**:
  * *The LBO Mortgage Mechanism & Maturity Wall*: Contrasts venture capital (investing equity into growth) and hedge funds (trading liquid securities) with LBO firms that load mature employers with debt; opens with a 2011–2015 stress-test scenario warning of cascading defaults at mega-buyouts (First Data, Clear Channel) affecting millions of U.S. workers, and charts how cheap 2001–2007 credit let PE firms take nearly a thousand public companies private (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, pp. 10–15, 25–57).

* **Part Two: The LBO Playbook (Chapters 3–8, pp. 58–153)**:
  * *Cost Stripping, Dividend Recaps & Exceptions*: Systematically documents how heavy debt service compels PE-owned companies to cut customer service staffing (Ch. 3), hike consumer prices (Ch. 4), starve long-term R&D and capital investment (Ch. 5), and issue new junk debt solely to pay PE owners **$1.2B+ special dividends and tens of millions in management fees** (e.g., Warner Music's 28% workforce cut paired with $1.2B in dividends and $73M in fees, Prologue & Ch. 6); balances the indictment in Chapter 8 (*A Different Approach*) by profiling PE operators that genuinely grow businesses rather than strip cash (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, pp. 12–14, 58–153).

* **Part Three & Appendix: European Contagion, Banking Distress & Policy Fallout (Chapters 9–11 & Appendix, pp. 154–224)**:
  * *Systemic Risk & Bailouts*: Examines Europe's parallel LBO debt overhang (Ch. 9), how cash-rich funds ($450B dry powder in 2008, such as Fortress) pivoted toward buying distressed banks during the financial crisis (Ch. 10), and the moral hazard of taxpayer rescues for PE-owned systemic firms like Cerberus's **GMAC** (Ch. 11), backed by an empirical Appendix auditing 1990s LBO bankruptcy rates (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, pp. 154–224).

## Key Quotes & Citations

* "They ran something of a legal shell game. They bought companies with other people's money by structuring acquisitions like mortgages: The critical difference was that while we pay our mortgages, PE firms had the businesses they bought take the loans, making them responsible for repayment." (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, Prologue, PDF p. 10)
* "He asked, 'Why are you interested in joining my firm?' 'I think you guys are sharp, and what you do is interesting.' 'That's not the right answer,' he said. 'The only reason you should want to join us is because you love making money.'" (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, Prologue, PDF p. 10)
* "The idea that PE firms put cash into companies was a widely held misconception. PE firms almost always saddled them with the bill and subsequently larger debt... Private-equity firms from 2000 through the first half of 2007 bought companies that employed close to 10 percent of the American private sector." (source: BOOKS/Private Equity/2009 The_Buyout_of_America_-_Josh_Kosman.pdf, Prologue, PDF pp. 10, 12)
