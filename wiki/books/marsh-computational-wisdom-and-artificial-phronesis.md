## Overview
Presented at IFIP AI 2016 by computer scientist Stephen Marsh, this pioneering philosophical and computing treatise investigates 'Computational Wisdom'—how to endow autonomous algorithmic systems with Aristotle's *phronesis* (practical wisdom). Marsh argues that wisdom is distinct from intelligence and knowledge, requiring compassion, long-term contextual judgment, and intentional moral friction.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Distinguishes computational wisdom from brute computational intelligence and raw data processing.
- **Key Finding**: Formulates algorithmic models of practical wisdom: balancing empathy, humility, courage, and proportional justice.
- **Key Finding**: Introduces 'computational friction'—deliberate delays forcing algorithms to reflect on broader societal consequences.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "An intelligent system knows how to calculate the optimal answer efficiently; a wise system knows when that calculation ought not to be made at all."
