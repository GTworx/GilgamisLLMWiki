## Executive Summary

Written by celebrated Turkish investigative journalist **Uğur Mumcu** (1942–1993) and originally published as a twenty-part documentary series in *Cumhuriyet* from 10 to 29 June 1990 (p. 2; 395 pp.), *Kâzım Karabekir Anlatıyor* examines the pivotal ideological, institutional, and personal divergence between **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk** and **Kâzım Karabekir**—the two chief military architects of the Turkish War of Independence—from 1920 through the proclamation of the Republic, the abolition of the Caliphate, the 1924 Progressive Republican Party, and the 1926 İzmir assassination trial.

Drawing systematically on Karabekir's unpublished/published diaries, parliamentary minutes, and telegrams, Mumcu traces key flashpoints (*Paşalar Bolşevizmi*, *Yol Ayrımı*, *Saltanat Kaldırılıyor*, *Mustafa Suphi Olayı*, *Cumhuriyetin İlanı*, *İstanbul'a İstiklâl Mahkemesi Gönderiliyor*, *Harp Oyunu*, TOC pp. 2–6) with forensic impartiality—illuminating how revolutionary comrades parted ways over the pace of secularization, parliamentary pluralism, and executive authority.

---

## Core Historical Insights

1. **The Founding Fault Line of Turkish Politics**: Documents the core tension between Mustafa Kemal's rapid, top-down republican-secular transformation and the more gradualist, parliamentary-constitutionalist vision of Karabekir, Rauf Orbay, Ali Fuat Cebesoy, and Refet Bele.
2. **Primary-Source Investigative Historiography**: Rather than taking partisan sides, Mumcu cross-examines Karabekir's claims against *Nutuk* and archival records, demonstrating why honest democratic societies must confront founding-era debates without censorship.
3. **Civil-Military Relations at the Birth of the Republic**: Traces the decisive 1924 separation of army commands from parliamentary seats (*Harp Oyunu* and commanders' resignations) that set precedents for Turkish constitutional politics for a century.
