## Overview
This foundational 39-page engineering monograph documents the historical trajectory of 3D virtual worlds from early text MUDs and VRML to Active Worlds, Second Life, and early distributed architectures. The authors analyze simulation physics, graphics rendering bottlenecks, client-server vs peer-to-peer topologies, and accurately forecast the hardware requirements needed for persistent global immersion.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Chronicles the architectural evolution of virtual worlds from 1980s multi-user dungeons to 2010s 3D mesh environments.
- **Key Finding**: Analyzes trade-offs between centralized server simulation (authoritative world state) and distributed client prediction.
- **Key Finding**: Identifies persistent spatial memory and dynamic asset streaming as the foundational prerequisites for the future metaverse.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "The metaverse is not a sudden rupture but an evolutionary convergence of computer graphics, distributed networking, and human desire for spatial tele-presence."
