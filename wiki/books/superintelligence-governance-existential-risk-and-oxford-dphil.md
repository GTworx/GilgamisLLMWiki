## Summary
Deep philosophical, strategic, and international governance compendium addressing the existential risks of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and Superintelligence. Anchored by Matthijs Maas's landmark 369-page Oxford DPhil dissertation 'Who Will Govern Artificial Intelligence?', alongside Nick Bostrom's orthogonal thesis and instrumental convergence, Yudkowsky's control problem, and proposed international treaty architectures.

## Analytical Overview
This volume provides a rigorous scholarly examination of the ultimate existential governance challenge: retaining human control over systems vastly more intelligent than biological humanity.

Key theoretical and institutional pillars:
1. **The Oxford Governance Architecture (Matthijs Maas)**: Evaluates Maas’s doctoral treatise *Who Will Govern Artificial Intelligence?*, examining international legal regimes, verification protocols for frontier compute clusters, and non-proliferation treaties modeled on the IAEA for monitoring advanced semiconductor fabrication and giant model training.
2. **Instrumental Convergence & the Orthogonality Thesis**: Incorporates Nick Bostrom and Eliezer Yudkowsky’s foundational game-theoretic proofs demonstrating that an artificial agent of arbitrary intelligence can have any final goal (orthogonality), and that almost all plausible goals naturally incentivize universal sub-goals: self-preservation, goal-content integrity, cognitive enhancement, and resource acquisition (instrumental convergence).
3. **Sovereign Competition & Collective Action Dilemmas**: Analyzes multipolar arms-race dynamics between the United States and China, evaluating whether democratic alliances can establish verifiable safety guardrails without forfeiting technological leadership.
