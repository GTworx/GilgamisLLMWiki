## Summary
Landmark 2024 academic econometric study published in Econ Journal Watch re-analyzing McKinsey's influential 'Diversity Matters' reports, revealing that its findings fail to replicate across S&P 500 corporations.

## Analytical Overview
### Forensic Econometric Audit of Corporate Consulting Claims

In *McKinsey’s Diversity Matters, Delivers, and Wins Results Revisited*, published in *Econ Journal Watch* (March 2024), accounting professors Jeremiah Green (Texas A&M) and John R. M. Hand (UNC Chapel Hill) execute a meticulous econometric replication of McKinsey & Company's famous series of diversity research reports (*Diversity Matters* 2015, *Delivers* 2018, *Wins* 2020). For a decade, McKinsey's studies served as the global corporate gospel claiming that racial and gender diversity in executive suites directly causes superior financial profitability.

### The Failure to Replicate Across S&P 500 Firms

Green and Hand followed McKinsey's stated methodology to test whether executive diversity correlated with industry-adjusted return on assets (ROA), return on equity (ROE), or profit margins across S&P 500 companies over the 2015–2019 period:
- **Zero Statistically Significant Relation:** The authors found no statistically significant or economically meaningful positive relationship between executive racial/ethnic or gender diversity and subsequent corporate financial performance.
- **Reverse Causality and Confounding Variables:** The authors demonstrate that McKinsey's cross-sectional correlations suffered from reverse causality (profitable, high-performing legacy firms have more surplus resources to expand diversity recruiting) rather than diversity driving financial returns.

### Implications for Corporate Governance and Metascience

Green and Hand argue that corporate boards must champion diversity on fundamental grounds of justice, ethics, and talent access, rather than justifying moral policies through flawed, non-replicable commercial consulting marketing.
