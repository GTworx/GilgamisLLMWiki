## Summary
Monumental 104-page labor economics investigation using US Census title matching from 1940 to 2018 to prove that over 60% of modern employment exists in job titles that did not exist in 1940, modeling how automation displaces labor while task augmentation creates new occupations.

## Analytical Overview
Develops a formal task-based framework distinguishing between automation (which displaces workers from existing routine tasks) and augmentation (which creates demand for entirely novel professional tasks). Quantifies how computerization hollowed out middle-skill clerical work while expanding high-skill managerial specialties and low-skill service occupations, evaluating the direct and indirect wage impacts.
