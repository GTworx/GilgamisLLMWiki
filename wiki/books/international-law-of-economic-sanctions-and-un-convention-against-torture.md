## Executive Summary
This 68-page international jurisprudence compendium examines two sovereign instruments of state power: the legal doctrine of extraterritorial economic sanctions and the international legal framework prohibiting torture and cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment (the UN Convention Against Torture - UNCAT and 2025 General Assembly Resolutions). The sanctions treatise audits the jurisdictional foundations of unilateral and multilateral sanctions (OFAC, EU regulations, UN Chapter VII), evaluating secondary sanctions, sovereign immunity exceptions, and humanitarian carve-outs. The UN torture resolution reaffirms the absolute, non-derogable *jus cogens* nature of the prohibition against torture, detailing state compliance mechanisms and universal jurisdiction prosecutions.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Extraterritorial Reach of Economic Sanctions**: Analyzes how the U.S. leverages dollar clearing dominance to impose secondary sanctions on foreign corporations, creating contentious jurisdictional clashes with European 'blocking statutes'.
- **Due Process and Asset Forfeiture Contradictions**: Explores constitutional and international legal challenges surrounding the seizure and permanent forfeiture of sovereign central bank assets and private oligarch property without criminal convictions.
- **Non-Derogable Jus Cogens Prohibition**: Reaffirms that freedom from torture is an absolute peremptory norm of international law; no exceptional circumstances—whether war, threat of war, or public emergency—may be invoked to justify torture.
- **Universal Jurisdiction Enforcement**: Examines landmark European trials prosecuting Syrian and foreign state torturers under the principle of universal jurisdiction for crimes against humanity committed abroad.
