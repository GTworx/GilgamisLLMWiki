## Summary
Commissioned by the Turkish Academy of Sciences (TÜBA), executes a massive empirical evaluation of Turkey's scientific ecosystem, identifying misaligned academic incentives and structural brain-drain dynamics.

## Analytical Overview
### Empirical Diagnosis of Turkey's Scientific Ecosystem

In *Türkiye Bilim Raporu 2020: Akademi, Teşvikler ve Bilimsel Üretim*, University of Chicago economist Ufuk Akcigit and Elif Özcan-Tok deliver the most exhaustive, data-driven diagnostic of the academic research landscape in Turkey. Combining administrative records from YÖK, TÜBİTAK, Web of Science, and patent registries, the authors evaluate the productivity of over 70,000 academic personnel and dozens of universities over a twenty-year period.

### Structural Pathology: Misaligned Incentives and Predatory Publishing

The authors uncover severe structural deficiencies in Turkey's academic incentive system:
1. **Perverse Quantity-Over-Quality Incentives:** Academic promotion criteria incentivized rapid paper output, fueling a catastrophic surge in predatory and low-tier journal publications while high-impact, globally cited research languished.
2. **Inbreeding and Nepotism:** High rates of academic inbreeding (universities hiring their own PhD graduates) stifled fresh research agendas and innovation.
3. **Misallocated Research Subsidies:** Public R&D and research grant allocations frequently failed to reward truly productive researchers, perpetuating mediocrity.

### Policy Roadmap for National Scientific Renaissance

Akcigit and Özcan-Tok propose actionable, structural reforms: redesigning academic tenure and promotion metrics around international citation benchmarks, establishing transparent peer-reviewed grant allocations, and creating attractive re-migration packages to reverse Turkey's intellectual brain-drain.
