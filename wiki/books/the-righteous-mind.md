## Executive Summary

Opening with Spinoza's plea *"not to hate human actions, but to understand them"* (p. 6) and Rodney King's question *"Can we all get along?"* (p. 8), NYU Stern moral psychologist **Jonathan Haidt**'s landmark *The Righteous Mind* (Pantheon, 2012, 322 pp., copyright p. 4) synthesizes twenty years of cross-cultural experiments and evolutionary psychology to explain why intelligent, well-meaning citizens inhabit irreconcilable moral universes.

Haidt structures the book around three foundational principles (TOC p. 7): **Part I — Intuitions Come First, Strategic Reasoning Second**; **Part II — There's More to Morality than Harm and Fairness**; and **Part III — Morality Binds and Blinds**. Using his vivid **Elephant and Rider** metaphor, Haidt argues that the conscious rational mind (the Rider) rarely steers moral judgment; instead, it functions mostly as a clever **press secretary** inventing post-hoc justifications for the gut intuitions of the massive emotional **Elephant** (Ch. 2–4).

## Moral Foundations Theory & Groupishness

1. **Beyond WEIRD Morality (Ch. 5–6)**: Shows that Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) populations are global outliers who narrow morality mainly to individual harm and fairness—whereas most human cultures draw on six innate **"moral taste buds."**
2. **The Six Moral Foundations (Ch. 6–8)**: **Care/Harm**, **Fairness/Cheating**, **Liberty/Oppression**, **Loyalty/Betrayal**, **Authority/Subversion**, and **Sanctity/Degradation**. Haidt argues that political liberals build moral matrices predominantly on the first three, whereas conservatives draw more evenly across all six—explaining persistent partisan mutual incomprehension.
3. **The Hive Switch & Religion as Team Sport (Ch. 9–11)**: Describes humans as *"90 percent chimp and 10 percent bee"*—possessing an evolved **Hive Switch** (activated by rituals, awe, music, and sacred values) that binds groups into high-trust teams while simultaneously blinding them to rival perspectives.
