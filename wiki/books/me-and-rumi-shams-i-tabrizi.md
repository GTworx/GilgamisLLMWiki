## Executive Summary

*Me & Rumi: The Autobiography of Shams-i Tabrizi* (2004, Fons Vitae, p. 4) is renowned Islamicist **William C. Chittick's** groundbreaking English translation and thematic reconstruction of the ***Maqalat*** ("Discourses") of **Shams al-Din Tabrizi**—the enigmatic, uncompromising wandering mystic whose explosive 1244 encounter with Jalal al-Din Rumi in Konya transformed a conventional jurist into one of humanity's supreme mystical poets.

Because the original Persian *Maqalat* survives as loosely arranged notes jotted down by listeners during Shams's fiery oral conversations, Chittick reorganizes the primary sayings into a coherent three-part spiritual autobiography (p. 5), rescuing the historical Shams from centuries of misty hagiography and revealing him as a razor-sharp, learned, fiercely devout follower of the Prophet Muhammad who despised pretentious scholars and complacent Sufi shaykhs alike.

## Three-Part Autobiographical Structure (Contents, p. 5)

| Part | Key Chapters | Core Revelations |
|---|---|---|
| **I. My Years without Mawlana** (pp. 1–36) | *Childhood*, *My Teaching Career*, *My Travels*, *Teachers and Shaykhs I Have Met* | Shams's early spiritual alienation from his own parents, his itinerant years supporting himself modestly, and his candid, often devastating assessments of famed contemporary Sufis (including Ibn Arabi and Awhad al-Din Kirmani) |
| **II. My Path to God** (pp. 37–176) | *The Profit and Loss of Study*, *Philosophy*, *Following Muhammad*, *The Religion of Old Women*, *The Guidance of the Shaykh*, *Avoiding Caprice*, *The Companion of the Heart*, *The Saints*, *My Interpretations of Scripture* | Shams's penetrating theology: critiquing sterile bookishness and dry rationalist philosophy while grounding authentic mysticism unconditionally in *mutaba'at* (living imitation of Prophet Muhammad) and direct experiential realization |
| **III. My Time with Mawlana** (pp. 177–330+) | *Our Encounter*, *My Spiritual Mastery*, *Mawlana's Exalted Station*, *Our Companionship*, *My Instructions to the Circle*, *My Critics*, *My Harshness with Friends*, *My Return from Aleppo* | Firsthand testimony of the legendary meeting with Rumi, the spiritual shockwaves it caused among jealous Konya disciples, Shams's brief exile to Damascus/Aleppo, and his fierce, bittersweet final teachings before his mysterious 1247 disappearance |

## Key Scholarly Breakthroughs

1. **Dismantling the "Illiterate Wildman" Myth**: Far from an antinomian bohemian indifferent to Islam, the *Maqalat* reveals Shams as deeply versed in *fiqh*, *hadith*, Arabic rhetoric, and Qur'anic exegesis—whose radicalism came from taking prophetic sincerity *more* seriously than institutional orthodoxy.
2. **The Mirror of Companionship**: Shams explicitly frames his bond with Rumi as mutual spiritual mirroring (*sohbet*)—recognizing Rumi's unmatched capacity to articulate states that Shams himself lived in incandescent silence.
3. **Unfiltered Voice**: Chittick's literal yet vivid translation preserves Shams's abrupt shifts, earthy humor, sharp rebukes, and electrifying intimacy.
