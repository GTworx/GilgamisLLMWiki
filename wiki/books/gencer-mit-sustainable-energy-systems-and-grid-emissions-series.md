## Executive Summary
Authored by Dr. Emre Gençer and researchers at the MIT Energy Initiative (MITEI), this four-paper research series—published across *PNAS*, *Applied Energy*, *Energy Policy*, and *Environmental Science & Technology*—establishes the SESAME (Sustainable Energy System Analysis Modeling Environment) architecture. Gençer formalizes dynamic, hourly life-cycle assessment (LCA) of power grid emissions, proving that the carbon abatement of electric vehicles (EVs) and electrolyzers depends critically on marginal hourly grid dispatch rather than annual average emissions factors. The studies model solar-coal co-generation, grid-level energy storage dispatch, and optimal EV charging schedules to prevent unintended emissions spikes during peak fossil ramp periods.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Marginal vs. Average Grid Emissions**: Charging an electric vehicle during midnight baseload hours on a coal-heavy grid can generate higher life-cycle CO2 emissions than an efficient hybrid vehicle; decarbonization benefits require synchronized renewable dispatch.
- **SESAME Systems Modeling Framework**: Develops an open-source, modular life-cycle simulation tool that evaluates emissions across 40+ interconnected industrial pathways spanning power, transport, and manufacturing.
- **Hourly Power Grid Dispatch Dynamics**: High solar penetration causes severe duck-curve dynamics where rapid evening ramping of peaker gas plants erodes renewable gains unless paired with 4-to-8-hour battery storage.
- **Hybrid Solar-Fossil Systems**: Early papers demonstrate that integrating concentrated solar thermal with combined-cycle natural gas achieves immediate emissions abatement while preserving firm grid reliability.
