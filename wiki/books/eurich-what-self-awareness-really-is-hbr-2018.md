## Analytical Overview
### The Pervasive Delusion of Executive Self-Awareness

In *What Self-Awareness Really Is (and How to Cultivate It)*, published in *Harvard Business Review* (2018), organizational psychologist Dr. Tasha Eurich reveals a startling empirical paradox uncovered across her multi-year research program: while **95% of people believe they are self-aware, the real figure is between 10% and 15%**. In executive suites, this delusion is exacerbated: the higher a leader rises in the corporate hierarchy, the more overconfident they become in their self-perception, because subordinates systematically withhold critical feedback.

### The Two Orthogonal Facets of Self-Awareness

Eurich demonstrates that self-awareness consists of two completely independent dimensions:
1. **Internal Self-Awareness:** How clearly we see our own values, passions, aspirations, and reactions.
2. **External Self-Awareness:** Understanding how other people view us.
- Leaders who have high internal but low external awareness are 'Introspectors' (self-absorbed, unaware of their blind spots).
- Those with high external but low internal awareness are 'Pleasers' (constantly chasing approval at the expense of authentic vision). True effectiveness requires mastering both.

### The Power of 'What' Instead of 'Why'

Eurich uncovers a transformative cognitive intervention: **stop asking 'Why' and start asking 'What'**. Asking *'Why do I feel this way?'* triggers emotional rumination and rationalization; asking *'What am I feeling right now?'* or *'What can I do differently next time?'* shifts cognition into objective, solution-oriented executive control.
