## Summary
A profound literary and musicological study of how contemporary Iranian rock/fusion composer Mohsen Chavoshi resurrects 13th-century classical Sufi ghazals by Jalal al-Din Rumi (*Maleka*) and Saadi Shirazi (*Marā khod bā to chizi dar miyān hast*) through powerful rock orchestrations, raspy baritone vocals, and driving modal rhythms, introducing classical mysticism to millions of contemporary listeners.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
Mohsen Chavoshi is one of the most culturally significant and enigmatic musicians in post-revolutionary Iran. Starting as an underground pop sensation, Chavoshi executed a dramatic artistic pivot in the 2010s: abandoning conventional commercial lyrics, he began setting the dense, ecstatic mystical poetry of Rumi (*Divan-e Shams*), Saadi, and Hafez to heavy electric guitars, driving drums, and traditional Middle Eastern reed instruments (*ney*).

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
The work explores the musicality of classical Persian mystical prosody: Rumi composed his ecstatic ghazals during whirling *Sama* ceremonies, resulting in poems with intense, galloping rhythmic meters. Chavoshi harnesses these intrinsic rhythmic structures, translating Rumi's ecstatic Sufi communion with the Divine into high-energy alternative rock arrangements. In *Marā khod bā to chizi dar miyān hast*, Saadi's sublime meditation on intimate love and mystical companionship is rendered through soaring vocal arcs.

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
The study presents line-by-line classical Persian text, metered phonetic transliteration, and philosophical translations of Chavoshi's benchmark classical adaptations (*Maleka*, Saadi's ghazals). Extensive philological annotations explain Sufi technical vocabulary—the annihilation of ego (*fana*), divine intoxication, celestial yearning, and the transcendence of dogmatic formal religion through radical love.

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
Chavoshi's adaptations represent an extraordinary bridge across eight centuries of Persian literature. By proving that medieval Sufi poetry possesses raw, electric contemporary vitality, his work constitutes a magnificent cultural revitalization of the world's greatest mystical literary heritage.
