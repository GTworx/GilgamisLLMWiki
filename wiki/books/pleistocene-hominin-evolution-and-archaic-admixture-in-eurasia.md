## Overview
This monumental 4-study paleoanthropology dossier (featuring Nature and Nature Communications studies by Christopher J. Bae and global teams) reconstructs the complex evolutionary mosaic of Late Pleistocene humans across Eurasia. Analyzing fossil crania from Xujiayao, Denisovan genetic introgressions, archaeological excavations at Tolbor-16 in Mongolia, and geographical contact corridors, the authors demonstrate that archaic hominins interbred continuously with modern humans across Asia.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Synthesizes fossil evidence from Xujiayao and Denisova Cave into a rich multi-lineage model of Pleistocene Asia.
- **Key Finding**: Documents the northern dispersal corridor of Homo sapiens through Mongolia into Northeast Asia ~45,000 years ago.
- **Key Finding**: Reconstructs ecological contact zones where Neanderthals, Denisovans, and Anatomically Modern Humans overlapped and interbred.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Human evolution in Asia was not a simple replacement wave, but a braided river of archaic lineages—Neanderthals, Denisovans, and distinct Xujiayao forms—whose genetic and cultural legacies persist in modern populations."
