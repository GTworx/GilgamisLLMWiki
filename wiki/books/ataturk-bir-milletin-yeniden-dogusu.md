## Executive Summary

Written by Scottish historian and diplomat **Patrick Balfour, 3rd Baron Kinross** (orig. 1964) and rendered into classic Turkish by **Necdet Sander** (Altın Kitaplar, 1994; consolidated here from both 699-pp. and 836-pp. library PDF editions), *Atatürk: Bir Milletin Yeniden Doğuşu* remains the landmark international biography of **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk** (1881–1938). Researched over five years with unprecedented access to the Çankaya Presidential Archives, Turkish War of Independence veterans, Enver Ziya Karal, and British Foreign Office papers of Sir Horace Rumbold (Teşekkürler, p. 8 / p. 5), Kinross crafts a sweeping sixty-chapter narrative divided into three grand movements.

**Part I** (Ch. 1–19) traces the twilight of the Ottoman Empire from Salonika through Harbiye, Damascus, Tripoli, Sofia, and the heroic defense of Gallipoli; **Part II** (Ch. 20–44) chronicles the Anatolian War of Independence from Samsun, Erzurum, and Sivas through Sakarya, Dumlupınar, and the Treaty of Lausanne; and **Part III** (Ch. 45–60 & Epilogue) examines the radical secular, legal, alphabetic, educational, and diplomatic revolutions that forged the modern Turkish Republic.

## Core Historical & Leadership Dimensions

1. **Realist Visionary Against Imperial Delusion**: Contrasts Mustafa Kemal's sober realism—rejecting pan-Turanian and pan-Islamic adventures in favor of defensible national borders (*Misak-ı Millî*)—with the reckless romanticism of late Ottoman elites.
2. **Military Genius Turned Institutional Modernizer**: Demonstrates how the same strategic discipline that won Anafartalar and the Great Offensive was redirected into abolishing the Sultanate and Caliphate, enacting the Swiss Civil Code, emancipating women, and adopting the Latin alphabet.
3. **Human Complexity & Solitude of Command**: Depicts Atatürk neither as a mythic demigod nor as a mere autocrat, capturing his intellectual curiosity, evening Çankaya table debates, personal loneliness, and unyielding devotion to *Yurtta sulh, cihanda sulh* ("Peace at home, peace in the world").
