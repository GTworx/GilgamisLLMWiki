## Executive Summary

Curated by Turkish businessman, writer, and lifelong archivist **Hanri Benazus** (1930–2024)—owner of the world's largest private collection of Atatürk photographs (thousands of original prints and negatives)—and published by Bizim Kitaplar (İstanbul, 2013, ISBN 978-605-5476-62-5, pp. 2–4; 353 pp.), *Büyük Atatürk Albümü, Cilt 1* presents a richly annotated visual chronicle of **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk** from his 1905 Harbiye graduation through the 1928 Alphabet Revolution.

Opening with Benazus's deeply personal introductory essay *"Niçin Atatürk?..."* (recounting his boyhood meeting with Atatürk in Aydın in 1937 and decades spent tracking down rare photographs across international archives), the album documents Ottoman fronts (Tripoli, Gallipoli, Eastern Front, Syria), the Anatolian Congresses (Erzurum, Sivas), the War of Independence (Sakarya, Kocatepe, İzmir), and the early Republican social, dress, legal, and educational transformations.

---

## Core Visual-Historical Value

1. **Primary Photographic Preservation (1905–1928)**: Brings together hundreds of rare, chronologically contextualized historical photographs that visually anchor the military and civic transformations narrated in textual biographies.
2. **Cultural History of Republican Modernization**: Tracks visually the dramatic sartorial, architectural, and civic shifts—from late-Ottoman military uniforms and traditional Anatolian towns to mixed-gender classrooms, public ceremonies, and the 1928 blackboard alphabet tours.
3. **Civic Collector Ethos**: Reflects Benazus's decades-long philanthropic mission donating curated Atatürk photo archives to Turkish municipalities, museums, and schools.
