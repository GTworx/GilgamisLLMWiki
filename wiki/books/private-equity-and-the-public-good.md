## Executive Summary

Authored by **Daniel J. Morrissey** (Professor of Law and former Dean of Gonzaga University School of Law), this comprehensive legal and policy treatise examines the systemic collision between the modern **private equity business model and the public interest**. Drawing on foundational corporate governance theory (Adolf Berle, Gardiner Means, Lynn Stout) and extensive empirical case studies across retail, housing, newspapers, and healthcare, Morrissey argues that the private equity model—characterized by aggressive debt loading, extraction of dividend recapitalizations, transaction fees, and asset-stripping of target company balance sheets—fundamentally repudiates the long-standing corporate legal compact that grants corporations limited liability in exchange for advancing societal welfare.

---

## Key Strategic & Financial Takeaways

- **The Structural Conflict of Interest in Buyouts**: Unlike traditional public corporate boards bound by fiduciary duties to the ongoing corporate enterprise, PE sponsors operate under finite fund horizons (3–5 year target exits) and contractual waivers of fiduciary liability in limited partnership agreements, incentivizing short-term cash extraction over long-term enterprise health.
- **Dividend Recapitalizations as Capital Extraction**: Details how sponsors force healthy operating companies to borrow hundreds of millions of dollars to pay immediate cash dividends to the private equity fund, recovering the sponsor's initial equity check before any operational improvements occur and leaving the target company dangerously overburdened with debt.
- **Case Studies of Economic Devastation**: Documents how leveraged buyouts catalyzed catastrophic liquidations across essential retail (Toys 'R' Us, Sears), journalism (Alden Global Capital dismantling local newsrooms), and single-family housing rollups, causing tens of thousands of job losses and pension defaults while sponsors walked away with hundreds of millions in advisory fees.
- **Erosion of the Corporate Limited Liability Compact**: Argues that limited liability was historically granted by sovereign states to encourage productive industrial investment, not to shield financial intermediaries extracting economic rents while shedding pension and labor liabilities in bankruptcy court.
- **Legislative and Regulatory Remedies**: Proposes sweeping reforms: eliminating the carried interest tax loophole, closing the corporate interest deductibility loophole for highly leveraged acquisitions, holding private equity firms jointly liable for the labor, environmental, and WARN Act violations of their portfolio companies, and enforcing SEC fiduciary oversight.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations

- "Private equity firms have reshaped the American economy not by inventing better products or improving operational efficiency, but by buying companies, loading them with debt, raising prices, cutting jobs, and shifting capital from ordinary businesses to themselves." (source: Private Equity and the Public Good, p. 884)
- "Limited liability was conceived as a privilege to foster productive commercial enterprise. It was never intended to serve as a legal shield for financial syndicates to hollow out vital community institutions and abandon the wreckage to the public purse." (source: Private Equity and the Public Good, p. 912)
