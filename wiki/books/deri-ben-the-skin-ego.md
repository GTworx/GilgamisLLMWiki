## Executive Summary

Written by eminent French psychoanalyst and philosopher **Didier Anzieu** (1923–1999)—who trained in both philosophy (ENS) and dermatology and underwent analysis with Lacan before co-founding the Association Psychanalytique de France (p. 2)—and published in Turkish by Metis Yayınları (translated by **Nesrin Tura Demiryontan**, 2008, 330 pp., pp. 3–8), *Deri-Ben* (*The Skin-Ego*) introduces one of postwar psychoanalysis's most original clinical concepts. Expanding Freud's famous 1923 remark that *"the ego is first and foremost a bodily ego... the projection of a surface,"* Anzieu defines the **Skin-Ego** as the mental representation by which the infant builds a psychic sense of self out of earliest tactile contact with the mother's body.

Drawing on four converging bodies of evidence—ethology (Harlow's attachment experiments), group dynamics, projective Rorschach testing, and psychosomatic dermatology (Ch. 2, TOC p. 6)—plus an incisive psychoanalytic reading of the Greek myth of **Marsyas** (Ch. 4), Anzieu explains narcissistic fragility, borderline states, and psychosomatic disorders as ruptures in the mind's protective sensory envelope.

## Eight Core Functions of the Skin-Ego (Ch. 7, p. 7)

1. **Maintenance (*Tutma*)**: Psychic holding corresponding to Winnicott's maternal support.
2. **Containment (*İçerme*)**: Providing a unified psychic bag holding emotions and drives together (preventing "sieve-head" leakage).
3. **Protective Shield (*Uyarılma Engeli*)**: Filtering overwhelming external stimuli (building on Freud's 1895 contact-barriers, Ch. 6).
4. **Individuation (*Bireyleşme*)**: Establishing the boundary separating *Self* from *Other*.
5. **Intersensoriality, Sexual Support, Libidinal Recharging & Inscription of Traces**: Integrating touch, sound, and smell into a shared sensory envelope capable of recording lived experience.
