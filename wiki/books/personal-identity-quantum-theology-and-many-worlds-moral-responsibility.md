## Summary
Rigorous analytic philosophy and philosophical theology compendium addressing the nature of the self through time, moral culpability across Everettian quantum branches, and cosmological theology. Centered around John Perry's landmark 'Time, Fission, and Personal Identity' alongside Emily Qureshi-Hurst's 'Quantum Theology Beyond Copenhagen', analyses of Many-Worlds moral agency, and the dialectics of ethical responsibility.

## Analytical Overview
This volume convenes leading philosophical inquiries at the intersection of metaphysics, quantum interpretations, and ethical theory.

Key analytical components:
1. **Personal Identity, Fission, and Temporal Worms**: Analyzes John Perry’s seminal 2019 treatise in *Argumenta*, confronting the classic fission paradox: if an individual’s brain is divided into two viable transplant recipients, who survives? Perry rigorously defends a four-dimensionalist, temporal-parts ontology, navigating the tension between Lewisian counterpart theory and Parfitian psychological continuity.
2. **Quantum Theology Beyond Copenhagen**: Incorporates Emily Qureshi-Hurst’s *Zygon* research evaluating the theological and metaphysical ramifications of non-Copenhagen interpretations of quantum mechanics. Contrasting Bohmian pilot-wave determinism and the Everett Many-Worlds Interpretation, the work examines how non-collapse models reshape concepts of divine action, eschatology, and free will.
3. **Moral Responsibility in Everettian Multiverses**: Evaluates recent 2024 scholarship on how moral responsibility functions when all quantum possibilities are realized across branching worlds—interrogating whether ethical choices retain normative weight if counterfactual alternatives are physically actualized in parallel branches.
