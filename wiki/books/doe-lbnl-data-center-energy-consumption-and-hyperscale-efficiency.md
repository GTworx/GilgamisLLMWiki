## Summary
Definitive governmental, industrial, and financial compendium on data center power consumption, compiling the Department of Energy / LBNL U.S. Data Center Energy Usage Reports, DC Byte’s 2025 hyperscale build race analysis, Bank of America supply chain primers, and Goldman Sachs utility grid demand forecasts.

## Analytical Overview
The explosion of generative artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and high-density GPU acceleration has transformed data center electricity demand into the most critical infrastructure bottleneck of the modern economy. This volume establishes the definitive empirical and financial record of this energy inflection. Anchoring the collection is the landmark *United States Data Center Energy Usage Report* produced by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) for the U.S. Department of Energy under Arman Shehabi and Sarah Smith. LBNL documents how data centers evolved from consuming ~2% of total U.S. electricity in 2014 (~70 billion kWh) to an estimated 4–9% by 2030, driven by the shift from Moore’s Law CPU servers to power-hungry GPU clusters consuming 30–100 kW per rack.

This empirical baseline is paired with market intelligence from DC Byte (*The Hyperscale Build Race 2025*) and Power Analyst studies examining the geographic geography of data center siting. Faced with catastrophic grid capacity constraints, multi-year transmission interconnection queues, and transformer backlogs in primary hubs like Northern Virginia's 'Data Center Alley', hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta) are aggressively decentralizing across secondary and tertiary markets endowed with baseload nuclear, hydroelectric, or geothermal power.

The volume is rounded out by institutional equity analyses from Bank of America Global Research (*Who Makes the Data Center*) and Goldman Sachs Equity Research (*Americas Energy: Dallas Data Center Trip*). These analyses detail the massive capital expenditure supercycle flowing into thermal management (liquid direct-to-chip cooling, rear-door heat exchangers), electrical distribution equipment (medium-voltage switchgear, uninterrupted power supplies [UPS], backup generators), and utility contracts, establishing power access as the ultimate arbiter of AI supremacy.
