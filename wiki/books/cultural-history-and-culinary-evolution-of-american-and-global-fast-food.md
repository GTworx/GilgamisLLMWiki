## Executive Summary
Comprising four interconnected culinary history monographs, this compendium investigates the social, economic, and technological evolution of iconic global fast-food archetypes: pizza, hamburgers, barbecue, and regional American heritage cuisines. The authors trace the transformation of Neapolitan street food into the global pizza industry through Italian immigration and industrialized cheese production; the lineage of the hamburger from Tatar raw meat and Hamburg steaks to midwestern railway food stalls and the assembly-line restaurant format (White Castle, McDonald's); the Caribbean Taíno and African-American roots of low-and-slow pit barbecue; and the regional culinary identities of authentic American dishes.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Pizza Industrialization & Trans-Atlantic Feedback**: Originally a peasant street dish in 18th-century Naples, pizza was transformed in mid-20th-century America through industrial deck ovens, vacuum-sealed mozzarella, and mechanized tomato processing, subsequently re-exported worldwide.
- **The Hamburger Assembly Line**: The hamburger's triumph was driven by factory-line rationalization—standardizing ground beef patties, griddle timing, and soft buns to serve fast, sanitary, low-cost lunches to industrial workers.
- **The Cultural Synthesis of Barbecue**: Barbecue evolved from indigenous Caribbean smoke preservation (*barbacoa*) combined with West African smoking techniques and European pork livestock, creating deep regional flavor traditions across the American South.
- **Industrial Food Processing and Public Health**: Chronicles how artisanal preservation methods (salting, smoking, fermentation) were replaced by industrial chemical preservatives, high-fructose corn syrup, and hyper-palatable fat-sugar formulations.
