## Summary
Master's thesis examining the cultural, behavioral, and operational hurdles of implementing Lean Manufacturing and the Toyota Production System in industrial manufacturing enterprises.

## Analytical Overview
### The Human Dimension of the Toyota Production System

In *Yalın Kültür ve Operasyonel Mükemmellik*, industrial engineer Zeynep Keserli investigates why over 70% of corporate Lean Manufacturing initiatives fail to achieve sustained operational gains. While organizations readily adopt technical lean tools—such as 5S, Value Stream Mapping, Kanban, and Kaizen events—they frequently treat lean as a mechanical cost-cutting program rather than an ongoing socio-cultural transformation.

### Technical Tools vs. Behavioral Habits

Keserli's thesis contrasts mechanical lean implementation against genuine Lean Culture:
- **Mechanical Lean:** Imposed top-down by management consultants, focused exclusively on inventory reduction and headcount cuts, triggering worker resistance and cynicism.
- **Cultural Lean (The Toyota Way):** Rooted in mutual respect for people and continuous problem-solving at the 'gemba' (the actual work floor). Every front-line worker is empowered to pull the 'Andon cord' to stop the production line when a defect is detected.

### Empirical Fieldwork and Implementation Framework

Through empirical surveys and field interviews across Turkish industrial manufacturing plants, Keserli articulates a structured transformation roadmap emphasizing executive coaching, frontline psychological safety, and visible visual management systems.
