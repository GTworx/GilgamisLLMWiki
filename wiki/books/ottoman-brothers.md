## Executive Summary

Written by historian **Michelle U. Campos** (Stanford University Press, 2011, Title Page p. 3, Copyright p. 4; 357 pp.), *Ottoman Brothers* recovers a pivotal, largely forgotten chapter of Middle Eastern history: the burst of **civic Ottomanism** (*Osmanlılık*) sparked by the **1908 Young Turk Constitutional Revolution** across late-Ottoman Palestine (Jerusalem, Jaffa, Haifa). Drawing on Arabic, Hebrew, Ladino, Ottoman Turkish, and European archival press records, Campos demonstrates that Muslims, Christians, and Sephardi/Ashkenazi Jews briefly imagined themselves not as mutually exclusive ethno-national enemies, but as co-equal citizens of a reformed multi-confessional constitutional empire.

Tracing joyous August 1908 constitutional rallies in Jaffa and Jerusalem (Ch. 1: *Sacred Liberty*, Ch. 2: *Brotherhood and Equality*, pp. 7–10), municipal elections, cross-communal Masonic lodges, Arabic/Hebrew newspaper debates (*Al-Quds*, *Ha-Herut*), parliamentary representation (such as Jerusalem MP **Ruhi al-Khalidi**), and the 1908 boycott of Austrian goods, Campos reconstructs how shared imperial citizenship worked on the ground—and how the Balkan Wars, WWI, rising Arab nationalism, and political Zionism ultimately tore that fragile civic consensus apart.

---

## Core Historical Breakthroughs

1. **Recovering Civic Ottomanism From Below**: Challenges retroactive nationalist teleologies (both Israeli and Palestinian) that project post-1917/1948 conflict backward into the Ottoman period, showing genuine cross-sectarian civic solidarity in 1908–1913.
2. **The Contested Boundaries of Liberty & Equality**: Reveals the structural tensions built into constitutional brotherhood—debates over military conscription for non-Muslims, language rights, land sales, and Sephardi Jewish intellectuals who championed Ottoman integration against European-backed separatist Zionism.
3. **Micro-History of Urban Pluralism**: Illuminates Jerusalem and Jaffa's vibrant public sphere of libraries (Khalidi Library), coffeehouses, chambers of commerce, and multilingual newspapers before British Mandate partitioning.
