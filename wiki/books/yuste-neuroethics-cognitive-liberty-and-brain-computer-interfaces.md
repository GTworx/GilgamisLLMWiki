## Executive Summary

This comprehensive 3-part neuroethics compendium, anchored by Rafael Yuste and Sara Goering's landmark Nature consensus paper, establishes the ethical, legal, and human rights frameworks required to govern emerging brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), neural decoding algorithms, and consumer neurotechnologies. Synthesizing insights across neuroscientists, neurosurgeons, and bioethicists, the collection outlines 'Neuro-Rights' across five core pillars: mental privacy, cognitive liberty/agency, personal identity, fair access to cognitive augmentation, and protection from algorithmic bias. The compendium evaluates state-of-the-art invasive neural implants (Neuralink, Synchron) and non-invasive brain-based speech decoders, addressing the legal imperative to classify neural data as human biological material protected under international human rights declarations.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **The Four Ethical Priorities**: Formalizes the Morningside Group's framework: Privacy and Consent, Agency and Identity, Augmentation, and Bias.
- **Cognitive Liberty and Mental Privacy**: Explains why neural telemetry is fundamentally distinct from web browsing or biometric data: it represents the pre-conscious, unfiltered source of human thought and intent, demanding absolute cryptographic protection.
- **Invasive vs. Non-Invasive BCI Trajectories**: Contrasts high-bandwidth intracortical microelectrode arrays (restoring communication to paralyzed patients) with consumer-grade EEG headbands that monetize attentional telemetry.
- **Neural Data Commercialization & Surveillance Capitalism**: Warns against business models that aggregate neural telemetry to train predictive consumer behavior models, advocating strict prohibitions on neural data harvesting.
- **Legislative Frontiers & Neuro-Rights Amendments**: Analyzes Chile's historic constitutional amendment protecting human brain data and neuro-rights, providing a legislative blueprint for global jurisdictions.
