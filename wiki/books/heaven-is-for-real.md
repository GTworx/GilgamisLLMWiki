## Executive Summary

*Heaven Is for Real* (2010, Thomas Nelson, pp. 7–8) by Nebraska Wesleyan pastor **Todd Burpo** and co-writer **Lynn Vincent** is a bestselling Christian family memoir recounting the near-death crisis and subsequent afterlife testimony of Burpo's nearly four-year-old son, **Colton Burpo**, following emergency surgery for a ruptured appendix in 2003 (pp. 4, 7).

Unfolding gradually over months after Colton's recovery, the narrative documents how the toddler casually shared startlingly specific details—claiming he watched his parents praying in separate hospital rooms during surgery, met his late great-grandfather "Pop" as a young man, encountered an unnamed miscarried sister his parents had never told him about, sat on Jesus's lap, and later recognized child prodigy **Akiane Kramarik's** painting *Prince of Peace* as the exact face of Christ (pp. 4–5).

---

## Narrative Arc & Cultural Context

1. **Medical Crisis & Parental Faith Tested**: Chronicles the Burpo family's cascading personal setbacks (broken leg, kidney stones, cancer scare) culminating in Colton's life-threatening misdiagnosed appendicitis and desperate hospital prayers.
2. **Emergence of Childlike Testimony**: Anchored in the Gospel epigraph on becoming "like little children" (p. 10), Todd and Sonja Burpo piece together Colton's unprompted remarks about heaven's colors, angelic music, absence of time/aging, and familial reunion.
3. **Phenomenon in Contemporary American Spirituality**: Selling over 10 million copies and inspiring a major film, the memoir exemplifies the early-21st-century surge in popular near-death-experience (NDE) literature—providing a vivid experiential counterpoint to the naturalistic critiques of religious experience in [[the-god-delusion]] and [[breaking-the-spell]].
