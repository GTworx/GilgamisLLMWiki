## Executive Summary

*An Autobiography, or The Story of My Experiments with Truth* (originally serialized in Gujarati in *Navajivan*, 1925–1929; translated into English by Mahadev Desai, Navajivan Publishing House / Gandhi Book Centre, 269 pp.) is [[mahatma-gandhi]]'s unflinchingly candid spiritual and political self-examination from his 1869 birth in Porbandar through the launch of India's non-cooperation movement in 1921 (source: BOOKS/Biography_/Gandhi-Truth.pdf, Title Page & Contents, pp. 2–3).

Framing his life not as a finished hagiography but as a series of fallible empirical **"experiments" with Truth (*Satya*) and Nonviolence (*Ahimsa*)**, Gandhi chronicles his childhood fears, child marriage, awkward years as a London law student, radical transformation after confronting racial discrimination in South Africa, and evolution into the architect of mass civil disobedience (*Satyagraha*)—arguing that self-mastery, truthfulness, and service to the marginalized are inseparable from genuine political liberation (pp. 3–269).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Part I: Childhood, Shame & London Apprenticeship (Chs. 1–25, pp. 3–45)
- **Moral Roots & Candid Confessions**: Traces his family's principled service as Diwans of Porbandar and Rajkot alongside raw confessions of childhood meat-eating, petty theft, and the haunting grief of his father's death (Chs. 1–9, pp. 3–16).
- **London & Comparative Ethics**: Recounts shedding his brief attempt to "play the English gentleman," discovering vegetarian societies, and encountering the *Bhagavad Gita*, the Sermon on the Mount, and comparative religion as enduring spiritual anchors (Chs. 13–25, pp. 23–45).

### Parts II–III: South Africa & the Birth of *Satyagraha* (Chs. 26–90, pp. 45–150)
- **The Pietermaritzburg Awakening**: Details his arrival in Natal, the traumatic confrontation with the racial colour bar on the road to Pretoria, and his organizing of the **Natal Indian Congress** to oppose the discriminatory £3 poll tax and disenfranchisement (Chs. 31–46, pp. 53–83).
- **Community, Diet & Self-Discipline**: Chronicles founding Phoenix Settlement and Tolstoy Farm, reading John Ruskin's *Unto This Last*, adopting *brahmacharya* (self-restraint), and testing nonviolent mass civil resistance (*satyagraha*—"truth-force") against colonial laws.

### Parts IV–V: Return to India & Mass Liberation (pp. 151–269)
- **Champaran, Kheda & Khadi**: Applies patient fact-finding, grassroots solidarity, and nonviolent non-cooperation to peasant indigo planters in Champaran, mill workers in Ahmedabad, and nationwide *swadeshi* (self-reliance through spinning *khadi*), insisting that ethical means can never be divorced from political ends.
