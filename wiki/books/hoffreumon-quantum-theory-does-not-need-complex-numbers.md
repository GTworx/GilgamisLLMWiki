## Executive Summary

This monumental 40-page foundational physics treatise by Timothée Hoffreumon (Inria QuaCS Team) and Mischa P. Woods (ETH Zurich / University of Geneva) challenges a central orthodoxy of modern physics: the indispensability of complex numbers in quantum theory. While recent experimental Bell-type tests (Renou et al., Nature 2021) claimed that complex Hilbert spaces are fundamentally necessary to reproduce the predictions of quantum mechanics, Hoffreumon and Woods present a mathematically rigorous counter-formulation. The authors prove that standard quantum theory can be completely and consistently formulated using only real Hilbert spaces, provided that one correctly accounts for the operational composition of physical systems and ancillary reference frames, resolving a fundamental philosophical debate in quantum foundations.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **The Complex Numbers Debate in Physics**: Reviews the foundational question of whether the imaginary unit $i = \sqrt{-1}$ is merely a mathematical convenience or an ontological feature of physical reality.
- **Deconstruction of Recent 'Complex Quantum' Proofs**: Rigorously analyzes recent theorems and experiments claiming complex numbers are essential, identifying hidden physical assumptions regarding tensor product composition and isolated subsystems.
- **Real-Hilbert-Space Operational Equivalence**: Constructs an exact, universal mathematical isomorphism showing that every complex quantum state, observable, and dynamical evolution can be mapped into a real Hilbert space with an invariant anti-symmetric operator $J$ ($J^2 = -\mathbb{I}$).
- **Reference Frames and Composite Systems**: Demonstrates that when physical reference frames and superselection rules are treated consistently, real quantum mechanics makes identical operational predictions to complex quantum mechanics across all physical experiments.
- **Philosophical Implications for Quantum Reality**: Establishes that nature does not inherently require imaginary numbers, liberating quantum ontology from non-operational mathematical conventions.
