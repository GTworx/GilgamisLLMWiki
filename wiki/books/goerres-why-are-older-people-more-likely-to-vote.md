## Analytical Overview
### The Political Sociology of the Silver Ballot

In *Why Are Older People More Likely to Vote? The Impact of Ageing on Electoral Turnout in Europe*, published in *The British Journal of Politics & International Relations* (2007), political sociologist Achim Goerres investigates one of the most consistent findings in electoral studies: the massive, persistent turnout gap between young and elderly citizens.

### Disentangling Cohort, Life-Cycle, and Habit Effects

Goerres employs multi-level statistical modeling across ten European democracies to evaluate competing hypotheses:
1. **The Life-Cycle Effect:** As citizens age, they accumulate homeownership, healthcare dependencies, and state pension benefits, expanding their material stake in government policy.
2. **The Habituation of Voting:** Voting functions as a learned psychological habit; individuals who vote in their first three elections continue voting throughout their lifetimes.
3. **Internalized Civic Duty:** Older generations socialized during post-war reconstruction possess significantly stronger normative beliefs that voting is a moral civic obligation rather than an optional personal preference.

### Democratic Implications

Goerres analyzes the risk of "gray power" skewing democratic public budgets away from public education and child care toward un-reformable pension and healthcare entitlements.
