## Executive Summary

*Math Puzzles: Classic Riddles in Counting, Geometry, Probability, and Game Theory* (2012, 488 pp.) by Stanford-trained mathematician, economist, and creator of the *Mind Your Decisions* platform [[presh-talwalkar]] curates and solves classic quantitative brainteasers drawn from mathematical competitions, probability paradoxes, technical interviews, and strategic game theory (source: BOOKS/Fun & Puzzles_/Math puzzles_ classic riddles...pdf, Title Page, Acknowledgements & Table of Contents, pp. 2–8).

From iconic optimization and geometric intuition checks (*Ants on a Triangle*, *Camel and Bananas*, *Rope Around Earth*, *Measuring Ball Bearings*, *Paying an Employee in Gold*) to counterintuitive Bayesian/conditional probability puzzles and backward-induction game-theory riddles, Talwalkar pairs crisp puzzle statements with transparent step-by-step mathematical solutions that sharpen analytical intuition (pp. 5–488).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Counting, Geometry & Optimization Puzzles (Section 1, pp. 5–160)
- **Counterintuitive Elegance**: Uses classics like *Ants on a Triangle* (collision probabilities), *Rope Around Earth* (linear circumference–radius invariance regardless of planet size), *Camel and Bananas* (multi-stage fuel optimization), and *Paying an Employee in Gold* (binary weight representation) to illustrate how reframing dissolves apparent complexity (pp. 5–7).

### Probability & Game Theory Puzzles (Sections 2–4, pp. 161–488)
- **Stochastic & Strategic Reasoning**: Explores carnival coin-tossing expectations, conditional probability traps, pirate/cake-cutting fair division, and backward-induction Nash equilibria—demonstrating how formal probability and game theory override misleading human intuition.
