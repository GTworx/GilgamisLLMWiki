## Executive Summary

*Enchanted America: How Intuition and Reason Divide Our Politics* (University of Chicago Press, 2018, 278 pp.) by political scientists [[j-eric-oliver]] (University of Chicago) and [[thomas-j-wood]] (Ohio State University) argues that the deepest fault line in contemporary American politics is no longer merely ideological ("liberal" vs. "conservative"), but epistemic: a cognitive divide between **Rationalists** and **Intuitionists** (source: BOOKS/Politics_/2018 Eric Oliver Enchanted America.pdf, Preface, pp. xi–xiv).

Drawing on six nationally representative surveys and an original, doctrine-free **Intuitionism scale** combining apprehension, pessimism, and symbolic/magical thinking, Oliver and Wood refute Max Weber's 1920 forecast of modern "disenchantment" (p. 5). They show that intuitive, gut-level cognition predicts a remarkably coherent cluster of attitudes—from religious fundamentalism, paranormal belief, and conspiracy theories to vaccine skepticism, anti-GMO sentiment, ethnocentrism, and populist support for Donald Trump—and that the sorting of Intuitionists into the Republican Party and Rationalists into the Democratic Party has eroded the shared epistemic ground required for democratic compromise (pp. xii–xiv, 56, 126).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Preface & Introduction: Rationalists vs. Intuitionists (pp. xi–xiv, 1–14)
- **Beyond Policy Preferences**: Opens with the paradox of voters who simultaneously hold seemingly contradictory left- and right-leaning positions (e.g., organic/anti-GMO skepticism alongside evangelical social conservatism), showing they are unified by intuitive rather than ideological reasoning (pp. 1–14).
- **Cognitive Polarity**: Contrasts Rationalists—who rely on abstract deduction, scientific institutions, and dispassionate analysis—with Intuitionists, who trust gut feelings, moral symbols, mythic narratives, and threat vigilance (pp. xii–xiii).

### Chapters 1–3: Measuring Intuitionism (pp. 15–86)
- **Evolutionary Heuristics & Symbolic Thinking (Chs. 1–2)**: Grounded in cognitive psychology and anthropology (Malinowski, Frazer), constructs the Intuitionism scale from non-political items measuring threat apprehension, pessimism, and sympathetic/contagion magic (pp. 15–66).
- **Predictive Power**: Demonstrates that high Intuitionism scores strongly predict supernatural beliefs (nearly 60% endorsing hidden Bible codes vs. 20% of Rationalists), end-times beliefs, and institutional distrust (pp. 51–60).
- **Demographics & Sorting (Ch. 3)**: Examines how education, income, authoritarian child-rearing values, and religious observance correlate with Intuitionism across the electorate (pp. 67–86).

### Chapters 4–7: Politics, Populism, Identity, and Wellness (pp. 87–170)
- **Ideology & Moral Traditionalism (Ch. 4)**: Separates free-market conservatism from moral traditionalism, showing Intuitionism primarily fuels the latter (pp. 87–106).
- **Truthers and *Trumpenvolk* (Ch. 5)**: Analyzes 2016 primary survey data to show that Trump succeeded not through policy orthodoxy, but by speaking directly to populist, conspiratorial, and visceral anxieties of Intuitionist voters (pp. 107–128, 126).
- **Ethnocentrism & Health Skepticism (Chs. 6–7)**: Links Intuitionism both to white ethnocentrism/nativism on the Right and to alternative-medicine, anti-vaccine, and "natural food" anxieties across the spectrum (pp. 129–170).

### Chapter 8: A Nation Divided by Magic (pp. 171–196, with Calvin TerBeek)
- **Bridging the Epistemic Chasm**: Documents the collapse of institutional trust since 1979 and argues that Rationalists cannot defeat illiberal populism through condescending technocracy; invoking Nelson Mandela's distinction between speaking to the head and the heart, they urge translating science, due process, and civil liberties into clear, emotionally resonant narratives grounded in immediate human concerns (pp. 179, 195).

## Key Quotes & Page Citations

- > "The most important political division in the United States is not simply between liberals and conservatives or between 'red' and 'blue' states; rather, it is between Rationalists and Intuitionists." (source: BOOKS/Politics_/2018 Eric Oliver Enchanted America.pdf, Preface, p. xii)
- > "Americans are divided not simply in how they see the proper role of government but in how they see reality." (source: BOOKS/Politics_/2018 Eric Oliver Enchanted America.pdf, Preface, p. xiv)
