## Executive Summary

Co-authored by Dutch high-tech VC general partner **Harm F. de Vries** (Innovation Industries), former investment banker/legal consultant **Menno J. van Loon**, and Benvalor M&A/VC partner **Sjoerd Mol** (copyright p. 3; About the Authors p. 4), *Venture Capital Deal Terms* (2016) provides a razor-sharp, clause-by-clause European and international legal-economic handbook for structuring startup investments. Anchoring theory in a continuous multi-round case study (**New Wave Energy**, tracked from incorporation through Seed, Series A, B, C, and IPO, Ch. 2, pp. 14–24), the authors present a complete sample term sheet (Ch. 3, pp. 25–39) and then systematically dissect **47 distinct contractual clauses** (Ch. 4.1–4.47, pp. 40–146).

Particularly valuable alongside U.S.-centric Silicon Valley texts, the book highlights practical drafting nuances under civil-law and international governance frameworks while providing a turnkey Term Sheet Template, case-study financial statements, glossary, and IRR return-multiple matrix (Annexes 1–4, pp. 147–189).

## Exhaustive Clause-by-Clause Coverage (47 Provisions)

1. **Valuation, Securities & Downside Protection (4.1–4.17)**: Explains milestone tranching, preferred vs. convertible securities, warrant coverage, pre/post-money cap tables, dividends, redemption, voluntary/automatic conversion, **full-ratchet vs. weighted-average anti-dilution** (pp. 70–78), **pay-to-play**, and **liquidation preference waterfalls** (non-participating vs. participating, pp. 80–88).
2. **Governance, Control & Liquidity Rights (4.18–4.29)**: Breaks down supervisory/management board representation, protective consent vetoes, registration rights, information rights, **pre-emptive (pro-rata) rights**, **ROFR**, **co-sale (tag-along)**, and **drag-along** thresholds (pp. 90–122).
3. **Founder Alignment & Closing Mechanics (4.30–4.47)**: Covers option pools, reverse founder vesting (good leaver / bad leaver), IP assignment, non-competes, key-man insurance, exclusivity/no-shop clauses, indemnities, and conditions precedent (pp. 123–146).
