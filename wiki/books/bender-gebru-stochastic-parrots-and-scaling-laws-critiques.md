## Summary
Foundational critique of foundation model scaling and epistemic validity. Centers around the landmark FAccT 2021 treatise 'On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?' by Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, and Mitchell, examining the environmental costs of massive training runs, linguistic form without communicative intent, training data biases, and the diminishing returns of brute-force parameter scaling.

## Analytical Overview
This volume constitutes the definitive critical counterweight to uncritical technological solutionism in generative AI.

Key critical arguments:
1. **The 'Stochastic Parrot' Paradigm**: Details Bender and Gebru’s rigorous linguistic critique demonstrating that deep autoregressive models are statistical pattern matchers that stitch together linguistic forms based on probabilistic distributions without reference to communicative intent, grounding in physical reality, or semantic understanding.
2. **Environmental & Carbon Costs of Mega-Scale Compute**: Quantifies the massive energetic expenditure and carbon footprints of training multi-hundred-billion parameter models, contrasting these environmental externalities with the marginal real-world utility for marginalized communities.
3. **Dataset Ingestion Biases & Data Exhaustion**: Interrogates the uncurated scraping of the public web, documenting how internet toxicity, racist and gender stereotypes, and hegemonic viewpoints are permanently baked into model weights, while analyzing contemporary limits on human text availability and the risks of model collapse from recursive synthetic data.
