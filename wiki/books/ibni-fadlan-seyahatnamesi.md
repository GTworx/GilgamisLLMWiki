## Executive Summary

Composed by tenth-century Abbasid diplomat and jurist **Ahmad ibn Fadlan** following his 921–922 CE embassy from Caliph al-Muqtadir in Baghdad to the King of the Volga Bulgars (Almısh / Ilteber), and presented here in **Lütfi Doğan**'s classic Turkish translation based on **A. Zeki Velidi Togan**'s landmark 1939 Leipzig critical edition of the Mashhad manuscript (*İbn Fadlān's Reisebericht*, 22 pp. scanned scholarly chapter), the *Risala* (*Seyahatname*) is one of the earliest and most vital eyewitness ethnographies of Eurasian Turkic peoples and the Rus' (Vikings).

Traveling across Bukhara, Khwarazm, the Ust-Yurt plateau, and the Volga basin, Ibn Fadlan records firsthand observations of **Oghuz Turks**, **Pechenegs**, **Bashkirs**, **Volga Bulgars**, **Khazars**, and **Rus' merchants**—documenting nomadic customs, burial rites, legal norms, diplomacy, religious conversion, and long-distance steppe trade routes.

---

## Core Historical & Ethnographic Contributions

1. **Primary Window on Early Turkic & Steppe Societies**: Offers indispensable pre-Seljuk primary evidence on Oghuz political titles, hospitality protocols, marriage customs, shamanic beliefs, and early Turkic encounters with Islam along the Volga.
2. **Eyewitness Account of the Rus'**: Contains medieval history's most celebrated firsthand account of a Norse ship-burial ceremony on the Volga, bridging Islamic historiography and Northern European archaeology.
3. **Diplomatic & Cross-Cultural Ethnography**: Models acute diplomatic observation—noting both cultural shock and pragmatic statecraft as Baghdad sought geopolitical and religious alliances across Eurasia.
