## Executive Summary

Prepared by Arman Shehabi and a team of researchers at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL), this landmark 79-page federal study provides the authoritative nationwide quantitative assessment of United States data center energy consumption. Updating the historic 2016 LBNL Data Center Energy Report, the authors model historical trends from 2014 to 2023 and project electricity demands through 2030 across hyperscale, colocation, enterprise, and edge facilities. The report details how the dramatic efficiency gains achieved through server virtualization, cloud migration, and hyperscale PUE optimization (dropping from 2.0 to ~1.15) are now being overtaken by explosive AI accelerator power density, projecting U.S. data center electricity consumption to reach 6%–9% of total national electricity generation by 2030.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Historical Efficiency Decoupling Reversal**: Between 2010 and 2018, data center compute workloads grew dramatically while total energy usage remained nearly flat due to hyperscale efficiency; the explosion of LLM training and inference has decoupled this trend, driving absolute power consumption sharply upward.
- **Hyperscale vs. Enterprise Redistribution**: Hyperscale cloud and colocation facilities now account for over 75% of total U.S. data center electricity consumption, completely marginalizing distributed on-premise enterprise server closets.
- **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) Limits**: Average hyperscale PUE has reached 1.15–1.20, leaving minimal remaining thermodynamic headroom for cooling-efficiency improvements; future energy reductions must come from silicon-level compute efficiency and software optimization.
- **Regional Grid Concentration**: Over 50% of U.S. data center electricity demand is concentrated in a handful of regional utility service territories (PJM / Northern Virginia, ERCOT / Texas, APS / Arizona, PacifiCorp / Oregon), creating severe localized grid stress.
- **Scenarios for 2030 Electric Load**: Under conservative AI adoption scenarios, U.S. data center load reaches 35 GW (~260 TWh); under aggressive generative AI deployment scenarios, load exceeds 50 GW (~400 TWh), necessitating substantial new grid generation capacity.
