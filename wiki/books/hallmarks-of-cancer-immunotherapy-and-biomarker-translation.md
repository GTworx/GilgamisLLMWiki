## Summary
Deep theoretical and critical oncology compendium. Features Hanahan & Weinberg's seminal 'Hallmarks of Cancer' framework, the epistemological debate between the Somatic Mutation Theory (SMT) and Tissue Organization Field Theory (TOFT), Prasad's sobering empirical analysis showing only a minority (~12%) of US cancer patients actually respond to checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapies, and systemic investigations into why protein cancer biomarkers fail clinical translation.

## Analytical Overview
This volume provides a rigorous epistemological and methodological critique of modern oncology, balancing biological paradigms with clinical trial reality.

Key analytical pillars:
1. **The Hallmarks of Cancer**: Examines Douglas Hanahan and Robert Weinberg’s defining conceptual framework, organizing cancer's chaotic phenotypic manifestations into ten hallmark capabilities (sustained proliferative signaling, evading growth suppressors, resisting cell death, enabling replicative immortality, inducing angiogenesis, activating invasion/metastasis, reprogramming energy metabolism, and evading immune destruction).
2. **The SMT vs. TOFT Epistemological Debate**: Features extensive NCI and academic debate papers contrasting the dominant *Somatic Mutation Theory* (cancer as a cell-autonomous genetic disease) with the *Tissue Organization Field Theory* (cancer as a failure of tissue architecture, stroma-epithelial signaling, and bioelectric morphogenetic fields).
3. **The Empirical Reality of Immunotherapy**: Incorporates Prasad and colleagues’ landmark study estimating the true percentage of US cancer patients who benefit from checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4), demonstrating that while media narratives portray universal cure, only ~43% of patients are eligible and only ~12% achieve durable responses, exposing massive biomarker translation bottlenecks.
