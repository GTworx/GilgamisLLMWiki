## Overview
Published in Computers (MDPI) by educational technologist Athanasios Christopoulos and collaborators, this comprehensive paper articulates an ethical framework for deploying augmented reality in K-12 and higher education. The authors balance pedagogy-enhancing 3D spatial visualization with risks of cognitive overload, socioeconomic hardware divides, and child biometric privacy.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Identifies cognitive friction: cognitive overload caused by poorly designed AR interfaces competing with real-world sensory inputs.
- **Key Finding**: Formulates student privacy safeguards against spatial classroom telemetry and tracking.
- **Key Finding**: Provides an empirical evaluation checklist for educators selecting AR software and wearable devices.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Augmented reality in the classroom must enhance pedagogical comprehension without colonizing young learners' visual and cognitive attention with commercial distractions."
