## Overview
This comprehensive 4-study academic dossier synthesizes Islamic spiritual psychology, Al-Ghazali's mystical epistemology (*Ihya Ulum al-Din*), and modern humanistic psychology (Abraham Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs and Peak Experiences). The authors establish rigorous theoretical mappings between Maslow's self-actualization/transcendence and the classical Sufi stages of the soul: from *Nafs al-Ammarah* (egocentric impulse) through *Nafs al-Lawwamah* (reproachful conscience) to *Nafs al-Mutma'innah* and *Sakinah* (divine spiritual tranquility).

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Correlates Maslow's hierarchical needs with classical Islamic developmental psychology: physiological, safety, social, self-actualization, and divine transcendence.
- **Key Finding**: Analyzes Al-Ghazali's critique of dry scholastic rationalism, showing how intuitive spiritual taste (*dhawq*) resolves existential doubt.
- **Key Finding**: Explores Bediuzzaman Said Nursi's *Risale-i Nur* synthesis of modern scientific reflection with Sufi contemplation (*tefekkür*).

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Where Western humanistic psychology terminates at self-actualization, Islamic Sufi psychology begins—guiding the human spirit beyond ego-affirmation into self-transcendence and the divine peace of Sakinah."
