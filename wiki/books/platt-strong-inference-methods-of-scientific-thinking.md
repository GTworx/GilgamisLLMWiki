## Analytical Overview
### The Manifesto for Intellectual Velocity

John R. Platt's 1964 *Science* paper, *Strong Inference*, is widely celebrated as one of the most influential essays on scientific method ever published. Platt poses a blunt, searching question: why do certain scientific fields—such as molecular biology (Watson, Crick, Jacob, Monod) and high-energy physics—advance with breathtaking speed, solving fundamental mysteries in months, while other disciplines (chemistry, psychology, geology) wander in incremental, descriptive stagnation for decades?

### The Four Steps of Strong Inference

Platt demonstrates that high-velocity scientific progress is not a function of money, laboratories, or luck, but of the relentless, intellectual discipline of **Strong Inference**, executed through four sequential steps:
1. Devising **multiple alternative hypotheses** for every observed phenomenon.
2. Devising a **crucial experiment (or several)** with alternative possible outcomes, each of which will exclude as nearly as possible one or more of the hypotheses.
3. Carrying out the experiment so as to get a clean, unambiguous result.
4. Recycling the procedure, making sub-hypotheses or sequential hypotheses to refine the remaining possibilities.

### The Antidote to Methodological Sclerosis

Platt warned against 'the intellectual trap of falling in love with a single hypothesis', which turns scientists into defensive advocates rather than objective investigators. He urged every working researcher to ask their colleagues during seminars: *'Sir, what experiment could disprove your hypothesis?'*
