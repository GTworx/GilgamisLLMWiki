## Executive Summary
This 21-page surgical bioengineering paper details the development of an in situ robotic 3D bioprinting system that directly prints customized, biodegradable, osteoinductive composite implants into critical-sized bone defects during live surgical procedures.

The system combines computer vision, robotic multi-axis path planning, and photo-curable bioactive composite inks (polycaprolactone, gelatin methacryloyl, and strontium-doped bioactive glass nanoparticles). The robotic arm tracks intraoperative patient movement, scanning the geometry of the shattered bone void and depositing the biomaterial layer-by-layer directly into the defect site. In preclinical orthopedic models, the in situ printed implants demonstrated perfect anatomical fit, robust initial mechanical stability, accelerated vascular ingrowth, and complete bony bridging within 12 weeks.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Direct In Situ Surgical Fabrication**: Eliminates the delay and fit errors of pre-fabricated implants by scanning and depositing biomaterials directly into the surgical defect in real time.
- **Bioactive Glass Nanoparticle Integration**: Incorporates strontium-doped bioactive glass that slowly releases osteogenic ions ($Sr^{2+}$, $Ca^{2+}$, $Si^{4+}$), stimulating osteoblast differentiation while suppressing osteoclast resorption.
- **Robotic Vision and Motion Compensation**: Employs real-time optical tracking to compensate for patient respiratory motion and minor limb movements during intraoperative printing.
- **Accelerated Functional Bone Healing**: Preclinical histomorphometry confirms rapid degradation of the scaffold matching the exact kinetics of new bone formation, avoiding permanent foreign body stress-shielding.
