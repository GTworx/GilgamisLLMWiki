## Analytical Overview
### The Paradox of Blockbuster Success

*Not All Success Is Created Equal: The Innovation Costs of Extreme Success*, published in *Research Policy* (Vol. 55, 2025/2026), investigates the organizational pathology termed the **Superstar Trap**. While conventional management wisdom assumes that massive commercial success provides the financial runway for bolder R&D experimentation, this empirical study proves that blockbuster hits systematically degrade an organization's subsequent innovative capacity.

### The Mechanisms of Innovative Calcification

Analyzing longitudinal data across creative and high-tech industries, the researchers uncover three core psychological and organizational drivers:
1. **Competency Trap & Formulaic Exploitation:** Once a product achieves extreme market dominance, executive teams become path-dependent, channeling resources into milking the existing formula rather than exploring orthogonal possibilities.
2. **Risk Aversion and Brand Fragility:** Extreme success creates reputational conservatism. Fear of tarnishing the flagship franchise leads to committee-driven decision-making and the suppression of eccentric internal mavericks.
3. **Talent Inflow of Exploitation Specialists:** High-growth cash-cow divisions attract risk-averse execution managers, gradually displacing the idiosyncratic creative visionaries who generated the original breakthrough.

### Escaping the Super-Success Curse

The authors formulate organizational structural remedies—such as autonomous skunkworks and structural ambidexterity—to insulate exploratory R&D from the gravitational pull of blockbuster cash cows.
