## Executive Summary
This 16-page methodological treatise in sensory food science and mathematical statistics examines the classical Triangle Test with replicated evaluations. The authors analyze statistical discrimination testing—where human sensory panelists are presented with three coded food samples (two identical, one different) and tasked with identifying the odd sample by taste, aroma, or texture. The paper compares generalized linear mixed models (GLMM), beta-binomial distributions, and Thurstonian psychophysical modeling to correct for panelist heterogeneity, overdispersion, and sensory fatigue, establishing rigorous statistical standards for consumer food reformulation and ingredient substitution.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Triangle Test Paradigm**: The three-sample forced-choice test is the gold standard in food and beverage sensory evaluation, with a baseline guessing probability of p = 1/3.
- **Correcting for Panelist Heterogeneity**: Standard binomial models fail when panelists perform repeated trials because individual sensory acuity varies; beta-binomial and mixed-effects models prevent false-positive differentiation.
- **Thurstonian d-Prime (d') Metric**: Converts raw correct response proportions into a scale-independent perceptual distance metric (d'), allowing direct comparison between different sensory modalities.
- **Food Industry Reformulation Applications**: Essential for validating that cost-cutting ingredient substitutions, sodium reductions, or artificial sweetener swaps produce no perceptible sensory difference for consumers.
