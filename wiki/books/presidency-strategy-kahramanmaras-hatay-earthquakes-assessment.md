## Summary
Definitive macroeconomic, public health, and humanitarian assessment of the twin February 6 and February 20, 2023 Kahramanmaraş and Hatay earthquakes (Mw 7.8 and 7.5), presenting the official Turkish government damage tally ($103.6 billion), USG fact sheets, TTB first-month health evaluations, and civil society recovery initiatives.

## Analytical Overview
This canonical work stands as the definitive official and civil society record of the catastrophic seismic sequence that devastated 11 provinces across southern and southeastern Turkey on February 6 and February 20, 2023. Led by the comprehensive 145-page official assessment published in March 2023 by the Presidency Strategy and Budget Directorate (*2023 Kahramanmaraş and Hatay Earthquakes Report*), the volume quantifies the total physical damage and economic loss at $103.6 billion (equivalent to 9.0% of Turkey's 2023 GDP). The report inventories the destruction or critical impairment of over 518,000 housing units across Hatay, Kahramanmaraş, Adıyaman, Malatya, and Gaziantep, detailing the collapse of municipal infrastructure, energy grids, and manufacturing clusters.

The official governmental record is cross-examined and enriched by critical civil society and humanitarian perspectives. The Turkish Medical Association (TTB) First Month Report provides unvarnished field epidemiological data on the collapse of regional hospitals, mortuary crises, potable water contamination risks, tetanus and infection outbreaks, and chronic disease disruptions among hundreds of thousands of displaced tent-city residents. Concurrently, gender-focused post-disaster evaluations from the EŞİK Platform highlight the disproportionate burdens placed on women regarding domestic care, sanitary security, and physical violence in informal encampments.

The volume also incorporates international relief documentation: U.S. Government (USAID/BHA) Disaster Assistance Response Team (DART) Fact Sheet #9 detailing emergency airlift logistics and search-and-rescue (DART/USAR) coordination; the Bridge to Türkiye Fund (BTF) One-Year Relief Report detailing the transparent disbursement of over $10 million in diaspora donations for education, orphan care, and mobile clinics; and corporate social innovation projects such as TÜBİSAD’s 'TEK' Technological Education Container classrooms restoring schooling to displaced youth in tent cities.
