## Overview
Published in Nature (August 1938) by Scottish-South African paleontologist Robert Broom, accompanied by clinical anthropological case archives, this historic paper documents the discovery of adult Australopithecus and Paranthropus robustus cranial and dental fossils at Sterkfontein and Kromdraai. Broom's discovery provided the decisive physical evidence confirming Raymond Dart's controversial claim that human bipedal origins lay in Africa.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Provides historic eyewitness documentation of the first adult Australopithecine fossils ever discovered.
- **Key Finding**: Proves bipedal locomotion evolved millions of years before massive brain encephalization occurred.
- **Key Finding**: Anchors the Cradle of Humankind as the definitive evolutionary birthplace of the hominin lineage.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "The discovery of adult anthropoid ape skulls in the cave limestone of Sterkfontein leaves no room for doubt: here we have beings that stood erect, walk bipedally, and represent the direct bridge between apes and humans."
