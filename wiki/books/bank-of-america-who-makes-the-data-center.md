## Executive Summary

Authored by Bank of America Global Research, this comprehensive two-part institutional primer provides an end-to-end supply chain mapping of modern hyperscale and enterprise data center construction. Spanning electrical systems (substations, high-voltage transformers, switchgear, uninterruptible power supplies, backup generators), thermal management (direct-to-chip liquid cooling, immersion cooling, chillers, cooling towers), and compute infrastructure (rack architectures, optical transceivers, network fabric), the report analyzes the unprecedented capex wave driven by generative AI cluster deployments. BofA quantifies the bottlenecks in electrical distribution equipment—where lead times for large power transformers have stretched past 100 weeks—and evaluates supplier market shares and competitive moats across Schneider Electric, Eaton, Vertiv, nVent, Cummins, and Caterpillar.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Electrical Value Chain Dominance**: Electrical distribution represents 40%–45% of mechanical and electrical (M&E) capex in AI-ready data centers, with power distribution units (PDUs) and switchgear experiencing unprecedented pricing power.
- **The Thermal Inflection to Liquid Cooling**: As AI rack densities surpass 40 kW/rack and head toward 100 kW+ with next-generation accelerators (e.g., Nvidia Blackwell and custom hyperscaler ASICs), air cooling reaches thermodynamic limits, making direct-to-chip (DLC) cold plates and rear-door heat exchangers mandatory for new builds.
- **Equipment Lead-Time Bottlenecks**: Lead times for high-voltage transformers (HVTs) and medium-voltage switchgear exceed 24–36 months, creating structural barriers to data center commissioning and giving integrated global suppliers significant backlog visibility.
- **Hyperscale Capex Multiplication**: Hyperscale cloud operators (Microsoft, AWS, Google, Meta) are accelerating infrastructure capital expenditure at 30%+ CAGR, with physical facility and power infrastructure absorbing a growing share of total project budgets relative to baseline IT hardware.
- **Supply Chain Moats & Qualification Cycles**: Stringent data center uptime and safety standards (Tier III and IV redundancy) create high switching costs and lengthy qualification cycles, favoring incumbent power and cooling vendors over commoditized white-label alternatives.
