## Executive Summary
Published in *Nature* (2025), this 29-page interdisciplinary treatise examines the epistemological methodology of speculative fiction as an essential tool for scientific inquiry, technological forecasting, and planetary risk assessment. The authors argue that science fiction operates not merely as entertainment, but as an iterative hypothesis-testing laboratory ('the Sci-Fi Science Method') that simulates the second- and third-order sociological, psychological, and ethical consequences of emerging technologies before they are physically instantiated. Case studies include early conceptions of communication satellites (Arthur C. Clarke), cyberspace (William Gibson), genetic engineering (Aldous Huxley), and autonomous weapons.

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **The Speculative Epistemology**: Formulates science fiction as a rigorous thought-experiment methodology for mapping non-linear civilizational risk landscapes.
- **Second-Order Consequence Simulation**: Demonstrates how speculative narratives reveal social dysfunctions and alignment failures that traditional engineering frameworks overlook.
- **Inspiration to Implementation Pipeline**: Documents historical precedents where fictional concepts directly seeded subsequent engineering research and funding mandates.
