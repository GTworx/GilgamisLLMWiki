## Summary
A foundational cognitive psychology and epistemology compendium anchored by Roy Baumeister's classic 2001 treatise *Bad Is Stronger Than Good* (establishing the evolutionary ubiquity of negativity bias), paired with M. Giulia Napolitano's epistemological theory of 'evidential self-insulation' in conspiracy theories, Jeffrey Sanford Russell on the logic of fanaticism, and Erika Branchini on contrarian thinking in human reasoning.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
In 2001, Roy Baumeister, Ellen Bratslavsky, Catrin Finkenauer, and Kathleen Vohs reviewed hundreds of psychological studies to establish that across virtually all domains of human psychology, negative events, emotions, and feedback exert vastly more psychological impact than positive ones. Concurrently, modern epistemologists investigated why conspiracy theories and political fanaticism demonstrate complete immunity to counter-evidence.

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
Baumeister models the evolutionary logic of asymmetry: survival requires prioritizing mortal threats over prospective benefits (an animal that misses a meal survives; an animal that misses a predator perishes). Napolitano introduces the concept of 'evidential self-insulation'—epistemic belief structures where any counter-evidence presented against the theory is reinterpreted as positive proof of the conspiracy's vast cover-up power, rendering the belief unfalsifiable and hermetically sealed.

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
The empirical evidence spans multiple psychological domains: 1) Relationship survival requires a 5:1 ratio of positive to negative interactions (Gottman); 2) Financial losses hurt roughly twice as intensely as equivalent gains please (Kahneman-Tversky loss aversion); 3) Traumatic experiences imprint in one trial, while positive habits require weeks of reinforcement. Russell's philosophical proof demonstrates that ideological fanaticism emerges from extreme infinite-value expected utility frameworks, driving actors to discount all mundane human costs.

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
This compendium is a cornerstone of behavioral science. Baumeister's negativity bias and Napolitano's self-insulating epistemology provide the essential analytical tools for understanding social media engagement algorithms, political polarization, and cognitive warfare.
