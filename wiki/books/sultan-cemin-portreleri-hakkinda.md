## Executive Summary

Authored by pioneering Turkish art and Byzantine-Ottoman architectural historian **Prof. Dr. Semavi Eyice** (1922–2018) and published in the Turkish Historical Society's flagship journal *Belleten* (Vol. XXXVII, No. 145, January 1973; 80 pp. scanned offprint with full iconographic plates, visually verified), *Sultan Cem'in Portreleri Hakkında* is a rigorous art-historical detective study investigating the European Renaissance visual representations of **Cem Sultan** (1459–1495)—son of Mehmed the Conqueror and exiled Ottoman claimant held in Rhodes, France, and Papal Rome.

Eyice systematically catalogs, compares, and debunks attributions across fifteenth- and sixteenth-century frescoes, medals, woodcuts, and manuscripts—most famously **Pinturicchio**'s Borgia Apartment frescoes in the Vatican, **Gentile Bellini**'s circle, Guillaume Caoursin's illustrated Rhodes chronicle, and later European engravings—separating authentic eyewitness depictions from generic "Orientalized" turbans and misidentified Ottoman envoys.

---

## Core Art-Historical Contributions

1. **Iconographic Source Criticism**: Cross-checks physical descriptions from contemporary Italian and French eyewitness accounts (including Matteo Bosso and Mantuan diplomatic dispatches) against surviving Renaissance paintings and woodcuts.
2. **Ottoman–Renaissance Cultural Encounter**: Demonstrates how Cem Sultan's dramatic exile at the courts of the Knights of St. John, Charles VIII, and Popes Innocent VIII and Alexander VI shaped early modern European visual tropes of Ottoman royalty.
3. **Visual Corpus Preservation**: Reproduces dozens of rare archival plates, enabling direct comparative verification of costume, headgear, facial features, and compositional borrowing across European workshops.
