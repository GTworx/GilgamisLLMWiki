## Executive Summary

Authored by McKinsey & Company's Technology, Media & Telecommunications (TMT) Practice, this strategic investment report evaluates private equity deployment, valuation multiples, and structural growth vectors across the global data center economy. McKinsey estimates that global data center demand will reach 35 GW of power capacity by 2030, requiring over $400 billion in direct capital expenditure. The report outlines five strategic imperatives for investors and operators: securing green power at scale, building modular and standardized facilities to compress delivery cycles, navigating supply chain bottlenecks in electrical equipment, implementing advanced direct-to-chip cooling, and establishing strong local community and municipal relationships.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Sustained Demand Tailwinds**: Forecasts a 10%–12% CAGR in global data center demand through 2030, driven by foundational cloud migration, data analytics expansion, and the generative AI compute shock.
- **Private Equity and M&A Dynamics**: Private capital has acquired an unprecedented share of independent data center operators, but winning in the current market requires operational capabilities in energy management rather than pure financial engineering.
- **Standardization & Modular Construction**: To compress construction timelines from 24–36 months down to 12–16 months, leading developers are standardizing modular electrical and mechanical skids pre-assembled offsite.
- **Thermal Density Economics**: Outlines how transitioning from legacy 5–10 kW per rack architectures to 30–50 kW+ liquid-cooled configurations maximizes compute throughput per square foot of whitespace, enhancing return on invested capital.
- **Decarbonization as a License to Operate**: Long-term enterprise and hyperscale tenants increasingly refuse to lease facilities that cannot guarantee traceable, hourly-matched renewable power supply, making carbon strategy a core valuation driver.
