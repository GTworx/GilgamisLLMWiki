## Overview
This groundbreaking 37-page paleogenomics paper led by Fulya Eylem Yediay and Guus Kroonen, accompanied by recent 2025 archaeological genomic reports from Remontnoye, examines ancient human genomes to resolve the deep branchings of Indo-European languages. The authors provide genetic evidence separating early Anatolian and Greek lineages from Western European Italo-Celtic branches, tracking steppe pastoralist Yamnaya admixture corridors across the Mediterranean.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Uncovers genomic divergence patterns corroborating early chronological splits between Eastern and Western Mediterranean Indo-European dialects.
- **Key Finding**: Demonstrates differential steppe ancestry ratios in Bronze Age Aegean, Anatolian, and Balkan populations.
- **Key Finding**: Reconciles linguistic divergence times with ancient DNA radiocarbon dates from Yamnaya and Remontnoye horizon burials.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Ancient DNA data reveals that the spread of Indo-European languages into the Mediterranean was not a uniform migration, but a multi-staged demographic admixture creating profound deep genetic and linguistic divergences."
