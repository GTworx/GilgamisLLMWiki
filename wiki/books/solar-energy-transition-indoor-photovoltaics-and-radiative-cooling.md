## Summary
Compendium of breakthrough peer-reviewed research tracking the global exponential solar transition, featuring Nature research on irreversible solar tipping points, 37.6% efficiency indoor perovskite photovoltaics, scalable glass-polymer metamaterials for passive radiative cooling, and Caltech’s space wireless power transfer arrays.

## Analytical Overview
This volume documents the exponential technological and thermodynamic advancements cementing solar radiation as humanity's dominant future primary energy source. The collection opens with Femke Nijsse and colleagues' widely cited *Nature Communications* study (*The Momentum of the Solar Energy Transition*), which utilizes data-driven evolutionary macroeconomic models to prove that solar photovoltaics have crossed an irreversible techno-economic tipping point. Due to relentless Wright’s Law learning curve dynamics (20–25% cost reduction per doubling of cumulative capacity), solar PV combined with short-duration battery storage will dominate global power generation decades ahead of earlier consensus projections, even without aggressive carbon pricing.

This macroeconomic momentum is mirrored by revolutionary material science breakthroughs in light-harvesting and optical thermal management. In advanced photovoltaics, researchers achieve a world-record 37.6% power conversion efficiency under low-intensity indoor LED illumination utilizing wide-bandgap perovskite solar cells engineered with triple-passivation molecular reassembly, opening massive self-powered operating windows for distributed IoT sensors, building electronics, and mobile wearables.

Simultaneously, Yao Zhai and team present their landmark *Science* treatise demonstrating passive daytime radiative cooling: a scalable, roll-to-roll manufactured glass-polymer hybrid metamaterial that reflects 96% of incident solar irradiance while radiating terrestrial heat directly into outer space through the atmospheric transparency window (8–13 micrometers), providing over 93 W/m² of sub-ambient cooling without electricity. The volume culminates in Ali Hajimiri’s Caltech Space Solar Power Project team demonstrating coherent, lightweight microwave phased arrays performing wireless power transfer in low-Earth orbit, proving the feasibility of space-based solar beaming.
