## Executive Summary

Written by Israeli "New Historian" and University of Exeter professor **Ilan Pappé** (Oneworld Publications, 2006, Outline p. 134; 384 pp.), *The Ethnic Cleansing of Palestine* presents a rigorously documented, fiercely argued revisionist reconstruction of the **1947–1949 Palestinian Nakba**—challenging the traditional Israeli narrative that over 750,000 Palestinians fled voluntarily or solely as unintended collateral of war.

Drawing on declassified Israeli military archives, David Ben-Gurion's diaries, Haganah **"Village Files"** (detailed intelligence dossiers compiled on Palestinian villages years before 1948, Ch. 2), and Palestinian oral histories, Pappé traces the deliberations of Ben-Gurion's inner **"Consultancy"** (Ch. 3–4) culminating in **Plan Dalet (Plan D, March 1948)** (Ch. 5)—which he analyzes as a systematic military blueprint resulting in the depopulation and destruction of over 500 Palestinian villages, urban depopulation (*urbicide*, Ch. 5), documented massacres (including Deir Yassin and Tantura, Ch. 6), and subsequent landscape erasure through afforestation and Hebraized toponymy (Ch. 7–11).

---

## Core Historical Arguments & Scholarly Debate

1. **Plan Dalet & Institutional Intent (Ch. 2–5)**: Argues that demographic engineering to secure a decisive Jewish majority inside the new state shifted from ad-hoc retaliation in December 1947 to systematic expulsion operations across April 1948–January 1949.
2. **International Legal Framing of Ethnic Cleansing (Ch. 1)**: Applies contemporary UN/Balkan-era definitions of ethnic cleansing to 1948 Palestine, insisting that honest acknowledgment of the Nakba is the indispensable moral precondition for any durable peace.
3. **Historiographical Controversy**: Widely praised by critics of Zionism and international-law scholars (Richard Falk, Ghada Karmi, p. 2) while sharply criticized by fellow New Historian Benny Morris—who accepts extensive 1948 expulsions occurred *in the heat of existential war* but rejects Pappé's thesis of a preexisting comprehensive master plan.
