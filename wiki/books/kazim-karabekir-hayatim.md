## Executive Summary

Composed by **General Kâzım Karabekir** (1882–1948)—Commander of the Eastern Front during the Turkish War of Independence, founder of the Progressive Republican Party (*Terakkiperver Cumhuriyet Fırkası*), and later Speaker of the Grand National Assembly (p. 2; Yapı Kredi Yayınları, 2008; 554 pp.)—*Hayatım* is a richly detailed autobiography chronicling Karabekir's formative decades from late-Ottoman childhood through the Military Academy (*Harbiye*) and Staff College (*Erkân-ı Harbiye*, graduated first in his class in 1905).

Moving vividly across **Van**, **Erzurum**, **Harput**, a three-year boyhood stay in **Mecca** (pp. 58–107), **Istanbul** (the 1894 earthquake, Kuleli Military High School, Harbiye), and **Salonika/Macedonia** during the 1903 Bulgarian uprisings and the clandestine rise of the Committee of Union and Progress (TOC, pp. 12–281+), Karabekir paints an extraordinary social, pedagogical, and military self-portrait of the generation of officers who inherited a crumbling empire and built the Turkish Republic.

---

## Core Historical & Biographical Themes

1. **Late-Ottoman Geography & Childhood Formation**: Captures imperial diversity and hardship from Anatolian garrisons to the Hijaz, revealing how early discipline, physical resilience, violin playing, chess, and maternal courage shaped Karabekir's character.
2. **Cadet Politics & Institutional Decay**: Documents firsthand the tensions inside Abdülhamid II's military academies—secret police searches for banned papers (*evrak-ı muzırra*, p. 261), incompetent instructors, and the patriotic awakening of young staff officers in Salonika.
3. **Archival Self-Documentation**: Reflects Karabekir's lifelong habit of keeping meticulous diaries and documents—the very archival ethos that later made his political memoirs a lightning rod in Republican historiography.
