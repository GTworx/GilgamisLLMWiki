## Analytical Overview
### The Mathematical Foundations of Optimal Stopping

In *Optimal Stopping and Applications: Finite Horizon Problems*, world-renowned UCLA mathematician and statistician Thomas S. Ferguson delivers the foundational text on the mathematics of sequential decision-making. Optimal stopping theory addresses problems where a decision-maker observes a sequence of random variables over time, and must decide when to stop and take a terminal payoff, balancing the cost of continued observation against the expected gain from future options.

### The Finite Horizon Architecture and Backward Induction

Ferguson rigorously formalizes finite horizon problems where decisions must terminate by a fixed step $N$:
- **The Principle of Optimality:** Expressed through Dynamic Programming and Bellman equations, calculating the value function $V_n(x_n) = \max\{g_n(x_n), E[V_{n+1}(X_{n+1}) | X_n = x_n]\}$.
- **Backward Induction:** Computing optimal decision thresholds by working backward from step $N$ to the present.
- **The Snell Envelope:** Demonstrating that the value process is the smallest supermartingale that dominates the payoff process.

### Classic Canonical Models

Ferguson details classic applications: the Burglar Problem, the House Selling Problem with offer costs, and American option financial derivatives, establishing the formal mathematical standard for dynamic optimization.
