## Summary
Multidisciplinary treatise on the psychosocial, financial, and institutional dimensions of disaster relief, uniting clinical psychological first aid protocols for traumatized children with corporate disaster philanthropy economics, non-profit volunteer governance, tax policy, and anti-fraud oversight.

## Analytical Overview
This wide-ranging compilation addresses the complex human, legal, and economic dynamics of humanitarian assistance in the aftermath of catastrophic natural disasters. The clinical core of the work is anchored by the seminal psychological trauma research of Atle Dyregrov and William Yule, along with official Turkish health ministry guidelines on Psychological First Aid (*Psikolojik İlkyardım*). The authors establish non-intrusive protocols for stabilizing acutely distressed survivors—particularly children—prioritizing basic physical security, family reunification, normalizing emotional shock, and preventing secondary retraumatization through sensitive communication.

This clinical perspective is paired with specialized pediatric orthopedic literature from Lisa DuShane (*JPOSNA*) on rapid-deployment child prosthetics following severe crush injuries, the Union of Turkish Bar Associations' (*Türkiye Barolar Birliği*) legal guide for disaster victims, the Turkish Catastrophe Insurance Pool (*DASK*) annual reports, and the World Health Organization’s minimum classification standards for Foreign Medical Teams (FMTs) in sudden-onset disasters.

On the financial and administrative side, the work presents groundbreaking corporate finance research by Hao Liang and Luc Renneboog examining the stock market valuation and stakeholder incentives driving corporate disaster philanthropy, alongside U.S. Congressional Research Service (CRS R45864) treatises on disaster tax write-offs and the OECD's landmark oversight report on combating the abuse of charities for money-laundering and tax evasion. The volume also includes rigorous non-profit management studies from the Urban Institute on volunteer coordination, accounting models for valuing unpaid volunteer labor, and Forbes analyses of blockchain-based charitable platforms (BitGive) ensuring real-time donation traceability.
