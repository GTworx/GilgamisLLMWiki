## Executive Summary

*On the Foundations of Quantum Mechanics* (PhD dissertation submitted to the Palmer Physical Laboratory, Princeton University, March 1, 1957, 36 pp., supervised by Professor John A. Wheeler while Everett served at the Pentagon's Weapons Systems Evaluation Group; published later that year in *Reviews of Modern Physics* as *"Relative State" Formulation of Quantum Mechanics*) by [[hugh-everett-iii]] is one of the most revolutionary doctoral theses in 20th-century physics—the original genesis of the **Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI)** of quantum mechanics (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, scanned facsimile, Title Page & Abstract, pp. 1–2).

Confronting the logical paradoxes of John von Neumann's dual-process Copenhagen formalism—which splits physics into smooth unitary Schrödinger evolution (Process 2) interrupted abruptly by non-unitary wavefunction collapse triggered by an external classical observer (Process 1)—Everett proposes a radical, mathematically austere solution: **omit the ad hoc collapse postulate altogether** (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, Abstract, p. 2). Treating observers themselves as physical quantum subsystems equipped with memory configurations inside a closed, isolated universal wavefunction—essential for quantizing general relativity, where no "outside" observer of the cosmos exists—Everett introduces the **"relativity of states"**, proving that unitary entanglement naturally branches observer-memory states whose internal statistical experiences reproduce the exact Born-rule probabilities of conventional quantum mechanics as a deduced metatheory (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, Abstract, p. 2).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Title Page, Acknowledgements & Historical Provenance (p. 1)**:
  * *Princeton & Copenhagen Debate*: Records submission on March 1, 1957 following a longer January 1956 draft circulated to Niels Bohr, H. J. Groenewald, Aage Petersen, A. Stern, and L. Rosenfeld—explicitly thanking them for their objections while acknowledging John A. Wheeler's guidance and NSF fellowship support (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, p. 1).

* **Abstract & Core Metatheory of Relative States (p. 2 & pp. 3–36)**:
  * *Pure Wave Mechanics Without Collapse*: Formulates quantum mechanics for isolated systems containing internal model observers (automata with memory records) obeying universal unitary Schrödinger evolution; proves that after interaction, each subsystem possesses a well-defined **relative state** conditioned on the state of any other subsystem, allowing the conventional Copenhagen external-observer statistics to be *deduced* within their domain of applicability rather than postulated externally (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, p. 2).

## Key Quotes & Citations

* "Quantum mechanics is reformulated in a way which eliminates its present dependence on the special treatment of observation of a system by an external observer. The result is believed to be a more suitable formulation for application to field theories, particularly general relativity." (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, Abstract, p. 2)
* "The new theory results from the conventional formulation by omitting the special postulates concerned with external observation. In their place a concept of 'relativity of states' is developed for treating and interpreting the quantum description of isolated systems within which observation processes can occur." (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, Abstract, p. 2)
* "Abstract models for observers are formulated that can be treated within the theory as physical systems subject at all times to the same laws as all other physical systems... When these changes are interpreted as the experience of the observer this experience is found to be in accord with the statistical predictions of the conventional 'external observation' formulation of quantum mechanics." (source: BOOKS/Physics/Everett short thesis as defended.pdf, Abstract, p. 2)
