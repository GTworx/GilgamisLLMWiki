## Executive Summary

Edited with historical introduction and biographical notes by Australian scholars **Russell Grigg**, **Dominique Hecq**, and **Craig Smith** (Rebus Press 1999 / Karnac Books 2015, 315 pp., pp. 4–8), *Female Sexuality: The Early Psychoanalytic Controversies* gathers the nineteen landmark primary papers published largely in the *International Journal of Psycho-Analysis* during the explosive 1920s–1930s "Freud–Jones debate" over feminine psychological development.

Rather than summarizing these debates secondhand, the volume restores the exact voices of pioneering female and male analysts who challenged Freud's phallocentric assumptions from inside the psychoanalytic movement: **Karen Horney** (*The Flight from Womanhood*, *The Dread of Woman*, *The Denial of the Vagina*), **Melanie Klein** (*Early Stages of the Oedipus Conflict*), **Joan Riviere** (*Womanliness as a Masquerade*), **Helene Deutsch**, **Jeanne Lampl-de Groot**, **Josine Müller**, **Marie Bonaparte**, **Karl Abraham**, **Ernest Jones**, **Otto Fenichel**, **August Stärcke**, and **J. H. W. van Ophuijsen** (TOC pp. 6–7).

## Major Theoretical Fault Lines

1. **Primary Femininity vs. Phallic Monism**: Documents how Karen Horney, Melanie Klein, and Ernest Jones contested Freud's claim that young girls experience sexuality solely through lack ("penis envy"), arguing instead for an innate early awareness of feminine bodily interiority and distinct female anxieties.
2. **Joan Riviere's *Womanliness as a Masquerade* (Ch. 12, p. 183)**: Presents the classic paper later canonized by both Jacques Lacan and Judith Butler, proposing that socially performed femininity operates as a defensive mask rather than a fixed biological essence.
3. **Institutional & Clinical Stakes**: Illuminates how debates between the Vienna (Freud/Deutsch) and London/Berlin (Jones/Klein/Horney) schools shaped modern object-relations theory, feminist theory, and Lacanian sexuation.
