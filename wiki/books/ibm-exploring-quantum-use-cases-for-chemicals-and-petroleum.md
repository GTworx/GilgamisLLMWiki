## Executive Summary

This 20-page executive study by the IBM Institute for Business Value explores the transformative potential of quantum computing across the global chemicals, petroleum, and energy materials industries. Recognizing that classical computational chemistry methods (Density Functional Theory / DFT, Coupled Cluster / CCSD) fail to accurately capture strongly correlated electron systems without exponential approximations, IBM evaluates how quantum simulation can revolutionize catalyst design, polymer synthesis, carbon capture sorbents, and battery electrolyte engineering. The report provides a strategic adoption framework for energy and chemical enterprise executives to establish quantum chemistry capabilities.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **The Failure of Classical Quantum Chemistry**: Classical computers struggle to simulate molecules with strong electron correlation (e.g., transition metal catalysts, metalloenzymes) because electronic wavefunctions scale exponentially with orbital count; quantum computers map these fermionic wavefunctions natively.
- **High-Impact Chemical Use Cases**: Identifies catalyst discovery for energy-efficient ammonia synthesis (Haber-Bosch replacement), novel metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) for point-source carbon capture, and high-energy-density solid-state battery electrolytes.
- **The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)**: Explains the algorithmic mechanics of VQE as a near-term NISQ algorithm that combines parameterized quantum state preparation with classical energy minimization to calculate molecular ground states.
- **Downstream Petroleum & Refining Optimization**: Details applications in refinery supply chain scheduling, pipeline network optimization, and crude assay blending using hybrid quantum optimization routines.
- **Strategic Capability Roadmap**: Outlines an adoption path: inventorying high-value chemical simulation bottlenecks, joining collaborative quantum research networks (IBM Quantum Network), and training computational chemists in quantum algorithms.
