## Executive Summary
Penned in 13th-century Damascus and Cairo by the polymath physician Ibn al-Nafis (famous for discovering the pulmonary transit of blood), *Theologus Autodidactus* (*Al-Risalah al-Kamiliyyah fil Sira al-Nabawiyyah*) is one of the earliest Arabic philosophical novels and proto-science fiction treatises. The narrative follows Kamil, a feral youth who spontaneously generates from mud on an uninhabited desert island. Using pure deductive observation and reason, Kamil deduces the laws of physics, biological anatomy, and metaphysics. In the book's climactic apocalyptic chapters, Ibn al-Nafis utilizes his advanced physiological knowledge to explain the eschatological destruction of the cosmos, planetary climate collapse, and the biological and physical mechanisms of bodily resurrection at the end of time.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Autodidactic Epistemology**: Champions empirical rationalism, demonstrating how human reason independently deduces the physical and physiological order without cultural indoctrination.
- **Physiological Science Fiction**: Ibn al-Nafis integrates medical anatomy and pulmonary circulation into theological eschatology, formulating a scientific model of cellular reassembly.
- **Cosmological Collapse & Catastrophism**: Foresees planetary climatic shifts, atmospheric depletion, and planetary ruin through natural physical cycles.
