## Overview
This analytical philosophy and argumentation theory monograph contrasts two fundamental debating postures: the destructive, soundbite-driven 'fragmentary disputational style' versus Socrates and Hegel's collaborative, truth-seeking 'dialectic'. Accompanied by rigorous counter-examples to the common ethical fallacy 'Two Wrongs Make a Right' (tu quoque), the text provides an operational rubric for ethical discourse.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Contrasts fragmented, combative online rhetoric designed to score points with dialectical inquiry designed to synthesize truth.
- **Key Finding**: Analyzes the logical pathology of the 'two wrongs make a right' fallacy across historical geopolitics and corporate warfare.
- **Key Finding**: Provides discursive protocols for collaborative problem-solving and ethical dispute mediation.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Fragmentary discussion weaponizes context-free quotes to inflict reputational damage; dialectic engages the strongest possible version of an opponent's argument to birth higher understanding."
