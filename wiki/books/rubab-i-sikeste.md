## Executive Summary

*Rübab-ı Şikeste* ("The Broken Lute") is the foundational poetry collection of the late-Ottoman *Servet-i Fünun* (Edebiyat-ı Cedîde) movement and one of the decisive turning points in Turkish literary modernity. This 1910 expanded post-Constitutional edition (*Yeni Tab'ı*, Tanin Matbaası, p. 3) brings together Tevfik Fikret's lyrical, philosophical, and civic verse across nine named divisions printed directly on the title page: *Rübab-ı Şikeste*, *Haluk'un Defterinden*, *Hemşirem İçin*, *Sis*, *Bir Lahza-i Teahhur*, *Millet Şarkısı*, *Aveng-i Tesavir*, *Aveng-i Şühur*, and *Eski Şeyler* (p. 3).

Moving from the ornate Parnassian aestheticism of his youth to fierce civic indictment of Hamidian autocracy, Fikret reshaped Ottoman *aruz* prosody into a flexible, enjambed, speech-cadenced vehicle capable of expressing urban alienation, humanitarian compassion, and secular ethical idealism. In the Gilgamis corpus, this primary Ottoman-script edition pairs directly with [[tevfik-fikretin-siirlerindeki-edebi-sanatlar]] (Ayşe Çevirici Çağın's exhaustive rhetorical catalog of Fikret's entire poetic corpus) and complements [[saatleri-ayarlama-enstitusu]] as twin poles of late-Ottoman and early-Republican cultural transformation.

|---|---|---|
| **Rübab-ı Şikeste** (core lyrics & *Kari'lerime*, pp. 11–12) | Fragility of the artistic sensibility, disillusionment, aesthetic melancholy | Enjambed *aruz*, intimate address to readers |
| **Haluk'un Defterinden** | Civic pedagogy, scientific humanism, ethical exhortation addressed to Fikret's son Haluk | Didactic-inspirational, progressive humanism |
| **Hemşirem İçin** | Personal grief and protest against domestic and social injustice | Elegiac, socially empathetic |
| **Sis** | Symbolic indictment of Istanbul shrouded in political tyranny and moral decay | High rhetorical invective, urban gothic imagery |
| **Bir Lahza-i Teahhur** | Reflection on the 1905 Yıldız assassination attempt against Abdülhamid II | Dramatic political apostrophe |
| **Millet Şarkısı** | Constitutional-era hymn to collective dignity and national awakening | Anthem-like civic lyric |
| **Aveng-i Tesavir** | Literary portraits of classical and contemporary poetic masters | Ekphrastic biographical sonnets/stanzas |
| **Aveng-i Şühur** | Twelve-month cycle linking seasonal moods with psychological states | Impressionistic nature-painting |
| **Eski Şeyler** | Early transitional pieces bridging classical Divan diction and new sensibility | Classical *aruz* with emerging modern imagery |

## Key Arguments & Literary Innovations

1. **Breaking Classical Prosodic Rigidity**: Fikret transformed *aruz* meter by breaking the syntactic closure of the classical *beyit* (couplet), spreading sentences fluidly across multiple lines (*anjambman*) and adopting Western stanzaic forms such as the sonnet and *terza rima* while retaining Ottoman musicality.
2. **From Aesthetic Melancholy to Civic Conscience**: The 1910 edition documents Fikret's intellectual evolution after the 1908 Young Turk Revolution, when previously censored political poems (*Sis*, *Bir Lahza-i Teahhur*, *Millet Şarkısı*) could finally appear in print alongside the 1900 lyrics (p. 3).
3. **Humanitarian Ethics Beyond Dogma**: In poems addressing poverty, earthquake victims, and youth (*Haluk'un Defterinden*), Fikret articulates a universalist moral stance grounded in reason, labor, and compassion rather than fatalistic resignation.
4. **Pictorial Modernism (*Tablo Şiir*)**: In *Aveng-i Şühur* and *Aveng-i Tesavir*, Fikret—himself a trained painter—translates Impressionist and Parnassian visual composition into verbal portraiture.
