## Overview
This historical and literary monograph investigates Leo Tolstoy's profound late-life spiritual encounter with Islam, the Hadith collections, and the Prophet Muhammad. Excommunicated by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1901 for his radical Christian anarchism and rejection of Church hierarchy, Tolstoy authored a celebrated compilation of the sayings of Muhammad, admiring their sublime moral simplicity, universal brotherhood, and pure monotheism.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Chronicles Tolstoy's spiritual epistolary correspondence with Egyptian Grand Mufti Muhammad Abduh.
- **Key Finding**: Translates and analyzes Tolstoy's selection of ethical Hadiths emphasizing humility, non-violence, and social justice.
- **Key Finding**: Situates Tolstoy's religious search within 19th-century Russian intellectual dissent and Christian pacifism.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
