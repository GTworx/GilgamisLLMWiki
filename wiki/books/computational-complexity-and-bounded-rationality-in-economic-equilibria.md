## Summary
Theoretical primer bridging computer science complexity classes (P, NP, PSPACE) and economic theory, demonstrating that computing Walrasian market equilibria and multi-player Nash equilibria is PPAD-complete and computationally intractable.

## Analytical Overview
Deconstructs the neoclassical assumption of instantaneous market clearing with perfect information. Proves that real-world economic agents faced with combinatorial optimization problems operate under fundamental algorithmic limits, meaning that decentralized markets cannot solve intractable allocation problems without relying on satisficing heuristics and institutional rules.
