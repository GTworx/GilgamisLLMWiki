## Executive Summary

Written by LSE-trained philosopher of science and cognitive writer **Dylan Evans** (2014, Hodder & Stoughton, pp. 2, 7), *Atheism: All That Matters* is a crisp, lucid 158-page primer distilling the history, logic, cognitive psychology, ethics, and existential outlook of atheism—capped by an original *"100 Ideas"* rapid-reference synthesis (p. 8).

Avoiding both dense academic jargon and shrill culture-war polemic, Evans surveys classical philosophical arguments for and against deities, charts the global history of unbelief from ancient India and Greece through Enlightenment Europe to modern secularization, draws on evolutionary psychology to explain why belief feels intuitive to human minds, and tackles the deepest existential question facing non-believers: how to build rich ethical integrity and genuine meaning without cosmic guarantees (pp. 8).

## Seven-Chapter Map (Contents, p. 8)

1. **Who Cares? (p. 1)**: Clarifies definitions (strong vs. weak atheism, agnosticism, secular humanism) and explains why unbelief matters politically and personally.
2. **Arguing About Gods (p. 19)**: Concisely evaluates cosmological, teleological, ontological, and problem-of-evil debates alongside modern scientific naturalism.
3. **The History of Unbelief (p. 39)**: Recovers deep historical lineages of skepticism—from Charvaka materialists and Epicureans to Spinoza, Hume, Baron d'Holbach, and Darwin.
4. **The Psychology of Belief (p. 55)**: Synthesizes cognitive science of religion (hyperactive agency detection, intuitive dualism, emotional comfort under uncertainty).
5. **Atheism and Religion (p. 73)**: Explores varieties of secular engagement with religious art, community, ritual, and pluralism.
6. **Atheism and Ethics (p. 89)**: Defends secular moral philosophy grounded in empathy, rational reciprocity, and human flourishing (Euthyphro dilemma and utilitarian/virtue frameworks).
7. **Atheism and the Meaning of Life (p. 101) & 100 Ideas (p. 117)**: Articulates existential affirmation—arguing that finitude makes love, curiosity, and creative purpose *more* precious rather than hollow.
