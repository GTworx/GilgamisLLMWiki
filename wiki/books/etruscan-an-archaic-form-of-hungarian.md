## Executive Summary

Written by Italian linguist and Professor Emeritus at Utrecht University **Mario Alinei** (1926–2018; 28-pp. English executive summary of his 2003 Italian / 2005 Hungarian monograph *Etrusco: una forma arcaica di ungherese*), this controversial heterodox treatise applies Alinei's **Paleolithic Continuity Theory (PCT)** (pp. 2–5) to propose a radical reassessment of European, Uralic, and Altaic linguistic prehistory.

Rejecting the mainstream Kurgan / steppe-migration consensus for Indo-European origins, Alinei extends the Uralic Continuity Theory to argue that Indo-European, Uralic, and Altaic (Turkic and Mongolic) languages all have Paleolithic roots in their respective zones, that fourth-millennium BCE Eurasian horse-riding nomads were already **Turkic-speaking** (p. 5), and that **Etruscan** represents an archaic Ugric/Hungarian language brought to Italy via prehistoric Carpathian-Danubian cultural links rather than an Anatolian migration or non-Indo-European isolate.

---

## Core Arguments & Historiographical Context

1. **Paleolithic Continuity Theory (PCT, pp. 2–4)**: Argues that major Eurasian language families split far deeper in prehistory (Paleolithic/Mesolithic) than standard 4,500–6,000-year historical-linguistic clocks allow.
2. **Early Turkic Steppe Presence & Magyar *Honfoglalás* Revision (p. 5)**: Attributes early Bronze Age Eurasian equestrian nomadism to Altaic/Turkic peoples and posits a pre-medieval presence of Ugric/proto-Hungarian speakers in the Carpathian basin.
3. **Scholarly Reception**: Mainstream Etruscologists, Uralicists, and Indo-Europeanists (represented directly in our wiki by [[the-horse-the-wheel-and-language]]) firmly reject both PCT and the Etruscan-Hungarian genetic link, treating Etruscan as a Tyrsenian language family unrelated to Uralic; the summary remains valuable for tracking heterodox linguistic debates.
