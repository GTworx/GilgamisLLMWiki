## Executive Summary

Exploring the intellectual and archaeological battleground of the late Ottoman Empire, *Homer, Troy and the Turks: Heritage and Identity in the Late Ottoman Empire, 1870–1915* investigates how late Ottoman intellectuals, statesmen, and museum directors (most notably **Osman Hamdi Bey**) contested Western European colonial appropriations of Aegean antiquity following Heinrich Schliemann's controversial excavations at **Troy (Hisarlık)**. The volume reconstructs how late Ottoman and early Republican thinkers claimed Trojan and Anatolian antiquities as part of their own sovereign heritage, anticipating modern Turkish humanist and republican historiography.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Contesting Archaeological Imperialism**: Late Ottoman antiquities laws enacted under Osman Hamdi Bey represented deliberate anti-colonial legal resistance against European plunder.
2. **The Avenging of Hector**: Analyzes the cultural meme claiming Trojans as proto-Anatolians/Turks, linking Mehmed II's visit to Troy in 1462 with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's celebrated declaration at Dumlupınar: *"Hektor'un öcünü aldık"* ("We have avenged Hector").
3. **From Imperial Dynasticism to Territorial Antiquity**: Documents the crucial intellectual transition from Islamic-dynastic self-identification to an Anatolian-rooted cultural identity that laid the foundation for the Turkish Historical Thesis (*Türk Tarih Tezi*).
