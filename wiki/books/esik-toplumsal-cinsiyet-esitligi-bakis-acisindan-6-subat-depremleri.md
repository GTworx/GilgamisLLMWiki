## Executive Summary

Prepared by the Women's Platform for Equality (Eşitlik İçin Kadın Platformu - EŞİK) and dedicated to fallen feminist advocates Kibar Özdemir and Hatice Can, this poignant and rigorously documented 72-page field report examines the February 6, 2023 earthquakes through the lens of gender equality and feminist disaster sociology. Authored by Özgül Kapdan, Gökçeçiçek Ayata, Özlem Altıok, Hülya Gülbahar, and Sevgi Uçan Çubukçu, the report uncovers the structural invisibilization of women and girls in disaster response.

The report reveals that disaster response mechanisms are deeply gender-blind: initial aid distributions neglected menstrual hygiene, pregnancy healthcare, and safe sanitation, while tent cities lacked basic security, locks, and external illumination, exponentially increasing gender-based violence and harassment risks. It documents the crushing domestic care burden imposed on displaced women (caring for children, injured family members, and the elderly in muddy tent camps) and demands feminist participation in urban reconstruction.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Gender-Disaggregated Data Absence**: Highlights the dangerous failure of official authorities to maintain and release gender- and age-disaggregated mortality, injury, and displacement statistics.
- **Sanitation and Reproductive Health Neglect**: Documents acute shortages of secure, illuminated, gender-segregated latrines and bathing facilities, resulting in severe reproductive tract infections and psychological distress.
- **Domestic Care Burden Amplification**: Demonstrates that the destruction of public services (schools, daycare, elder care, piped water) shifted colossal physical labor onto women under extreme deprivation.
- **Security and Gender-Based Violence**: Details heightened vulnerability to intimate partner violence, sexual harassment, and the security threats facing unaccompanied minors and single women in informal encampments.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations
