## Executive Summary

This 12-page empirical study from Arcadia University's Computational Psychology Group examines the correlation between offline Big Five personality traits (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism / OCEAN) and digital telemetry behaviors on social media platforms (Instagram, TikTok, X/Twitter). Analyzing posting frequencies, visual self-presentation styles, caption sentiment, and engagement metrics across hundreds of participants, the study proves that individual personality profiles leave clear, predictive behavioral footprints in digital space, providing a framework for understanding algorithmic micro-targeting and digital wellbeing.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Digital Behavioral Footprints of the Big Five**: Maps specific digital habits: Extraverts post frequently and engage heavily with comments; Neurotic individuals post reactive updates and exhibit compulsive scrolling; High-Openness users curate artistic, conceptual content.
- **Neuroticism and Problematic Social Media Use**: Documents a strong statistical correlation between high Neuroticism, low Conscientiousness, and compulsive social media checking driven by fear of missing out (FOMO) and social comparison.
- **Algorithmic Resonance and Trait Exploitation**: Analyzes how recommendation algorithms autonomously detect Big Five trait markers from engagement telemetry, serving hyper-tailored content that exploits psychological vulnerabilities.
- **Self-Presentation vs. Authentic Expression**: Explores the psychological tension between authentic digital sharing and hyper-curated aesthetic performance, linking discrepancy to digital fatigue and depression.
- **Ethical Guidelines for Behavioral Targeting**: Proposes privacy regulations to restrict commercial platforms from inferring psychological personality profiles from ambient browsing telemetry without explicit consent.
