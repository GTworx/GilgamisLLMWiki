## Executive Summary

Entrepreneur and IMVU co-founder **Eric Ries** (b. 1978, copyright p. 9) crystallized a global movement with *The Lean Startup* (2011), adapting Toyota Production System principles and Steve Blank's Customer Development model into a rigorous management discipline for **extreme uncertainty**. Ries defines a startup not by company size or industry, but as *"a human institution designed to create a new product or service under conditions of extreme uncertainty"*—applicable equally to garage founders and Fortune 500 innovation labs (p. 2, Ch. 1–2).

Organized into three sweeping parts—**Part One: Vision** (Start, Define, Learn, Experiment), **Part Two: Steer** (Leap, Test, Measure, Pivot or Persevere), and **Part Three: Accelerate** (Batch, Grow, Adapt, Innovate, TOC p. 11)—the book replaces five-year waterfall business plans with continuous **Build–Measure–Learn** feedback loops engineered to minimize total cycle time to **validated learning**.

## Five Core Principles & Mechanics

1. **Validated Learning & Leap-of-Faith Assumptions (Ch. 3–5)**: Treats startup strategy as two testable hypotheses—the **value hypothesis** (does the product truly deliver value?) and the **growth hypothesis** (how will new customers discover it?).
2. **Minimum Viable Product (MVP, Ch. 6)**: Ships the fastest, lowest-effort experiment capable of spinning through the Build–Measure–Learn loop—illustrated by Zappos's manual shoe-store test, Dropbox's explainer video, and Groupon's WordPress blog.
3. **Innovation Accounting vs. Vanity Metrics (Ch. 7)**: Replaces feel-good cumulative totals with actionable cohort analyses, split tests, and learning milestones that expose whether product iterations genuinely move unit economics.
4. **Pivot or Persevere (Ch. 8)**: Catalogs ten structured pivot archetypes (zoom-in, zoom-out, customer segment, platform, business architecture, value capture, engine of growth, channel, technology) to shift strategy without abandoning vision.
5. **Small Batches, Engines of Growth & Five Whys (Ch. 9–12)**: Champions continuous deployment, three sustainable **Engines of Growth** (*Sticky, Viral, Paid*), and Toyota's **Five Whys** root-cause discipline to build adaptive, self-correcting organizations.
