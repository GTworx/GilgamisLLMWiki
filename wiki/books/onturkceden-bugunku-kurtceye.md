## Executive Summary

In this provocative short comparative linguistic study, **Kâzım Mirşan** examines the vocabulary, grammar, and phonetics of contemporary Kurdish dialects (Kurmanji and Sorani) alongside ancient Proto-Turkic runic inscriptions. Mirşan argues that despite Kurdish being classified within the Indo-Iranian branch of Indo-European languages, it preserves a substantial stratum of agglutinative grammatical structures, pastoral vocabulary, and ancient root cognates inherited from centuries of shared geographic coexistence with ancient Turanian and Proto-Turkic nomadic populations in the Zagros and Anatolian highlands.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Substrate and Linguistic Convergence**: Suggests that intensive historical interaction between Iranian-speaking and Ural-Altaic nomadic populations created a profound linguistic substrate in regional dialects.
2. **Shared Pastoral Vocabulary**: Identifies dozens of agricultural, pastoral, and animal husbandry terms shared across Turkish and Kurdish that reflect a common highland way of life.
3. **Cultural Fraternity over Division**: Concludes that philological research should serve to reveal shared historical roots and mutual fraternity among Anatolian peoples rather than political hostility.
