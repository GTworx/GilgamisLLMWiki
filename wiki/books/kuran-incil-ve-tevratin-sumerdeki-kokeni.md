## Executive Summary

*Kur'an, İncil ve Tevrat'ın Sumer'deki Kökeni* ("The Sumerian Origins of the Qur'an, Gospel, and Torah," first published November 1995; 9th printing April 2005, pp. 3–4) is pioneering Turkish Sumerologist **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ's** widely read and fiercely debated popular-archaeological study tracing foundational narratives and religious customs across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam back to third-millennium BCE **Sumerian cuneiform tablets** preserved in the Istanbul Archaeology Museums and decoded alongside Samuel Noah Kramer.

Writing in accessible, plainspoken Turkish for general readers, Çığ juxtaposes translated Mesopotamian literary tablets with passages from the Torah, New Testament, Qur'an, Islamic *hadith*, and Qumran/Dead Sea texts—arguing that the three Abrahamic scriptures inherited a deep shared substratum of ancient Near Eastern mythology, cosmology, and ritual law (p. 5).

## Cultural Impact & Critical Context

1. **Popularizing Cuneiform Philology in Turkey**: Building on decades of cataloguing tens of thousands of Nippur tablets at the Istanbul Archaeology Museums, Çığ brought comparative Assyriology out of narrow journals into mainstream Turkish civic debate—sparking major public controversies (including a famous 2006 legal acquittal over her historical arguments on head-covering).
2. **Deep Continuity Across Ancient Near Eastern Myth**: Aligns with mainstream comparative-religion findings on Flood, Creation, and Righteous Sufferer parallels, while pushing bolder diffusionist claims regarding later Islamic customs that historians continue to debate.
