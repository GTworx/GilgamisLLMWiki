## Summary
In-depth ethnographic and urban sociological study examining why Japanese first-graders safely navigate Tokyo's dense subway networks alone, analyzing collective social responsibility, low crime rates, and transit design.

## Analytical Overview
Investigates the cultural concept of 'shitsuke' (early social training) alongside the material architecture of Japanese neighborhoods: narrow streets, active pedestrian eyes-on-the-street, neighborhood Koban police boxes, and a profound cultural expectation that any adult will assist an unaccompanied child, contrasting this high-trust equilibrium with Western car-dependent, hyper-vigilant parenting.
