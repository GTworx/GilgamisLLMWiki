## Executive Summary

Authored by independent energy advisory firm Power Analyst, this 21-page grid analytics report addresses the critical geographical and electrical question: 'Where Will US Data Centers Go?' Analyzing transmission queue bottlenecks, regional utility reserve margins, and state-level policy incentives, the report maps the migration of data center construction away from saturated markets like Northern Virginia (PJM) and Silicon Valley toward regional electric grids with available transmission headroom. Power Analyst evaluates regional reliability risks in the Midwest (MISO), Southeast (SERC), Texas (ERCOT), and Mountain West (WECC), forecasting where power shortages and interconnection delays will constrain cluster energization.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Transmission Queue Gridlock**: Interconnection study queues across major Regional Transmission Organizations (RTOs) average 5 to 7 years from initial application to energization, creating severe disconnects with hyperscaler 18-to-24-month buildout horizons.
- **Regional Grid Capacity Triage**: Identifies regions with short-term power headroom (parts of MISO, SPP, and SERC with retiring coal sites and strong dual-fuel transmission) versus regions facing imminent reserve margin deficits (PJM, ERCOT, CAISO).
- **Repurposing Brownfield Energy Sites**: Developers are aggressively acquiring decommissioned coal-fired power plants and industrial industrial sites to capitalize on existing high-voltage substation switchyards and transmission rights-of-way.
- **Utility Tariffs and Cost-Allocation Battles**: Rising electricity demand is sparking regulatory friction over whether hyperscalers or residential ratepayers will shoulder the multi-billion-dollar costs of transmission upgrades and new peaking generation.
- **Geographic Arbitrage and Latency Decoupling**: While user-facing applications require sub-20ms latency near metropolitan centers, asynchronous model training and bulk data indexing are migrating to remote, low-cost power basins in Wyoming, North Dakota, and rural Texas.
