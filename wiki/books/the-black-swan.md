## Executive Summary

Second and most globally famous volume of the *Incerto*, **Nassim Nicholas Taleb's** *The Black Swan* (2007; 2nd ed. 2010, pp. 3–5) defines a **Black Swan** event through three strict criteria: extreme rarity outside regular expectations, massive transformative impact, and retrospective predictability manufactured by human storytelling after the fact.

Opening with **Umberto Eco's Antilibrary**—celebrating unread books as a monument to the vastness of what we do *not* know (p. 5)—Taleb draws an incisive boundary between **Mediocristan** (scalable physical domains like human height governed by Gaussian bell curves) and **Extremistan** (social, financial, technological, and geopolitical domains dominated by power-law outliers where a single observation can dominate the entire aggregate).

## Key Epistemological & Risk Diagnostics

1. **The Ludic Fallacy**: Mistaking neat casino games with known probabilities for messy real-world uncertainty where the rules themselves mutate unexpectedly.
2. **The Narrative Fallacy & Platonicity**: Humans instinctively compress chaotic history into tidy cause-and-effect stories and clean mathematical models (bell curves, VaR), blinding institutions—especially banks—to existential tail risks.
3. **Positive vs. Negative Black Swans**: Rather than wasting energy attempting to *predict* rare shocks, individuals and portfolios should cap catastrophic downside while maximizing exposure to serendipitous upside (**barbell strategy**).
