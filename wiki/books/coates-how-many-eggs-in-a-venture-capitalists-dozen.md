## Analytical Overview
### The Mathematical Optimization of Venture Portfolios

In *How Many Eggs in a Venture Capitalist's Dozen?*, Castor Ventures fund principal Cainon Coates tackles one of the most fiercely debated questions in private equity: how many startup investments should a venture capital fund make to maximize the probability of achieving a top-decile return while minimizing the probability of catastrophic capital loss?

### Power-Law Dynamics vs. Modern Portfolio Theory

Coates proves that traditional Markowitz Modern Portfolio Theory completely breaks down in venture capital because venture returns are not normally distributed (Gaussian), but follow an extreme Pareto **Power Law**:
- In a typical venture fund, 65% of companies fail to return invested capital, 30% return 1x to 3x, and a single outlier (the "fund returner", ~4%) generates over 80% of total fund profits.
- Using Monte Carlo simulations over thousands of simulated funds, Coates demonstrates that a concentrated portfolio of 12 to 15 startups has a shockingly high probability (>40%) of missing an outlier hit entirely, resulting in sub-market returns.
- To achieve statistical confidence of capturing a power-law outlier, a fund must construct a diversified portfolio of at least **35 to 50 companies**, combined with disciplined pro-rata reserve capital allocation.

### The Quantitative Guide for Fund Allocators

The paper provides institutional Limited Partners with mathematical criteria to evaluate venture manager strategy.
