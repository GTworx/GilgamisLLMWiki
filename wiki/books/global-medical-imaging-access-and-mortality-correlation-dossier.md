## Executive Summary
This 23-page three-part clinical and epidemiological research dossier investigates the direct statistical and empirical correlation between population access to advanced medical imaging (CT, MRI, Ultrasound, PET) and national age-standardized mortality rates across infectious diseases, trauma, cardiovascular conditions, and malignancies.

The papers examine the global distribution of computed tomography and magnetic resonance scanners per million population, demonstrating that lack of rapid cross-sectional imaging accounts for substantial delays in diagnosing intracranial hemorrhages, acute ischemic strokes, traumatic internal bleeding, and early-stage resectable tumors. It establishes an econometric health production function proving that investments in diagnostic imaging infrastructure yield some of the highest returns in averted Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs).

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Diagnostic Imaging Density vs. Mortality**: Demonstrates a statistically robust inverse correlation between per-capita CT/MRI density and preventable mortality from stroke, trauma, and cancer.
- **Trauma Triage and the 'Golden Hour'**: Documents that access to whole-body trauma CT within 60 minutes of injury arrival reduces in-hospital trauma mortality by over 25%.
- **The Imaging Desert in LMICs**: Details that over two-thirds of the world's population has zero or negligible access to diagnostic ultrasound or basic radiography.
- **Radiation Safety and Low-Dose Protocols**: Discusses advances in iterative and AI-based image reconstruction algorithms that reduce diagnostic ionizing radiation dose while preserving image quality.
