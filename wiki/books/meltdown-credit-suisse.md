## Executive Summary

Written by Bloomberg News London editor and former *Wall Street Journal* reporter **Duncan Mavin** (2024, Macmillan Business, pp. 299–300), *Meltdown* is the definitive investigative post-mortem of the **March 2023 implosion of Credit Suisse**—a 167-year-old pillar of global finance and Swiss national identity forcibly absorbed into arch-rival UBS in an emergency weekend state-brokered rescue.

Drawing on dozens of interviews with former senior Credit Suisse executives, regulators, and internal records (p. 7), Mavin proves that Credit Suisse's collapse was not a sudden 2023 liquidity accident triggered by Silicon Valley Bank's failure. Instead, it was the slow-motion culmination of a **forty-year cultural identity crisis**: an uneasy, toxic marriage between conservative Swiss private banking and hyper-aggressive, poorly controlled Wall Street investment banking (inherited through First Boston / CSFB and DLJ).

## Four-Part Anatomy of Institutional Decay (Contents, pp. 4–5)

1. **Part One — From Wealth We Came (Chapters 1–5)**: Traces Alfred Escher's 1856 founding, the 1977 Chiasso slush-fund scandal, and Credit Suisse's fateful alliance with Wall Street's First Boston.
2. **Part Two — An International National Bank (Chapters 6–12)**: Chronicles Allen Wheat's high-flying dot-com star bankers (Frank Quattrone), the 2000s credit-bubble buildup, and how Credit Suisse survived 2008 without a bailout—breeding dangerous executive hubris.
3. **Part Three — The Great Decline (Chapters 13–21)**: Catalogs a relentless cascade of governance failures: U.S. sanctions busting and tax-evasion guilty pleas, Mozambique's corrupt "Tuna Bonds," Patrice Lescaudron's client-fraud scheme, and the bizarre 2019 corporate-espionage scandal (**Spygate**) that toppled CEO Tidjane Thiam.
4. **Part Four — The Last Gasp (Chapters 22–28+)**: Recounts the catastrophic twin 2021 blowups of **Greensill Capital** ($10B supply-chain funds) and **Archegos Capital Management** ($5.5B loss), chairman António Horta-Osório's quarantine scandal, social-media-accelerated deposit runs, and the March 2023 shotgun merger with UBS.
