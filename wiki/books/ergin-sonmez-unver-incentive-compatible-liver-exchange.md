## Analytical Overview
### Market Design for Life-Saving Organ Transplantation

In *Efficient and Incentive-Compatible Liver Exchange*, delivered as the prestigious **14th Kenneth Arrow Lecture** to the Society for Social Choice and Welfare, market design pioneers Tayfun Sönmez, M. Utku Ünver, and Haluk Ergin expand their world-famous kidney-exchange mechanisms into the far more complex and perilous domain of living-donor liver transplantation.

### The Asymmetric Biophysics of Living-Donor Liver Donation

Unlike kidney exchange (where donors have two identical kidneys and can safely donate one with low surgical mortality), living-donor liver transplantation is fraught with immense medical asymmetry:
- Donating the right lobe involves substantial surgical risk (0.5% mortality, 20-40% complication rate), while left-lobe donation carries much lower donor risk but provides smaller graft volume that can only save smaller recipients.
- This creates severe donor-recipient size and health compatibility constraints that make simple two-way swaps virtually impossible.

### The Incentive-Compatible Matching Algorithm

The authors design a revolutionary multi-hospital organ-matching mechanism:
- Formulating a top-trading cycles algorithm that guarantees **Incentive Compatibility (Strategy-Proofness)**: hospitals cannot manipulate patient data to hoard organs for internal patients, aligning private hospital incentives with global societal welfare.
- The mathematical architecture enables multi-way cyclic exchanges and non-directed altruistic donor chains that maximize life-years saved across national healthcare systems.
