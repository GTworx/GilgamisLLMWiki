## Executive Summary

Expanded from Harvard Business School professor **Clayton M. Christensen's** legendary final-day classroom talk—written alongside **James Allworth** and former *Harvard Business Review* editor **Karen Dillon** after Christensen's recovery from cancer and stroke (2012, HarperBusiness, pp. 4–7, 155)—*How Will You Measure Your Life?* applies rigorous management and strategy theories directly to personal happiness, enduring family love, and moral integrity.

Observing how brilliant HBS classmates systematically wrecked their marriages or even landed in federal prison despite extraordinary intelligence (p. 7), Christensen proves that individuals make the exact same fatal **resource-allocation mistake** as failing corporations: pouring time and energy into activities offering fast, tangible short-term feedback (bonuses, titles, emails) while starving long-term investments that pay off slowly (spouses, children, personal character).

## Three-Section Personal Strategy Architecture (Contents, pp. 4–5)

1. **Section I — Finding Happiness in Your Career**: Applies Herzberg's **Motivation-Hygiene Theory** (money prevents dissatisfaction, whereas meaningful responsibility creates fulfillment), balances **deliberate vs. emergent strategy**, and insists that *your real strategy is where your time and energy actually flow, not what you claim on paper*.
2. **Section II — Finding Happiness in Your Relationships**: Adapts **Jobs to Be Done** (*"What Job Did You Hire That Milkshake For?"*, p. 5) to understand what loved ones truly need, warns against **outsourcing your children's character development** (*Theseus's Ship*), and builds intentional family culture.
3. **Section III — Staying Out of Jail**: Introduces the razor-sharp doctrine of **"Marginal Thinking vs. 100% Rule"** (*"Just This Once..."*, Chapter 10, p. 5)—showing why holding to an ethical principle 100% of the time is infinitely easier than holding it 98% of the time.
