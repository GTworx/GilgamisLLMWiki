## Overview
Published in the Seton Hall Law eRepository, this comprehensive 29-page legal scholarship monograph analyzes patent eligibility under 35 U.S.C. Section 101 following the Supreme Court's landmark Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank decision. The author dissects how patent examiners and courts evaluate blockchain consensus mechanisms, cryptography, and neural networks against the judicially created 'abstract idea' doctrine.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Deconstructs the Alice two-step framework and its chilling impact on software and cryptographic patent prosecution.
- **Key Finding**: Formulates drafting strategies for blockchain patents, grounding claims in specific technical hardware improvements and novel cryptographic steps.
- **Key Finding**: Compares US patent office approaches with the European Patent Office (EPO) 'technical effect' jurisprudence.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "To survive Section 101 scrutiny post-Alice, a blockchain patent cannot simply claim 'doing financial clearing on a distributed computer'; it must invent a specific technical solution to a network synchronization problem."
