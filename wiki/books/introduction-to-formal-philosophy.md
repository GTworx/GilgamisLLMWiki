## Executive Summary

Edited by **Sven Ove Hansson** (KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm) and **Vincent F. Hendricks** (University of Copenhagen, pp. 4–7), *Introduction to Formal Philosophy* (Springer, 2018, 727 pp.) is an encyclopedic multi-author textbook demonstrating how formal methods from symbolic logic, probability theory, decision theory, game theory, and computer science sharpen classical philosophical puzzles into rigorous, tractable inquiry.

Opening with Hansson's programmatic treatise on **Formalization** (Ch. 1, pp. 22–78)—explaining how idealizing natural-language arguments into formal models clarifies hidden assumptions and exposes inferential aberrations—the volume unfolds across core domains of contemporary formal philosophy: **Dung's Abstract Argumentation Frameworks** (Henry Prakken, Ch. 2), **Formal History of Philosophy** (Catarina Dutilh Novaes, Ch. 3), **Nonmonotonic Reasoning** (Alexander Bochman, Ch. 4), **Induction & Bayesianism** (Urbaniak & Batens, Ch. 5), **Conditionals & Counterfactuals** (Ch. 6), modal/epistemic/deontic logics, belief revision, social epistemology, and formal ethics/decision theory.

---

## Key Methodological Pillars

1. **Formalization as Clarification, Not Reduction**: Defends formal modeling as a magnifying glass that uncovers subtle conceptual splits rather than an attempt to replace natural-language philosophy.
2. **Defeasible & Inductive Logic (Ch. 2, 4–6)**: Moves well beyond classical deductive syllogisms into structured argumentation dialogues, default/nonmonotonic inference, Carnapian confirmation functions, Bayesian updating, Popperian falsification, and counterfactual conditionals.
3. **Interdisciplinary Bridge**: Connects philosophical epistemology directly to computer science, AI knowledge representation, statistics, and rational choice theory.
