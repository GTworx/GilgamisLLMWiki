## Executive Summary

*536 Puzzles & Curious Problems* (Charles Scribner's Sons, New York, 1967, 442 pp.) unites the two undisputed titans of modern recreational mathematics: England's greatest puzzle inventor [[henry-ernest-dudeney]] (1857–1930) and *Scientific American*'s legendary *Mathematical Games* columnist [[martin-gardner]] (1914–2010), who edited, modernized, and annotated Dudeney's classic collections (*Modern Puzzles* and *Puzzles and Curious Problems*) into a single definitive omnibus (source: BOOKS/Fun & Puzzles_/536.pdf, Title Page, Contents & Introduction, pp. 2–6).

As Gardner observes in his Introduction, Dudeney's output surpassed every other puzzlist in both quantity and genuine mathematical depth (p. 6). Spanning arithmetical/algebraic puzzles (including Dudeney's immortal cryptarithm `SEND + MORE = MONEY`), geometric dissections, paper folding, combinatorial magic squares, graph-theoretic routes, map coloring, dominoes, and matchstick puzzles, the volume remains a foundational sourcebook for computer science, combinatorics, and mathematical pedagogy (pp. 4–442).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Arithmetical & Algebraical Problems (pp. 3–79)
- **Word-to-Equation Mastery**: Encompasses money, age, clock, speed-and-distance, weight-balancing, digital, skeleton-division, and **cryptarithm** puzzles demonstrating the elegance of Diophantine reasoning (p. 4).

### Geometrical Problems (pp. 81–138)
- **Spatial & Dissection Ingenuity**: Features triangle/polygon/circle theorems, plane-dividing, solid geometry, paper folding, moving-counter challenges, and Dudeney's signature **geometric dissections** (cutting one polygon into minimum pieces to reassemble another) (pp. 4–5).

### Combinatorial, Topological, Game & Matchstick Problems (pp. 139–222) & Solutions (pp. 223–416)
- **Discrete Math Pioneers**: Collects magic squares and stars, liquid-pouring state-space graphs, route/network traversals, point alignment, four-color map puzzles, domino layouts, and complete worked answers curated by Gardner (p. 5).
