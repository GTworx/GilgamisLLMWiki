## Executive Summary

*Karl Marx: A Biography* (originally published by Macmillan in 1973; revised Papermac edition, 1995, 495 pp.) by University of Kent political theorist [[david-mclellan]] is widely regarded as the benchmark single-volume intellectual and personal biography of **Karl Marx** (1818–1883) (source: BOOKS/Biography_/Karl Marx_ A Biography ( PDFDrive ).pdf, Cover, Title Page & Copyright, pp. 1–6).

Integrating rigorous exposition of Marx's major manuscripts—from the 1844 *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts* and *The Communist Manifesto* to the *Grundrisse* and *Das Kapital*—with an intimate, unsentimental portrait of his family life, lifelong partnership with Friedrich Engels, chronic poverty in London exile, and factional battles inside the First International, McLellan reveals a figure far more complex, humanist, and philosophically nuanced than either Soviet orthodoxy or Cold War caricature allowed (pp. 1–495).

## Key Arguments & Structural Breakdown

### Early Years, Hegel & Revolutionary Journalism (Trier, Bonn, Berlin, Paris, Brussels)
- **Young Hegelian to Historical Materialist**: Traces Marx's Rhineland upbringing, doctoral study of Greek atomism, radical journalism at the *Rheinische Zeitung*, Parisian encounter with French socialism and classical political economy, and decisive intellectual alliance with Friedrich Engels culminating in the 1848 *Communist Manifesto*.

### London Exile, the *Grundrisse*, and *Das Kapital*
- **The British Museum Years**: Chronicles the severe material hardship, child mortality, and relentless archival labor in Soho and Haverstock Hill during which Marx composed his critique of capitalist value, surplus labor, alienation, and crisis dynamics (*Grundrisse* and *Capital*, Vol. I, 1867).
- **The First International & Late Thought**: Examines Marx's organizational leadership in the International Workingmen's Association, his celebrated clash with Mikhail Bakunin, his response to the 1871 Paris Commune, and his late ethnographic and Russian-commune inquiries showing growing openness to non-linear paths of historical development.
