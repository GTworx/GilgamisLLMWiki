## Executive Summary
Published in 2026 in mathematical social choice theory, this 32-page advanced mathematical treatise formalizes the geometric and topological foundations of voting paradoxes (the Condorcet Paradox, Arrow's Impossibility Theorem, and the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem). By mapping preference profiles onto higher-dimensional topological manifolds, the authors prove that voting cycles (A > B > C > A) arise inherently from non-orientable topology (isomorphic to a Möbius strip or Klein bottle) in rank-order voting spaces. The paper demonstrates that cyclical electoral instability is not an empirical flaw of voters, but a fundamental topological property of pairwise aggregation in democratic decision-making.
