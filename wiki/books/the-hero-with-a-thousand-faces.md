## Executive Summary

Comparative mythologist **Joseph Campbell**'s 1949 landmark (Bollingen Series XVII / Fontana Press, copyright p. 330) synthesizes thousands of myths, folktales, religious scriptures, and initiation rites from Sumer, Greece, India, Mesoamerica, Polynesia, and medieval Europe into a single recurring archetypal architecture: the **Monomyth**—a term Campbell borrowed directly from James Joyce's *Finnegans Wake*. Using Freudian and Jungian depth psychology as his initial key to decoding symbolic grammar (Preface p. 4), Campbell argues that myths are public dreams while dreams are private myths, both guiding the human psyche through life's developmental thresholds.

Divided into **Part I: The Adventure of the Hero** (pp. 40–175) and **Part II: The Cosmogonic Cycle** (pp. 176–268), the book profoundly shaped modern narrative craft, cinema (George Lucas's *Star Wars*), and comparative religious studies.

---

## The Three-Act Monomyth Cycle (17 Stages)

1. **Departure (Ch. I, pp. 41–70)**: *The Call to Adventure → Refusal of the Call → Supernatural Aid → Crossing the First Threshold → The Belly of the Whale* (symbolic death and ego dissolution).
2. **Initiation (Ch. II, pp. 71–135)**: *The Road of Trials → Meeting with the Goddess → Woman as Temptress → Atonement with the Father → Apotheosis → The Ultimate Boon*.
3. **Return (Ch. III, pp. 136–171)**: *Refusal of the Return → Magic Flight → Rescue from Without → Crossing the Return Threshold → Master of the Two Worlds → Freedom to Live*—bringing renewed wisdom back to revitalize the community.
4. **The Cosmogonic Round (Part II)**: Expands personal initiation to universal creation, emanation, hero transformations (warrior, lover, emperor, world redeemer, saint), and cosmic dissolution.
