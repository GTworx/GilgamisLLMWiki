## Executive Summary

Written by acclaimed Turkish poet and *Diriliş* ("Resurrection") thinker **Sezai Karakoç** (1933–2021; Diriliş Yayınları, 1st ed. 1968, 5th ed. 1987, p. 3; 39 pp.), *Mehmet Akif* is a concentrated biographical and critical monograph on **Mehmet Akif Ersoy** (1873–1936)—poet of *Safahat* and author of the Turkish National Anthem (*İstiklâl Marşı*). Divided into two taut sections—**Hayatı, İnanç ve Düşünce Oluşumu, Savaşı** (pp. 4–25) and **Şiiri** (pp. 26–39)—Karakoç frames Akif not merely as a patriot or didactic versifier, but as the moral archetype of civilizational renewal (*diriliş*) amid imperial collapse.

Diagnosing the late-Ottoman Tanzimat crisis as an inverted cultural trauma that left society's innermost core exposed while sealing off healthy creative exchange (p. 5), Karakoç portrays Akif—born in Istanbul's Fatih district of Rumelian and Bukharan lineage (p. 6)—as a living synthesis of Eastern, Western, and Anatolian Islamic sensibility who fused ethical incorruptibility, social realism, and uncompromising poetic conscience.

---

## Core Critical & Biographical Themes

1. **Inner Renewal vs. Superficial Imitation (pp. 5–15)**: Argues that authentic modernization requires rooted self-renewal rather than alienated mimicry—a diagnosis Karakoç traces through Akif's lifelong rejection of both fatalistic stagnation and rootless Westernization.
2. **Moral Courage & Voluntary Exile**: Honors Akif's personal asceticism, his refusal of the parliamentary prize for *İstiklâl Marşı*, his Anatolian preaching during the War of Independence, and his dignified decade in Egypt.
3. **Poetics of Social Realism (*Şiiri*, pp. 26–39)**: Evaluates *Safahat* as a revolutionary bridge bringing vernacular street dialogue, urban poverty, and epic historical tragedy into classical *aruz* prosody.
