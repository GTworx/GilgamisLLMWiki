## Overview
Published in Zygon and Theology and Science by Emily Qureshi-Hurst and Mark Harris, this rigorous philosophical dossier interrogates Everett's Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI) of quantum mechanics and its profound theological and ethical ramifications. The authors examine how infinite branching timelines challenge moral responsibility, divine providence, soteriology, and personal free will.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Analyzes the ethics of branching: if every physically possible action is actualized in some Everettian world, does individual moral choice lose meaning?
- **Key Finding**: Reconstructs quantum theology beyond the standard Copenhagen collapse model, evaluating God's role in a deterministic multiverse.
- **Key Finding**: Formulates an ethical framework for Everettian decision theory based on objective branching weights (Born rule probabilities).

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "If the Many-Worlds Interpretation is true, quantum mechanics presents theology with its most radical challenge yet: an ontology where every tragedy is unavoidable in some world, and every virtue is matched by a vice in another."
