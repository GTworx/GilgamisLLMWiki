## Overview
This 3-study dossier investigates radical revisionist and long-range linguistic hypotheses proposing ancient affinities between Celtic, Etruscan, Basque, and Turanian (Turkic) languages, including critical evaluations of Mel Copeland's studies and Kaan Arslanoğlu's critiques of mainstream Indo-European theory. The dossier surveys both historical scholarly arguments and the methodological hazards of uncontrolled mass lexical comparison.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Audits 19th and 20th-century long-range comparative hypotheses linking Celtic to Eurasian agglutinative substrates.
- **Key Finding**: Critiques methodological flaws in mass lexical comparison lacking regular sound-law correspondence tables.
- **Key Finding**: Catalogs international scholarly perspectives and historical controversies surrounding pre-Indo-European European toponyms.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Long-range linguistic comparisons excite the imagination, but without strict, non-negotiable phonological correspondence laws, parallels remain fascinating coincidences rather than genealogical proof."
