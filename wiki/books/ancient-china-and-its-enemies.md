## Executive Summary

Written by Institute for Advanced Study historian **Nicola Di Cosmo** (Cambridge University Press, 2002, Title Page p. 3, Contents p. 5; 379 pp.), *Ancient China and Its Enemies* overturns the millennia-old Sinocentric caricature contrasting "civilized" Shang/Zhou/Han China against static "barbarian" raiders on its northern frontier (p. 1). Combining steppe archaeology with close philological readings of early Chinese chronicles and **Sima Qian's *Shiji*** (*Records of the Grand Historian*), Di Cosmo reconstructs the co-evolution of Chinese territorial states and nomadic steppe empires across the first millennium BCE.

Across four rigorously argued parts—**The Steppe Highway & Northern Frontier Archaeology** (Part I, Ch. 1–2), **Early Chinese Perceptions & Wall-Building** (Part II, Ch. 3–4), **The Rise of the Xiongnu (Hsiung-nu) Empire & Han Military Engagement** (Part III, Ch. 5–6), and **Sima Qian's Ethnographic Rationalization of the North** (Part IV, Ch. 7–8)—Di Cosmo demonstrates that early "Great Walls" were often *offensive* instruments of Chinese northward expansion into pasturelands, which in turn catalyzed the political unification of the **Xiongnu** under Modu Chanyu (209 BCE).

---

## Core Historical & Strategic Contributions

1. **Co-Evolution of Walls and Steppe Empires (Ch. 4–5)**: Refutes the notion that Chinese walls were purely defensive barriers against timeless nomad invasions, showing how Warring States and Qin frontier expansion triggered Xiongnu imperial centralization.
2. **From *Heqin* Appeasement to Han Imperial Offense (Ch. 6)**: Analyzes the strategic shift from Han treaty-tribute diplomacy (*heqin*) to Emperor Wu's (Han Wudi) costly expeditionary cavalry campaigns deep into the Hexi Corridor and Tarim Basin—opening the historical Silk Road.
3. **Historiographical Breakthrough in Sima Qian (Ch. 7–8)**: Shows how Sima Qian transformed northern nomads from mythological "beasts and birds" into an intelligible, ecologically grounded civilization occupying a parallel structural pole of world history.
