## Summary
Historical treatise tracking the political and scientific evolution of world maps from the Babylonian clay tablet (c. 700 BCE) and Ptolemaic projections to medieval Mappa Mundi, Ottoman Piri Reis, and the imperial Mercator projection.

## Analytical Overview
### Maps as Instruments of Imperial Power and Epistemology

*The Cartographic Imagination: Historical Evolution of World Maps from the Babylonian Imago Mundi to Mercator* provides a sweeping historical survey of how human civilizations have visualized planetary space. Far from being objective neutral representations, world maps have historically functioned as instruments of religious cosmology, imperial conquest, and geopolitical power.

### Epochs of Spatial Projection

The treatise examines transformative cartographic milestones:
1. **The Babylonian Map of the World (c. 700 BCE):** The earliest surviving world map, centering Babylon as the cosmic axis surrounded by the salt sea (*marra*).
2. **Ptolemy's Geographia (c. 150 CE):** Introducing mathematical latitude and longitude coordinates and conic projections.
3. **Medieval Mappae Mundi:** Christian cosmological maps (e.g. Hereford Mappa Mundi) placing Jerusalem at the absolute center of the terrestrial world.
4. **Islamic Golden Age Cartography:** Al-Idrisi's *Tabula Rogeriana* (1154) and Ottoman Admiral Piri Reis's world maps (1513/1528) incorporating early New World maritime discoveries.
5. **The Mercator Projection (1569):** Revolutionizing maritime navigation at the cost of extreme Eurocentric landmass distortion.
