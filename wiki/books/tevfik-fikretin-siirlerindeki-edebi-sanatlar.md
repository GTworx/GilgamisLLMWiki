## Executive Summary

Ayşe Çevirici Çağın's 2006 Istanbul University master's thesis, *Tevfik Fikret'in Şiirlerindeki Edebî Sanatlar* ("Literary Arts in Tevfik Fikret's Poems"), provides a systematic stylistic and rhetorical taxonomy of Tevfik Fikret's entire poetic corpus, keyed to İsmail Parlatır's critical edition *Tevfik Fikret: Bütün Şiirleri* after initial collation against the original *Rübab-ı Şikeste* (pp. 4–5). Grounded in the classical maxim *üslûb-ı beyân ayniyle insandır* ("style is the person," p. 4), the study argues that charting a poet's figures of speech exposes the underlying architecture of their worldview, emotional obsessions, and transitional position between classical Ottoman *belâgat* and French-inflected *Servet-i Fünun* modernism.

## Structural Breakdown

- **Giriş (Introduction, pp. 1–10 in thesis pagination)**: Surveys classical rhetorical theory (*edebî sanatlar*) from Divan poetics through Tanzimat and *Servet-i Fünun* transformations.
- **Bölüm I — Tevfik Fikret'in Kullandığı Edebî Sanatlar (pp. 11–48)**: Defines and inventories the repertoire of tropes and schemes deployed across Fikret's youth (*Eski Şeyler*), mature lyric/civic works (*Rübab-ı Şikeste*, *Haluk'un Defteri*), and didactic children's verse (*Şermin*).
- **Bölüm II — Teşbih ve İstiareler (Similes and Metaphors, pp. 49–200+)**: Exhaustively categorizes Fikret's figurative vehicles by semantic domain—ranked from highest to lowest frequency—including human states and anatomy, nature (water, landforms, seasons), cosmic elements, place names, animals, plants, and abstract concepts (pp. 5–6).
- **Bölüm III — Diğer Söz Sanatları (Other Rhetorical Figures, through p. 468)**: Catalogs and evaluates twenty-four additional devices ranked by frequency, including *teşhis* (personification), *tezat* (antithesis), *intak* (prosopopoeia), *mecaz-ı mürsel* (metonymy), *tenasüb*, *cinas*, *hüsn-i ta'lil*, *tecahül-i arif*, *tedric* (gradation), *kat'* (aposiopesis/dramatic break), *telmih*, *mübalağa*, *nida*, and *tekrir* (pp. 5, 464–468).
- **Sonuç (Conclusion, pp. 469–476)**: Synthesizes quantitative frequencies to reconstruct Fikret's imaginative cosmos—nature, humanity, cosmography, heaven/hell, beauty, and art (pp. 5, 469).

## Key Analytical Findings

1. **Dual Heritage in Rhetoric**: Çağın demonstrates how Fikret's *Eski Şeyler* retains classical Divan art-for-art's-sake ornament, whereas *Rübab-ı Şikeste* fuses classical *belâgat* with French Parnassian/Symbolist visuality and *Şermin* strips rhetoric down to plain didactic clarity (pp. 4, 469).
2. **Rising Intensity (*Tedric-i Sâid*)**: Analyzing Fikret's gradation figures (*tedric*, p. 464)—escalating from *dalga* (wave) to *kasırga* (hurricane), or *tokat* (slap) to *yumruk ve topuz* (fist and mace)—the thesis links his ascending rhetorical climaxes to a tempestuous, morally uncompromising temperament.
3. **Tragic Aposiopesis (*Kat'*)**: Examining rare but potent rhetorical breaks (*kat'*, pp. 465–467) in *Balıkçılar*, *Verin Zavallılara*, *Sühâ ve Pervin*, and *Sis*, Çağın shows how Fikret uses abrupt silence to heighten maternal dread, earthquake horror, and social grief where explicit statement would weaken impact.
4. **Quantitative Worldview Mapping**: By ordering figurative domains by frequency, the thesis moves Turkish stylistics beyond impressionistic commentary toward evidence-based literary analytics.
