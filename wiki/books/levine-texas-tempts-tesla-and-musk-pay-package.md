## Summary
Investigates the corporate governance shockwaves following the Delaware Chancery Court's voiding of Elon Musk's $56 billion Tesla compensation package and Tesla's subsequent corporate reincorporation in Texas.

## Analytical Overview
### The Delaware Shock and Executive Compensation Jurisprudence

In *Texas Tempts Tesla*, Bloomberg's Matt Levine and the Financial Times analyze one of the most consequential corporate governance confrontations in modern legal history: Chancellor Kathaleen McCormick's landmark 2024 Delaware Court of Chancery ruling voiding Elon Musk's unprecedented $56 billion compensation grant at Tesla, followed by Musk's retaliatory move to reincorporate Tesla in Texas.

### The Failure of Board Independence

Levine unpacks the core legal doctrine: Delaware law enforces strict fiduciary duties of loyalty and care. Because Tesla's compensation committee was dominated by Musk loyalists, personal friends, and conflicted directors, the transaction was judged under the rigorous **'Entire Fairness'** standard rather than the permissive Business Judgment Rule. The court found that stockholders were misled regarding board independence, rendering the package void.

### The Reincorporation Frontier and Forum Shopping

The dossier examines the broader structural threat to Delaware's century-old corporate legal monopoly. By moving to Texas and Nevada, mega-cap founders seek business courts friendlier to autocratic founder control. The authors debate whether this represents healthy regulatory competition between states or a race to the bottom in shareholder protection.
