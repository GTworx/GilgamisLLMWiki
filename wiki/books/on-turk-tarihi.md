## Executive Summary

Building on the pioneering epigraphic decipherment methods of Kazım Mirşan, French-educated cultural historian **Halûk Tarcan** (1927–2016) delivers in *Ön-Türk Tarihi* (*Proto-Turkic History*) an ambitious alternative narrative of world civilization. Tarcan contends that global history did not begin in 4000 BCE Sumer or Egypt, but in the Upper Paleolithic petroglyphs and runic tamga rock inscriptions of the **Tien Shan and Saymalıtaş (Central Asia)** dating back to 10,000 BCE, arguing that Proto-Turkic (*Ön-Türk*) migrations carried writing, cosmic theology (*Oğ-Uş* / *Gök Tanrı*), and metallurgy across Eurasia, Mesopotamia, the Americas, and Europe.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Saymalıtaş as the Cradle of Written Language**: Argues that human phonetic writing originated thousands of years prior to Sumer in the high-altitude Central Asian rock sanctuaries of Saymalıtaş.
2. **The Tamga-Runic Evolution**: Demonstrates how symbolic tribal tamgas evolved systematically into the runic letters found later in the Orkhon, Yenisei, and European runic inscriptions.
3. **The Universal Diffusion of Ön-Türk Culture**: Proposes that many undeciphered prehistoric inscriptions across the globe can be made intelligible by applying Kazım Mirşan's Proto-Turkic phonological keys.
