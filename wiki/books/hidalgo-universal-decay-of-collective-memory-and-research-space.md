## Executive Summary

This two-part quantitative sociological and computational physics treatise by César A. Hidalgo, Albert-László Barabási, and collaborators at MIT's Collective Learning Group models the mathematical dynamics governing human collective memory, cultural attention decay, and the evolution of global scientific research. In Nature Human Behaviour (2018), the authors analyze the decay of collective attention across cultural products (music, film, biographies, scientific papers, patents), proving that collective memory is bimodal: an initial fast decay driven by active oral/social communication followed by a long-tail slow decay sustained by physical recording media and institutional archives. In the accompanying research space paper, Hidalgo models national and institutional scientific research trajectories through the lens of human career mobility and relatedness networks.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Bimodal Decay of Collective Memory**: Proves that cultural attention decays according to a universal mathematical function combining an exponential decline (communicative memory transmitted peer-to-peer) and a power-law decline (cultural memory preserved in institutional archives).
- **Communicative vs. Cultural Memory Timelines**: Shows that communicative memory has a half-life of months to years (pop songs, viral memes), whereas cultural memory persists across decades or centuries (scientific breakthroughs, classical literature).
- **The Research Space Network Topology**: Constructs a multidimensional network of scientific disciplines based on career mobility, demonstrating that nations and universities diversify their research portfolios by branching into topologically proximate scientific fields.
- **Path Dependency in Scientific Discovery**: Shows that institutions cannot leap into distant, unrelated technological domains without first developing foundational intermediate research capabilities.
- **Empirical Universality Across Cultural Domains**: Validates the mathematical model across Wikipedia pageviews, Spotify streaming logs, patent citations, and physics preprint downloads.
