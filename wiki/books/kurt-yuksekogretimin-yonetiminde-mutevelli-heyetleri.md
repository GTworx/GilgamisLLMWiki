## Summary
Analyzes the governance model of Boards of Trustees (Mütevelli Heyetleri) in higher education, comparing Anglo-Saxon and continental European systems to propose a balanced governance reform for Turkish universities.

## Analytical Overview
### University Governance and the Crisis of Autonomy

In *Yükseköğretimin Yönetiminde Mütevelli Heyetleri: Türkiye İçin Bir Model Önerisi*, educational policy analyst Türker Kurt examines the structural impasse characterizing Turkish university governance: the tension between hyper-centralized bureaucratic control (under YÖK) and the demand for institutional, financial, and academic autonomy. Kurt evaluates the international history and operational mechanics of **Boards of Trustees (Mütevelli Heyetleri)** as a potential reform model.

### Comparative Analysis: Anglo-Saxon Autonomy vs. Continental Bureaucracy

The study compares two dominant global higher education paradigms:
1. **The Anglo-Saxon (US/UK) Trustee Model:** Independent governing boards comprised of civic, academic, and industrial leaders that oversee strategic budgets, appoint presidents, and shield universities from day-to-day political interference.
2. **The Continental / Turkish Model:** State-dominated civil service appointments and bureaucratic micromanagement, paired with foundation universities where trustees frequently blur the boundary between strategic oversight and commercial interference.

### A Hybrid Governance Blueprint for Turkish Higher Education

Kurt formulates a concrete legislative blueprint proposing hybrid Boards of Trustees for Turkish state universities that balance civic representation, academic senatorial freedom, financial auditability, and merit-based executive leadership.
