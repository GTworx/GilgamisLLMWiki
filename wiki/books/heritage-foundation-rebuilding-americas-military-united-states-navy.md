## Analytical Overview
### Naval Power in the Indo-Pacific Century

*Rebuilding America's Military: The United States Navy*, authored by naval strategist Brent D. Sadler for The Heritage Foundation (2021, 102 pp.), provides an exhaustive operational and budgetary audit of the U.S. Navy's readiness to deter and defeat a great-power adversary in maritime conflict.

### The Expanding Sino-American Naval Disparity

Sadler provides rigorous comparative data detailing the rapid erosion of American naval dominance:
- **Battle Force Inventory:** China's People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) has surpassed the U.S. Navy in total hull numbers (>355 ships vs. ~296 U.S. ships), backed by commercial shipyard capacities over 200 times larger than America's domestic shipbuilding infrastructure.
- **Geographic Asymmetry:** While the U.S. Navy operates as a globally dispersed force guarding sealanes worldwide, the PLAN operates with concentrated interior lines of communication in the Western Pacific, supported by land-based anti-ship ballistic missiles (DF-21D, DF-26).
- **Maintenance Backlogs & Drydock Shortages:** American public shipyards suffer decades of maintenance deferrals, resulting in nuclear attack submarines sitting idle for years awaiting overhaul.

### Blueprint for Naval Revitalization

Sadler outlines actionable defense reforms: expanding auxiliary unmanned surface vessels, revitalizing the domestic commercial merchant marine, and financing forward-deployed submarine tender repair capacity in allied ports (Japan, Australia).
