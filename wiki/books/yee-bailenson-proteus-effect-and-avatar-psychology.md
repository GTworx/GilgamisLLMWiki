## Overview
Published in Communication Research by Nick Yee, Jeremy N. Bailenson, and Nicolas Ducheneaut, this landmark experimental psychology study articulates the 'Proteus Effect'—the empirical phenomenon wherein an individual's behavior, self-perception, and interpersonal negotiations conform to the visual characteristics and stereotypes of their chosen digital avatar.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Demonstrates that avatar attractiveness and height systematically modulate confidence, interpersonal distance, and aggressive bargaining.
- **Key Finding**: Proves that behavioral changes induced by virtual avatar embodiments persist into subsequent physical reality tasks.
- **Key Finding**: Establishes a quantitative psychological foundation for avatar design ethics in spatial computing and education.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Users in attractive avatars exhibited significantly closer interpersonal proximity and higher self-disclosure, confirming that our digital skins shape our psychological dispositions."
