## Executive Summary

This 3-part cognitive science and human-computer interaction compendium applies Peter Pirolli and Stuart Card's foundational 'Information Foraging Theory' (IFT) to human problem-solving, cognitive aging, and software engineering workflows. Adapting optimal foraging theory from evolutionary behavioral ecology to human information consumption, IFT models humans as 'informavores' who navigate informational environments (web pages, codebases, knowledge graphs) by following perceptual 'information scents' (semantic cues, typography, hyperlinks) to maximize rate of valuable information gain per unit of navigation time. The studies investigate age-related differences in patch-leaving decisions and validate IFT-based predictive recommender systems designed to guide software engineers through complex codebases.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **The Informavore Paradigm**: Models human cognitive search not as random browsing, but as an optimal predatory search where actors balance patch-exploitation (reading current content) with patch-exploration (traversing links to new sources).
- **Information Scents and Semantic Cues**: Demonstrates that users assess proximal cues (snippets, button labels, link titles) to estimate the likelihood that following a path will yield relevant information, abandoning patches when scent decays.
- **Lifespan & Age Differences in Information Foraging**: Reveals that older adults exhibit more conservative patch-leaving thresholds, spending more time exploiting familiar patches due to working memory constraints, whereas younger informavores forage broadly.
- **Recommender Systems for Programmers**: Demonstrates that toolchains incorporating IFT predictive models reduce code navigation overhead by 35%, directing developers directly to relevant API dependencies.
- **Engineering Implications for Digital Knowledge Architectures**: Outlines design principles for personal knowledge bases and documentation: maximize information scent density and minimize cognitive friction between conceptual nodes.
