## Summary
Landmark, explosive Harvard Kennedy School and Middle East Policy treatise arguing that unconditional U.S. material and diplomatic support for Israel cannot be justified on either strategic or moral grounds, but is primarily driven by the domestic political influence of the Israel lobby.

## Analytical Overview
### Offensive Realism and the Domestic Capture of Foreign Policy

In *The Israel Lobby and U.S. Foreign Policy* (Harvard Kennedy School Faculty Research Working Paper RWP06-011, 2006, 83 pp.), renowned political scientists John J. Mearsheimer (University of Chicago) and Stephen M. Walt (Harvard Kennedy School) deliver the most influential and controversial analysis of contemporary American grand strategy ever published. Applying rigorous structural realist international relations theory, the authors analyze the profound divergence between the objective national interests of the United States and its actual foreign policy in the Middle East.

### The Strategic and Moral Balance Sheet

Mearsheimer and Walt methodically dismantle the two primary conventional justifications for Washington's unconditional support:
1. **The Strategic Liability:** While Israel was a valuable Cold War proxy against Soviet client states, the post-Cold War relationship became a severe net strategic liability—fueling anti-American violent extremism, complicating relations with vital energy-producing Arab partners, and bogging the U.S. military down in costly regional wars.
2. **The Moral Dimension:** While Israel's creation was a just response to European antisemitism, its ongoing military occupation, settlement expansion, and refusal to grant Palestinian statehood violate democratic norms, undermining American claims to champion international human rights.

### Anatomy of the Lobby's Influence

The treatise defines the "Israel Lobby" not as a cabal, but as a broad, loose coalition of individuals and organizations (such as AIPAC, the WINEP think tank, and Christian Zionist groups) exercising constitutionally protected political influence: campaign contributions, think-tank funding, media monitoring, and congressional lobbying to ensure that criticism of Israel remains political suicide in Washington.
