## Executive Summary

First published in March 2005 (Timaş Yayınları, ISBN 978-975-263-096-0, p. 3; 277 pp.), **Ali Çimen**'s *Tarihi Değiştiren Konuşmalar* curates and contextualizes forty-one watershed speeches from antiquity to the twenty-first century, demonstrating how disciplined oratory crystallizes collective resolve, legitimizes revolutions, or warns empires away from folly.

Each entry situates the speaker's historical crisis before presenting the text of the address—spanning **Socrates**'s defense, **Alexander**, **Cicero**, **Mark Antony**, **Tariq ibn Ziyad**, **Alparslan**, **Pope Urban II**, **Osman Gazi**, **Mehmed II**, **Robespierre**, **Napoleon**, **Abraham Lincoln**, **Bismarck**, **Woodrow Wilson**, **Lenin**, **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk** (p. 87), **Muhammad Iqbal**, **Hitler** (as a cautionary study in demagogic propaganda), **Roosevelt**, **Gandhi**, **Churchill**, **George Marshall**, **Castro**, **JFK**, **Martin Luther King Jr.** (p. 162), **Reagan**, **Nelson Mandela**, **Gorbachev**, **Yitzhak Rabin**, **Alija Izetbegović** (p. 216), and **Robin Cook**'s 2003 resignation speech over Iraq (TOC, pp. 4–7).

---

## Core Rhetorical Insights

1. **Kairos & Moral Clarity**: Shows that epochal speeches succeed when moral framing meets an acute historical hinge—whether Lincoln at Gettysburg, Atatürk addressing Turkish youth and parliament, Churchill in 1940, or King at the Lincoln Memorial.
2. **Rhetoric as Double-Edged Weapon**: Contrasts liberating civic oratory (Gandhi, Mandela, King, Izetbegović) with manipulative crisis rhetoric (Pope Urban II, Robespierre, Hitler), illustrating why democratic publics must cultivate rhetorical literacy.
3. **Bridging East and West**: Places Ottoman, Islamic, and Turkish foundational addresses (Quss ibn Sa'ida, Alparslan, Osman Gazi, Fatih, Atatürk, Iqbal) in direct dialogue with the Greco-Roman and modern Euro-American canon.
