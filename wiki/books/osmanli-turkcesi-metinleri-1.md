## Executive Summary

*Osmanlı Türkçesi Metinleri I* ("Ottoman Turkish Texts I") is a structured university paleography and historical text-reading textbook published by Anadolu University's Open Education Faculty (pp. 3–4). Edited by Recep Ahıshalı with four specialist Ottoman historians, the volume trains students to read, transcribe, gloss, and grammatically analyze authentic primary Ottoman sources spanning five centuries—progressing systematically from four units of printed (*matbu*) chronicles and periodicals to four units of handwritten (*el yazması*) manuscripts (pp. 5–6, TOC).

Each reading pairs original Ottoman-script facsimiles with vocabulary glossaries (*Metne Ait Sözlük*), modern Turkish translations (*Metnin Bugünkü Dile Çevirisi*), grammatical breakdowns (*Dilbilgisi Unsurları*), and self-assessment drills (*Kendimizi Sınayalım*), bridging archival philology with modern distance pedagogy.

## Key Intellectual Contributions

1. **Diachronic Arc of Ottoman Prose**: By moving from 15th-century *Tevârîh-i Âl-i Osmân* narratives through 17th-century imperial historiography (Kâtip Çelebi, Peçevî, Na'îmâ) and 18th–19th-century *vak'anüvis* and reform prose (Ahmed Resmî, Cevdet Paşa, Ağaoğlu Ahmed), the anthology illuminates how Ottoman administrative, diplomatic, and literary language evolved across eras.
2. **Bridge Between Printed Typesetting and Archival Hands**: Scaffolding learners from clear *matbu* print (Units 1–4) into manuscript scripts (Units 5–8) demystifies primary-source historical research.
3. **Pedagogical Design for Open Access**: Structured summaries, glossaries, modern-Turkish renderings, and answer keys make specialized philology accessible outside elite seminar rooms.
