## Executive Summary

Written by retired Turkish Ambassador and international legal scholar **Pulat Y. Tacar**, *The Truth Will Out: Facing the Armenian Allegations* (AVİM, May 2024, 170 pp.) provides an incisive diplomatic, archival, and international legal critique of the 1915 events in late Ottoman Anatolia. Grounded in the 1948 UN Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide and verdicts of the European Court of Human Rights (notably *Perinçek v. Switzerland*, 2015), Tacar deconstructs politicized parliamentary genocide resolutions, highlighting historical context, inter-communal civil conflict in wartime Eastern Anatolia, and legal criteria of *special intent* (*dolus specialis*).

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Legal Rigor vs. Political Declarations**: Argues that genocide is a strictly defined legal crime that can only be determined by a competent international criminal tribunal based on verified evidentiary intent, not by foreign parliamentary vote.
2. **The Principle of 'Fair Memory' (*Adil Hafıza*)**: Recognizes the immense human suffering and tragic loss of Armenian lives while demanding that the simultaneous slaughter of hundreds of thousands of Muslim Turkish and Kurdish civilians by Russian forces and Armenian volunteer units be incorporated into historical memory.
3. **The Precedent of the ECHR**: Emphasizes the European Court of Human Rights' ruling establishing that debate over 1915 constitutes legitimate historical inquiry protected under democratic free speech.
