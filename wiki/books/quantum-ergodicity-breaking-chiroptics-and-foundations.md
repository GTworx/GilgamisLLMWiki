## Summary
Frontier physics compendium investigating non-equilibrium quantum matter, including experimental observation of constructive interference at the edge of ergodicity, mathematical proofs demonstrating that quantum mechanics can be formulated without complex numbers, and room-temperature valley-selective photon emission in Si-MoSe2 chiroptical cavities.

## Analytical Overview
This volume gathers profound 2025 theoretical and experimental investigations pushing the fundamental frontiers of quantum mechanics and condensed matter physics. The first investigation, led by Dmitry A. Abanin and collaborators, tackles one of the deepest questions in statistical physics: how closed quantum systems thermalize. According to the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH), generic interacting many-body quantum systems act as their own heat baths, rapidly scrambling local information into ergodic thermal chaos. Abanin and team report the direct experimental observation of constructive quantum interference that breaks ergodicity at the phase boundary, demonstrating that quantum many-body scars can protect quantum coherence against thermalization across macroscopic timescales even under arbitrary external perturbations.

In quantum foundations, Timothée Hoffreumon and Nicolas Gisin present a tour-de-force mathematical treatise (*Quantum theory does not need complex numbers*). Re-examining the historic debate over whether the imaginary unit i is an indispensable physical reality or merely a mathematical convenience, the authors prove that standard Hilbert space quantum mechanics can be completely and rigorously reformulated using exclusively real vector spaces and Jordan algebras without losing any empirical predictions, including Bell non-locality and entanglement-swapping tests.

The volume concludes with a major nanoscience breakthrough: achieving room-temperature valley-selective circularly polarized emission in silicon-molybdenum diselenide (Si-MoSe2) heterostructures. By integrating monolayer TMDs with ultra-high quality-factor chiral photonic cavities, researchers successfully break optical time-reversal symmetry at room temperature, enabling valleytronic logic gates that manipulate electron valley pseudospin without cryogenic cooling.
