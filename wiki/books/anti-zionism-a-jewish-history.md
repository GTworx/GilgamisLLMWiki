## Executive Summary

Written by 2020 Pulitzer Prize–winning biographer **Benjamin Moser** (*Sontag*, *Why This World*; Doubleday, 2026, About the Book p. 2, Copyright p. 6; 298 pp.), *Anti-Zionism: A Jewish History* recovers a rich, long-suppressed century-and-a-half intellectual tradition of **Jewish rabbis, poets, lawyers, philosophers, journalists, and activists** across Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and the Americas who opposed political Zionism on religious, ethical, universalist, or pragmatic grounds.

Opening personally with his own family's Texas Reform heritage (*Introduction: My Grandfather's Herzl* & Ch. 5: *The Houston Schism*), Moser weaves twelve biographical-historical chapters (Outline, p. 152)—from early Reform and Orthodox critics (*The Sensitive Rabbi*, *A Fight in the Cousinhood*, *Murder on Jaffa Street* [Jacob Israël de Haan], *The Man of the Impossible*) through **Iraqi Jewish** upheaval (*The Baghdad Bombs*, Ch. 6), **Hannah Arendt**'s ethical dissent (*The Conscious Pariah*, Ch. 7), **Maxime Rodinson** (*The Biggest Antisemite in France*, Ch. 8), anti-apartheid parallels (Ch. 11), and contemporary post-2023 Gaza debates (*The Holocaust on Our Phones*, Ch. 12).

## Core Historical & Ethical Contributions

1. **Separating Judaism from Political Nationalism**: Demonstrates through primary Jewish voices—from nineteenth-century rabbis and Bundists to Arendt, Judah Magnes, and contemporary Jewish dissenters—that Jewish identity has historically encompassed vibrant anti-nationalist and diasporic ethical traditions.
2. **Global, Non-Monolithic Jewish Geography**: Recovers voices beyond Europe and the US, including Arab-Jewish communities in Baghdad and Cairo caught between colonial partition, Arab nationalism, and Israeli state-building.
3. **Moral Memory & Universalism**: Probes the painful twentieth- and twenty-first-century tension between post-Holocaust survival imperatives and universal human-rights ethics in an era of instant digital witness.
