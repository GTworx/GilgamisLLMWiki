## Executive Summary

This 101-page comprehensive best-practices manual published by AFAD examines the theories, sociotechnical methodologies, and grassroots models for fostering Disaster Resilient Communities (*Afete Dirençli Toplumlar*). Acknowledging that institutional emergency response cannot reach every household during the critical initial hours following a catastrophic event, the monograph focuses on community-based disaster risk management (CBDRM).

The volume surveys grassroots preparedness initiatives, neighborhood disaster volunteer organizations (e.g., MAG - Mahalle Afet Gönüllüleri in Türkiye, CERT in the United States, and Jishu-bosai-soshiki in Japan), school disaster education curricula, and inclusive community engagement for persons with disabilities, elderly residents, and children. It demonstrates how social capital, collective efficacy, and neighborhood trust serve as vital physical and psychological survival buffers during disasters.

---

## Key Strategic, Operational & Technical Takeaways

- **Social Capital as Disaster Infrastructure**: Demonstrates that communities with high bonding and bridging social capital experience significantly lower mortality and accelerated socioeconomic recovery during disasters.
- **Neighborhood Volunteer Networks**: Analyzes the structural organization, training regimens, light search-and-rescue equipment caches, and communication protocols of neighborhood volunteer units (MAG).
- **Vulnerable Population Inclusivity**: Formulates targeted safety and evacuation frameworks for individuals with mobility challenges, neurodivergent populations, single mothers, and unaccompanied minors.
- **Community Emergency Preparedness Drills**: Evaluates the behavioral efficacy of participatory drills, shake-out exercises, and household non-structural hazard mitigation campaigns.
