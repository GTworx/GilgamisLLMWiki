## Executive Summary

*Blockchain, Crypto and DeFi: Bridging Finance and Technology* (published by John Wiley & Sons, 2025/2026, 749 pp.) is Harvard Business School Professor [[marco-di-maggio]]'s comprehensive 15-chapter treatise on the economic architecture, game-theoretic incentives, and institutional transformation of distributed ledger technology, cryptocurrencies, decentralized finance (DeFi), Web3, and AI-blockchain convergence (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 5–13, 21–51). Tracing the lineage of decentralized trust from Stuart Haber and W. Scott Stornetta's 1991 cryptographic timestamping protocols and Adam Back's Hashcash to Satoshi Nakamoto's 2008 Bitcoin whitepaper, Di Maggio explains how Proof-of-Work (PoW) and Proof-of-Stake (PoS) consensus solve the Byzantine Generals Problem without centralized financial intermediaries (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 21–56).

Moving beyond Bitcoin, the book systematically dissects Vitalik Buterin's Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM), Turing-complete smart contracts, and decentralized oracles (Ch. 2); the Blockchain Scalability Trilemma across Layer-1 sharding and Layer-2 Optimistic/ZK-Rollups (Ch. 3); the monetary economics of fiat-backed, crypto-collateralized, and algorithmic stablecoins (Ch. 4) vs. Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs, Ch. 5); Automated Market Makers (AMMs), liquidity pools, flash loans, and yield farming in DeFi (Ch. 6–9); NFTs and tokenized real-world assets (Ch. 10); global regulatory frameworks (MiCA, SEC, Ch. 11); quantitative crypto market microstructure (Ch. 12–13); Web3 community branding (Ch. 14); and the convergence of autonomous AI agents with cryptographic payment rails (*'When HAL Meets Satoshi'*, Ch. 15) (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 7–8, 57–702).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Chapters 1–3: Bitcoin, Ethereum Smart Contracts & The Scalability Trilemma (pp. 1–180)**:
  * *From Cryptographic Timestamping to Programmable State Machines*: Details Haber & Stornetta's 1991 hash chains, Satoshi Nakamoto's UTXO model and halving supply schedule (pp. 1–56), Ethereum's account-based smart contracts and Chainlink oracle architectures (pp. 57–106), and Layer-2 scaling via Lightning Network, Arbitrum, Optimism, and ZK-Rollups overcoming Vitalik Buterin's Scalability Trilemma (decentralization, security, scalability) (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 21–180).

* **Chapters 4–5: Stablecoins & Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) (pp. 181–270)**:
  * *Private vs. Sovereign Digital Money*: Contrasts fiat-collateralized stablecoins (USDT, USDC), over-collateralized DAI, and the endogenous death-spiral of algorithmic TerraUST (pp. 181–222) with retail and wholesale CBDCs, analyzing privacy trade-offs, commercial bank disintermediation risks, and cross-border mBridge settlement (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 181–270).

* **Chapters 6–9: Decentralized Finance (DeFi) Primitives & Tokenomics (pp. 271–444)**:
  * *AMMs, Lending Protocols & MEV*: Examines constant-product Automated Market Makers ($x \cdot y = k$ in Uniswap), impermanent loss, over-collateralized lending and liquidation mechanics (Aave, Compound), flash loans, Maximal Extractable Value (MEV), and DAO governance tokenomics (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 271–444).

* **Chapters 10–15: NFTs, Regulation, Crypto Trading, Web3 Branding & AI Convergence (pp. 445–702)**:
  * *Web3 Ownership and Autonomous AI Agents*: Analyzes ERC-721/1155 NFT provenance and real-world asset (RWA) tokenization (Ch. 10), SEC Howey Test and EU MiCA regulation (Ch. 11), on-chain alpha and market microstructure (Ch. 12–13), Web3 loyalty tokenomics (Ch. 14), and Chapter 15 (*'When HAL Meets Satoshi'*, pp. 661–702), where blockchain provides cryptographic provenance against deepfakes, decentralized compute marketplaces, and native wallets for autonomous AI agents (source: BOOKS/Blockchain_/2026 Blockchain, Crypto & DeFi.pdf, pp. 445–702).
