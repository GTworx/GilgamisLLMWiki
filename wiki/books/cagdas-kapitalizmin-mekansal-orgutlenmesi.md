## Executive Summary

Derived from architect and urban theorist **Hakkı Yırtıcı's** 2003 Istanbul Technical University (İTÜ) doctoral dissertation and published by Istanbul Bilgi University Press (2005; 2nd ed. 2009, pp. 2–3), *Çağdaş Kapitalizmin Mekânsal Örgütlenmesi* ("The Spatial Organization of Contemporary Capitalism") applies critical spatial theory—drawing on Henri Lefebvre, David Harvey, and Manuel Castells—to analyze how post-Fordist global capitalism actively manufactures urban architecture, logistics corridors, and consumption spaces to accelerate capital turnover.

Grounded in concrete architectural fieldwork in Istanbul, the study culminates in a detailed morphological dissection of suburban big-box hypermarkets—specifically **Carrefour İçerenköy** and **Real Kartal** (Chapter 5, pp. 97–136)—showing how highway access, gigantic windowless shells (*biçim-kabuk*), standardized interior infrastructures, and privatized parking deserts reorganize everyday Turkish civic life around frictionless consumption.

## Structural Overview (Contents, pp. 6–7)

1. **Bölüm I — Rethinking Space in the Contemporary World (pp. 9–20)**: Reframes space not as an empty geometric container, but as a dynamic social product generated by capitalist production and reproduction relations.
2. **Bölümler II–IV — Dynamics of Spatial Mobility & Global Restructuring (pp. 21–96)**: Traces the shift from Fordist industrial cities to flexible global networks, time–space compression, and real-estate financialization.
3. **Bölüm V — Infrastructural Transformation of Consumption Spaces (pp. 97–150+)**: Analyzes the ideology of consumption and dissects Istanbul's **Carrefour İçerenköy** and **Real Kartal** hypermarkets across six architectural axes: context/site, scale, shell/form, infrastructure, service function, and consumer-subject production (p. 7).
