## Overview
Published in the Vanderbilt Journal of Entertainment & Technology Law (Vol. 23) by human rights attorney Brittan Heller, this landmark 52-page legal treatise coins the term 'Biometric Invalidation' to conceptualize the existential threat posed by extended reality (XR) hardware. Heller exposes how eye-tracking, galvanic skin responses, and kinetic movement patterns allow commercial platforms to deduce sexual orientation, political ideology, health conditions, and emotional vulnerability without user consent.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Defines 'Biometric Invalidation': the unconsented algorithmic inference of intimate, protected traits from kinetic XR sensor telemetry.
- **Key Finding**: Proves that existing US privacy statutes (BIPA, HIPAA, FTC Section 5) and GDPR are ill-equipped to govern non-static biometric inferences.
- **Key Finding**: Proposes a comprehensive regulatory paradigm: biometric fiduciary duties, mandatory hardware kill-switches, and local-only telemetry processing.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "XR devices do not just observe our external actions; they map the autonomic nervous system. Biometric invalidation occurs when platforms know our subconscious reactions before we ourselves have processed them."
