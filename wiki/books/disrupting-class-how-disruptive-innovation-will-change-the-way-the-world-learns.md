## Executive Summary

*Disrupting Class: How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns* (published by McGraw-Hill, 2008, 258 pp.) by Harvard Business School Professor [[clayton-m-christensen]], [[michael-b-horn]], and [[curtis-w-johnson]] applies Christensen's renowned theory of **Disruptive Innovation** to diagnose why public K–12 schools have struggled for decades to improve despite massive funding increases and why student-centric online learning will fundamentally transform education (source: BOOKS/Education_/Disrupting Class_ How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 2–9, 21). The authors explain that schools are not 'broken'—rather, they were brilliantly engineered as an **interdependent, factory-batch system** (standardized by age-grade cohorts, uniform curricula, and lecture-based instruction) to meet industrial-era societal mandates, which makes it structurally impossible for them to customize instruction for students who possess diverse Howard Gardner multiple intelligences and learning paces (source: BOOKS/Education_/Disrupting Class_ How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 21–70).

Analyzing why spending over **$60 billion** placing computers in classrooms over two decades yielded almost zero pedagogical transformation (Ch. 3), Christensen, Horn, and Johnson demonstrate that incumbent school systems *crammed* computers into existing teacher-led classrooms as a **sustaining innovation** rather than deploying them disruptively (pp. 3, 71–88). True disruptive transformation occurs when computer-based learning enters through areas of **nonconsumption** (such as AP courses in rural schools, credit recovery for dropouts, homebound students, and tutoring) and evolves along an S-curve toward **student-centric, modular learning platforms** where teachers transition from batch lecturers to one-on-one learning coaches and mentors (source: BOOKS/Education_/Disrupting Class_ How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 89–146, 197–230).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Introduction & Chapters 1–2: Why Schools Struggle to Teach Differently When Each Student Learns Differently (pp. 1–70, PDF pp. 21–90)**:
  * *Interdependent Factory Schooling vs. Diverse Brains*: Identifies society's four evolving mandates for schools (maximizing human potential, democratic citizenship, economic competitiveness, and equity, p. 21). Explains how modular vs. interdependent product architectures (illustrated by Henry Ford's River Rouge steel integration, p. 51) force traditional schools to standardize how they teach and test, producing artificial learning disabilities among students whose cognitive profiles differ from the batch norm (source: BOOKS/Education_/Disrupting Class_ How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 21–70).

* **Chapters 3–5: Crammed Classroom Computers vs. Disruptively Deploying Student-Centric Learning (pp. 71–146, PDF pp. 91–166)**:
  * *Competing Against Nonconsumption*: Shows why $60 billion in classroom IT was co-opted by the existing instructional model (Ch. 3). Demonstrates how disruptive innovations take root in **nonconsumption**—where the alternative is nothing at all (rural AP courses, home-schooling, credit recovery)—forecasting a classic logistic substitution curve toward online student-centric platforms powered by user-generated and modular learning software (Ch. 4–5) (source: BOOKS/Education_/Disrupting Class_ How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 71–146).

* **Chapters 6–9: Early Childhood Language Exposure, Education Research & Heavyweight Innovation Teams (pp. 147–230, PDF pp. 167–250)**:
  * *Hart & Risley's 30-Million-Word Gap & Organizational Structure*: Examines Betty Hart and Todd Risley's early childhood language findings (Ch. 6), critiques descriptive correlation-based education research in favor of anomaly-seeking normative theory (Ch. 7), applies the 'Tools of Cooperation and Consensus' to school politics (Ch. 8), and prescribes **heavyweight vs. autonomous organizational structures** so school leaders can 'run two businesses in tandem' (Ch. 9) (source: BOOKS/Education_/Disrupting Class_ How Disruptive Innovation Will Change the Way the World Learns ( PDFDrive ).pdf, pp. 3, 147–230).
