## Executive Summary

In *İbrahim Peygamber: Sümer Yazılarına ve Arkeolojik Buluntulara Göre* (*The Prophet Abraham: According to Sumerian Inscriptions and Archaeological Findings*), **Muazzez İlmiye Çığ** examines the historical, archaeological, and textual evidence surrounding the founding patriarch of monotheism, **Prophet Abraham (Ibrahim)**. Investigating the biblical claim that Abraham departed from **Ur of the Chaldees (*Ur Kasdim*)** and settled in **Harran**, Çığ cross-references Old Testament narratives and Qur'anic passages with third- and second-millennium BCE cuneiform tablets from Ur, Mari, Ebla, and Nuzi, exploring how Mesopotamian laws, religious customs, and nomadic migrations formed the historical context for the Abrahamic tradition.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **Mesopotamian Legal Context**: Demonstrates that patriarchal behaviors that appear anomalous in modern scripture (e.g., Sarah giving her maidservant Hagar to Abraham to bear children) were standard, legally binding procedures codified in second-millennium BCE Nuzi and Babylonian marital contracts.
2. **The Urban Geography of Ur and Harran**: Anchors Abraham in authentic Bronze Age trade corridors connecting lower Mesopotamian commercial centers (Ur) with northern Syrian and Anatolian trading hubs (Harran).
3. **Evolutionary Monotheism**: Argues that Abrahamic monotheism did not emerge in a vacuum, but was forged through a complex dialogue and gradual abstraction away from Mesopotamian astral polytheism (the cults of Nanna/Sin and Shamash).
