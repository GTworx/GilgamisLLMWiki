## Executive Summary

In this extensive follow-up work, *Tarihin Başladığı Ön-Türk Uygarlığı: Resmî Tarihin Çöküşü* (*The Proto-Turkic Civilization Where History Began: The Collapse of Official History*), **Halûk Tarcan** expands his epigraphic and civilizational thesis. Drawing on thousands of petroglyphs and inscriptions decoded by Kazım Mirşan, Tarcan systematically challenges classical Eurocentric archaeology, presenting detailed photographic catalogs of rock art, burial stelae, and inscriptions from Siberia, Central Asia, Anatolia, and Western Europe to argue that a unified Proto-Turkic spiritual and linguistic civilizational sphere dominated prehistoric Eurasia.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Epigraphic Primacy of Central Asia**: Asserts that Proto-Turkic petroglyphic inscriptions date from 10,000 BCE to 3,000 BCE, preceding Mediterranean writing systems.
2. **Hakkâri Stelae as Steppe Hero Monuments**: Interprets the 13 monumental carved stone stelae discovered in Hakkâri (1998) as representations of Eurasian steppe warrior chieftains (*balbals*), proving early steppe penetration into the Zagros/Anatolian highlands.
3. **Challenging Academic Orthodoxy**: Denounces conventional Western and Turkish academic departments for ignoring Indigenous epigraphic discoveries in favor of Western-approved classical chronologies.
