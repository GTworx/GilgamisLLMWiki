## Overview
Presented at ACM CHI 2019 by an international cadre of prominent HCI theorists, this foundational workshop monograph explores philosophical frameworks—including postphenomenology, critical theory, and mediated perception—to understand how interfaces co-constitute human reality. The authors articulate how spatial computational interfaces mediate bodily habitus, social agency, and epistemic horizons.

## Key Takeaways
- **Key Finding**: Synthesizes Don Ihde's technological mediation theory (embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, and background relations) for interactive design.
- **Key Finding**: Examines how VR/AR devices reshape user sensorimotor boundaries and subjective sense of agency.
- **Key Finding**: Proposes critical speculative design methods that make visible hidden algorithmic assumptions embedded in user experiences.

## Critical Excerpts & Analysis
> "Technology is never an inert tool; it actively mediates how the world presents itself to human consciousness, co-shaping both the subject and the object of experience."

## Methodological Significance & Legacy
