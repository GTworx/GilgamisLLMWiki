## Summary
Monograph published by Harvard Business School evaluating the financial returns, skill acquisition, and long-term career compensation of individuals who choose entrepreneurship over corporate careers.

## Analytical Overview
### The True Economic Returns to Entrepreneurship

*Return to Entrepreneurship: Human Capital, Financial Returns, and Career Trajectories*, authored by researchers in the Harvard Business School Entrepreneurial Management Unit, addresses a fundamental empirical puzzle in labor economics and corporate finance: given that the overwhelming majority of venture startups fail and that median entrepreneurial financial returns are lower than corporate salaries, why do talented individuals systematically pursue entrepreneurship?

### Skewed Distributions and Option Value

The paper evaluates decades of longitudinal career data from elite business school graduates and technical founders:
- **Power-Law Skewness:** While the median entrepreneur earns less than their corporate counterparts, the extreme right-tail skewness of successful venture exits provides massive positive expected option value.
- **Accelerated Human Capital Accumulation:** Founders acquire broad executive, operational, and capital-raising skills at 3x to 5x the velocity of corporate middle managers.
- **The Post-Failure Re-Entry Premium:** Counterintuitively, failed founders who return to the corporate labor market command higher compensation and seniority than peers who remained continuously in corporate tracks, proving that the corporate sector values founder grit and executive breadth.

### Policy Implications

The study establishes that robust social safety nets and non-punitive bankruptcy laws are vital institutional prerequisites for encouraging high-ability risk-taking.
