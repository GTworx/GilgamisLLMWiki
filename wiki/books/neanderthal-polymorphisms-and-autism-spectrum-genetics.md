## Executive Summary
Published in *Molecular Psychiatry* (2024) by Clemens Gross and an international team of neurogeneticists, this groundbreaking 14-page study uncovers a statistically significant enrichment of rare archaic Neanderthal-derived single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in individuals diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). By analyzing whole-genome sequences from large cohorts (SPARK and Simons Simplex Collection), the researchers identified specific introgressed Neanderthal alleles regulating synaptic transmission, axonal growth, and ion channel clustering that are overrepresented in autistic probands and their unaffected siblings compared to ethnic-matched controls, providing new evolutionary insights into neurodivergent cognitive architectures.

---

## Core Thematic Insights & Structural Framework

- **Archaic Introgression and Neurodevelopment**: While modern non-African humans carry 1–2% Neanderthal DNA on average, autistic individuals harbor a specific subset of rare introgressed alleles at significantly higher frequencies.
- **Synaptic and Neuronal Pathway Localization**: The enriched Neanderthal alleles cluster heavily within genes governing glutamatergic neurotransmission, synaptic pruning, and calcium signaling cascades in the cerebral cortex.
- **Evolutionary Trade-Offs of Cognitive Diversity**: Suggests that alleles associated with modern autism may have conferred evolutionary advantages in ancestral Neanderthal populations—such as hyper-focused visual-spatial processing, sensory acuity, and mechanical aptitude in hunting.
- **Polygenic Risk Background**: Proves that archaic alleles act not as single monogenic determinants, but as potent modulators within a complex polygenic background influencing synaptic plasticity and brain lateralization.
