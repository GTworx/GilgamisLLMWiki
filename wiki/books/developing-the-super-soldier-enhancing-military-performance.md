## Executive Summary

*Developing the Super Soldier: Enhancing Military Performance* (2017, *The Kingston Conference on International Security Series*, Centre for International and Defence Policy at Queen's University, the Strategic Studies Institute of the U.S. Army War College, the Canadian Army Doctrine and Training Centre, and the NATO Defense College, 132 pp.), edited by [[william-g-braun-iii]], [[stefanie-von-hlatky]], and [[kim-richard-nossal]], provides a multidisciplinary strategic, neuroscientific, psychological, and ethical examination of **Human Performance Enhancement (HPE)** in modern armed forces. As contemporary warfare shifts toward hyper-complex, multi-domain, and cognitively saturated operational environments, military advantage depends less on industrial mass alone and increasingly on pushing individual soldiers beyond natural biological limits across four dimensions: **physical endurance, cognitive bandwidth, emotional/psychological resilience, and social/ethical cohesion** (source: BOOKS/Military_/2017 USA Developing the Super Soldier - Enhancing Military Performance.pdf, ch. 1, pp. 1–5).

Across nine chapters authored by U.S. Army Research Laboratory (ARL) neuroscientists, NASA/special-operations operational psychologists, Canadian Armed Forces officers, and military ethicists, the volume identifies a central strategic paradox: **enhancing individual technical or physical capability at the expense of unit social cohesion, psychological resilience, or moral autonomy degrades rather than improves combat effectiveness**. From real-time brain-network telemetry (*"The Quantified Soldier"*) and special-forces psychological selection to the ethics of pharmacological/bionic augmentation and virtue ethics under combat stress, the authors conclude with a clear operational maxim: *"People First, Mission Always"*—technological and neuro-cognitive enhancements must augment, never supplant, human agency, unit trust, and moral judgment (source: BOOKS/Military_/2017 USA Developing the Super Soldier - Enhancing Military Performance.pdf, ch. 2–3, pp. 7–45; ch. 5–7, pp. 65–107; ch. 9, pp. 115–122).

## Key Arguments / Chapter Breakdown

* **Chapter 1: Introduction (William G. Braun III, Stéfanie von Hlatky, Kim Richard Nossal, pp. 1–6)**:
  * *The Multidimensional "Super Soldier"*: Defines the modern super soldier not as a science-fiction cyborg, but as an integrated human platform enhanced across physical, cognitive, social, cultural, and ethical domains to retain competitive overmatch in ambiguous, high-tempo battlespaces (pp. 1–4).

* **Chapter 2: Capability and Connection — Social Cohesion and Soldier Performance Enhancement in Canada and the United States (Maj. H. Christian Breede, pp. 7–28)**:
  * *The Capability–Cohesion Trade-Off*: Distinguishes between **invasive** (surgical, genetic, pharmacological) and **non-invasive** (exoskeletons, wearables, training) enhancements. Applies social capital theory (**bonding** vs. **bridging** social capital) to warn that unequal or invasive enhancements risk fracturing unit cohesion by creating a two-tiered caste divide between "enhanced" and "unenhanced" warfighters, or inducing hyper-bonded insular cliques alienated from broader military norms (pp. 8–25).

* **Chapter 3: The Quantified Soldier — Using Brain Networks to Enhance Future Operations (Jean M. Vettel, Amar R. Marathe, Evan C. Carter, Gregory M. Gremillion, Jason S. Metcalfe, Javier O. Garcia — U.S. Army Research Laboratory, pp. 29–46)**:
  * *Dynamic Brain Network Telemetry & Individualized Neuro-Augmentation*: Demonstrates how U.S. Army Research Laboratory (ARL) neuroscientists use multi-scale brain network modeling (structural white-matter tracts and functional EEG/fMRI connectivity) and physiological sensors to track a soldier's cognitive workload, fatigue, and attentional state in real time. Rather than one-size-fits-all automation, **human-autonomy teaming** dynamically adapts information displays and AI assistance to the individual soldier's momentary neural state (pp. 29–42).

* **Chapter 4: Soldier Resilience — Lessons Learned from the Assessment and Selection of High-Risk Military Operational Personnel (James J. Picano, NASA Johnson Space Center, pp. 47–64)**:
  * *Psychological Architecture of Elite Operators*: Synthesizes decades of empirical assessment and selection data from Tier-1 special mission units and NASA astronauts. Identifies the core predictors of extreme stress resilience: high emotional stability, stress hardiness, cognitive flexibility under ambiguity, team orientation, and rapid physiological recovery after acute sympathetic nervous-system arousal (pp. 47–60).

* **Chapter 5: Moral Autonomy and the Ethics of Soldier Enhancement (C. Anthony Pfaff, U.S. Army War College, pp. 65–80)**:
  * *Preserving Moral Agency Under Pharmacological & Neural Modification*: Evaluates the Jus in Bello and military-ethical implications of cognitive and emotional enhancements (such as fear-dampening beta-blockers, wakefulness agents like modafinil, or aggression/compliance modulators). Warns that any enhancement that diminishes a soldier's capacity for independent moral deliberation or remorse undermines both the **moral equality of combatants** and chain-of-command accountability (pp. 65–78).

* **Chapter 6: Virtuous Super Soldiers? (Jesse Kirkpatrick, George Mason University, pp. 81–94)**:
  * *Aristotelian Virtue Ethics and Martial Courage*: Investigates whether artificially induced fearlessness still counts as the martial virtue of **courage**. Argues that true military virtue requires conscious risk appraisal and moral choice; chemically erasing fear risks replacing Aristotelian *andreia* (courage) with reckless rashness or robotic amorality (pp. 81–92).

* **Chapter 7: Ethical Implications in Generating or Selecting for Super Soldiers (Col. James Ness & Steve Kornguth, pp. 95–108)**:
  * *Epigenetics, Genomics, and Duty of Care*: Examines genetic screening, epigenetic trauma markers, microbiome modulation, and post-deployment reintegration, emphasizing the state's lifelong medical and ethical duty of care when altering a service member's biology for temporary operational utility (pp. 95–106).

* **Chapters 8–9: Metrics: Training the Mind of the Super Soldier (Jason Dozois, pp. 109–114) & Conclusion: People First, Mission Always (Ryan Anderson, pp. 115–122)**:
  * *Narrative, Transhumanist Foresight, and Human-Centric Doctrine*: Contrasts cyberpunk *Deus Ex* transhumanist dystopias with grounded military policy, concluding that the true "super soldier" is cultivated by investing first in psychological resilience, ethical education, and unit cohesion (*"People First, Mission Always"*) before layering on hardware or neuro-technologies (pp. 109–122).

## Key Quotes & Citations

In the future, senior military leaders and defence officials will strive to develop what might be called 'super soldiers'—in other words, developing and enhancing critical skills, from physical and cognitive abilities, to social, cultural and ethical understandings of the environment in which soldiers operate." ## Thematic Connections

* **Neuro-Somatic Regulation and Adaptive Intelligence (AQ)**: Picano's analysis of special-operations stress hardiness (Chapter 4) and Vettel et al.'s real-time brain-network telemetry (Chapter 3) provide the military-operational counterpart to [[john-sanei]]'s neuroscience of **Adaptive Intelligence (AQ)** and nervous-system regulation in [[the-adaptability-advantage]]. Both works demonstrate that peak cognitive performance under extreme uncertainty requires regulating sympathetic "fight-or-flight" hijacks and maintaining flexible prefrontal awareness.
* **Algorithmic C2, Cross-Domain Deterrence, and Human-Autonomy Teaming**: The ARL's **"Quantified Soldier"** brain-computer teaming framework directly complements [[roy-lindelauf]], [[alex-wilner]], and [[samuel-zilincik]]'s analyses of algorithmic command-and-control, artificial intelligence, and emotional dynamics in [[deterrence-in-the-21st-century]] (edited by [[frans-osinga]] and [[tim-sweijs]]), as well as the human-centered AI co-piloting frameworks of [[leading-for-tomorrow]] and [[rapid-reculturing]].
* **Bonding Social Capital and the Evolution of Cooperation**: Major Christian Breede's warning (Chapter 2) that individualistic performance enhancements can destroy unit cohesion if they erode mutual trust and shared vulnerability directly echoes [[robert-axelrod]] and [[william-d-hamilton]]'s findings on reciprocal trust in [[the-evolution-of-cooperation]] and [[richard-dawkins]]'s evolutionary analysis of kin and reciprocal altruism in [[the-selfish-gene]].
