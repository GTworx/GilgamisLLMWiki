## Summary
Comparative propaganda study examining the tactical doctrine of 'Accusation in a Mirror' (accusing one's adversary of the exact crimes one intends to commit), comparing historical radio propaganda in the 1994 Rwandan Genocide with contemporary state wartime rhetoric.

## Analytical Overview
### The Rhetorical Anatomy of Preemptive Justification

*Accusation in a Mirror: The Psychological and Rhetorical Propaganda Doctrine of Inverting Victim and Aggressor* analyzes one of the most lethal and effective propaganda techniques identified in the history of mass violence: **Accusation in a Mirror** (*l'accusation en miroir*).

### Origins and Theoretical Mechanics

First formulated in a 1992 political treatise found among the archives of Hutu Power extremists prior to the Rwandan Genocide, the tactic functions through psychological projection:
1. **The Inversion Principle:** The aggressor falsely accuses the targeted victim population of plotting their total annihilation or extermination.
2. **Psychological Preemption:** By convincing one's own population that an existential, genocidal massacre is imminent, extreme violence against the target is reframed not as an aggressive crime, but as a morally mandatory act of self-defense.
3. **Comparative Analysis:** The dossier compares the RTLM radio broadcasts in Rwanda (claiming the Tutsis were preparing to exterminate all Hutus) with contemporary political rhetoric justifying total civilian blockades and carpet bombing in the Levant.

### Epistemic Warning

The treatise serves as a vital analytical guide for detecting the linguistic precursors to ethnic cleansing and war crimes.
