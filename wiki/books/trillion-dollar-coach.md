## Executive Summary

Written by former Google CEO **Eric Schmidt**, former SVP of Products **Jonathan Rosenberg**, and Google executive communications director **Alan Eagle**, with a Foreword by [[adam-grant]] (2019, Contents p. 2, Foreword p. 8; 168 pp.), *Trillion Dollar Coach* distills the leadership principles of **Bill Campbell** (1940–2016)—the former Columbia University football coach turned Intuit CEO who mentored Steve Jobs, Larry Page, Sergey Brin, Eric Schmidt, Sheryl Sandberg, Sundar Pichai, Jeff Bezos, and Ben Horowitz, helping build well over a trillion dollars in collective market value.

Shunning publicity to keep the spotlight on his players (Foreword, p. 8), Campbell operated as a relentless truth-teller and community builder. Across six tightly distilled chapters—*The Caddie and the CEO*, *Your Title Makes You a Manager. Your People Make You a Leader.*, *Build an Envelope of Trust*, *Team First*, *The Power of Love*, and *The Yardstick*—the authors codify Campbell's playbook for high-trust, people-first executive teams.

---

## Core Frameworks & Insights

1. **People First & Operational Rigor (Ch. 2)**: Managers exist to support, respect, and build trust with their people; start staff meetings with trip reports and personal check-ins, run structured 1:1s, and never let the "elephant in the room" go unspoken.
2. **The Envelope of Trust & Free-Form Listening (Ch. 3)**: Coaches only the *coachable* (honest, humble, willing to work hard and learn), practices radical candor paired with deep caring, and asks probing questions rather than issuing commands.
3. **Team First & The Power of Love (Ch. 4–5)**: Works the team first, then the problem; picks players for smarts, grit, integrity, and "team-first" attitude; tolerates brilliant "aberrant geniuses" only when their value outweighs toxicity; and normalizes genuine human affection, loyalty, and family care inside high-performance tech companies.
