## Executive Summary

Authored by renowned Istanbul University historian **Prof. Dr. İbrahim Kafesoğlu** (1914–1984), *Türk Millî Kültürü* (*Turkish National Culture*) is the preeminent classical academic synthesis of pre-Islamic and early medieval Turkic culture, society, state-structure, and worldview. Kafesoğlu systemizes archaeology, Chinese annals, Byzantine reports, and Orkhon runic inscriptions to articulate the unique concept of the **Eurasian Steppe Empire (*Bozkır Kültürü*)**, distinguishing its egalitarian, meritocratic, and dynamic nomadic ethos from sedentary Mesopotamian and Chinese agrarian despotisms.

## Key Theses & Analytical Frameworks

1. **The Steppe Empire as an Autonomous Civilizational Type**: Rejects the Eurocentric view that nomadic steppe peoples were 'barbarians' lacking culture, proving they created an advanced, distinct socio-political civilization with sophisticated constitutional law (*Töre*).
2. **Kut and the Concept of Sovereignty**: Analyzes *Kut* as a divine favor granting charismatic authority to the Kağan, conditioned on providing justice, food, and clothes to the people—a conceptual precursor to the social contract.
3. **Egalitarian Social Fabric**: Demonstrates that steppe geography precluded hereditary landed aristocracies or slave-based agrarian economies, fostering personal independence, martial valor, and high social standing for women.
