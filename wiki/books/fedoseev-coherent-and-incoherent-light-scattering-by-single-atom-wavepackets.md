## Executive Summary

This 6-page quantum optics and atomic physics paper by Vitaly Fedoseev, Hanzhen Lin, and collaborators investigates the fundamental quantum mechanics of coherent and incoherent light scattering from localized single-atom wavepackets. Moving beyond the classical dipole approximation where atoms are treated as point particles pinned in space, the authors formulate a rigorous quantum field theoretical treatment that incorporates the atomic center-of-mass motional wavepacket and photon recoil momentum. The study demonstrates how atomic spatial delocalization and wavepacket dispersion alter the spectral interference between Rayleigh (elastic coherent) and Mollow (inelastic incoherent) fluorescence scattering, providing critical precision limits for optical tweezer neutral-atom quantum processors and quantum networking interfaces.

---

## Key Strategic, Scientific & Engineering Takeaways

- **Quantum Wavepacket Recoil Effects**: Formulates the exact light-matter scattering Hamiltonian accounting for the atomic center-of-mass momentum transfer, demonstrating that photon emission entangles atomic internal electronic states with external motional wavepackets.
- **Coherence Suppression via Spatial Delocalization**: Proves that atomic zero-point motion in optical tweezers and dipole traps induces a fundamental dephasing mechanism in Rayleigh scattering, reducing the coherent scattering fraction relative to fixed-point models.
- **Implications for Neutral-Atom Quantum Gates**: Provides quantitative tolerance boundaries for motional temperatures in neutral-atom quantum computing arrays (e.g., QuEra, Harvard architectures), establishing required vibrational ground-state cooling thresholds.
- **Photon-Qubit Networking Fidelity**: Analyzes how motional wavepacket recoil degrades the indistinguishability of single photons emitted from atomic nodes in quantum repeater networks, limiting remote entanglement distribution fidelity.
- **Experimental Test Protocols**: Proposes high-resolution laser spectroscopic protocols utilizing single trapped ytterbium or rubidium atoms in ultra-high vacuum optical cavities to detect wavepacket recoil signatures directly.
