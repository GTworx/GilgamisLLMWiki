## Summary
An exhaustive automotive engineering and institutional equity research compendium combining Ricardo Inc.'s comprehensive 66-page cradle-to-grave Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) benchmarking battery electric vehicles (BEVs) against internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles across diverse global grids, alongside Wall Street institutional equity analyses by Goldman Sachs and UBS dissecting Tesla's automotive profit margins, Robotaxi timelines, and Full-Self-Driving (FSD) commercialization.

## Analytical Overview
### Historical Context & Institutional Setting
Between 2022 and 2025, the global transition to electric mobility reached a pivotal geopolitical and financial inflection point. Skeptics questioned the net carbon benefit of BEVs given battery manufacturing emissions, while automotive OEMs engaged in an intense EV price war. In early 2025, equity analysts scrutinized Tesla as automotive profit margins compressed and valuation multiples became increasingly dependent on its end-to-end neural network autonomy architecture (FSD V12/V13).

### Core Theoretical Foundations & Methodological Architecture
The compendium unites ISO 14040/14044 Life Cycle Assessment methodology with institutional discounted cash flow (DCF) and sum-of-the-parts equity valuation models. Ricardo models the carbon footprint across vehicle extraction, cell chemistry manufacturing (NMC vs LFP), logistics, in-use grid emissions (measuring marginal grid carbon intensity across US, European, and Asian grids), and end-of-life battery recycling (pyrometallurgical vs hydrometallurgical recovery). Goldman Sachs and UBS evaluate Tesla's corporate transition from an automotive manufacturer into an AI and robotics platform.

### Key Empirical Findings, Case Studies & Frameworks
Ricardo's empirical findings definitively resolve the EV environmental debate: while BEV manufacturing produces 50–70% higher upfront greenhouse gas emissions than ICE manufacturing due to battery cell production, the operational efficiency of electric drivetrains creates a carbon breakeven point after just 15,000 to 25,000 miles of driving on average European and US electric grids. Over a 150,000-mile vehicle lifespan, a BEV generates 50% to 70% fewer lifetime GHG emissions than an equivalent gasoline vehicle. The Wall Street reports detail Tesla's 2025 financial cross-currents: automotive gross margins (excluding regulatory credits) compressing to ~14–15%, offset by multi-billion-dollar potential in robotaxi mobility networks and humanoid robotics (Optimus).

### Critical Assessment, Methodological Rigor & Strategic Implications
This compendium provides an extraordinary bridge between physical environmental engineering and capital market pricing. It definitively establishes the environmental superiority of battery electrification while providing a realistic, sober appraisal of the commercialization hurdles facing autonomous robotaxis.
