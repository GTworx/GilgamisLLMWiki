## Executive Summary

Written by University of Melbourne classicist and Eurasian historian **Hyun Jin Kim** (Routledge, *Peoples of the Ancient World*, 2016, Copyright p. 5, Contents pp. 6–7; 177 pp., visually verified), *The Huns* dismantles the Eurocentric footnote casting the Huns as uncultured destroyers of Rome. Treating the Huns as a **pan-Eurasian political phenomenon** spanning China, Iran, India, and Europe, Kim synthesizes Chinese dynastic records, Sogdian letters, numismatics, archaeology, and Greco-Roman historiography into a unified eight-chapter narrative.

Tracing their lineage from **The Xiongnu Hun Empire** in Inner Asia (Ch. 1) through the **Two-Hundred Years Interlude** (Ch. 2) and the **Kidarite and Hephthalite White Huns** of Central and South Asia (Ch. 3) to **The Huns of Europe** (Ch. 4), **Attila the Hun** (Ch. 5), post-Attila successor kingdoms (Ch. 6), and the **Utigur-Kutrigur 'Bulgar' Huns, Oghurs, and Avars** of the Pontic steppes (Ch. 7), Kim argues that the Huns exported Inner Asian decimal military-administrative statecraft straight into early medieval Europe (Ch. 8).

---

## Core Historical Arguments

1. **Pan-Eurasian Xiongnu–White Hun–European Hun Continuity (Ch. 1–4)**: Marshals Sogdian, Chinese, and archaeological evidence (cauldrons, composite bows, cranial deformation, political titles) to link European Huns and Central Asian Kidarite/Hephthalite Huns to the political heritage of the Xiongnu empire.
2. **Heterogeneous Agro-Pastoralist Statecraft (Introduction & Ch. 4–5)**: Refutes the caricature of chaotic nomad hordes, showing that Attila ruled a sophisticated, multi-ethnic Inner Asian dual-wing empire incorporating Germanic, Alanic, Iranian, and Turkic elites.
3. **Redrawing Early Medieval Europe (Ch. 7–8)**: Credits Hunnic expansion with catalyzing the collapse of the Western Roman Empire, forging early Germanic kingdoms (Ostrogoths, Gepids, Franks) modeled partly on Inner Asian aristocratic norms, and inaugurating Bulgar and Avar statehood.
