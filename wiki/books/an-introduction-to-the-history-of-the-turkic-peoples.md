## Executive Summary

Written by Rutgers University historian **Peter B. Golden** and dedicated to Tibor Halasi-Kun (Otto Harrassowitz, Wiesbaden, 1992, pp. 1–3; 494 pp.; consolidated with the byte-identical duplicate in `Turkiye and Region/`), *An Introduction to the History of the Turkic Peoples* is universally recognized as the standard English-language academic reference on Turkic ethnogenesis, linguistic classification, and nomadic state-formation across two millennia of Eurasian history.

Opening with a masterly analytical framework on **The World of the Steppes** (Introduction, pp. 1–14)—deconstructing simplistic myths of monolithic bloodlines by showing how steppe "tribes" functioned as fluid political-military confederations—Golden maps all branches of the **Turkic language family** (Oğuz, Qıpçaq, Qarluq/Türkî, Oğuric/Bulğar, Siberian, Yakut, Ch. I) before tracing Eurasia's imperial succession: Iranian steppe nomads, Xiongnu (Hsiung-nu), Hsien-pi, Jou-Jan (Avar) Qağanate, European Huns and Oğuric Bulğars (Ch. II–IV), the **First and Second Türk (Göktürk) Qağanates** (Ch. V), Uyğurs, Qırğız, **Khazars**, **Qarakhanids**, **Seljuks**, **Qıpçaqs/Cumans**, Mongol-Chinggisid synthesis, and the early modern Ottoman, Timurid, Uzbek, and Kazakh formations.

---

## Core Historical Frameworks

1. **Ethnogenesis as Political Coalition, Not Static Biology**: Explains how Inner Asian steppe empires constantly absorbed, renamed, and linguistically Turkicized Iranian, Mongolic, Uralic, and Tocharian populations around charismatic royal clans (such as the Ashina).
2. **Nomad–Sedentary Symbiosis**: Rejects crude stereotypes of pure nomad raiding, showing how Turkic qağanates relied on Silk Road trade, Sogdian scribes, diplomatic tribute, and agrarian borderlands to fund imperial hierarchies.
3. **Philological & Multi-Archival Mastery**: Synthesizes Chinese dynastic annals, Old Turkic Orkhon runiform inscriptions, Byzantine chronicles, Arabic/Persian geographers, Slavic primary chronicles, and Armenian/Georgian sources into a unified Eurasia-wide narrative.
