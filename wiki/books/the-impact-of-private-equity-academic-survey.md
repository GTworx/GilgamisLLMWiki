## Executive Summary

Authored by leading finance scholars **Morten Sorensen** (Tuck School of Business at Dartmouth) and **Ayako Yasuda** (Graduate School of Management, UC Davis), this peer-reviewed academic survey delivers a comprehensive meta-analysis of the empirical corporate finance literature on the **real economic impacts of private equity buyouts and venture capital**. Moving beyond polarized political narratives, Sorensen and Yasuda synthesize hundreds of empirical studies spanning forty years across the U.S., Europe, and emerging markets, rigorously evaluating how PE interventions affect portfolio company productivity, employment, wages, patenting innovation, financial distress risk, and LP investor returns.

---

## Key Strategic & Financial Takeaways

- **Operational vs. Financial Engineering (The Value Creation Decomposition)**: Finds that while early 1980s buyouts generated returns primarily through tax shields and multiple expansion, contemporary PE returns are increasingly driven by operational engineering—specifically operational streamlining, accelerated IT adoption, commercial sales-force optimization, and strategic add-on M&A.
- **Total Factor Productivity (TFP) Gains**: Robust micro-data demonstrates that PE-backed manufacturing and service firms exhibit statistically significant gains in Total Factor Productivity (typically 2%–4% higher than matched control firms within three years of acquisition), largely driven by reallocating resources away from underperforming units toward high-return core segments.
- **The Nuanced Employment Reality**: Reconciles conflicting employment findings: buyouts of mature public companies (public-to-private LBOs) routinely result in net job shedding (especially in routine administrative roles), whereas buyouts of private mid-market firms (private-to-private transactions) and growth equity deals frequently lead to net employment growth.
- **Innovation and Patenting Velocity**: Finds that private equity ownership does not suppress patenting; instead, PE-backed firms streamline their R&D portfolios, abandoning diffuse exploratory research to produce more focused, highly cited, and commercially valuable patents.
- **Bankruptcy Risk and Capital Structure Resiliency**: Acknowledges that higher leverage increases the baseline probability of financial distress (bankruptcy rates of PE-backed firms are ~2–3 percentage points higher than matched peers), but highlights that PE sponsors frequently rescue distressed assets through debt-for-equity swaps and injection of emergency sponsor capital.

## Notable Excerpts & Citations

- "The empirical academic literature reveals private equity to be neither purely a heroic agent of operational rejuvenation nor purely a predatory asset-stripper; it is a powerful governance mechanism that aggressively accelerates economic restructuring and resource reallocation." (source: Sorensen & Yasuda 2022, Abstract, p. 1)
- "Where private equity succeeds, it does so by breaking through bureaucratic corporate inertia, aligning managerial equity incentives, and installing specialized operational expertise that public corporate boards rarely possess." (source: Sorensen & Yasuda 2022, p. 44)
