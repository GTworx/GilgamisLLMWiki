# Theme: Political Intuitionism, The Administrative State, and Capitalist Democracy

**Summary**: Structural and cognitive tensions inside modern constitutional democracies: intuitive vs. rationalist epistemology, unitary-executive battles over the bureaucracy, and corporate capital's dual role in enabling yet constraining political equality.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-24

---

## Synthesis
**Political Intuitionism, The Administrative State, and Capitalist Democracy** brings together four complementary diagnostics of contemporary American and liberal-democratic governance.

Starting from the constitutional baseline compiled by [[nick-ragone]] in [[us-declaration-of-independence-and-constitution-appendix]], [[j-eric-oliver]] & [[thomas-j-wood]]'s [[enchanted-america]] uncovers an epistemic polarization splitting Rationalist and Intuitionist voters over how reality itself is known. On the institutional front, [[paul-dans]], [[steven-groves]], and [[kevin-d-roberts]]'s [[mandate-for-leadership-2025]] articulates a sweeping conservative blueprint to subordinate the federal Administrative State to direct presidential authority on Day One. Meanwhile, [[aleksandra-evelyn-petkovic]]'s [[the-relationship-between-capitalism-and-democracy]] synthesizes Friedman, Dahl, Lindblom, and Christiano to expose the structural paradox beneath both left and right debates: market private property protects citizen independence from the state, yet campaign-finance gatekeeping and business's 'privileged position' systematically imprison democratic reform—echoing the psychological traits of democratic vs. authoritarian leaders profiled in [[the-psychological-assessment-of-political-leaders]].
