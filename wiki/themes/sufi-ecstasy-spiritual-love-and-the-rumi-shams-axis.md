# Theme: Sufi Ecstasy, Spiritual Love, and the Rumi–Shams Axis

**Summary**: How the transformative encounter between Jalal al-Din Rumi and Shams-i Tabrizi forged an enduring mystical philosophy of *ishq* (love), ego-dissolution, ring-compositional poetry, and interfaith mercy.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-22

---

## Synthesis
**Sufi Ecstasy, Spiritual Love, and the Rumi–Shams Axis** examines one of world spirituality's most catalytic human relationships and the vast literary cosmos it unleashed.

Beginning with the raw primary voice of [[shams-i-tabrizi]] reconstructed by [[william-c-chittick]] ([[me-and-rumi-shams-i-tabrizi]]) and the 13th-century historical crucible mapped by [[afzal-iqbal]] ([[the-life-and-work-of-jalal-ud-din-rumi]]), the theme traces [[jalal-al-din-rumi]]'s masterwork across four distinct English lenses—[[jawid-mojaddedi]]'s rhyming Oxford verse ([[the-masnavi-books-one-and-two-mojaddedi]]), [[edward-henry-whinfield]]'s annotated 1898 abridgment ([[the-masnavi-whinfield]]), and [[coleman-barks]]'s colloquial anthologies ([[the-essential-rumi]], [[rumi-the-book-of-love]])—culminating in contemporary critical scholarship edited by [[leonard-lewisohn]] ([[the-philosophy-of-ecstasy-rumi-and-the-sufi-tradition]]).
