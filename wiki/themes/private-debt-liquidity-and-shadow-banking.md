## Synthesis

### Macroeconomic Transformation and Credit Engineering
The rapid migration of corporate lending from regulated commercial banks to private debt funds marks the most profound restructuring of global corporate credit since the birth of the junk bond market in the 1980s. Driven by post-2008 banking regulations (Dodd-Frank, Basel III) and regional banking vulnerabilities, private credit offers borrowers speed, execution certainty, and confidential bespoke covenant structuring at the cost of higher interest burdens.

However, prolonged elevated base rates introduce acute challenges: compressed interest coverage ratios, increased reliance on Payment-in-Kind (PIK) interest capitalization, and illiquid mark-to-model valuations that postpone rather than resolve fundamental solvency stress.
