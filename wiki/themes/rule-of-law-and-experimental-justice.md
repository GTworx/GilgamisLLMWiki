# Theme: Rule of Law, Legal Certainty, and Experimental Justice

**Summary**: The interplay between comparative legal philosophy (legal certainty, human rights, Hart vs. Dworkin) and quantitative/experimental criminology (hot-spots RCTs and trajectory modeling).
**Last updated**: 2026-11-01

---

## Synthesis
**Rule of Law, Legal Certainty, and Experimental Justice** integrates the doctrinal and philosophical architecture of law with empirical, place-randomized evaluation of criminal justice institutions.

In the wiki, [[jaap-hage]], [[antonia-waltermann]], and [[bram-akkermans]]'s [[introduction-to-law]] establishes the principles of legal certainty, *Trias Politica*, and comparative jurisprudence; [[alex-r-piquero]] and [[david-weisburd]]'s [[handbook-of-quantitative-criminology]] demonstrates how quantitative trajectory modeling and randomized field trials reform policing; and [[thomas-sowell]]'s [[a-conflict-of-visions-ideological-origins-of-political-struggles]] contrasts Constrained procedural justice with Unconstrained substantive justice.
