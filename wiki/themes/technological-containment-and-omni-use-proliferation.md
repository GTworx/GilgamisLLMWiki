# Theme: The Containment Problem, Omni-Use Proliferation, and AI Statecraft

**Summary**: Why general-purpose technologies characterized by asymmetry, hyper-evolution, omni-use, and autonomy resist historical containment, amplifying nation-state fragility between the poles of catastrophe and surveillance dystopia.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-03

---

## Synthesis
**The Containment Problem, Omni-Use Proliferation, and AI Statecraft** investigates why humanity has historically struggled to limit, control, or halt general-purpose technologies once their economic and geopolitical incentives are unleashed—and why the convergence of artificial intelligence, synthetic biology, quantum computing, and autonomous robotics makes containment the existential dilemma of the 21st century.

Anchored by DeepMind co-founder [[mustafa-suleyman]] and [[michael-bhaskar]]'s [[the-coming-wave-ai-power-and-our-future]] (2023), this theme identifies four structural features driving the coming wave: **omni-use** (dual-use across civilian and lethal domains), **hyper-evolution** (recursive compounding), **asymmetric impact** (empowering non-state actors and small teams to strike dominant powers), and **autonomy** (operating beyond human-in-the-loop oversight). Across the wiki, this framework dialogues directly with [[andrei-kucharavy]] et al.'s Swiss Cyber-Defence Campus treatise [[large-language-models-in-cybersecurity-threats-exposure-and-mitigation]] (2024), [[chris-miller]]'s [[chip-war-the-fight-for-the-worlds-most-critical-technology]], [[frans-osinga]] and [[tim-sweijs]]'s [[deterrence-in-the-21st-century]], [[leopold-aschenbrenner]]'s [[situational-awareness-the-decade-ahead]], and [[daniel-kokotajlo]] et al.'s [[ai-2027]].
