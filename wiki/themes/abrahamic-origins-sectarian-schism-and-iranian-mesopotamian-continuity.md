# Theme: Abrahamic Origins, Sectarian Schism, and Iranian–Mesopotamian Continuity

**Summary**: Tracing shared ancient Near Eastern flood, creation, and ethical motifs from Sumer and pre-Islamic Iran into Abrahamic scripture and the political birth of the Sunni–Shia divide.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-22

---

## Synthesis
**Abrahamic Origins, Sectarian Schism, and Iranian–Mesopotamian Continuity** illuminates the deep historical layers beneath modern Middle Eastern religious identities.

[[muazzez-ilmiye-cig]]'s [[kuran-incil-ve-tevratin-sumerdeki-kokeni]] traces shared creation, Eden, Flood, and Job motifs back to third-millennium BCE Sumerian tablets; the scholarly reviews by [[burzine-k-waghmar]] and [[dale-bishop]] on [[richard-c-foltz]] ([[spirituality-in-the-land-of-the-noble-reviews]]) probe Iran's pluralistic heritage from Zoroastrianism and Manichaeism to Shiism; and [[lesley-hazleton]]'s [[after-the-prophet-shia-sunni-split]] narrates the 632–680 CE succession crisis from Saqifa to Karbala that permanently shaped the Islamic world.
