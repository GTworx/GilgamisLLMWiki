# Theme: Music as Prophecy and Political Economy of Sound

**Summary**: How the organization of noise into music across ritual sacrifice, concert representation, mass-recorded repetition, and autonomous composition anticipates future political economies.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-01

---

## Synthesis
**Music as Prophecy and Political Economy of Sound** draws on [[jacques-attali]]'s [[noise-the-political-economy-of-music]] to show that because sound is fluid and rapidly reorganizable, changes in musical networks herald structural transformations in capitalism and state power decades before they solidify in material production.

Across the wiki, Attali's four networks—*Sacrificing*, *Representing*, *Repeating*, and *Composing*—dialogue with [[asik-borani]]'s Anatolian oral minstrelsy in [[asik-borani-soz-insanin-aynasi]], [[louise-bennett]]'s Jamaican creole broadcasts in [[mongrel-nation-colonization-in-reverse]], and [[thorsten-hennig-thurau]] & [[mark-b-houston]]'s cultural econometrics in [[entertainment-science-data-analytics-and-practical-theory]].
