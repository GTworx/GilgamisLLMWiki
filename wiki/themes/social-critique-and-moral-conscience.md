# Theme: Social Critique and Moral Conscience

**Summary**: The use of literature, satire (*taşlama*), and public testimony to denounce corruption, economic exploitation, hypocrisy, and ignorance while defending civic justice and integrity.  
**Last updated**: 2026-09-12  

---

## Synthesis

Across world literature and political thought, art functions as an indispensable mechanism of civic accountability and moral conscience. In the Anatolian bardic tradition, this role is formalized through the genre of *taşlama* (verse satire), where minstrels speak truth to power on behalf of disenfranchised communities.

In *Söz İnsanın Aynasıdır*, Âşık Borani exemplifies the minstrel as civic conscience. He views the poet as "the seeing eye, hearing ear, and speaking tongue of the people" (source: BOOKS/Art/73010,cilt1pdf.pdf, Intro, pp. 41–43). His poetry relentlessly censures societal parasites (*asalaklar*), corrupt officials, religious hypocrites (*yobazlar ve bezirgânlar*), and thieves regardless of their political or religious affiliation:

> *"Kim çalarsa çalsın adı hırsızdır / Ali’si, Veli’si, Bekir’i yoktur / Karnı haram dolu yüzü nursuzdur / Ömer’i, Osman’ı, Şakir’i yoktur"* (source: BOOKS/Art/73010,cilt1pdf.pdf, Intro, p. 42).

Borani identifies ignorance (*cehalet*) as humanity's most lethal enemy, contrasting it with scientific enlightenment and education. Following Hacı Bektaş Veli's famous maxim ("The end of a road not guided by science is darkness") and Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's principles of reason and progress, Borani argues that moral conscience must be anchored in reason, science, and transparent civic honesty (source: BOOKS/Art/73010,cilt1pdf.pdf, Intro, pp. 42–43; BOOKS/Art/73011,cilt2pdf.pdf, ch. 5, p. 568).

In dialogue with [[rationality-and-decision-making]], Borani’s social critique warns against opportunistic short-term gain achieved through moral degradation, affirming that an honorable life lived in poverty (*yamalı hırka ile dürüst yaşamak*) far surpasses corrupt prosperity.
