# Theme: Violent Non-State Actors

**Summary**: The theoretical concepts, operational methodologies, and challenges of applying deterrence, coercion, and counter-influence against terrorists, insurgent groups, and violent non-state actors (VNSAs).  
**Last updated**: 2026-08-23  

---

## Synthesis

Following the 9/11 attacks, a widespread consensus emerged among security analysts that violent non-state actors (VNSAs)—such as transnational terrorist groups, insurgent movements, and ideological militias—were "undeterrable" because they lacked return addresses, territory to hold hostage, or conventional rational cost calculations. Part III of *Deterrence in the 21st Century* systematically refutes this simplistic view, demonstrating how modern states apply tailored, localized, and cumulative deterrence against non-state adversaries (source: raw/Deterrence in 21st Century Nuclear NetherlandsAnnualReviewO.pdf, ch. 14–17, pp. 263–347; ch. 26, pp. 520–524).

### Key Dimensions of Non-State Deterrence
1. **Cumulative Deterrence & "Mowing the Lawn" (Eitan Shamir, ch. 14, pp. 263–285)**:
   * Analyzes Israel's deterrence relationship with Hamas and Hezbollah. While VNSAs with high ideological commitment cannot be permanently deterred from their ultimate goals, states can achieve long periods of tactical and operational quiescence through cumulative deterrence.
   * By imposing disproportionate costs on leadership, infrastructure, and military assets ("mowing the lawn"), the state raises the threshold required for the group to initiate hostilities.
2. **Localised Deterrence in Counterinsurgency (Martijn Kitzen & Christina van Kuijck, ch. 15, pp. 287–309)**:
   * Shifts deterrence from the macro-state level to the granular micro-level in irregular warfare.
   * Localised deterrence operates by understanding village-level social networks, tribal elders, economic patronage, and kinship systems. COIN forces deter local collaboration with insurgents by combining localized security denial with social and economic incentives.
3. **Deterring Democratic Revolts (Maarten Rothman, ch. 16, pp. 311–326)**:
   * Explores how authoritarian regimes (particularly the Russian Federation) use coercive demonstrations, selective domestic repression, and proxy warfare to deter civil society mobilization and popular uprisings ("color revolutions").
4. **Deterrence in Peace Operations (Peter Viggo Jakobsen, ch. 17, pp. 327–347)**:
   * Details how UN and multinational peacekeepers can deter local warlords and recalcitrant militias by looking beyond the immediate battlefield and deploying multi-target influence mechanisms—combining diplomatic isolation, economic interdiction, targeted sanctions, and decisive rapid-reaction military force (e.g., UNAMSIL in Sierra Leone).
