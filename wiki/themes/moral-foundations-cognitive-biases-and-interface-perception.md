# Theme: Moral Foundations, Cognitive Biases, and Interface Perception

**Summary**: Investigating how evolution shaped human sensory interfaces, intuitive heuristics, loss aversion, strategic game rationality, moral taste buds, and the long-run pacification of human violence.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-22

---

## Synthesis
**Moral Foundations, Cognitive Biases, and Interface Perception** gathers modern cognitive, evolutionary, and behavioral sciences' sharpest challenges to naive rationality and realism.

[[simon-grondin]]'s [[psychology-of-perception]] and [[donald-d-hoffman]]'s [[the-case-against-reality]] debate whether evolution tuned our senses for truth or fitness; [[michael-lewis]]'s [[the-undoing-project]] and [[hans-peters]]'s [[game-theory-a-multi-leveled-approach]] contrast empirical heuristics with formal equilibria; and [[jonathan-haidt]]'s [[the-righteous-mind]] and [[steven-pinker]]'s [[the-better-angels-of-our-nature]] examine how moral intuitions both divide political tribes and drive civilizational restraint.
