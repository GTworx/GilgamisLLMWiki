# Theme: Organizational Culture and Rewilding

**Summary**: The transformation of rigid, over-engineered corporate bureaucracies into thriving, psychologically safe human ecosystems through positive psychology, relational connection, simplicity, and frontline empowerment.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-01

---

## Synthesis
For over a century, industrial management treated organizations as machines to be optimized through standardization, surveillance, and top-down key performance indicators. While mechanistic control produced predictable outputs in stable markets, it systematically stripped workplaces of intrinsic motivation, trust, and psychological vitality—leaving modern enterprises brittle and exhausted.

In [[rapid-reculturing]] (2026), organizational psychologist [[alex-bailey]] and Chief People Officer [[kerri-oneill]] introduce **Organizational Rewilding**: an ecological and psychological paradigm inspired by conservation biology (such as the reintroduction of wolves to Yellowstone National Park, which restored the entire trophic cascade of rivers, forests, and wildlife; Chapter 1, pp. 15–28). Rather than imposing another exhausting 18-month top-down restructuring, Organizational Rewilding removes artificial bureaucratic dams ("process debt" and fear-based micromanagement) and reintroduces natural human behaviors across four interlocking pillars:
1. **Human (Whole-Person Authenticity)**: Replacing sterile "professional masks" with emotional literacy, strengths-based work design, and nervous-system safety, operationalized through the five-step **HUMAN Model** (**H**onesty, **U**nderstanding, **M**eaning, **A**utonomy, **N**urturing) ([[rapid-reculturing]], Chapters 3–5, pp. 49–98).
2. **Connect (Relational Energy & Cross-Silo Trust)**: Leveraging Barbara Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory of positive emotions and mirror-neuron contagion to turn every team check-in and **5-5-5 Coaching Conversation** into a generator of relational trust ([[rapid-reculturing]], Chapters 6–8, pp. 99–148).
3. **Simplicity (Subtracting Bureaucratic Friction)**: Auditing and dismantling redundant approval chains, performative reporting, and meeting bloat that drain cognitive bandwidth ([[rapid-reculturing]], Chapters 9–11, pp. 149–196).
4. **Empowerment (Distributed Agency)**: Shifting leaders from heroic bottleneck decision-makers to ecological stewards who grant frontline teams clear guardrails and autonomous ownership ([[rapid-reculturing]], Chapters 12–14, pp. 197–246).

This rewilding framework converges directly with [[amy-c-edmondson]]'s work on **psychological safety** and **intelligent failure** in [[leading-for-tomorrow]] (Chapter 20, pp. 247–256), [[jonathan-haidt]]'s analysis of rebuilding institutional trust and viewpoint pluralism across anxious generations ([[leading-for-tomorrow]], Chapter 23, pp. 281–291), and [[john-sanei]]'s call in [[the-adaptability-advantage]] to replace fear-driven corporate cultures with curiosity-driven learning ecosystems.
