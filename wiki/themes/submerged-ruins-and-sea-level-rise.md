# Theme: Submerged Ruins and Sea-Level Rise

**Summary**: The study of underwater megaliths, drowned coastal settlements, and drowned prehistoric landmasses submerged by the 120-meter global sea-level rise at the end of the last Ice Age.  
**Last updated**: 2026-09-12

---

## Synthesis

At the Last Glacial Maximum, sea levels were approximately 120 meters (400 feet) lower than today, exposing over 25 million square kilometers of fertile coastal plains and land bridges (Sundaland, Doggerland, the Persian Gulf basin, Kumari Kandam). When the ice sheets collapsed between 17,000 and 7,000 years ago during catastrophic meltwater pulses, these prime littoral habitats were rapidly inundated. Hancock's *Underworld* documents underwater explorations off the coasts of India (Dwarka, Gulf of Cambay/Khambhat, Poompuhar), Malta, and Japan (Yonaguni), arguing that the cradle of early maritime civilization now lies submerged on continental shelves.
