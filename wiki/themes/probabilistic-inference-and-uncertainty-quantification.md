# Theme: Probabilistic Inference, Epistemic Uncertainty, and Safe Sequential Decision-Making

**Summary**: Treating probability as the logic of uncertainty to decompose aleatoric noise from epistemic ignorance across Bayesian deep learning, Gaussian processes, active learning, clinical biostatistics, and safe reinforcement learning.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-03

---

## Synthesis
**Probabilistic Inference, Epistemic Uncertainty, and Safe Sequential Decision-Making** addresses a foundational flaw of standard point-estimate machine learning: high predictive accuracy without calibrated uncertainty can be catastrophic in safety-critical environments such as autonomous driving, medical diagnosis, and robotic control.

In [[probabilistic-artificial-intelligence]] (2025), ETH Zurich professors [[andreas-krause]] and [[jonas-hubotter]] unify Bayesian inference (Kalman filtering, Gaussian processes, Variational Inference via ELBO, and gradient-based MCMC / Langevin dynamics) with sequential decision-making (active learning via submodular mutual information, Bayesian optimization via Upper Confidence Bound and Thompson sampling, and safe model-based reinforcement learning). This probabilistic foundation directly connects with [[wolfgang-ertel]]'s maximum-entropy and Bayesian reasoning in [[introduction-to-artificial-intelligence]], [[miroslav-kubat]]'s Bayesian and ensemble classifiers in [[an-introduction-to-machine-learning]], [[ton-j-cleophas]] and [[aeilko-h-zwinderman]]'s clinical risk-binning and Markov chains in [[machine-learning-in-medicine-a-complete-overview]], and [[laura-igual]] & [[santi-segui]]'s statistical estimation and A/B testing in [[introduction-to-data-science-a-python-approach-to-concepts-techniques-and-applications]].
